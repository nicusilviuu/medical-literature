**No new guidelines from tracked societies today.** The guideline-titled sweep across 4–5 October returned
**eleven records and one borderline item**, a correction to an ultra-processed-foods review. Two dead windows
in a row.

So the **SFMU seam audit** was run instead — the first time, though SFMU had already co-signed four documents
reported here. It returned **seven Practice Guidelines in a five-year window, six of which had never been
reported.** Four are below. **All seven are in *Anaesthesia Critical Care & Pain Medicine*, which confirms
yesterday's refinement: ACCPM is the deposit route for French society guidance, and the society website is
only needed for documents that never reach a journal.**

**One correction to yesterday's entry comes out of this**, and it is in item 3.

## 1. SFAR / SFMU — emergency intubation of an adult outside the operating room and ICU

**Published:** *ACCPM*, **22 January 2026** — eight months old. Twenty-four experts, GRADE,
industry-independent, four rounds of scoring.
Clavier, Cesareo, Frasca, Adnet, Bonnet, Bruneau, Combes, Contenti, Feral-Pierssens, Galinski et al.

> **Design** A consensus committee of **24 experts** from SFAR and SFMU… **The potential drawbacks of making
> strong recommendations in the presence of low-quality evidence were emphasised.**
>
> **Results** … **32 recommendations** for adult patient intubation in emergency settings outside the
> operating room and intensive care unit. Among the formalised recommendations, **5 have high levels of
> evidence (GRADE 1), and 12 have low levels of evidence (GRADE 2). For 15 recommendations, the GRADE method
> could not be applied, resulting in expert opinions. 4 questions did not find any response in the
> literature.** After **4 rounds** of scoring and amendment, strong agreement was reached for all the
> recommendations.
>
> **Conclusions** There was strong agreement among experts for **36 recommendations**…

**Interpretation.** **This completes an airway triad, and the division of territory is the point.** On 30
September this watch reported [the SFAR adult airway recommendations for the operating
theatre](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/). This document covers intubation **outside theatre and
outside the ICU** — the prehospital setting and the emergency department. Two documents, same lead society,
explicitly non-overlapping domains, and the out-of-theatre one has the better evidence base: **5 of 32 at
GRADE 1 against 1 of 27 in theatre.**

That ordering is worth pausing on because it inverts what you would expect. **The more controlled
environment has the thinner evidence.** The likely reason is that emergency intubation has been the subject
of large randomised trials — videolaryngoscopy, preoxygenation, induction agents, bougie use — precisely
because the event rate is high and the population unselected, while elective theatre airway management has
outcomes too rare to randomise against.

**The methodological sentence deserves quoting on its own: "The potential drawbacks of making strong
recommendations in the presence of low-quality evidence were emphasised."** That is a panel saying, in its
own abstract, that it discussed the hazard of overclaiming. Set against [yesterday's
finding](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-04/) that a different French panel downgraded its whole
document class for the same reason, **a pattern is forming in which these panels treat the strength of their
own language as a decision to be made explicitly rather than a formality.**

**And this abstract settles something this watch had only inferred.** On 1 October, across six documents,
I concluded that **where a French panel reports questions it could not answer, the stated recommendation
total includes them** — an inference from a consistent pattern, which I flagged as not stated by any
document. **Here both numbers appear in one abstract: the Results say 32 recommendations, and 5 + 12 + 15 =
32 exactly; the Conclusion says 36, and 32 + 4 unanswered questions = 36.** The convention is now
demonstrated rather than inferred, by a document that states both figures and leaves the reader to reconcile
them.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2026.101744)

## 2. SFMU / SFAR / SFTH / GIHP — anticoagulant management in emergency settings

**Published:** *ACCPM*, **26 June 2025** — fifteen months old, 2 citations. French and Belgian experts from
four societies, **endorsed by the French Neurovascular Society.** GRADE with GRADE Grid voting, literature
from 1990, PRISMA, industry-independent.
Douillet, Godon, Rousseau, Ruiz, Vardon, De Maistre, Garrigue, Gouin, Gruel, Lanoiselée, Lapostolle et al.

> **Five areas were defined: (1) The role of laboratory testing in determining anticoagulant use and the
> level of anticoagulation; (2) Management of anticoagulant-associated bleeding; (3) Management of
> asymptomatic overdoses; (4) Management of non-elective invasive procedures on anticoagulants; and (5)
> Thrombolysis for acute ischaemic stroke on anticoagulants.**
>
> **Results** … **103 recommendations concerning 21 questions.** After two rounds of voting and several
> amendments, **strong agreement was reached on 97 recommendations.** Out of these, **19 have a high level of
> evidence (19 GRADE 1), 35 have a low level of evidence (35 GRADE 2), and 48 are expert opinions.** Finally,
> for one question, no recommendation could be made.

**Interpretation.** **One hundred and three recommendations is more than twice the largest document this
archive had catalogued** — the SRLF cardiogenic shock recommendations, at 41. And the evidence profile is
near the top of the table: **19 of 103 at GRADE 1 (18.4%), 48 expert opinion (46.6%)**, which places it
above cardiogenic shock and below perioperative nutrition.

| [SFAR/SFMU procedural sedation](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-02/) | 33 | 1 (3.0%) | 29 (87.9%) |
| [SRLF ICU nutrition, children](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-01/) | 29 | 1 (3.4%) | 23 (79.3%) |
| [SFAR in-hospital emergencies](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) | 36 | 0 | 28 (77.8%) |
| [SFMU/SRLF ED respiratory distress](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-04/) | 20 | 2 (10.0%) | 12 (60.0%) |

| [SFAR airway in theatre](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) | 27 | 1 (3.7%) | 13 (48.1%) |
| **SFAR/SFMU emergency intubation (item 1)** | **32** | **5 (15.6%)** | **15 (46.9%)** |
| **SFMU/SFAR anticoagulants (this item)** | **103** | **19 (18.4%)** | **48 (46.6%)** |
| [SRLF/SFC cardiogenic shock](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-01/) | 41 | 7 (17.1%) | 17 (41.5%) |
| [SFAR perioperative + ICU nutrition](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) | 40 | 11 (27.5%) | 16 (40.0%) |

**The same counting convention appears again: 19 + 35 + 48 = 102 against the stated 103, with one question
unanswerable.** Two documents in one entry, both confirming it.

**One number here is unusual and should not be skipped: strong agreement was reached on 97 of 103, so six
recommendations did not achieve it.** Almost every French document catalogued in the last week reports
strong agreement on 100% of its output. **A panel publishing six recommendations it did not strongly agree
on, and saying so, is more informative than one reporting unanimity** — and a reader of the full text should
want to know which six.

**Why this document matters most of the four.** This archive has been assembling an antithrombotic picture
from four directions and this is the piece that was missing:

- [The ESAIC/ESRA guideline](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-06/) on antithrombotics and **neuraxial
  procedures** — stopping them safely for anaesthesia.
- [The AHA scientific statement](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-02/) on the harms of **antithrombotic
  undertreatment** — omission, non-adherence, underdosing.
- [The SFAR/ANARLF peri-thrombectomy recommendations](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-04/), whose
  field 3 covers antiplatelet and anticoagulant management around thrombectomy.
- **This document: what to do when a patient on an anticoagulant arrives as an emergency** — bleeding,
  asymptomatic overdose, a procedure that cannot wait, and **thrombolysis in a stroke patient already
  anticoagulated.**

**Area 5 is the direct join to the stroke documents reported on 3 and 4 October**, and the French
Neurovascular Society's endorsement is the same society that co-signed the peri-thrombectomy guideline.
**Four documents, four decision contexts, one drug class** — and the thing worth noticing is that the AHA
statement is about giving too little while three of the four French documents are about safely giving less.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2025.101584)

## 3. SFMU / SFAR — mild traumatic brain injury, and a correction to yesterday's entry

**Published:** *ACCPM*, **5 June 2023** — **three years and four months old**, and **the most cited document
in today's entry (34 citations).** Twenty-two experts.
Gil-Jardiné, Payen, Bernard, Bobbia, Bouzat, Catoire, Chauvin, Claessens, Douay, Dubucs et al.

> **Given the impossibility of obtaining a high level of evidence for most of the recommendations, it was
> decided to adopt a "Recommendations for Professional Practice" (RPP) format, rather than a Formalized
> Expert Recommendation (FER) format**, and to formulate the recommendations using the terminology of the
> SFMU and SFAR Guidelines.
>
> **Three fields were defined: 1) pre-hospital assessment, 2) emergency room management, and 3) emergency
> room discharge modalities.** The group assessed **11 questions**… resulted in the formulation of **14
> recommendations.** After two rounds of rating, strong agreement was obtained for all. **For one question,
> no recommendation could be made.**

**Interpretation — and a correction first.** [Yesterday's entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-04/)
reported the SFAR/ANARLF peri-thrombectomy document as **"the first document in this archive whose panel
changed the class of thing it was writing because the evidence would not support the stronger class,"** and
said such a move is **"as far as this archive can tell, rare."**

**This document did the same thing, in the same year, for the same stated reason.** The peri-thrombectomy
guideline was published 1 January 2023 and this one 5 June 2023. **So the move was not unique and the
characterisation "rare" was an overreach from a sample of one.**

**The correct reading is better than the one I published, not worse.** **The French system has a recognised
lower tier — RPP, PPR, RBP — that panels select when the evidence will not carry a Formalized Expert
Recommendation.** That is a structural feature, not an act of individual conscience, and it is a feature
worth having: **the class of the document carries information about the evidence before a reader reaches any
GRADE table.** Two documents now, and the honest statement is that this is an available and used option
whose frequency this archive cannot yet estimate.

**On the content: field 3 is "emergency room discharge modalities," which is exactly the decision [the Brain
Injury Guidelines decision rule reported in yesterday's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-04/) exists
to support.** That meta-analysis found pooled sensitivity of 98.2–98.7% against specificity of 12.7–14.4%
for ruling out deterioration, intervention and death. **So: a French guideline from 2023 setting out how to
decide on discharge after mild head injury, and a 2026 meta-analysis of the American decision rule for the
same question, found and reported within two days of each other by different routes.** Reading them together
is the obvious next step and requires the full text of this one.

**Fourteen recommendations from 11 questions is a small document** — the smallest French one catalogued here
— and that is consistent with the RPP format: fewer, more cautiously worded statements.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2023.101260)

## 4. SFAR / SFCTCV / SPLF / SFMU — chest drainage for pleural effusion in critical care

**Published:** *ACCPM*, **23 April 2025** — seventeen months old. Fifteen experts from four societies
including the **French thoracic and cardiovascular surgery society (SFCTCV)** and the **French-language
pulmonology society (SPLF).** Four rounds of voting.
Bouhemad, Arbelot, Artaud-Macari, Brouchet, Collange, Froudarakis, Godier, Hamada, Garnier-Kepka et al.

> **Chest drainage is a very common procedure in critical care. It is performed by practitioners from various
> specialties in a wide range of clinical settings.** … **However, practices regarding chest tube insertion,
> monitoring, and removal vary considerably between institutions.** The objective … **(excluding purulent
> pleurisy, haemothorax, and malignant pleural effusion)**…
>
> **Four thematic areas: (1) diagnosis and consequences of pleural effusions, (2) procedure for chest tube
> placement, (3) monitoring the efficacy and complications of pleural drainage, (4) chest tube removal
> procedure.**
>
> **Results** A total of **25 recommendations**… After **four rounds of voting, strong agreement was reached
> for all 25**. **For three additional questions, no recommendation could be formulated.**

**Interpretation.** **The rationale in the first three sentences is the most honest statement of purpose in
today's entry: a very common procedure, done by many specialties, with practice varying considerably between
institutions.** That is a guideline written because of variation rather than because of new evidence, and
the four areas follow the procedure from indication to tube removal — including **removal**, which is the
step least often protocolised and the one where recurrence and pneumothorax risk sit.

**The exclusions are doing heavy work and a reader must notice them: purulent pleurisy, haemothorax, and
malignant pleural effusion are all out.** Those are three of the commonest reasons a critical care patient
has a chest drain. **So this is guidance on draining simple effusions**, and applying it to an empyema or a
trauma haemothorax would be going outside the document.

**No GRADE breakdown is given.** GRADE was applied and the voting used the GRADE Grid, but the 25
recommendations are not classified by certainty, so this document cannot be placed in the table above —
**the fourth French document in a week with that limitation**, and like the others it is not available open
access, so the full text is the only route.

**Note the society mix.** SFCTCV is the cardiothoracic surgery society, which makes this the first chest
drainage document this archive has from the people who also operate on the chest, and **SPLF appears again**
— the pulmonology society that co-authored the SPILF community-acquired pneumonia update. **SPLF's own seam
audit is still unrun**, and two appearances as co-signatory now argue for running it.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2025.101527)

### The SFMU seam audit — complete, and three documents remain

Seven Practice Guidelines in the five-year window. Four reported above; **two were already sent** — [ED
respiratory distress](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-04/) and [oxygen
therapy](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-04/) — and [the SPILF community-acquired pneumonia
update](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-02/) also carries SFMU endorsement. **Three in-scope
documents remain unreported:**

- **Guidelines for the management of urgent obstetric situations in emergency medicine** (*ACCPM*, 5 August
  2022, 2,131 chars). Obstetric emergency care outside a delivery suite; in scope via obstetric anaesthesia
  and never covered here.
- **Guidelines for the choice of intravenous fluids for vascular filling** (*ACCPM*, 5 May 2022, 2,352
  chars). **Pairs directly with [the ESICM fluid guideline reported on 2
  September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-02/)**, which gave isotonic saline over albumin in
  traumatic brain injury at very low certainty.
- **Emergency management of patients with Glanzmann thrombasthenia: consensus** (*Orphanet Journal of Rare
  Diseases*, 29 June 2023). Rare bleeding disorder; borderline, logged.

**Also surfaced by the audit and not a guideline:** a national survey of **organisation and communication in
critical situations** (*International Journal for Quality in Health Care*, 1 January 2026, 1,887 chars) —
**routed to the brief**, where it belongs with the human-factors thread; and a **C-PIV study of peripheral
intravenous catheter use in French emergency departments** (*Lancet Regional Health*, 10 April 2026, in PMC),
also for the brief.
