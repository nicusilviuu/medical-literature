**A new guideline from a tracked society — the first in some time.** The ingest stall that ran from 24
to 27 September [broke overnight](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-28/), and the wide guideline sweep
across the recovered 24–28 September window returned **85 hits**, from which two documents are in
scope. **One of them is co-authored by the Society of Critical Care Medicine**, which is on this
project's tracked list.

**The French sweep, run across the now-healthy window, returned zero.** Combined with the control
established on [25 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-25/) — the same query returning 13
hits over 1–23 September, most recent **18 September** — **SFAR, SPILF, SRLF and their journals have
now published nothing at all for ten days**, and have produced no guideline output at any point during
this archive's daily tracking in September.

## 1. NCS/SCCM: antithrombotic-associated intracranial haemorrhage

**Societies · Published:** **Neurocritical Care Society** with the **Society of Critical Care
Medicine**. *Neurocritical Care*, **24 September 2026** — **four days old**
Vallejo, Kennedy, Dengler, Barthol, **Belley-Cote**, Burns, Chau, **Cordonnier**, **Cuker**, Honarmand,
Mahmoud, **Wilcox**, Kumar

A **focused update** of the 2016 guidelines, built on **five PICO questions** — three updating prior
recommendations, two new — with a **systematic review and meta-analysis** and **GRADE** methodology.
The questions: andexanet alfa versus 4F-PCC for oral factor Xa inhibitor effects; platelet transfusion
versus none with antiplatelet agents; desmopressin versus none; treating anticoagulant effects in small
intraparenchymal haemorrhage; and viscoelastic haemostatic assays in traumatic intracranial
haemorrhage.

**Eight recommendations, all conditional:**

> …use **4F-PCC rather than andexanet alfa** for the treatment of the anticoagulant effects of factor
> Xa inhibitors, **avoid platelet transfusion in non-neurosurgical patients with spontaneous
> intraparenchymal haemorrhage on antiplatelet agents**, **administer platelet transfusion in patients
> with spontaneous IPH on aspirin who are undergoing surgery**, and **use viscoelastic haemostatic
> assays to guide treatment of coagulopathy in traumatic intracranial haemorrhage.**

And, unusually prominently, what they would not say:

> The panel was **unable to issue recommendations** regarding **platelet transfusion for treatment of
> antiplatelet effects in traumatic ICH**, **use of desmopressin** for treatment of antiplatelet
> effects, or **treatment of anticoagulant effects in small IPH.**

**Interpretation.** A substantial document, and the recommendation that will generate most argument is
the first one.

**Recommending 4F-PCC over andexanet alfa is a statement against the specific agent.** Andexanet alfa
is the licensed, purpose-built reversal agent for factor Xa inhibitors; 4F-PCC is the older,
non-specific, considerably cheaper alternative. **A guideline panel using GRADE and preferring the
non-specific agent is saying the specific one has not earned its place** — and it is conditional, not
strong, which is the appropriate grade for a contested comparison. **For an intensivist this is the
single most actionable line in the document**, because it concerns a drug given in the first minutes of
a life-threatening bleed and the two options differ substantially in cost and availability.

**The two platelet recommendations are in opposite directions, and the distinction is surgical.**
**Avoid** transfusion in non-neurosurgical patients with spontaneous haemorrhage on antiplatelets;
**give** it to patients on aspirin who are **going to surgery**. That is a coherent position — the
evidence against routine platelet transfusion in spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage is reasonably
established, while a patient about to have their skull opened is a different bleeding problem — and
**it is exactly the kind of distinction that gets lost when a recommendation is summarised as "don't
give platelets."**

**The three refusals are the mark of quality.** A panel that ran five questions and declined to answer
on desmopressin, on platelets in traumatic ICH, and on treating anticoagulation in small bleeds is
reporting the shape of the evidence rather than filling the gaps with opinion. **This archive has spent
the month cataloguing documents that did fill those gaps** — the
[videolaryngoscopy guidelines](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-21/) reaching strong consensus on
low-quality evidence, [GPR-WEAN](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-22/) generating a 0–140 score
from five patients. **This is the other behaviour, and it should be named as such.** It is the same
choice the [Latin-American tracheostomy decannulation
panel](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-27/) made when it declined to set a cough peak flow
threshold.

**And it arrives with the fragility question live.** [Today's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-28/)
carries a survey finding that randomised trials cited in perioperative guidelines have a **median
fragility index of 4**. **Eight conditional recommendations, three refusals, and GRADE throughout is
what a panel looks like when it takes that problem seriously.**

*Two related records worth noting: a **commentary** by Frontera published alongside it (no abstract
deposited), and a **preprint version of the full guideline** posted on 4 March 2026
(10.21203/rs.3.rs-8928593/v1) whose abstract is identical. **The preprint is not cited here as a
separate document** — it is the same work — but its existence means the recommendations have been
publicly available for nearly seven months.*

## 2. AATS 2026 revised recommendations on early-stage lung cancer

**Society · Published:** **American Association for Thoracic Surgery**. *Journal of Thoracic and
Cardiovascular Surgery*, **25 September 2026** — **three days old.** Indexed as a **Practice Guideline**
Kidane, Bott, Spicer, Backhus, Chaft, Chudgar, Colson, **D'Amico**, David, Lee, Najmeh, **Sepesi**,
Shu, Yang, Lee, Fiset and colleagues

A revision of the **2023 AATS expert consensus**, which had established chemoimmunotherapy as standard
of care for locally advanced patients without targetable mutations.

> Key modifications made to the previous version reflect **additional randomized trials supporting the
> efficacy of pre-operative or peri-operative chemoimmunotherapy, adjuvant targeted therapy for
> eligible patients, and evolving practice standards regarding adjuvant immunotherapy** for previously
> untreated patients.

> …the current document continues to support the critical need for **accurate diagnosis and staging of
> non-metastatic patients, including an emphasis on molecular diagnostics**, as well as
> **multi-disciplinary evaluation of patients who are eligible for multi-modality treatment.**

**Interpretation.** A thoracic oncology document, and it matters to this project for two reasons
neither of which is oncological.

**First, and structurally: the AATS was not on this project's society sweep.** The tracked
cardiothoracic bodies were STS, EACTS and IACTS. **The American Association for Thoracic Surgery is one
of the two major North American cardiothoracic societies**, and it publishes practice guidelines in
*JTCVS*. **That is the fifth society-list gap this archive has found in a month** — after ERC/ILCOR,
ISHLT, SEDAR and ITACTAIC — and unlike the others it is not a non-Anglophone or subspecialty body but a
large, central, English-language society. **AATS is now on the sweep.** The lesson recorded earlier
holds: an acronym list assembled once is a list that decays.

**Second, and clinically: neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy changes who arrives in theatre.** This is my
inference rather than a claim the document makes, and it is worth stating explicitly. A revision whose
central modification is **more pre-operative and peri-operative chemoimmunotherapy** means more
thoracic surgical patients presenting after immune checkpoint blockade — with the toxicity profile that
carries, including **pneumonitis, myocarditis and colitis** — and with the altered tissue planes and
fibrosis that follow a response to neoadjuvant treatment. **A document about oncological sequencing is
also a document about who the thoracic anaesthetist will be anaesthetising**, and nothing in the
abstract addresses that.

**Third, the multidisciplinary thread continues.** *"Multi-disciplinary evaluation of patients who are
eligible for multi-modality treatment"* is now the **fifth** specialty in a month to place a standing
multidisciplinary structure at the centre of its recommendations, after the endocarditis team, the
heart team, integrating nephrology into ECMO programmes, and the SECTCV/SEDAR **aortic team**. The
pattern remains more informative than any single instance.

*Access note: the abstract describes **what changed and why**, which is genuinely useful, but **contains
no individual recommendation** — no staging algorithm, no sequencing rule, no eligibility criteria. The
full text was not reachable.*

[Read the recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtcvs.2026.08.024) · PMID 42790620
