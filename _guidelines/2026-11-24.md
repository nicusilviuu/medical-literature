## 1. The document, and why it was nearly not found

**The index route behaved exactly as designed and still produced nothing.** The pubType route returned 32
guidance records indexed between 15 and 24 November. The scope filter cut them to one, and the dedupe log
recognised that one — the international expert consensus statement on postoperative delirium in cardiac
surgery, `10.1016/j.athoracsur.2026.10.056` — as already reported in the 20 November entry. **On the tools
alone, today was an empty day.**

**Reading the 31 discarded records by eye changed that.** Among them: `10.1016/j.healun.2026.09.024`,
pubType `Practice Guideline`, Journal of Heart and Lung Transplantation, first published 5 November 2026,
**a consensus statement from the International Society for Heart and Lung Transplantation.** ISHLT has been
a tracked society in this archive since 13 September.

**Both routes of the scope filter missed it, for two separate reasons.** The journal route is matched
against the record's journal abbreviation, and the guidance journal list did not contain
`j heart lung transplant`. The title route looks for scope terms, and the title says "Thoracic
Transplantation", which is not "thoracic surgery" and matches nothing in the term list. **A tracked society
with a journal missing from the list and a vocabulary outside the term list is invisible to the whole
filter, and the 20 November recall test could not have caught it** — that test asserts the five EACTS
documents survive, and EACTS publishes in a journal that is on the list.

**The list now also carries** `j heart lung transplant` **along with** `interact cardiovasc thorac surg`,
`eur heart j` **and** `ann intensive care`, each of which is a journal a tracked society publishes guidance
in and none of which was there this morning.

## 2. What the statement says

ISHLT convened its **second Transplant Nursing Consensus Conference on 26 April 2025 in Boston**. The
premise stated in the document is that thoracic transplantation has grown more complex — new donor
technologies, higher-acuity candidates and recipients — and that the resulting demands fall on transplant
coordinators and Advanced Practice Registered Nurses, including Advanced Nurse Practitioners in some
international centres.

**The method was explicitly multi-source:** a pre-conference international survey of transplant nursing
professionals, two virtual international roundtables with nurses from eight countries, and four facilitated
in-person breakout sessions. **The document states that findings converged across all three sources**,
which is the sort of claim worth noting because it is checkable and because consensus conferences do not
always make it.

**The threats it names are organisational, not clinical:** incremental accumulation of duties without
resource relief; administrative and after-hours burden; role ambiguity between transplant coordinators and
APRNs; and absent career structures. International input showed wide variation in roles, prescribing rights
and care models, while shared priorities held across countries — retention, role clarity, work-life balance
and recognition.

Seven themes organise the recommendations: staffing models and workload; work environment and retention;
workforce roles and qualifications; professional development and impact measurement; clinical processes and
protocols; technology and innovation; and patient education access.

**What the panel endorsed, as listed:** acuity-driven staffing ratios; hybrid call structures with protected
recovery; offloading non-clinical tasks to dedicated personnel; competency-based career ladders linked to
certification and compensation; standardised nurse-led protocols with clear escalation; digital tools that
reduce documentation duplication; and a universal modular patient education curriculum.

**The clinically significant part, and the honest limit on it.** The endorsements that bind are the ones
with a number or a mechanism attached — acuity-driven ratios, career ladders tied to certification and
compensation, protected recovery after call — because those can be audited. **The document gives no
thresholds for any of them in its abstract**: no ratio, no hours, no certification level. **A staffing
recommendation without a number is a direction of travel, and it is reported here as one.** The abstract
calls the output "an internationally informed blueprint", which is the right register for it.

## 3. A pointer, not an item

The same sweep returned `10.1016/j.healun.2026.08.002`, issue-dated 1 December 2026, carrying no abstract in
the index: **"Engagement of Social Support Systems in Patients Being Considered for and Following Advanced
Heart and Lung Surgical Therapies: A Perspective on an ISHLT Expert Consensus Statement."**

**This is secondary — a perspective about guidance, not guidance** — so it is not an item here.

**CORRECTED 25 November.** This section first said the perspective names an underlying ISHLT Expert
Consensus Statement that this archive does not hold, and added the hunt for it as an action. **The archive
holds it.** `_guidelines/2026-10-10.md` carries "Engagement of Social Support Systems in Patients Being
Considered for and Following Advanced Heart and Lung Surgical Therapies: An ISHLT Expert Consensus
Statement" as a full item, published 10 October 2026, with its abstract quoted. **The dedupe log was right
that the perspective itself was unreported; the error was mine, and it was an omission — I searched for the
commentary and never for its subject.** The action is struck. **A commentary is evidence that a document
exists, not evidence that it is missing here.**

## 4. The recall test had its fixture in a scratch directory

**The same failure as this morning, discovered a second time within one run.** `scope_filter.py` ships a
`--recall-test` that asserts all five EACTS consensus documents survive the filter. **The test is in the
repository. Its input file was in `/tmp/claude-0/` and went with the container.** So the guard on the
instrument I had just changed could not be run at the moment it was most needed.

**It is now `claude/fixtures/recall-eacts.json`, committed, and `--recall-test` defaults to it.** The
patched filter passes it on both journal lists, **5 of 5 EACTS documents retained**.

**One caveat recorded in the code and repeated here: the fixture is a reconstruction.** The original input
held 304 records and this one holds 37 — the five EACTS documents plus today's 32 guidance records as
decoys. **The pass on the five is meaningful. The 37 to 8 ratio is not comparable to the 304 to 16 figure of
20 November, and must not be quoted as though it were.**
