## The pattern completes: 48 recommendations across three French-speaking societies, 10 of them gradeable

**Three days ago this watch noticed that a French society guideline reported its grade distribution and its
abandoned questions in the abstract. Two days ago a second did the same. Today's GFRUP guideline is the third,
and the three together are worth adding up.**

| Document | Recommendations | Graded | Expert opinion | Questions abandoned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SFAR / SOFMER, withdrawing life-sustaining treatment ([19 Oct](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-19/)) | 9 | **3** (1 GRADE 1, 2 GRADE 2) | 6 | 2 |
| SRLF, atrial fibrillation in the adult ICU ([20 Oct](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-20/)) | 18 | **2** (GRADE 2+) | 16 | 6 |
| **GFRUP, supraventricular tachycardia in the paediatric ICU (today)** | **21** | **5** | **16** | not stated |
| **Total** | **48** | **10 (20.8%)** | **38 (79.2%)** | **at least 8** |

**Four fifths of the recommendations in three French-speaking society guidelines are expert opinion, by the
documents' own accounting, because GRADE could not be applied to the evidence.** **And all three say so in the
abstract, which is the part that matters.** **Two of the three independently arrive at exactly sixteen expert
opinions**, which is coincidence and should be read as nothing more.

**Three observations, in descending order of confidence.**

**First, this is a property of the questions, not of the societies.** Withdrawing life-sustaining treatment,
new-onset atrial fibrillation in a catecholamine-supported adult, and supraventricular tachycardia in a
neonate are three questions that cannot be randomised at scale — the first for ethical reasons, the other two
for want of patients. **A framework built to grade randomised evidence returns "not applicable" because there
is no randomised evidence to grade, and that is the correct output.** **The alternative — issuing the same
advice without the label — is what most guidelines do.**

**Second, "strong agreement on all of them" appears in all three and means something different from a grade.**
SFAR and SOFMER, the SRLF and GFRUP each record near-unanimous panel approval. **Unanimity among thirteen to
thirty experts about what to do in the absence of data is a real and useful thing; it is not evidence, and the
two numbers have to travel together.** **A reader in another country can import the GRADE levels and should
treat the expert opinions as a record of what French-speaking intensivists agreed in 2024–2026.**

**Third, the disclosure is the transferable practice.** **A reader of any of these three knows the ratio before
opening the PDF.** **Set against today's other two new documents, neither of which gives one** — the STS
consensus reports no statement count and no grades, and the Canadian guideline reports 15 recommendations
without saying how many reached grade A and how many were rated insufficient — **the French-speaking documents
are the outliers in transparency, not in weakness.**

**An absence to report plainly, because the society is a tracked one.** **ESICM published a position paper on
equity, diversity and inclusion in intensive care in *Intensive Care Medicine* on 19 October.** **No abstract
is deposited anywhere in Europe PMC — a single record, no sibling, no PMCID, not open access — and the DOI
redirects through Springer to an authentication endpoint rather than to a page.** **So this watch can confirm
the document exists and can say nothing about its content.** It is on the needs-a-PDF list; a reader who has
institutional access can send it.

## 1. STS on borderline left heart: numeric thresholds and no statement count

**Society · Published:** **the Society of Thoracic Surgeons**, in *The Annals of Thoracic Surgery*,
**20 October 2026** — **one day old**, the newest document in today's reading. **A panel of congenital cardiac
surgeons, cardiologists and imaging experts**, questions framed in **PICO**, literature searched per question,
**consensus statements developed by modified Delphi**. Scope: **borderline left heart from the neonatal period
through one year**, excluding unbalanced atrioventricular septal defect.

> Strategies to recruit the left ventricle in borderline left heart (BLH) … **are challenged by lack of
> consensus regarding optimal substrate, timing, and technique.** …
>
> **For neonates with BLH, primary biventricular repair should be adopted cautiously, and only with favorable
> anatomic and physiologic criteria determined by echocardiography and/or magnetic resonance imaging. Neonates
> in the 'grey-zone', with smaller mitral and aortic valve sizes, endocardial fibroelastosis, indexed left
> ventricular diastolic volume < 25 ml/m2 and ascending aortic flow < 2.0 L/min/m2, should undergo staged
> recruitment** to potentially facilitate growth of left-sided structures … **Timing and type of subsequent
> procedures should be guided by interval cardiac catheterization and magnetic resonance imaging data. Initial
> palliation of grey-zone neonates can be accomplished with either surgical Norwood or hybrid stage 1 with
> possible atrial level restriction** … **Biventricular approaches in BLH should be guided by integration of
> anatomic and physiologic criteria, with most neonates recommended for staged recruitment rather than primary
> repair.**

**Interpretation.** **This abstract does something almost no consensus abstract does: it gives the actual
numbers.** **Indexed left ventricular diastolic volume below 25 ml/m², ascending aortic flow below
2.0 L/min/m².** **Those are thresholds a surgeon and a cardiologist can apply at a cot side on the strength of
the abstract alone**, and this watch has spent a month reporting documents whose abstracts name their topics
and withhold their content. **Credit where it is due — and note what it costs: there is no statement count, no
grading scheme and no agreement figure anywhere in the abstract.** **The STS document is the mirror image of
the French-speaking ones: all content, no structure.**

**The clinical substance is a recommendation against enthusiasm, which is the harder thing for a surgical
society to publish.** **The default position is staged recruitment, not primary biventricular repair** —
"primary biventricular repair should be adopted cautiously", and "most neonates recommended for staged
recruitment". **A single-ventricle palliation with the possibility of later conversion is a slower, more
operations, more catheterisations pathway than going for two ventricles at the outset**, and the panel is
telling its own membership to take it in most cases. **That is a consensus document arguing for less
immediate surgery, which is the direction in which such documents are least often written.**

**The grey zone is where the document earns its place and also where its limits show.** Smaller valves,
endocardial fibroelastosis, a small left ventricle and low ascending aortic flow define a group in whom
neither strategy is clearly right — **and the recommended path is to stage, re-image, re-catheterise, and
decide later on interval data.** **That is an explicit decision to defer a decision, justified by the one
thing the panel can be confident of: that left-sided structures may grow.** **What it does not tell a reader
is how often staged recruitment actually achieves biventricular conversion, because the comparative data to
answer that is exactly what the opening sentence says is missing.**

**Honest limitation: a modified Delphi among experienced operators is the appropriate method for a rare lesion
and it is still expert opinion.** Borderline left heart is uncommon, the series are small and single-centre,
and **the numeric thresholds quoted above almost certainly come from observational cohorts rather than trials.**
**They are usable because they are specific, not because they are proven.**

[Read the consensus document](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.athoracsur.2026.09.059) · *The Annals of Thoracic Surgery*

## 2. Canadian guidelines on spinal procedures for chronic pain: a different grading framework, and 15 recommendations with no distribution

**Society · Published:** **a Canadian panel of clinicians, researchers, patient partners and representatives of
provincial healthcare regulatory and funding bodies**, in *Anaesthesia*, **18 October 2026** — **three days
old**. **Recommendations graded A (strongest) to D (not recommended), or insufficient (I), using US Preventive
Services Task Force criteria**, with certainty rated high, medium or low. **Consensus required at least 75%
panel agreement. 15 key recommendations**, plus good clinical practice statements, covering **cervical and
lumbar spine and sacroiliac joint procedures**.

> Interventional procedures are commonly used to diagnose and treat chronic pain of spinal origin but
> **guidance on: their indications; frequency; technical conduct; requirements for image guidance; outcome
> measurement; and management of complications is lacking.** … **Evidence-based recommendations for each
> procedure were graded on a scale from A (strongest level) to D (not recommended), or as insufficient (I),
> using US Preventive Services Task Force criteria with the level of certainty rated as high, medium or low.
> All consensus recommendations required at least 75% agreement among the guideline panel.** … **We provide 15
> key recommendations.** … **Clinical indications, evidence for efficacy and suggested frequency for repeated
> procedures have also been proposed.**

**Interpretation.** **Two things here are genuinely new to this archive and both are structural.**

**The first is the grading framework.** **This is the first document in a month of guideline reporting here to
use USPSTF grades rather than GRADE** — and the difference is not cosmetic. **GRADE separates the strength of a
recommendation from the certainty of the evidence and has no category for "do not do this"; the USPSTF scale
runs from A to D with D meaning not recommended, and adds I for insufficient evidence.** **A framework with an
explicit "not recommended" grade can say no to a procedure in a way GRADE's machinery makes awkward**, and for
a field where the central question is which injections are worth doing, that is the more useful instrument.
**The addition of "insufficient" also gives the panel somewhere to put a procedure without either endorsing or
condemning it** — the same job the French documents do with expert opinion, done by a different route.

**The second is who was in the room.** **Patient partners and representatives from provincial healthcare
regulatory and funding bodies sat on the guideline panel.** **A funder on the panel that writes the
recommendations is a structural choice with consequences in both directions**: it makes the guidance more
likely to be implementable and reimbursed, and it puts the body that pays in the room where frequency of
repeat procedures is decided. **The abstract specifically says "suggested frequency for repeated procedures"
was proposed, which is the single most financially consequential output a spinal-injection guideline can
have.** **This watch has spent the month on documents about the gap between a recommendation and its funding —
the HRS prior-authorization statement and the Canadian transcatheter referral update. Here is a guideline that
closed that gap by inviting the funder in.** Whether that is good practice or regulatory capture is not
answerable from an abstract, and it should be named either way.

**And the familiar omission: 15 key recommendations, and not one word on how many are A, how many are D, and
how many are I.** **For a document whose whole value is telling clinicians which spinal procedures to stop
doing, the distribution across A to D is the finding, and it is not in the abstract.** **A reader cannot tell
from this whether the guideline endorses most of these procedures or rejects most of them.** On the second
axis of this archive's catalogue — does the document disclose its own structure — **this one scores badly
despite describing its framework in unusual detail.** Describing the scale is not reporting the result.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1111/anae.70440) · *Anaesthesia*

## 3. GFRUP on supraventricular tachycardia in the paediatric ICU: 21 recommendations, five gradeable

**Society · Published:** **Groupe Francophone de Réanimation et Urgences Pédiatriques**, in *Annals of
Intensive Care*, **16 September 2026** — **thirty-five days old**, newly indexed and carried here from
yesterday's backlog. **Thirteen French-speaking experts**, working 2024 to 2025, **PICO questions**, searches
of MEDLINE, Cochrane and Embase, **GRADE where applicable and expert opinion where not**, **each
recommendation voted by Delphi**.

> **The guidelines cover four areas: (1) epidemiology and criteria for admission to a PICU, (2) paraclinical
> assessment, (3) non-specific rhythm management, and (4) specific rhythm management.** … The texts were
> analyzed and classified according to the GRADE methodology. **When this method was not applicable, an expert
> opinion was given.** … **The group proposes 21 recommendations. The GRADE methodology could be applied for
> five of them, and an expert opinion was given for the remaining 16. All of the recommendations received
> strong approval.**

**Interpretation.** **This is the third document in three days to report the same structure, and the one that
makes the pattern legible: five of twenty-one graded, sixteen expert opinions, strong approval throughout.**

**The reason is demographic rather than methodological and it is worth being explicit about.**
**Non-postoperative supraventricular tachycardia in a neonate admitted to a paediatric intensive care unit is
rare, and the population that could be randomised is small, geographically scattered and acutely unwell.**
**There will not be a large trial of adenosine dosing in neonatal SVT, and a framework that reports "GRADE not
applicable" sixteen times is describing that fact accurately.** **The failure would be to issue sixteen strong
recommendations anyway.**

**The four-area structure tells a reader what the document is actually for, and three of the four areas are
not about drugs.** **Epidemiology and PICU admission criteria; paraclinical assessment; non-specific rhythm
management; specific rhythm management.** **Only the last is the pharmacology and electrical therapy most
readers would expect to be the whole document.** **The admission-criteria section is the one with the widest
reach** — deciding which child with SVT needs intensive care at all is a question every emergency department
faces and almost no evidence addresses, and it is precisely where a Delphi among thirteen experienced
paediatric intensivists is the best available instrument.

**Separating what travels from what does not, as this archive now does routinely for a national document.**
**The rhythm pharmacology and the diagnostic assessment travel; a French-speaking group's PICU admission
thresholds reflect French and Belgian and Swiss and Canadian service configurations and do not.** **A unit
elsewhere should read areas 2 and 4 as guidance and area 1 as a description of how one health system allocates
paediatric intensive care beds.**

**One limitation the abstract leaves open and it is the same one as yesterday's SRLF document: the five graded
recommendations are not identified.** **Knowing that five of twenty-one are evidence-based is less useful than
knowing which five**, and that requires the full text. Unlike the ESICM paper, this one is readable — the
record carries a full abstract and the journal is *Annals of Intensive Care*.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100160) · *Annals of Intensive Care*

## 4. The Chinese renal replacement guideline, as promised: 34 recommendations and the question yesterday left open

**Society · Published:** **Chinese Society of Critical Care Medicine**, in *Journal of Intensive Medicine*,
**18 August 2026** — **sixty-four days old**, carried from the backlog. **GRADE approach, 34 recommendations.**

> …**there remains a lack of unified clinical standards for core issues including the timing of RRT
> initiation, treatment modality, anticoagulation strategy, and weaning from RRT.** … developed **through
> systematic literature retrieval, meta-analysis, and evidence-based medical evidence synthesis, followed by
> multiple rounds of manuscript review and revision** … **A total of 34 recommendations were finalized,
> covering key clinical topics including timing of RRT initiation, establishment and maintenance of vascular
> access, selection of treatment modalities, application of specialized membrane filters, anticoagulation
> regimens, replacement fluid formulations, prescribed treatment dose, and RRT weaning strategies.**

**Interpretation.** **Yesterday this watch reported the stress-ulcer guideline from the same society and said
this one was on the backlog and would say more. It says the same thing, and that is the finding.** **Thirty-four
recommendations described as developed under GRADE, with no grade distribution in the abstract** — identical in
form to the 23 recommendations reported yesterday. **Fifty-seven recommendations across two documents in six
weeks from one national society, and for neither can a reader tell how much is evidence.**

**Set the two societies side by side and the contrast is the clearest thing in today's reading.** **The
French-speaking documents grade a fifth of their recommendations and tell you which fifth is missing; the
Chinese documents issue three to four times as many recommendations and tell you nothing about their basis.**
**On the questions involved, the second pattern is harder to credit** — **timing of RRT initiation and weaning
from RRT are among the few questions in this field that genuinely have large randomised trials** (STARRT-AKI,
AKIKI, ELAIN, IDEAL-ICU), **so a guideline covering them should be able to report high-certainty
recommendations and name them.** **Not doing so leaves its best-supported advice indistinguishable from its
weakest.**

**On the clinical scope, the eight named topics are the right ones and two of them are unusual inclusions
worth noting.** **"Application of specialized membrane filters" and "replacement fluid formulations" are
consumable-level questions that most guidelines leave to local protocol** — and they are where cost and
industry interest concentrate. **A guideline that takes positions on which filter and which replacement fluid
is making recommendations with procurement consequences, and the absence of a grade distribution matters more
there than anywhere else in the document.**

**It also sits directly beside what this watch reported yesterday.** **The [joint ADQI and ELSO statements](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-20/) on running continuous
renal replacement therapy during ECMO** covered the circuit configurations and explicitly
delivered a research agenda alongside its statements. **This document covers vascular access, modality,
anticoagulation, dose and weaning for renal replacement in general, and claims none of the uncertainty.**
**Two documents on overlapping territory, eight weeks apart, one of which says what it does not know.**

[Read the guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jointm.2026.08.001) · *Journal of Intensive Medicine*
