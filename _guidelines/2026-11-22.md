## No new guidelines from tracked societies today

**No new or updated guidance appeared from SFAR, SPILF, SRLF, ESAIC, ESC, ESPEN, ESICM, SCCM, IACTS, EACTS,
STS or ASA, and the index sweep produced no in-scope guidance document that this archive has not already
reported.**

**Today's index date is almost empty.** `FIRST_IDATE:2026-11-22` holds **61 records in total**; the
title-word guidance sweep for today returns **one** of them and the pubType sweep returns **none**. The
literature brief three hours earlier recorded the same thing from the other direction — both of its scoped
sweeps returned zero — and recorded that the whole database gained **92 records in 23 hours 43 minutes with
MEDLINE entirely flat.** **A near-empty guidance day on a near-stall day is not a finding about guidance.**

**Sixteen collections, 15 to 22 November, one pubType and one title-word sweep per day, every one matching
its own hit count with shortfall zero.** 29 unique pubType records of which **1** survives the scope filter
— yesterday's cardiac surgery delirium consensus, already reported. 501 unique title-word records, 29 in
scope, **and none of the 29 is new.**

**That last number is the day's work, because the old dedupe log said 14 of them were new.**

## 1. Eight documents that kept coming back, and the two reasons

**Eight 2020 Spanish society documents — SEMICYUC and SEDAR, COVID-era — have appeared in the "new" list on
20, 21 and 22 November.** They were correctly handled on 20 November, reported there as an **index event**
rather than as items, because they are six years old and the rule is never to blur new and old. **They then
came back the next day, and the next.**

**Three consecutive days of re-reading the same eight records is cheap and it is also a symptom, so today it
was traced rather than tolerated.** There are two independent causes.

### Cause one: they have no DOI

| Document | Identifier | DOI | PMID |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recomendaciones éticas, difficult decisions in ICU | `PMC7158790` | **none** | **none** |
| Recomendaciones prácticas, perioperative management | `PMC7102628` | **none** | **none** |
| Recomendaciones, CPR in suspected infection | `PMC7229968` | **none** | **none** |
| Recomendaciones, elective and urgent thoracic surgery | `PMC7470699` | **none** | **none** |
| Recomendaciones de «hacer» y «no hacer» | `PMC7142677` | **none** | **none** |
| SEMICYUC consensus documents (three) | `PMC7283053`, `PMC7205735`, `PMC7211599` | **none** | **none** |

**All eight are PMC-only deposits.** The dedupe log has always been built from DOIs, so **no amount of
correct reporting could have made these eight dedupe** — a DOI-based log cannot match a record that has no
DOI.

### Cause two, and it is the larger one: the regex excluded the wrong characters

**The ad-hoc regex rebuilt each run was `10\.[0-9]{4,}/[^ )"]+` — it excluded space, closing parenthesis and
quotation mark, and it did not exclude the backtick.** This archive writes DOIs inside code spans
throughout its notes and its outstanding list. **So a DOI written as a code span entered the log as the DOI
plus a trailing backtick, and could never match a clean DOI arriving from the index.**

**That was measured rather than estimated:**

| Measurement across `_briefs/`, `_guidelines/` and `claude/` | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Distinct DOIs appearing outside code spans | 829 |
| Distinct DOIs appearing inside code spans | 493 |
| **Distinct DOIs appearing ONLY inside code spans** | **147** |

**One hundred and forty-seven DOIs that this archive has reported or examined were invisible to its own
dedupe log.** And the old regex's apparent richness was the tell that nobody read: it produced **1,344**
distinct strings where a backtick-safe regex produces **957**, because it was counting each backticked DOI
twice — once cleanly where the entry also wrote it bare, and once with a trailing backtick attached.

**Two of the 147 matter concretely.** `10.1002/erv.70199` is **the record cursorMark paging dropped on
20 November**, named in that entry inside a code span — so the one document this archive has proved it lost
to a paging fault was also invisible to its dedupe. And `10.1007/s00068-026-03385-0`, the Portugal frailty
cohort, is still sitting in the brief queue and would have read as new whenever it resurfaced.

### What was built, and the verification

**`claude/tools/build_dedupe_log.py`**, in the repository rather than retyped each run, per the standing
rule. It harvests three identifier families — **DOIs, PMC identifiers and PMIDs** — from the entries *and*
from `claude/*.md`, excludes the backtick, and **falls back to the record's exact title** for anything
carrying none of the three.

**Verified against today's own sweep rather than asserted:**

| Dedupe log | Of 29 scoped records, "new" |
| --- | --- |
| old DOI-only regex | **14** |
| plus the eight PMC identifiers recorded | 6 |
| **plus the backtick fix** | **0** |

**The clean log holds 1,200 identifiers against the old one's 1,553 — fewer, because 353 of those were
malformed.**

### And the policy fault underneath both

**The dedupe log was harvested only from what the archive has published.** A record examined and
deliberately *not* published — out of scope, veterinary, oncology — left no trace, so it returned every day
until its window rolled past. **Seven such records were identified today and recorded**, with the reason for
each, in a new `DEDUPE LOG — RECORDS EXAMINED AND SET ASIDE` section of `claude/outstanding.md`.

**NEW RULE: when a record is examined and rejected, record its identifier the same run. The cost of not
doing so is re-reading it every day for a week** — which is exactly what happened, three times over, to
eight documents.

**Nothing on either page is dated October or November 2026; the newest is Neuromuscular Block in
Anaesthetised Children, March 2026, which this archive holds.** **Eighteen documents across eight years is
roughly two a year** — which is the cadence that makes the SPLF document reported yesterday, describing
itself as annually updated, the outlier it is.

**One thing could not be established and is recorded as unestablished.** The page carries "Published",
"Ongoing" and "Infographics" filters. **A static fetch returns no items under "Ongoing", and that is as
consistent with client-side rendering as with an empty list, so whether ESAIC has guidelines in development
is unknown from this route.** It is not recorded as "none in development". **The ESICM, SCCM and ESC
in-development lists were all readable when checked on 2 and 14 November; ESAIC's was recorded then as an
empty tab, and today's attempt cannot distinguish an empty tab from an unrendered one either.** Carried as
an open question rather than an answer.

## 3. Sweep and classifier notes

**The classifier ran on the 29 scoped records and its output is unchanged from yesterday in substance**, so
the four-day tally recorded yesterday stands without addition: **seven bare-word false positives, two
"Delphi consensus" false negatives, one `insights from` false negative.** **No new fault type appeared
today, which on a day with no new records is what should happen and is worth recording as such rather than
as a clean run.**

**The pubType route's in-scope yield is now 1 of 29 over this window and 1 of about 100 across five days,
and the single hit remains yesterday's delirium consensus.** The decision to keep the route stands; **five
days is still not the month of data the December audit will have.**
