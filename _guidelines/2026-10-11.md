## 1. SFAR on liver resection — and the clearest evidence profile this archive has recorded

**Society · Published:** **SFAR** with the **French Society for the Study of the Liver (AFEF)** and the
**Association for Hepato-Biliary-Pancreatic Surgery and Liver Transplantation (ACHBPT)**, *Anaesthesia,
Critical Care & Pain Medicine*, **7 August 2026** — nine weeks old. `Practice Guideline`. **GRADE, PICO,
PRISMA, literature 2004–2024, GRADE-grid voting, conflicts declared and no industry funding.**
Levesque, Weiss, Aubrun, Boudjema, Breteau, De Jong, Delignette, Dewitte, Elbaz, Guerci, Hakkakian, Maulat,
Merle, Monet, Monsel, Pardo, Restoux, Roullet and colleagues

> **Methods** Three areas were defined: **(1) preoperative assessment; (2) optimization of intraoperative
> management; and (3) optimization of postoperative management.** …
>
> **Results** … **40 recommendations addressing 14 questions. After two rounds of voting and several
> revisions, strong agreement was achieved for all 40 recommendations. Among these recommendations, 7 were
> supported by a high level of evidence (GRADE 1), 23 by a low level of evidence (GRADE 2), and 10
> corresponded to expert opinions (EO). Finally, no recommendation could be formulated for four questions.**

**Interpretation. This is the most precisely enumerated evidence profile in this archive, and the numbers say
something uncomfortable: 7 of 40 recommendations rest on high-level evidence and 33 of 40 do not — yet all
40 achieved strong agreement.** **Seventeen and a half per cent of the output is GRADE 1; eighty-two and a
half per cent is low-level evidence or declared expert opinion** — and the agreement figure is 100% either
way.

**That is the pattern this archive has been circling for two weeks, now with exact denominators.** The
Chinese lung-biopsy consensus had five strong recommendations on level 4 evidence; the Brain Trauma
Foundation found no includable study for 12 of 26 questions; the Brazilian exercise recommendations graded
their own evidence moderate to very low. **Here the document itself hands you the arithmetic, and the honest
reading is that unanimity among experts and strength of evidence are independent quantities.** **Strong
agreement on an expert opinion is a statement about the panel, not about the literature**, and this panel is
transparent enough to let a reader separate them — which is the opposite of a criticism.

**The counting convention appears for the fourth time and again explicitly.** **Fourteen questions, forty
recommendations, and four questions for which no recommendation could be formulated** — so **ten questions
generated all forty recommendations, four averaging each.** This archive inferred that convention across six
documents on 1 October, saw it demonstrated inside one abstract on 5 October, and found the SFMU/SFAR
obstetric guidelines reporting it in three separate figures on 7 October. **The French societies are now
consistently disclosing their failures, and this document does it in the same sentence as its evidence
grades.**

**What the document covers and what that means for the backlog.** Preoperative assessment, intraoperative
optimisation, postoperative optimisation — **the standard three-phase anaesthetic frame, which is why this is
primary scope despite being about a hepatobiliary operation.** **It closes "hepatic resection" on the SFAR
website catalogue**, a list assembled when sfar.org was first read and which has been worked down since. And
it arrived by the **ACCPM journal route**, not the website route — **the route distinction established on
4 October holds: SFAR's journal publications are in Europe PMC and its website RFEs are not.**

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2026.101879) — paywalled beyond the abstract.

## 2. Three societies on endosonography, and a recommendation to stop doing the confirmatory operation

**Society · Published:** **European Respiratory Society**, **European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy**
and the **European Society of Thoracic Surgeons**, *European Respiratory Journal*, **6 August 2026** — nine
weeks old, **4 citations.** `Practice Guideline`. **12 guideline questions, MEDLINE and Embase to April 2025,
GRADE.**
Korevaar, Kovacevic, Papadopoulou, van den Broek, Bertolaccini, Catarata, Denswil, Facciorusso, Gnass,
Gompelmann, Herth, Kontogianni, Koukaki, Marc-Malovrh and colleagues

> **In (suspected) non-small cell lung cancer, endosonography is recommended over mediastinoscopy for
> mediastinal nodal tissue staging. Systematic staging is suggested over targeted staging as the minimal
> standard. Ideally, combined EBUS-TBNA + EUS(-B)-FNA is performed instead of EBUS-TBNA alone. Add-on
> mediastinoscopy after a negative endosonography is not recommended.** Endosonography is suggested over
> mediastinoscopy **for restaging after induction therapy.** … **Both EUS-B-FNA and EUS-FNA are suggested for
> left adrenal gland analysis. It is suggested that competence is acquired in a simulation-based environment
> and ensured using valid assessment methods. 21G/22G TBNA needles are considered the standard; there is
> insufficient evidence to support the structural use of alternative needle sizes/types or cryobiopsy.
> EBUS-TBNA has a high suitability rate for programmed death-ligand 1 assessment.**

**Interpretation. "Add-on mediastinoscopy after a negative endosonography is not recommended" is the
load-bearing sentence and it is a de-implementation recommendation aimed at surgeons.** The practice it
retires is confirmatory: endosonography says the mediastinum is clear, and a mediastinoscopy is done anyway
to be sure. **A joint guideline whose signatories include the European Society of Thoracic Surgeons telling
thoracic surgeons to stop performing a confirmatory operation is a stronger document than one written by
physicians alone**, and the inclusion of ESTS and ESGE alongside ERS is what gives it that standing.

**Set it beside the lung-biopsy pair reported here on [6 October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-06/)
and there is an apparent contradiction worth resolving carefully.** That Chinese Thoracic Society consensus
made **transbronchial cryobiopsy its preferred modality in expert centres**, under general anaesthesia with a
prophylactic occlusion balloon. **This guideline finds "insufficient evidence to support the structural use
of… cryobiopsy."** **Both can be right, and the reason is the indication: cryobiopsy for diffuse
interstitial lung disease is a parenchymal sampling question, and cryobiopsy for mediastinal nodal staging in
lung cancer is a different procedure for a different target.** **The lesson is not that two guidelines
disagree; it is that a technique's evidence base is indication-specific and a reader who carries "cryobiopsy
is recommended" across indications will be wrong.**

**The needle recommendation is the kind of specificity that makes a guideline usable: 21G/22G is standard and
alternatives are unsupported.** That is a procurement-level statement, and like the Chinese consensus's
balloon requirement it tells a department what to stock, not just what to aim for.

**And one recommendation belongs to a thread running through this archive: competence should be acquired in a
simulation-based environment and ensured with valid assessment methods.** The [6 October
brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-06/) found **81% of 1,493 French emergency clinicians had minimal
exposure to simulation-based training**, and the human-factors documents catalogued here keep recommending
simulation into a workforce that does not receive it. **This is the same recommendation from a procedural
specialty, and it is the first in this archive to pair simulation with a requirement for validated
assessment of competence** rather than exposure alone.

**The PD-L1 sentence is small and consequential.** EBUS-TBNA samples are adequate for programmed death-ligand
1 testing at a high rate — **which means a staging procedure doubles as the tissue source for a treatment
decision**, and it is the reason the systematic-over-targeted staging recommendation matters beyond nodal
mapping.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.00097-2026) — paywalled beyond the abstract.

## 3. Fifty-five recommendations on operating on older patients with lung cancer

**Society · Published:** **National Clinical Research Center for Geriatrics, West China Hospital of Sichuan
University**, *Journal of Evidence-Based Medicine*, **11 August 2026** — nine weeks old. **Open access**
(PMC13613263). `Practice Guideline`. **GRADE plus the RIGHT reporting checklist.** **The sixth Chinese
document found since that route was established on 6 October.**
Li, Liang, Dong, Liu, Ge, and the National Clinical Research Center for Geriatrics

> With the accelerating population aging … **These patients often present with multiple chronic diseases and
> geriatric syndromes, resulting in more complex perioperative management and significantly increased
> surgical risks.** … **Focusing on 28 key clinical issues commonly encountered among older adults with lung
> cancer, such as frailty, malnutrition, and falls, a total of 55 recommendations were formulated.** This
> guideline aims to provide standardized perioperative management strategies through **comprehensive
> geriatric assessment, comorbidity management, and early identification and intervention for
> complications.**

**Interpretation. Fifty-five recommendations across 28 questions with no evidence grades in the abstract is
the weakness here, and it is worth saying plainly against item 1.** The SFAR document gave 7 / 23 / 10 and
named its four unanswerable questions; **this one gives a count and a methodology label.** **Both used
GRADE; only one lets a reader see what the grades were.** The RIGHT checklist is for reporting completeness,
so the grades exist in the full text — **but the abstract is where most clinicians meet a guideline, and the
difference in disclosure is large.**

**On substance, the framing is the contribution: frailty, malnutrition and falls treated as perioperative
problems in thoracic surgery.** Those are geriatric-medicine constructs, and a thoracic surgical pathway
that names them is importing comprehensive geriatric assessment into an operative specialty. **That is the
same move the ISHLT frailty document makes for transplant candidacy** — and this archive now holds **two
frailty-assessment guidelines, of which this one is readable and the ISHLT one is not** (see the footer).

**The limit to state: this is a single-centre national research centre document**, from one hospital's
geriatrics centre rather than a national society, and like the cardio-obstetric pathway reported yesterday
**it is indexed `Practice Guideline` while being institutional in origin.** It is also **`other` in its
pubType list**, which is the third unusual type combination in four days. **Say what convened it when citing
it.**

[Read the guideline](https://doi.org/10.1111/jebm.70178) — open access.
