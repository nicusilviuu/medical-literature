## Today's zeros are uninformative, and that is the finding

**All three sweeps returned zero hits**: the guideline-titled sweep for 24–26 September, the French
sweep for 20–26 September, and the society sweep for 24–26 September.

**Those zeros mean nothing, and reporting them as negatives would be wrong.** As documented in
[this morning's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-26/), Europe PMC has deposited **6 records
for 24 September, 4 for the 25th and 0 for the 26th** — ten records in total across the window, all in
small regional journals. **A sweep of an empty window returns zero regardless of what was published.**
The index itself is healthy: the older buckets are stable, and the canary record retrieves normally.

**So: no new guidelines from tracked societies were found today, and that is not the same as none
having appeared.** The window will be re-swept once deposits resume.

**The French position can be stated properly, because it rests on the healthy part of the index.** The
sweep for **19–23 September** — dates the index holds fully — returns **zero**, with a control over
1–23 September returning **13 hits** to prove the query works. **So SFAR, SPILF, SRLF and their
journals published nothing between 19 and 23 September**, their last deposit being 18 September, and
guideline output stands at **twenty-six days**. What happened after the 23rd is currently unknowable.

**The day went to the backlog**, where all three items below were retrieved and verified during this
run.

## 1. ERC 2025 Paediatric Life Support

**Society · Published:** **European Resuscitation Council**. *Resuscitation*, **October 2025** —
**twelve months old.** **Cited 14 times.** Indexed as a **Practice Guideline**
Djakow, Turner, Skellett, Buysse, Cardona, de Lucas, Castillo, Kiviranta, Lauridsen, Markel,
Martinez-Mejias, Roggen, **Biarent** and the ERC Paediatric Life Support writing group

Basis: the **ILCOR Consensus on Science with Treatment Recommendations**, plus **rapid reviews
performed by the ERC writing group on topics not covered by ILCOR** — a detail worth noting, since it
means parts of this document rest on ERC's own evidence review rather than on international consensus.

Scope:

> recommendations on the **prevention of cardiac arrest, basic life support, advanced life support,
> resuscitation in special circumstances, post-resuscitation care, prognostication, and post-discharge
> care** in infants, children and adolescents **(aged 0–18 years)**, but **does not cover resuscitation
> of the neonate at birth.**

> The recommendations have been formulated **for healthcare providers who take care of children and
> for the general public** and includes recommendations on **how to implement the Guidelines and
> system factors.**

And the sentence that makes this section different from the rest of the set:

> Stakeholders from a range of health care settings were involved… and **the views of the community
> advisors representing families of paediatric cardiac arrest survivors and non-survivors were also
> considered.**

**Interpretation.** The ninth of twelve ERC 2025 sections now on the record, and the one with the most
interesting process.

**"Families of paediatric cardiac arrest survivors and non-survivors" is an unusual constituency to
name in a guideline's methods**, and the inclusion of *non-survivors'* families is deliberate. Those
are the people best placed to say what the aftermath of a resuscitation attempt is actually like, and
they have no stake in the intervention appearing effective. **Given that this archive has spent the
month on documents whose recommendations rest on expert opinion, a guideline that went looking for the
one group with no professional interest in the answer is worth noting.**

**The scope is wider than an algorithm, and two inclusions matter.** **Prevention of cardiac arrest**
at the front — recognising the deteriorating child before arrest, which is where paediatric outcomes
are actually determined — and **post-discharge care** at the back, which most resuscitation guidance
omits entirely. A document that runs from prevention to post-discharge is describing a pathway rather
than a procedure.

**Writing for healthcare providers and the general public in one document is a genuine tension**, and
the abstract does not say how it was resolved. Lay rescuer guidance and paediatric ALS are different
literatures with different failure modes, and how the two audiences are separated within the text is
exactly the sort of thing the abstract cannot tell us.

*A small observation about the record rather than the guidance: **the deposited abstract contains an
unexecuted editorial instruction** — the phrase reads "adolescents add brackets (aged 0–18 years)".
It is a copy-editing artifact, not a content problem, and it is quoted above with the instruction
removed. It is mildly notable only because **three other ERC 2025 sections received corrigenda on 21
September** — [reported here on the 22nd](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-22/) — while this
visible slip persists, which suggests the corrections addressed something other than typography.*

*Access note: as with every ERC section, **the abstract lists topics and contains no recommendations.**
Nothing above is a recommendation. **ERC 2025 now stands at nine of twelve sections**; remaining are
Newborn Life Support, Epidemiology, and Education.*

## 2. ISHLT paediatric heart failure — eleven years on, and an abstract of two sentences

**Society · Published:** **International Society for Heart and Lung Transplantation**. *Journal of
Heart and Lung Transplantation*, **21 August 2025** — **thirteen months old.** Cited 5 times. Indexed
as a **Practice Guideline**
Irving, Azeka, Adorisio, **Blume**, Bogle, Chubb, Conway, Cousino, Edelson, Ford, Holinski, Janousek,
Lal, Lee, **Lorts**, Nakano, **Rosenthal** and colleagues

The abstract in its entirety:

> Pediatric heart failure secondary to cardiomyopathies, acquired heart disease, and congenital heart
> disease is **associated with significant morbidity and mortality.** These guidelines represent an
> **update from the ISHLT guidelines for the management of pediatric heart failure that were published
> in 2014** and **incorporate interval advancements in medical therapies and new approaches in the
> evaluation and management of children with heart failure.**

**Interpretation.** This is the least informative abstract-to-importance ratio this archive has
recorded, and the gap it fills is eleven years wide.

**Paediatric heart failure guidance was last issued in 2014.** In the interval, adult heart failure
has been transformed — SGLT2 inhibitors, sacubitril/valsartan, the whole quadruple-therapy
architecture — and paediatric practice has had to decide what of that transfers, largely without
guidance. **A 2025 update is therefore a significant document, and its abstract says only that
advancements have been incorporated.** Which advancements, for which lesions, at what ages, is
entirely in the full text.

**For this project's scope the relevance is concrete rather than cardiological.** These are the
children who present for non-cardiac surgery on heart failure therapy, for cardiac catheterisation
under anaesthesia, for ventricular assist device implantation, and for transplantation. **Lorts and
Rosenthal in the author list point at the mechanical-support end of that spectrum**, which is where
anaesthetic and intensive care involvement is heaviest.

**It is also the fifth ISHLT document in this archive and the fifth to deposit nothing usable.** The
[14 September entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-14/) closed the ISHLT gap with the
three-part perioperative ECLS consensus; the graft dysfunction update and the lung transplant frailty
consensus followed on the 16th. **In every case the abstract gave scope and the recommendations
required a full text that was not reachable.** That is now a documented property of this society's
output rather than bad luck, and it belongs in the source notes alongside the equivalent findings for
ERC sections, AHA statements and the Healthcare Infection Society.

*Access note: everything above except the interpretation is in the two sentences quoted. **No
recommendation from this guideline has been read.***

## 3. Lidocaine for airway topicalisation in children — dosing guidance that did not exist

**Published:** International expert **multidisciplinary, multi-society working group**. *Anaesthesia*,
**27 July 2025**, October 2025 issue — **fourteen months old.** Indexed as both a **Systematic Review**
and a **Consensus Statement**
**Iliff**, Parnell, **Baker**, Baxter, Chapman, Coulson, Dotherty, Endlich, **Frykholm**, Harkin,
**Jagannathan**, Kubba, Kwizera Ndekezi, McGuire, Mesbah and colleagues

The problem, stated in one sentence:

> **Lidocaine is commonly used to provide airway topicalisation in children. However, there is a
> paucity of evidence and no guidance available on safe dosing practices.**

Method: systematic review, then a **three-round Delphi process.**

> The systematic review identified **26 articles**, with **21 recommendations agreed across five
> domains of practice: dosing; recovery; adverse reactions; institutional responsibilities; and
> learning from events.** **Evidence was limited and mainly grades C and D**, but the strength of the
> recommendations, based on expert consensus, was **mainly moderate (65–79% consensus) and strong
> (≥ 80% consensus).**

**Interpretation.** A small document about a real hazard, and it reports the mismatch at its own
centre rather than hiding it.

**The framing is the finding: a drug "commonly used" in children with "no guidance available on safe
dosing practices."** Lidocaine toxicity is weight-based, the therapeutic margin in a small child is
narrow, topical absorption from the airway is rapid and variable, and the early signs of toxicity are
unavailable in an anaesthetised patient. **That combination has existed for decades without a dosing
recommendation.** Twenty-six articles is the entire literature.

**It states both the evidence grade and the consensus strength, which is the honest version of a
pattern this archive has catalogued.** Evidence **mainly grades C and D**; recommendations **mainly
moderate to strong**. That is the same structure as the
[videolaryngoscopy implementation guidelines](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-21/) — strong
consensus on weak evidence — and, as there, **the document's virtue is that it publishes both numbers
so the reader can see the gap.** For a dosing recommendation this matters more than usual: a
clinician needs to know that the number is expert opinion rather than pharmacokinetic data.

**Two of the five domains are organisational rather than clinical** — **institutional
responsibilities** and **learning from events** — which places obligations on departments to record
and review toxicity events. **Given that the evidence base is 26 articles, a recommendation to
systematically learn from events is arguably the most important one in the document**: it is the
mechanism by which the evidence base stops being 26 articles.

**Iliff leads this and appears in both [DAS 2025](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-21/) and the
SOBA obesity airway recommendations.** That is the third document in this archive's airway seam with
overlapping authorship, and it closes the paediatric-topicalisation item from that seam.
