## 1–2. ERC 2025: First Aid and Adult Basic Life Support

**Society · Published:** **European Resuscitation Council**. *Resuscitation*, **October 2025** —
**twelve months old.** Both indexed as **Practice Guidelines**, both **cited 19 times**

**First Aid** — Djärv, Rogers, Semeraro, Brädde, Cassan, Cimpoesu, van Goor, Klaassen, Laermans,
Meyran, Singletary, Mellett-Smith, Thilakasiri, **Zideman**

This is **the most informative ERC section abstract this archive has encountered**, and it is worth
quoting at length precisely because the others have not been:

> **General topics** include expectations of a first aid provider, first aid courses, **contents of a
> first aid kit**, how to approach a person with impaired responsiveness, **recovery position, use of
> a pulse oximeter and administration of oxygen.**

> **Medical emergencies** include **anaphylaxis, choking, asthma, chest pain, hypoglycaemia, opioid
> overdose, recognition of stroke, and suicidal thoughts.**

> **Trauma-related emergencies** include **cervical spinal motion restriction, control of
> life-threatening bleeding, management of open chest wounds, concussion and preservation of an
> amputated body part.**

> **Environmental emergencies** include **drowning, hypothermia, hyperthermia and snake bite.**

Basis: the **2025 ILCOR International Consensus on Science with Treatment Recommendations on First
Aid**, expert group review, and selected peer-reviewed literature.

**Adult Basic Life Support** — Smyth, van Goor, Hansen, Fijačko, Nakagawa, Raffay, **Ristagno**,
Rogers, Scquizzato, Smith, Spartinou, Wolfgang, **Perkins** and the ERC Adult BLS Collaborators

> The topics addressed include **how to recognise cardiac arrest, alerting the emergency services,
> delivering chest compressions, performing rescue breaths, how to use an automated external
> defibrillator (AED) and safety considerations for rescuers.** **Quality of CPR and the use of
> technology has been embedded into the relevant sections, rather than reported separately.**

**Interpretation.** These bring the archive to **eight of the twelve ERC 2025 sections**, and each
contributes something different.

**First Aid is the section this archive most needed to cover, for a reason that has nothing to do
with its content.** It is one of the three sections **corrected by a corrigendum on 21 September**,
and [that corrigendum deposits no abstract and no text and is
subscription-only](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-22/) — so what changed is unknowable. The
other two corrected sections had already been reported here; this one had not. **It is now on the
record, and the standing caveat applies to it as it does to the other two: nothing described above is
known to be affected by the correction, and nothing is known to be unaffected.**

**Its topic list is genuinely useful, which is rare for an ERC abstract.** Two things stand out.
**"Use of a pulse oximeter and administration of oxygen" in a first-aid document** is a notable
inclusion — a first-aid provider is now expected to interpret a device, which is a meaningful
expansion of what lay rescue means. And **"suicidal thoughts" sitting in the medical-emergencies list
between opioid overdose and stroke recognition** is a deliberate placement: it treats a mental-health
crisis as a first-aid emergency with a protocol, not as something outside the scope of the person who
happens to be there.

**Adult BLS is the reverse — a scope-only abstract with one structural detail worth extracting.**
*"Quality of CPR and the use of technology has been embedded into the relevant sections, rather than
reported separately"* is an editorial decision with a consequence: **CPR quality is no longer a
separate chapter to skip.** Feedback devices, compression depth and rate metrics now appear where the
skill is taught rather than in an appendix for the interested. For a document whose primary audience
is lay and first-responder training, that is the sort of change that alters what actually gets
taught.

**Remaining ERC 2025 sections: Paediatric Life Support, Newborn Life Support, Epidemiology, and
Education** — four of twelve still uncovered, with paediatric the priority.

*Access note: as recorded throughout this archive, **ERC section abstracts list topics and never
contain recommendations.** Nothing above is a recommendation; it is a description of what the
documents cover. The substantive changes in ERC 2025 were taken, on 12 September, from a separate
peer-reviewed review and attributed there.*

## 3. Where cardiovascular prevention guidelines disagree about what *not* to do

**Published:** *European Journal of General Practice*, **21 September 2026** — **four days old.**
A narrative review
Brotons, Moral, Axelrod, Brodersen, Martins, Öztora, Petričušić, Piessens, Reyes, Rodriguez-Benito,
Terebessi

> **Low-value practices are interventions that do not provide health benefits** due to lack of
> effectiveness or associated risks that outweigh the potential benefits.

Method: narrative review of CVD prevention guidelines published in English **January 2017 to December
2025**, from **major European, British, North American and Australian scientific societies**, including
**only guidelines containing explicit recommendations on low-value or unnecessary practices.** The
authors then traced the evidence behind each recommendation and **searched for ongoing trials where a
recommendation rested on a single randomised trial, large non-randomised studies, retrospective data,
or expert opinion.**

> Guidelines show **significant discrepancies** regarding recommendations for low-value practices.
> These primarily relate to **the coronary artery calcium (CAC) score, determination of
> lipoprotein(a), screening for abdominal aortic aneurysm, and the initiation of lipid-lowering
> treatment in older adults.** **Controversy and uncertainty were most evident when recommendations
> were based on non-randomised clinical trials or expert opinion.**

**Interpretation.** The **fifth distinct variety of guideline divergence** this archive has
catalogued, and arguably the most consequential.

The previous four: **societies disagreeing on the same evidence**
([ESC vs ACC/AHA on imaging, 18 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-18/)); **societies
diverging on management** ([aortic arch, 19 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-19/));
**a regulator against the societies implementing it**
([SGLT2 withholding, 20 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-20/)); and **strong
consensus on admittedly weak evidence**
([videolaryngoscopy, 21 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-21/)). **This is
disagreement about what should *not* be done.**

**That asymmetry matters.** A disagreement about whether to do something resolves, in practice,
toward doing it — the clinician who acts has a guideline to cite. **A disagreement about whether
something is low-value resolves the same way, which means the practice continues.** De-adoption
requires near-unanimity in a way that adoption does not, and this review documents four areas where
that unanimity does not exist.

**The methodological finding is the one to keep**: controversy concentrated where recommendations
rested on **non-randomised data or expert opinion**. That is unsurprising and it is useful, because it
is diagnostic — **the presence of inter-guideline disagreement is itself a signal about the underlying
evidence grade**, and a reader comparing two guidelines that differ can infer something about the
evidence without reading it.

**Scope note: this is general-practice cardiovascular prevention, at the outer edge of this project's
secondary scope**, and it is reported for its structure rather than its content. The four contested
practices — CAC scoring, Lp(a), AAA screening, statins in the elderly — are not perioperative
decisions. **The pattern is what transfers.**

## 4. Exercise prescription in children with cardiovascular disease

**Published:** *European Journal of Preventive Cardiology*, **23 September 2026** — **two days old.**
A **clinical consensus statement**
Tiberi, **D'Ascenzi**, Cavarretta, Orchard, Abela, **Bäck**, Bahls, Casey, Castelletti, Claessen,
**Drago**, Eijsvogels, Finocchiaro, **Gati** and colleagues

> Regular physical activity offers numerous benefits to children's health… **many young patients
> remain insufficiently active because of concerns regarding exercise related risks, limited
> expertise.** This clinical consensus statement provides practical guidance for the prescription of
> exercise and physical activity in **children and adolescents aged 6 to 17 years with cardiovascular
> disease.**

> …an **individualised, patient-centred approach** based on clinical assessment, **cardiovascular risk
> stratification, and the FITT principle: frequency, intensity, time, and type.** Specific
> recommendations are provided for **congenital and acquired heart disease, cardiomyopathies,
> arrhythmias, pulmonary hypertension, cardio-oncology patients, and children with physical
> disabilities or syndromes.**

**Interpretation.** Reported with two caveats stated up front, both of which are the point.

**Scope.** This is paediatric cardiology and exercise prescription — **outside this project's primary
scope and at the edge of its secondary scope.** It is included because the populations it names
(congenital heart disease, cardiomyopathy, pulmonary hypertension, cardio-oncology) are exactly the
children who arrive in theatre for non-cardiac surgery, and because **a document that risk-stratifies
those children for exertion is implicitly stratifying them for physiological stress** — which is a
perioperative question wearing different clothes. **That is an inference about relevance, not a claim
the document makes.**

**Attribution.** The statement appears in an ESC journal and its author list is drawn from the
European sports-cardiology community, **but the abstract does not name a sponsoring society**, and the
full text was not reachable. Following the rule set on
[23 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-23/), **no society attribution is claimed here
beyond what the record states.**

**The substance, so far as the abstract carries it, is the framing of the problem**: children with
cardiovascular disease are **under-exercised because of clinician concern and limited expertise**,
not because exercise has been shown to harm them. A consensus statement whose stated purpose is to
**bridge the gap between evidence and practice** is addressing a behaviour of doctors rather than a
gap in knowledge — the same structure as the SPAQI device statement covered
[yesterday](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-24/), which called itself educational material
because the barrier was unfamiliarity.
