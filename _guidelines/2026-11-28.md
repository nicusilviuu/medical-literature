## No new guidelines from tracked societies today

**The pubType route returned 33 records** over `FIRST_IDATE` 19 to 28 November. **The filter kept 4** — the
SCARTD airway guideline, the SEDAR paediatric consensus, the cardiac-surgery delirium statement and the
ISHLT transplant-nursing statement — and **the dedupe log recognised all four as already reported.**

**All 29 discards were read individually and all 29 are out of scope**: nine French urology guidelines
again, plus oncology, gastroenterology, dermatology, endocrinology, neurology, two nephrology documents,
radiology appropriateness criteria, tuberculosis, immunisation schedules, hepatocellular carcinoma, insulin
use, and a journal awards notice.

**So today is an empty day, and this entry records that the check ran.**

## The other half of the audit

**Yesterday closed the journal half of the scope-filter audit** — three tracked societies' journals were
missing and were added, with their exposure measured at zero. **The term half was left open, and it is the
half that produced the 26 November miss**, when the word `airway` turned out to be absent while
`intubation` and `extubation` were present.

**The method was to stop reacting and enumerate.** Twenty-seven candidate terms were derived by walking the
domains of an anaesthesia, critical care and cardiothoracic syllabus rather than by looking at what had just
been missed: airway and its instruments, oxygenation and respiratory failure, shock and vasoactive support,
bypass and resuscitation, neurological injury and cognitive outcome, pain and regional technique, infection
and antimicrobials, kidney injury and replacement, transfusion and coagulation, monitoring, transplantation,
and the perioperative phases.

**The most immediately obvious of them was `preoperative`**, which is absent while `perioperative` and
`postoperative` are both present — exactly the shape of the `airway` gap.

## The first test was wrong, and the way it was wrong is the finding

**Measured against today's brief sweep, all twenty-seven candidates admitted nothing.** Not one record in
491 would have entered scope that was not already in it.

**That result is not evidence about the term list. It is evidence about the test.** The brief sweep's own
collection query is built from the same term list the filter applies afterwards, **so every record in the
sweep already matches a term by construction, and the filter keeps 490 of 491.** The filter is very nearly
a no-op on the sweep's own output.

**Which means the term route cannot be tested on brief-sweep data at all**, and had the measurement been
taken at face value it would have read as "no gap" for the wrong reason. **The gap found two days ago would
also have been invisible to it** — the SCARTD guideline came through the guidance route, not the sweep.

**The right test set is the guidance route**, because it is collected by publication type and never touches
the term list, so it contains records the filter has genuinely never seen.

## Twenty-two of twenty-seven admit nothing

**290 unscoped guidance records over 1 September to 28 November.** The filter currently keeps 22 of them and
rejects 268. The question is whether any candidate term would admit a rejected record.

**Twenty-two of the twenty-seven candidates admit nothing whatsoever.** `preoperative`, `tracheostomy`,
`oxygenation`, `ards`, `respiratory failure`, `weaning`, `shock`, `vasopressor`, `haemodynamic`,
`cardiopulmonary bypass`, `cpr`, `cognitive dysfunction`, `epidural`, `opioid`, `antimicrobial`,
`ventilator-associated`, `renal replacement`, `transfusion`, `capnography`, `echocardiography` and the rest
— no rejected record in three months carries any of them in its title.

**Five admit something, and what they admit is the answer:**

| Term | Rejected records admitted | What they are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `transplantation` | 9 | Pretransplant infection guidance, AI in organ transplantation, chimerism after allogeneic stem-cell transplant — **transplant medicine, not cardiothoracic** |
| `dialysis` | 3 | Renal nutrition and haemodiafiltration — **nephrology** |
| `brain injury` | 1 | A blast and penetrating traumatic brain injury guideline — **already held** |
| `acute kidney injury` | 1 | Brazilian nephrology position statement on starting and stopping dialysis — **nephrology** |
| `anticoagulation` | 1 | Atrial fibrillation in advanced kidney disease — **nephrology and cardiology** |

**The nine transplantation records are the clearest case for leaving a term out.** Thoracic transplant
guidance does belong here, and it already arrives — the ISHLT documents of 24 and 26 November came through
`j heart lung transplant`. Adding `transplantation` would admit solid-organ and haematopoietic transplant
medicine wholesale to catch nothing new.

**So the term list has no recall gap on three months of unscoped guidance data, and nothing was added
today.** That is a better outcome than adding twenty-seven words on intuition, and it carries a second
conclusion: **the `airway` omission was a specific hole, not a symptom of a systematically thin list.**

## The one candidate that needed checking rather than reasoning

**`brain injury` admitted one rejected record and it looked like a genuine miss.** The EXTRACCT blast injury
clinical practice guideline, `10.1002/wjs.70522`, World Journal of Surgery, 31 August 2026 — **inside the
coverage window** — whose abstract covers initial assessment, **haemodynamic and airway management**, medical
treatment of intracranial hypertension, noninvasive neuromonitoring, surgical decision-making and
interfacility transport, built with AGREE II. **That is critical care content and it is in scope.**

**It is already held.** `_guidelines/2026-10-07.md` reports it, and `_guidelines/2026-11-12.md` refers to it
again. **Checked across `_guidelines/`, `_briefs/` and `claude/*.md` with six search strings before any
claim was made**, per the rule added on 25 November after an absence was asserted from a partial search.

**But how it was caught matters, because neither filter route caught it.** Its title — "Traumatic Brain
Injury From Blast and Penetrating Mechanisms in Settings With Variable Resource Availability" — carries no
scope term, and `World J Surg` is not in the journal list. **It reached the archive by a different route
entirely.**

## A design limit, confirmed rather than assumed

**The filter reads the title and the journal. It never reads the abstract.** Confirmed in the code:
`record_fields` returns exactly two things, the title and a journal name resolved from three possible
fields, and `in_scope` tests the terms against the title alone.

**The blast injury guideline is the demonstration.** Its abstract names airway management, haemodynamic
management, intracranial hypertension and neuromonitoring — **four scope terms or near-terms, none of them
in the title.** A document can therefore be unambiguously in scope by its content and invisible to both
routes of the filter at once.

**This is a limit rather than a bug, and it should be recorded as one.** Reading abstracts would widen
recall and would also widen false positives considerably, since an abstract that merely mentions sedation or
ventilation in passing is not thereby in scope. **The honest position is that the filter is a title-and-
journal instrument, that this is a deliberate trade, and that it is the reason the non-filter routes — the
society pages and the manual discard read — are not redundant.** Today's audit says the term list is sound;
it does not say the filter sees everything.

<em>Method. The pubType route queried Europe PMC for Practice Guideline, Guideline and Consensus Development
Conference over FIRST_IDATE 19 to 28 November: 33 records, hit count matching, 4 retained, 0 not previously
reported, and all 29 discards listed and read individually. The term audit derived 27 candidates from a
syllabus before looking at any data, then tested them twice: against the 491-record brief sweep, where the
result was uninformative for the structural reason given above, and against 290 unscoped guidance records
from 1 September to 28 November, which is the only one of the two that can test a term list. Each of the
five terms that admitted a rejected record was inspected record by record rather than counted. The one
apparent miss was searched for by six strings across all three archive paths before being described, and
was found to be held. The filter's title-only behaviour was confirmed by reading record_fields and in_scope
rather than inferred from the results.</em>
