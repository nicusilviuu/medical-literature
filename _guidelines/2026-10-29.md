## The society route denied the day's lead document, for the second day running

**Today's lead item is a GIHP-SFAR document published on 28 October in *Anaesthesia, Critical Care & Pain
Medicine* — SFAR's own journal — with a 1,863-character abstract in the index.**

**Today's French society search, run first as the skill requires, returned this:**

> Je n'ai trouvé aucune recommandation GIHP/SFAR publiée en 2026 sur la gestion périopératoire des
> anticoagulants. … **Je n'ai pas trouvé de texte 2026 ni de référence précise sur l'évaluation du risque
> hémorragique par procédure.**

**No 2026 GIHP/SFAR recommendation on periprocedural anticoagulant management, and no reference to a
procedure-specific bleeding risk assessment.** It offered the 2024 emergency-anticoagulation recommendations
(which this archive already holds), a 2024 GIHP/SFAR/SFTH/SFMV perioperative venous thromboprophylaxis update,
and 2018–19 antiplatelet proposals.

**Yesterday the same thing happened with a different society.** The
[28 October entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-28/) reported that **ESICM's own guidelines page
still listed its human-centred partnership consensus statement as "development in progress"** while
*Intensive Care Medicine* carried the finished article.

**So the pattern now has a sharper form than "the index route is faster".** **Six consecutive days, six lead
documents, none of them found by the society route — and on the last two the route did not merely miss the
document, it asserted the document does not exist.** 24 October EUSEM, 25 October AAST, 26 October APHRS,
27 October an international Delphi panel, 28 October ESICM, 29 October GIHP-SFAR.

**The practical rule, which this archive has now evidenced twice rather than asserted: a society search cannot
establish that a document does not exist.** It can corroborate identity — yesterday's search got ESICM's six
domains right while getting its status wrong, and today's search correctly identified the parent guideline's
society cluster — **but a negative from it is not a finding.** The index is the only route that can support a
negative, and even then only for what has been deposited.

**One useful positive came out of today's search and is flagged as a lead, not a fact:** a **2024 GIHP /
SFAR / SFTH / SFMV update of SFAR's 2011 perioperative venous thromboprophylaxis recommendations**, endorsed
by three further bodies, **which is not in this archive.** Added as a named gap to find and report.

## 1. GIHP-SFAR — four risk tiers across twenty-four procedural domains, and no grades, for a good reason

**Societies · Published:** **GIHP (Groupe d'Intérêt en Hémostase Périopératoire) and SFAR**, in collaboration
with **24 surgical and interventional specialty societies**, *Anaesthesia, Critical Care & Pain Medicine*,
**28 October 2026** — one day old.

**What it is, and the word "companion" is doing real work:**

> To develop a comprehensive, standardized, procedure-specific bleeding risk classification **to accompany the
> 2026 GIHP-SFAR periprocedural anticoagulant management guidelines.**

**The problem it addresses is stated precisely, and it is a structural one:**

> Existing procedure bleeding risk classifications are **fragmented across specialties, inconsistent in
> methodology, and rarely extend beyond a few procedural domains**, limiting their clinical applicability.

**Method and output:**

- **A formal multidisciplinary consensus** under GIHP and SFAR, with **24 surgical and interventional
  specialty societies**; **a working group per procedural domain, each performing a systematic literature
  review.**
- **Procedures classified into four tiers: no bleeding risk, low, high, and very high.**
- **A fifth, separate category** for procedures carrying **particular contextual risk** — *"anatomical site,
  patient factors, or need for reversible anticoagulation."*
- **Twenty-four procedural domains classified**, *"ranging from regional anaesthesia and digestive endoscopy
  to cardiac surgery, neurosurgery, and ophthalmology"*, each with **a procedure-specific bleeding risk table
  and narrative supporting evidence.**
- **Procedures warranting delayed resumption of anticoagulation (≥ 72 hours), or short-acting or reversible
  anticoagulants, are identified.**

**Interpretation.** **This is the most directly usable document this watch has reported in weeks, and the
reason is its shape rather than its content.** A clinician holding an anticoagulated patient and a scheduled
procedure needs one thing from a guideline: which tier is this procedure in. **Twenty-four domains with a
table each, built by the specialty society that performs the procedure, is the form that answers the question
at the bedside** — and the stated problem, that existing classifications are fragmented and stop after a few
domains, is exactly the complaint a reader would make of the alternatives.

**On the absence of grades, and why this archive should not score it as a defect.** The structural table this
watch has kept all week would show no strength or certainty attached to anything here. **But this is not a
recommendation set — it is a risk-stratification classification**, and a bleeding-risk tier is not the kind of
statement that takes a GRADE certainty. **The 22 October rule applies and applies cleanly: read the design
decision before scoring the disclosure axis.** The document is a companion to a guideline that presumably
carries the grades; **the tiers are the input to those recommendations, not recommendations themselves.**

**What the abstract does disclose, and it matters:** the classification *"covers common and complex surgical
scenarios, **including those with limited published evidence where structured expert opinion was
applied**."* **So it tells a reader that expert opinion was used and does not say in which of the 24 domains**
— which is the partial form of the disclosure. **A tier assigned from trials and a tier assigned from the
room's judgement are different objects, and in a four-tier scheme the difference between "high" and "very
high" is where that distinction will bite.**

**Archive context, and it is a direct and instructive pair.** This archive already holds
**[the SFMU / SFAR / SFTH / GIHP recommendations on anticoagulant management in emergency settings](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-05/)** —
the same society cluster, the **non-elective** side of the same problem. **That document reported
103 recommendations across 21 questions, with 19 at GRADE 1, 35 at GRADE 2 and 48 expert opinions, and one
question on which no recommendation could be made.** **Same societies, two documents, and two completely
different instruments: a fully graded recommendation set for emergencies, and an ungraded four-tier risk table
for elective procedures.** **That is the clearest demonstration in this archive that an absent grade
distribution is sometimes a format choice and not a withholding** — and it is the same cluster of authors
making both choices deliberately.

**One more thing worth recording: GIHP is on this archive's list of French collaborating societies whose
search route has never been run.** It sits in a row of seventeen societies marked "not run". **Today the index
found a GIHP document anyway**, which is the same lesson as the section above from a different direction.

[Read the consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2026.101962) · *Anaesthesia, Critical Care & Pain Medicine*

## 2. Six conditional recommendations on procalcitonin, and one sentence that separates what the test can do from what it cannot

**Society · Published:** **World Society of Emergency Surgery**, *Antibiotics*, **21 October 2026** — eight
days old. **Held back from yesterday's entry for a fuller read rather than compressed into a fourth slot**,
which is the first time this watch has deliberately deferred an in-scope document, and it is reported today as
promised.

**Method.** A structured literature review covering **January 1980 to December 2025, with targeted updates
through August 2026**, informing **six PICO-based clinical questions**. Evidence appraisal drew on
**selected GRADE domains — consistency, directness, and precision** — rather than full GRADE. Recommendations
developed by iterative expert review and consensus.

**Output: six conditional recommendations**, covering intra-abdominal infection diagnosis, antibiotic
discontinuation after source control, suspected source-control failure, infected pancreatic necrosis,
infectious complications after trauma, and postoperative anastomotic leak.

| Domain | Panel's certainty rating |
| --- | --- |
| Antibiotic discontinuation after source control | **Moderate** |
| Infected pancreatic necrosis | **Moderate** |
| The remaining four domains | **Low** |

**All six recommendations are conditional. None is strong.**

**The sentence that makes the document worth reading:**

> **Evidence for reduced antibiotic exposure with predefined PCT algorithms is more established than evidence
> that PCT-informed imaging, reintervention, or discharge decisions improve patient outcomes.**

**Interpretation.** **That is a clean separation of two different uses of one biomarker, and the asymmetry is
the finding.** Procalcitonin has reasonable evidence for **stopping** something — a protocolised
de-escalation of antibiotics — and much weaker evidence for **starting** something, whether that is a CT, a
return to theatre, or a discharge. **The two uses are not interchangeable and a guideline that treats them as
one would be actively misleading.** The two domains rated moderate are both of the first kind; the four rated
low include the ones that would send a patient back to the operating theatre.

**The document is unusually explicit about its own limits, and the three statements should be quoted
together:**

> **Current evidence does not support universal thresholds or routine serial testing in all surgical
> patients.**
>
> **Biomarker kinetics should strengthen, but not replace, surgical reasoning or independently determine
> antimicrobial, imaging, or interventional decisions.**
>
> Future research should … **establish whether PCT provides actionable information before clinical
> deterioration.**

**That last one is the honest admission.** **The entire clinical case for serial biomarker monitoring rests on
it providing warning earlier than the patient does** — and the panel lists it as an open question rather than
a premise. **A position paper that declines to assume its own mechanism is doing the thing this archive keeps
looking for.**

**My reading.** **The operational content is narrow and usable: a predefined algorithm for stopping
antibiotics after source control, in a patient where the decision is otherwise finely balanced.** Everything
else is framed as adjunctive, and the paper says so. **"Selected GRADE domains" rather than full GRADE is a
real limitation and is declared** — consistency, directness and precision without risk of bias or publication
bias is a partial appraisal, and the certainty ratings should be read as the panel's qualitative judgement,
which is how the abstract describes them.

**One index observation worth logging.** **This record appears twice, and the open-access copy is the one
without an abstract.** The PMC sibling (PMC13700824, open access, dated 1 October) is **abstract-free**; the
publisher record (dated 21 October, no PMCID, not flagged open access) carries the full 2,565 characters.
**That is the reverse of the dual-deposit pattern this archive has logged repeatedly** — usually the
abstract-free copy is the one with the PMCID and the open-access flag, which is what makes the two-field
recovery route work. **Here a reader relying on the open-access record's metadata would find nothing, and the
paywalled record is the one that describes the paper.**

[Read the position paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/antibiotics15101042) · *Antibiotics*

## 3. An Inter-American consensus, and a third distinct cause of a 1 January date

**Society · Published:** **Inter-American Society of Cardiology (SIAC)**, *Archivos de Cardiología de México*,
volume 96, Supplement 4. **The date needs the section below.** Publication types: Systematic Review,
Consensus Statement.

**Method and scope.** SIAC convened a committee of experts from countries across the Americas; a systematic
review of the evidence, then recommendations **validated by formal Delphi methodology with consensus defined
as agreement among more than 80% of participants.** The document covers epidemiology, pathophysiology,
multimodality diagnosis, genetics and family counselling, phenocopies, pharmacological and invasive treatment,
complications, **sudden cardiac death risk assessment**, special populations including **paediatric and
pregnant patients**, physical exercise, and follow-up — plus recommendations aimed at implementation across
Latin America.

**The stated reason for a regional document is the substantive part:**

> HCM … poses major diagnostic and therapeutic challenges in Latin America, owing to **heterogeneity in
> resource availability, access to advanced technologies, and the organization of health systems.**

**Interpretation.** **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is in this archive's secondary scope as cardiology, and its
perioperative relevance is specific and well known** — dynamic outflow obstruction makes vasodilatation,
hypovolaemia and tachycardia all hazardous, so anaesthetising a patient with HCM is a recognised problem and
the sudden-death risk assessment bears directly on it. **But the abstract gives no recommendation count, no
grading scheme and no distribution**, so there is nothing here for this archive's structure catalogue beyond
the 80% Delphi threshold. **The resource-stratification rationale is the document's distinctive feature**, and
it is the same argument APHRS made on 26 October for an Asia-Pacific guideline — **regional guidelines
justified by what is actually available rather than by different biology.**

**And the date, which is a genuinely new finding.** The record carries **`firstPublicationDate` 2026-01-01** —
but this is neither of the two causes this archive has already logged.

| Cause | Signature | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dual-deposit placeholder | abstract-free sibling dated 1 January | logged repeatedly |
| **Future print issue** | `printPublicationDate` in a later year | *Acta Anaesthesiol Scand*, 2027-01-01, logged 26–27 October |
| **Year-only publication date** | **`monthOfPublication` = 0, `dateOfPublication` = "2026 "** | **this record** |

**The journal gives a year and no month for this supplement, so 1 January is simply the year's first day.**
`electronicPublicationDate` is null; the record was created 27 October and entered the index 28 October.

**The discriminator can now be named, which is the useful part: `monthOfPublication` = 0 means the journal
supplied no month at all.** **Where it reads 0, a 1 January date is a year stamp and should be reported as
"2026, month not stated in the index, entered the index on 28 October" — never as 1 January.** Where it reads
a real month and the year is in the future, it is a print-issue date. **Three causes, one field that tells
them apart.**
