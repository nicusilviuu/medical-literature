## The rule again, and it found more than expected

[Yesterday](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-19/) this watch applied the [17 September
rule](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-17/) prospectively for the first time. Today it applied
it again, prompted by [this morning's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-20/), whose lead item
was a review of perioperative SGLT2 inhibitor management that stated:

> **Most guidelines recommend withholding SGLT2 inhibitors for 3-4 days before elective surgery**,
> although recommendations vary by agent, procedure, fasting duration, diabetes status, and
> monitoring capacity.

Perioperative SGLT2 inhibitors have now appeared in this archive three times over four weeks —
[the cardiac surgery AKI trial on 24 August](/medical-literature/brief/2026-08-24/), [the Basel
discontinuation cohort on 5 September](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-05/), and this morning's
review — and **not one of those entries named a guideline.** The archive has been discussing what
"the guidelines" say for a month without ever establishing which guidelines, or what they actually
say.

A targeted search returned **201 documents**. Two matter, and they do not agree with each other.

## 1. The SPAQI consensus statement — the document behind this morning's review

**Society · Published:** **Society for Perioperative Assessment and Quality Improvement (SPAQI)**,
multidisciplinary. *British Journal of Anaesthesia*, **first published 1 May 2026**, June 2026 issue
(volume 136, issue 6) — **four and a half months old**. Indexed as a **Consensus Statement**
Oprea, Mohamed, Hepner, Auron, Richman, Umpierrez, Edmonston, Ionescu, Kumar, Sweitzer

The abstract, quoted closely:

> The perioperative management of patients using sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2is)
> **remains controversial**. The US Food and Drug Administration currently recommends **stopping
> SGLT2is for 3-4 days before surgery, irrespective of a diagnosis of diabetes mellitus. Other
> multisociety recommendations vary significantly** in terms of SGLT2i management, perioperative
> monitoring, and mitigating strategies for euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis.

> This multidisciplinary consensus statement led by SPAQI provides updated recommendations based on
> a **modified Delphi process** and supported by a **systematic review of the literature**. The
> recommendations include **a tailored approach to management of SGLT2is perioperatively based on
> the presence of diabetes mellitus and other comorbidities**, surgical and periprocedural dietary
> considerations, as well as monitoring and prevention strategies for euglycaemic diabetic
> ketoacidosis.

**The recommendations themselves are not in the abstract** — the same limitation this archive has
now recorded for ERC sections, AHA scientific statements, ISHLT consensus documents and the
SECTCV/SEDAR arch consensus. What is available is a **BJA editorial** on the statement (Garcia,
Dixit, Legrand, *BJA*, 12 June 2026; **indexed as an Editorial, not a guideline**), which
characterises it as follows:

> The SPAQI consensus recommends a perioperative management strategy for SGLT2 inhibitors **based on
> surgical type, metabolic context, and underlying indication.** This risk-stratified approach
> **balances the low but real risk of euglycaemic ketoacidosis against established cardio-renal
> benefits.** Current evidence is limited and suggests that **euglycaemic ketoacidosis is driven more
> by metabolic context, particularly diabetes mellitus and fasting, than by drug continuation alone.**
> …these recommendations **shift toward a strategy based on euglycaemic ketoacidosis risk factors and
> careful metabolic monitoring rather than systematic discontinuation** pending large randomised
> controlled trials.

*That second block is an editorial's summary of the consensus, not the consensus text, and it is
reported here as such.* This morning's brief noted the danger of quoting a review's
characterisation instead of the underlying document; the same caution applies to an editorial, and
in this case the underlying document's own abstract does not contain the recommendations to check it
against.

**Interpretation.** This is the fifth structural gap of the month, and the second found by the rule
rather than by accident.

**The substance is that the FDA's 3–4 day rule now has a US perioperative society's Delphi consensus
recommending something else** — stratification by diabetes status, comorbidity, surgical type and
fasting duration. The [Basel discontinuation cohort](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-05/) covered
here on 5 September was published on 11 June 2026, five weeks after this consensus, and its finding
(cardiac complications rising monotonically with days stopped, one euDKA in 451) is precisely the
evidence the consensus was moving toward before that paper existed.

**The editorial's mechanistic claim is the part worth carrying into practice**: that euglycaemic
ketoacidosis is driven **more by metabolic context — diabetes and fasting — than by drug
continuation alone.** If that is right, the blanket withholding rule is aimed at the wrong variable,
and the thing to manage is the fast, not the tablet.

*Access note: full text not reachable. **The stratification table is the usable output of this
document and it has not been read here.** This is now the second-highest-priority full text on the
outstanding list, behind the SECTCV/SEDAR arch consensus.*

## 2. The multidisciplinary consensus in *Anaesthesia* — which the GLP-1 gap-closure should have found

**Published:** A multidisciplinary consensus — anaesthetists, surgeons, physicians, pharmacists and
**people with lived experience**. *Anaesthesia*, **9 January 2025**, April 2025 issue
— **twenty months old**, **cited 84 times**. Indexed as both a **Practice Guideline** and a
**Consensus Statement**
El-Boghdadly, Dhesi, Fabb, Levy, Lobo, McKechnie, Mustafa, Newland-Jones, Patel, Pournaras, Clare,
Dhatariya

Unusually for a society document, **this abstract contains the actual recommendations**:

> Patients taking **glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists and dual glucose-dependent
> insulinotropic peptide receptor agonists should: continue these drugs before surgery**; have full
> risk assessment and stratification; and receive peri-operative techniques that may mitigate risk of
> pulmonary aspiration before, during and after sedation or general anaesthesia. **Patients taking
> sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors should omit them the day before and the day of a
> procedure.** All patients should have risks and mitigation strategies discussed with a **shared
> decision-making approach.**

Method: directed literature review, then a **three-round modified Delphi process**.

**Interpretation.** Two things, and the first is a correction to this archive's own record.

**The [17 September entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-17/) announced the perioperative
GLP-1 guideline gap as closed**, with the ADS/ANZCA/GESA/NACOS recommendations and the Korean
Society of Anaesthesiologists document. **It was not closed.** This consensus is indexed as a **Practice
Guideline** on the same question, published eight months before the Australian one, cited 84 times,
**and published in *Anaesthesia* — a journal on this project's priority list.** The gap-closing
search on 17 September was built around the phrase "GLP-1"; this document's title leads with the
full drug-class names and pairs them with SGLT2 inhibitors, so it fell outside that search and only
surfaced today through a query about a different drug. **A gap declared closed after one targeted
search is a gap searched once, not a gap closed** — that lesson is now in the source notes.

**The second thing is the divergence, which is stark and clinically concrete.** Three documents, one
drug class:

| Source | SGLT2 inhibitor before elective surgery |
|---|---|
| **US FDA** (as quoted by SPAQI) | **Stop 3–4 days**, irrespective of diabetes diagnosis |
| **Consensus in *Anaesthesia*** (Jan 2025) | **Omit the day before and the day of** the procedure |
| **SPAQI consensus** (May 2026) | **Tailored** by diabetes status, comorbidity, surgical type, fasting duration |

**Between a regulator and a specialty practice guideline there is a three-day difference in how long
a cardioprotective drug is withheld**, and the Basel data put an adjusted odds ratio of 1.58 for
cardiac complications on each of those days. This is not an academic disagreement about emphasis;
it is the difference between a patient stopping empagliflozin on Thursday for a Monday list and
stopping it on Sunday night.

**This archive has now catalogued three distinct kinds of guideline divergence** — ESC versus ACC/AHA
on the same evidence ([18 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-18/)), societies
diverging from each other on aortic arch management
([19 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-19/)), and now **a regulator's label
instruction diverging from the specialty societies that have to implement it.** The third kind is
the hardest to resolve, because the FDA's 3–4 days is not a recommendation a clinician can weigh
against evidence — it is what the drug's label says, and it will be what a complaint or a claim is
measured against.

*Also logged from today's search, as evidence rather than guidance: a **systematic review and expert
evaluation of all 128 published case reports of perioperative SGLT2 inhibitor-associated
ketoacidosis** (Snel et al., Acta Anaesthesiologica Scandinavica, 1 July 2026). Its panel judged the
