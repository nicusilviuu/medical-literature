## 1. ISTH on factor concentrates in perioperative bleeding — the first update since 2018

**Society · Published:** **ISTH Scientific and Standardization Committee**, Subcommittee on **Perioperative
and Critical Care Thrombosis and Hemostasis**, *Journal of Thrombosis and Haemostasis*, **28 August 2026** —
six weeks old, 1 citation. `Practice Guideline`.
Levy, Shaw, Siegal, Callum, Frere, Karkouti, Maier, Mazzeffi, Samama, Shorr, Tanaka, Dyke, Connors

> **Perioperative bleeding remains a major cause of morbidity and resource utilization** in patients
> undergoing major surgery, including **cardiac, trauma, obstetrical, and organ transplant surgery.**
> Acquired perioperative coagulopathy is multifactorial and may result from **blood loss, hemodilution,
> hypothermia, acidosis, fibrinolysis, inflammation, and consumption of coagulation factors and platelets.**
> … coagulation factor concentrates have increasingly been incorporated into perioperative bleeding
> management algorithms because of their **rapid availability, standardized dosing, lower infusion volumes,
> and pathogen inactivation, reducing dependence on plasma products. Since the 2018 publication of the ISTH
> guidance** … additional clinical studies, systematic reviews, and evolving practices **have expanded the
> evidence base for the use of fibrinogen concentrate, prothrombin complex concentrate, recombinant activated
> factor VII, and FXIII concentrate** for surgically acquired coagulopathy. This guidance document …
> **provides pragmatic guidance … based on available evidence and expert consensus.**

**Interpretation. The four named products are not equivalent claims and the abstract gives no hierarchy among
them**, which is the main limitation of reading this from the deposit. **Fibrinogen concentrate and
prothrombin complex concentrate** have accumulated real trial evidence in cardiac surgery and in
anticoagulant reversal respectively; **recombinant activated factor VII** has a long history of off-label
rescue use and a known thrombotic signal; **factor XIII concentrate** is the thinnest of the four. **A
document that lists all four in one sentence is telling you the subcommittee reviewed all four, not that it
endorses all four equally**, and which of them moved since 2018 is what the full text is for.

**The rationale paragraph is worth reading as an argument rather than a preamble.** Rapid availability,
standardised dosing, lower infusion volumes, pathogen inactivation — **every one of those is a comparison
against plasma, and the stated aim is "reducing dependence on plasma products."** That is a logistics and
safety case as much as a haemostatic one, and in a trauma or obstetric haemorrhage it is the volume argument
that often decides: giving a few hundred millilitres of concentrate instead of litres of plasma matters to a
patient who is already diluted and cold. **The document names haemodilution and hypothermia in its own list
of causes**, so the internal logic is consistent.

**Where it sits in this archive.** The antithrombotic picture assembled here is **four-sided and about giving
less or safely interrupting** — the AHA statement on harms of undertreatment, and three French documents on
peri-thrombectomy, emergency anticoagulant management, and (still unreported) planned invasive procedures.
**This is the fifth side and it is the opposite problem: the patient is already bleeding and the question is
what to give.** It also lands on the obstetric thread opened on 7 October — **obstetrical surgery is named in
the first sentence**, and the French obstetric emergency guidelines reported that day cover postpartum
haemorrhage prevention and management as one of their eight areas.

**One scope note.** The subcommittee's remit is **perioperative and critical care** thrombosis and
haemostasis — not haematology in general — which makes this squarely a primary-scope document despite
appearing in a haematology journal. **A society-acronym sweep on anaesthesia and intensive care societies
would never return it**, and that is the third route-blindness example this archive has recorded in two
weeks.

[Read the guidance](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtha.2026.07.030) — paywalled beyond the abstract.

## 2. The same society, the same journal, and this time the abstract is there

**Society · Published:** **ISHLT**, *Journal of Heart and Lung Transplantation*, **5 September 2026** — five
weeks old. `Practice Guideline`. **Multidisciplinary international writing committee, structured consensus
process, adult and paediatric populations.**
McIlvennan, Anthony, Dobbels, Kennedy, Petty, Sanchez, Saylor, Blumenthal, Breathett, Clancy, Coleman,
Cousino, Dave, DuBrock, Duerinckx, Fregoso and colleagues

> **Social support systems, particularly caregivers, play a critical role** in the evaluation, treatment, and
> recovery of patients undergoing heart or lung transplantation or **left ventricular assist device (LVAD)
> implantation. However, definitions, assessment practices, and expectations for social support vary
> considerably across programs, and evidence guiding optimal caregiver engagement remains limited.** … Social
> support encompasses **medical management, logistical, emotional, informational, psychological, and
> companionship assistance**, with caregiver needs and responsibilities **evolving across the treatment
> trajectory. Existing assessment approaches and interventions are heterogeneous, with limited longitudinal
> evidence.** Consensus recommendations emphasize **early caregiver identification and engagement, structured
> and tailored education and skills training, longitudinal assessment of caregiver needs and burden,
> integration of caregivers into clinical workflows, access to psychosocial and palliative care resources,
> and peer support. A strengths-based, individualized approach that recognizes social and structural
> barriers** is recommended to promote **equitable access.**

**Interpretation, and start with the correction, because this document refutes something written here
yesterday.** Yesterday's entry recorded a rule from the ESOT and ISHLT pairing: *deposit behaviour is a
property of the publisher and the society, not of the topic.* **This is an ISHLT expert consensus statement,
in the same journal as the three abstract-free ISHLT records, and it deposits its abstract in full.** So the
rule is too strong. **Deposit behaviour is not even consistent within one society in one journal in one
year** — it varies document by document, and the only reliable statement is the one the record itself makes.
**The practical consequence is unchanged and now better founded: never infer from one abstract-free record
that a society or journal does not deposit. Check each document.**

**On the substance: this is a guideline about who is standing next to the patient, and that is a
transplant-specific hard requirement rather than a courtesy.** Caregiver availability is used by transplant
programmes as a **listing criterion** — a patient without adequate social support can be declined for
transplantation or for an LVAD. **So "definitions, assessment practices, and expectations vary considerably
across programs" is not an administrative observation; it means an allocation decision is being made against
an unstandardised assessment.** That is the sharpest reading of the document and the reason it matters more
than its subject matter suggests.

**The recommendation that follows from that is the equity one**, and the statement names it: a
**strengths-based, individualized approach that recognizes social and structural barriers**, to promote
**equitable access.** A support requirement applied uniformly disadvantages patients who are poor, isolated,
or far from the centre — **the requirement is real and the assessment of it is where the inequity enters.**

**And the honest part is the evidence statement.** *"Evidence guiding optimal caregiver engagement remains
limited"*; *"existing assessment approaches and interventions are heterogeneous, with limited longitudinal
evidence."* **Six recommendations on an acknowledged thin base, labelled as consensus** — the same structure
this archive has recorded repeatedly, and here declared in the abstract rather than buried.

**With this, the lung-transplant set in this archive has six pieces and is now mostly readable:** ISHLT on
baseline allograft dysfunction (consensus and perspective, **abstract-free**), ISHLT on frailty in candidates
(**abstract-free**), **ISHLT on social support (readable, today)**, SPLF on indications and contraindications
(readable, queued), and ESOT on chronic allograft dysfunction (readable, reported yesterday). **Candidate
selection now has two readable documents and one unreadable one; graft function has one of each.**

[Read the consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healun.2026.07.037)

## 3. A care pathway from two hospitals, indexed as a practice guideline

**Published:** *European Journal of Obstetrics, Gynecology and Reproductive Biology*, **27 August 2026** —
six and a half weeks old. Indexed `Practice Guideline`. **Developed by an interdisciplinary expert panel
drawn from Antwerp University Hospital and Erasmus MC**, via review of international cardiac and obstetric
guidelines plus consensus meetings.
Vanharen, Mannaerts, Kauling, Roos-Hesselink, Cornette, Trongthiang, Goossens, Van Berendoncks

> **Women with pre-existing cardiovascular disease (CVD) face elevated maternal and fetal risks and
> implementing existing cardio-obstetric guidelines remains challenging.** … the aim … was to develop a
> practical, expert-based **integrated care pathway (ICP) for women with intermediate to high CVD risk during
> antepartum and postpartum periods.** …
>
> **Key ICP steps include the first visit, second and early third trimester visits to a specialised clinic,
> birth planning, and postpartum follow-up.** At the first visit … patients should **see a dedicated
> cardiologist, undergo medication review with substitution for pregnancy-safe options if needed, and receive
> psychosocial assessment** … **genetic counselling when indicated.** … **In the late second to early third
> trimester, a birth plan should be defined, covering delivery methods, (duration of) haemodynamic
> monitoring, pain management, and medication or fluid restrictions.** Postpartum care includes **close
> monitoring for CVD complications, cardiovascular assessment at predefined timepoints, and ongoing
> (psychosocial) symptom surveillance.**

**Interpretation. The document class needs stating before the content: this is a two-hospital care pathway,
not a society guideline, and Europe PMC has indexed it as `Practice Guideline`.** It says so plainly —
*"expert-based"*, developed by a panel from two named university hospitals, *"complemented based on clinical
experience"*. **That is a local implementation document of real quality, and it is not a national or society
recommendation.** Anyone citing it should say which it is. **This archive has now seen the pubType field get
the class wrong or ambiguous three times in three days** — a two-author `Practice Guideline` on arterial
injuries, a document that is simultaneously `Systematic Review` and `Practice Guideline`, and this.

**On the substance, the anaesthetic content is in one sentence and it is the right sentence.** The birth plan
is to cover **delivery method, the duration of haemodynamic monitoring, pain management, and medication or
fluid restrictions** — **four decisions that are all anaesthetic and all made better in the late second
trimester than in labour.** The pathway's contribution is not new evidence; **it is a schedule that forces
those decisions to be made at a defined timepoint by a defined team**, which is exactly what the
implementation literature this archive has been tracking says is missing.

**Its stated problem is implementation, not knowledge** — *"implementing existing cardio-obstetric guidelines
remains challenging"* — and that is the fourth document in two weeks whose rationale is variation or
implementation rather than new evidence: the French chest drainage guidelines, the Chinese renal replacement
therapy guideline, the ESPEN practical reformatting, and this. **A noticeable share of current guidance
exists to make existing guidance happen.**

**And it extends the obstetric thread with the piece that thread most lacked.** The French obstetric
emergency guidelines reported on [7 October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-07/) **could not issue a
recommendation on maternal cardiac arrest**; this archive logged that absence and the search for
maternal-arrest guidance as a named gap. **This document is not that** — it is antenatal and postpartum
planning for known cardiac disease, the elective end of the same problem — **but it is the first document
here that treats the pregnant cardiac patient as a scheduled anaesthetic problem rather than an emergency**,
and the gap on arrest stays open.

[Read the pathway](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejogrb.2026.115397) — paywalled beyond the abstract.

a separate prose grep run afterwards for context rather than for exclusion. **Three days of use, two true

the more dangerous failure of the two, because a duplicate is visible on the page and a suppression is not.*
