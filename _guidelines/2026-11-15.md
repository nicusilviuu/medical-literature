## No new guidelines from tracked societies today

**No new or updated guidance appeared from SFAR, SPILF, SRLF, ESAIC, ESC, ESPEN, ESICM, SCCM, IACTS, EACTS,
STS or ASA.** The daily guidance sweep for 8 to 15 November returned **23 records, 23 collected, shortfall
zero**, of which five were absent from this archive and **all five were out of scope** — a genetics follow-up
protocol, a nurse triage audit instrument, a Canadian implementation study, a high-altitude
preacclimatisation position statement, and a postanaesthesia nursing case library.

**So the watch spent the day on the audit that this week argued for.**

---

## 1. The 2026 society-name audit: what 176 records actually contain

**On 13 November a society web page found a guideline the daily index route had missed. On 14 November
another one did. The action logged that evening was to sweep the whole of 2026 by tracked-society NAME and
find the rest in one pass.** That sweep ran today.

**Query: any of twenty-four tracked-society names or abbreviations in the title, publication date in 2026, and
a guidance word in the title. Result: 176 records, 176 collected, shortfall zero. 142 are not in this
archive.**

**142 is not 142 missed guidelines, and the classification is the point of the exercise.**

| Category | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Not primary guidance — commentaries on guidelines, corrections, errata, retractions, adherence and large-language-model studies, "ten commandments" summaries, implementation appraisals | **85** |
| Genuine society guidance, in scope, **published before this archive existed** (before 17 August 2026) | **48** |
| Genuine society guidance, in scope, **inside the coverage window** | **9** |

**The 85 are the sweep working as designed and finding the wrong thing.** A query for a society name plus a
guidance word catches every paper *about* a guideline as well as the guideline, and in cardiology that is
most of the yield — a 2026 literature of ChatGPT-adherence studies, "key takeaways for surgeons" pieces and
corrections to the 2025 valvular heart disease guidelines. **This archive routes documents about guidance to
the watch by rule, so these are not out of scope by category; they are out of scope by being derivative, and
a name-based sweep cannot tell the difference.** **The filter that worked was a hand-built exclusion list,
which is not a durable instrument.**

**The 48 are a bounded backlog and not a failure.** This archive's first brief is dated 17 August 2026 and
its first guidelines entry 28 August, so a society document published in March 2026 was never within its
reach. **What the audit changes is that the backlog is now counted and named rather than unknown.** The
in-scope ones worth having include **four Society of Critical Care Medicine guidelines from March** — on
neuromuscular blockade in acute respiratory failure, on allocating critical care resources in crisis, on
caring for older adults in the intensive care unit, and the 2026 paediatric and neonatal intensive care
guidelines — **the 2026 ESAIC consensus document on mitigation strategies in intensive care medicine** from
July, **two ESPEN practical guidelines**, **three 2026 American Association for Thoracic Surgery expert
consensus documents** from early May, **two American Society of Regional Anesthesia corticosteroid
guidelines** from June and July, and the **updated SFAR and SFA guidelines on perioperative immediate
hypersensitivity** from July. **None is new, all are dated, and they are now a queue rather than a blind
spot.**

**The 9 are the real finding and four of them are one society in one week.**

## 2. Four EACTS expert consensus documents from the first week of October

**All four were published inside this archive's coverage window, all four are squarely in cardiothoracic
scope, and none has been reported here.**

### Aortic valve repair and valve-sparing root replacement

**[EACTS Expert Consensus Document on aortic valve repair and valve-sparing aortic root replacement
procedures](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejcts/ezag182)** · *European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery* ·
**1 October 2026** · indexed as Systematic Review, Consensus Statement, Journal Article

> This European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery (EACTS) Expert Consensus Document critically
> evaluates the current evidence on the common causes and mechanisms of aortic regurgitation, the use of
> echocardiographic parameters in patient selection, intraoperative assessment, and prognostic evaluation as
> well as the surgical techniques in practice. … **Where high-quality evidence is unavailable, the guidance
> relies on the collective experience and expert judgement of the multidisciplinary author team.** This
> document presents the official stance of the EACTS…

**The quoted sentence is the one this archive credits.** A consensus document that states, in its own
abstract, which parts rest on expert judgement rather than evidence is doing the thing the format-decision
rule asks for. **It is addressed to paediatric and adult cardiologists, echocardiographers, imaging
specialists and cardiac surgeons — and not, notably, to anaesthetists**, although intraoperative assessment
is one of its four domains.

### Fitness to fly after cardiothoracic surgery

**[EACTS Expert Consensus Document on Civil Aviation Following Cardiothoracic Surgery and Transcatheter
Procedures](https://doi.org/10.1093/icvts/ivag217)** · *Interdisciplinary CardioVascular and Thoracic
Surgery* · **1 October 2026** · indexed as Review, Consensus Statement, Journal Article

> Professional aircrew and other safety-critical aviation personnel must meet legally defined medical
> standards that are designed around **an engineering approach to risk, including a low annual tolerance for
> sudden incapacitation**, whereas passengers and cabin crew are exposed to the physiological constraints of
> the flight environment (hypobaric hypoxia, gas expansion, limited access to medical care, immobility, and
> circadian rhythm disruption). Yet, the evidence base guiding fitness-to-fly decisions after cardiothoracic
> procedures … **remains sparse and unevenly distributed across procedures, licensing classes, and
> operational contexts.**

**This is the most unusual document in today's entry and the most interesting to read against the rest of the
week.** **It is explicit that two different risk frameworks are in play: an engineering tolerance for sudden
incapacitation that governs aircrew licensing, and a physiological-exposure framework that governs
passengers.** **A surgeon discharging a patient is answering one question; an aeromedical examiner
certifying a pilot is answering another, with a numerical annual risk tolerance behind it.** **That is the
clearest statement this archive has seen all fortnight of why one clinical fact can produce two different
decisions — which is the definitional theme in a new setting.** The determinants it names are ones a
cardiothoracic team already discusses: procedure durability, residual ischaemia or haemodynamic burden,
arrhythmia risk, anticoagulation, devices, rehabilitation and surveillance.

### Iatrogenic aortic dissection

**[EACTS Expert Consensus Document on Iatrogenic Aortic Dissection](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejcts/ezag221)** ·
*European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery* · **7 October 2026** · **indexed only as "Journal Article"**

> Iatrogenic aortic dissection (IAD) is a rare but life-threatening complication of open cardiac surgical
> procedures and catheter-based interventions… **Unlike spontaneous aortic dissection, IAD typically results
> from direct intimal injury during cannulation, clamping, catheter manipulation, or device deployment** —
> while its clinical course is strongly influenced by the procedural context and the speed of recognition.
> Evidence to guide the management of IAD remains limited and is largely derived from registries and
> observational reports… **This … Expert Consensus Document provides the first dedicated, multidisciplinary
> guidance on IAD**, covering definitions and classification, risk factors, prevention, early diagnosis,
> acute management pathways, and post-discharge surveillance.

**This is the one with the strongest claim on an intensive care and anaesthesia readership and it was the
most invisible.** **A dissection caused by cannulation or clamping happens in the operating theatre with the
anaesthetist present, and the document says its course depends on "the speed of recognition".** **It
describes itself as the first dedicated guidance on the condition, and it includes definitions and
classification — so it is also a definitional document for a condition managed in intensive care, which this
archive's 31 October rule puts in scope whatever specialty publishes it.**

### Lung volume reduction for emphysema

**[EACTS Expert Consensus Document on Lung Volume Reduction for
Emphysema](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejcts/ezag226)** · *European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery* ·
**7 October 2026** · **indexed only as "Journal Article"**

> …**LVR remains a considerably underutilized intervention partly due to a paucity of practice guidelines.**
> … There have been expert statements on EBLVR, but **this is the first multidisciplinary statement also to
> consider lung volume reduction surgery (LVRS).** … Where no clear comparative evidence is available, the
> pros and cons of each debate are clearly illustrated. … **The benefits of LVR are detailed, but
> importantly, so are the perioperative risks.**

**A document that names under-use as the problem and attributes it to the absence of guidelines is making an
unusually direct claim about its own purpose.** **And the sentence about perioperative risk matters for this
archive's readership: lung volume reduction surgery in a patient on maximal medical therapy for emphysema is
one of the highest-risk elective thoracic anaesthetics there is.**

## 3. Why they were missed: two reasons, both checkable

**This archive has been asking since 13 November why documents with ordinary index dates escape the daily
route. Today there are two answers for one set of documents, and they are independent.**

### Reason one: 16 October, which is now implicated five times

| Document | Publication date | **First index date** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aortic valve repair | 1 October | **2026-10-16** |
| Civil aviation | 1 October | **2026-10-16** |
| Iatrogenic aortic dissection | 7 October | **2026-10-16** |
| Lung volume reduction | 7 October | **2026-10-16** |
| Robotic cardiac surgery (the one the October audit DID find) | 1 October | **2026-10-16** |

**All five EACTS documents from that week share a first index date of 16 October 2026 — the rollback day.**
On that day this archive measured the database 80,022 records short, with every record first indexed on or
after 25 September absent, and the brief was written during the nineteen minutes the loss lasted.

**On 13 November this archive wrote that the rollback was "a real candidate" for one missed document — the
7 October vasopressin emulation, first indexed 16 October — "and no candidate at all" for three missed
*JAMA* trials first indexed on the 19th.** **Today adds four more documents at 16 October.** **So the
candidate has become a pattern: five documents first indexed on 16 October 2026 have been missed by this
archive, found later by two unrelated investigations.** **That is not proof the rollback caused it** — the
fifth document with the same index date was found — **but it is now the single most-implicated date in the
archive's history, and it is checkable: re-run the 16 October sweep as recorded and see what comes back.**

### Reason two: two of the four carry no guidance publication type at all

**`ezag182` and `ivag217` are indexed as "Consensus Statement". `ezag221` and `ezag226` are indexed as
"Journal Article" and nothing else.**

**The October month audit of 1 November was, in its own words, a "guideline-pubType sweep". It therefore
could not have found the iatrogenic aortic dissection or lung volume reduction documents however carefully it
was run.** **That is a structural limit of the instrument, not an error in its execution** — and it means the
audit's reported hit rate of 83.0% on in-scope guideline-class documents was measured against a denominator
that excluded two documents it had no way to see.

**RULE, and it changes the November audit due at the start of December: a publication-type sweep cannot find
guidance that the index does not type as guidance. The month audit must run a TITLE-WORD sweep alongside the
pubType sweep, and the two must be compared.** **Today's society-name sweep found both untyped documents
precisely because it keyed on title words instead.**

## 4. The ASA page, reached on the second attempt — the seven-society action is complete

**Yesterday's entry recorded that the ASA in-development page returned 404 and that the failure was mine
rather than evidence about the ASA. A different URL worked today.**

**The page shows no documents in development, under revision, or open for public comment, and it carries a
"date of last update" of 20 October 2026** — which makes it the most current of the seven society pages
checked, against ESPEN's, last updated in September 2023.

**It lists 15 practice guidelines, 10 practice advisories, 3 standards and 110 statements.** Two entries fall
inside this archive's coverage window and had not been recorded: **the Statement on Fatigue, revised
20 October 2026, and the Statement on Anesthetic Care during Interventional Pain Procedures for Adults,
reaffirmed 20 October 2026.** **A reaffirmation is a dated editorial act and worth logging** — it is the
society saying a document still stands, which is information the index does not carry.

**One near-miss worth reporting because it shows the dedupe working.** The page's newest guideline, marked
"New!", is the **2026 ASA Practice Guideline on Perioperative Pain Management Using Local and Regional
Analgesia for Cardiothoracic Surgeries, Mastectomy, and Abdominal Surgeries** — core scope, and exactly the
kind of document the last two days have found missing. **It is not missing: this archive published it on
22 August 2026**, with its journal citation and its January 2026 date. **And it carries a fourth
year-in-title mismatch of the week, in the opposite direction from the others: titled 2026 and first indexed
9 December 2025.** After ASRA (metadata twenty-one months late), ESAIC (title a year early) and the German
ulcerative colitis document (title honestly dated), **this one is titled a year AHEAD of its publication** —
which is ordinary journal practice for a guideline dated to its issue year, and worth naming only because
four variants have now appeared in four days.

**With this, the in-development action closes across all seven societies: SCCM 13 documents in progress,
ESICM 6, ESC 9 scheduled with chairpersons, ESAIC an empty tab, EACTS no section at all, ESPEN a page three
years stale, and ASA no section and a current page.** **Twenty-eight documents are in progress at the three
societies that publish a list.**

## 5. The hash rule's last piece: equal length, different hash, one letter

**[Management of Direct Oral Anticoagulants in Adult Patients Undergoing Cardiac Surgery: A Joint Consensus
Statement by the Society of Cardiovascular Anesthesiologists and The Society of Thoracic
Surgeons](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.athoracsur.2026.01.005)** · co-published in *The Annals of Thoracic
Surgery* and *Anesthesia & Analgesia* · **both 29 January 2026 — before this archive existed** · paywalled

**On substance this is a document a cardiac anaesthetist should have: a systematic review plus expert
consensus covering when to stop a direct oral anticoagulant before cardiac surgery, how to monitor it,
whether to bridge, which reversal agents to use, and when to restart.** It is pre-coverage and is reported
here with its date.

**It is in this entry for the method finding, which completes a rule this archive has been building since
22 October.**

| Case | Lengths | Hashes | What it meant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AHA/ACC 2026 co-publications | equal | **identical** | genuinely byte-identical |
| German ECPR, two journals | 917 / 917 | **identical** | genuinely byte-identical |
| SCCM/ESICM refractory shock | 2,283 / 2,275 | different | near-identical, real edits |
| EACTS/STS/AATS temporary support, four journals | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 | identical | **no information at all** |
| **This one** | **777 / 777** | **different** | **differ — and the difference is one letter's case** |

**Two deposits of exactly 777 characters with different hashes.** `81d0e3066c2c7c36` against
`c9898f92a808e3e2`. **A character-level diff returns a single opcode: position 65, where one journal has "The
Society of Thoracic Surgeons" and the other has "the Society of Thoracic Surgeons".** Similarity 0.998713.

**So the taxonomy is complete and the rule gets its final clause.** **Equal length never settles identity —
here it is exactly equal and the deposits differ. A differing hash never establishes a substantive
difference — here it is one letter's case. All empty deposits hash identically and say nothing.**
**Therefore: use the length as a flag, the hash to detect, and a DIFF to interpret. The hash tells you
whether to look; only the diff tells you whether it matters.**
