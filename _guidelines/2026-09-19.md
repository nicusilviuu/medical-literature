**No new guidelines from tracked societies today.** The trailing-window sweep for 17–19 September
returned 71 guideline-titled hits; **everything in scope was either covered yesterday** — the
[STS oesophageal perforation consensus](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-18/), the Heart

**The French sweep returned seven *Infectious Diseases Now* items**, including a phage therapy news

nineteen days without guideline output.**

## Applying the rule from 17 September

Two days ago this watch recorded a rule: **when a topic recurs in the briefs for more than about
three days with no guideline behind it, search for the guideline directly.** Today is the first time
it has been applied prospectively rather than after noticing a gap.

The trigger was [this morning's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-19/), whose fourth item
showed **carotid perfusion cannula resistance varying three- to four-fold with no relation to French
size**, halving left-sided inflow during bilateral selective antegrade cerebral perfusion. Aortic
arch surgery and cerebral protection have now appeared in this archive four times in a fortnight —
the [EACTS/STS aortic organ guidelines](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-04/), still only
partially covered; the [frozen elephant trunk landing-angle paper](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-18/);
today's cannula study — **and no guideline on cerebral protection had ever appeared here.**

A targeted search returned **22 documents**. Three are worth reporting.

## 1. The SECTCV/SEDAR anaesthetic-surgical consensus — the document this archive should have had

**Societies · Published:** Spanish Society of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery and the **Spanish
Society of Anaesthesiology, Resuscitation and Pain Therapy (SEDAR)**. *Revista Española de
Anestesiología y Reanimación*, **12 March 2022** — **four and a half years old**. Indexed as a
**Consensus Statement**
López Gómez, Rodríguez, Zebdi, Ríos Barrera, Forteza, Legarra Calderón, Garrido Martín, Hernando,
Sanjuan, González Bardanca, Varela Martínez, Fernández, Llorens, Valera Martínez, Gómez Felices,
Aranda Granados, Sádaba Sagredo, Echevarría

**This is the only joint anaesthesia-and-cardiac-surgery consensus on aortic arch surgery this
archive has located**, and its framing is explicit about why it exists:

> Aortic pathology is always a challenge for the clinician, and **must be diagnosed and treated by a
> multidisciplinary team** due to the technical and technological complexity of the resources used.
> Ongoing efforts to implement a systematic, protocolized approach involving **"Aortic teams"** made
> up of cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, vascular surgeons, **anaesthesiologists** and radiologists,
> among others are now leading to improved outcomes.

On the aortic team concept, which it takes from EACTS/ESVS:

- The aortic team should be **closely involved from diagnosis to treatment and follow-up**, formed of
  cardiac and vascular surgeons **working together with anaesthesiologists, cardiologists,
  radiologists and geneticists**
- **Treatment should be centralised in large centres**, because *"this is the only way to effectively
  understand the natural course of the disease, provide the entire range of treatment options under
  one umbrella and treat potential complications"*
- A **streamlined emergent care pathway with 24/7 availability**, adequate transport and transfer
  capability, and **rapid activation of the multidisciplinary team** must be available

And an unusual closing admission: *"Some questions will no doubt remain unanswered, and future
versions will include new techniques that, though implemented in some centres, are still not widely
recommended."*

**Interpretation.** Three points, and the first is about the archive rather than the document.

**An anaesthesia society co-authoring a surgical consensus on arch surgery is exactly the kind of
document this project exists to surface, and it took a targeted search to find it — four and a half
years after publication.** The daily watch could never have seen it. The landmark backlog didn't
look for it because "aortic arch" was filed under cardiothoracic surgery, and the societies on the
list that write about the aorta are EACTS and STS, not SEDAR. **That is the same structural failure
as ERC, ISHLT and GLP-1 — the fourth instance, and the first caught before it became a visible
gap.**

**The "aortic team" is the substantive content, and it is the same organisational move this archive
has now seen four times this month** — the endocarditis team in the
[AHA statement](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-09/), the heart team in the
[TAVI durability review](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-10/), the call to
[integrate nephrology into ECMO programmes](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-11/), and now
this. **Four specialties independently concluding that their hardest problem needs a standing
multidisciplinary structure rather than an ad hoc referral.** The pattern is more informative than
any one instance.

**Centralisation is the recommendation with teeth and the one least likely to be followed**, because
it is a statement about where patients should *not* be operated on. Coupled with the 24/7 pathway
requirement, it is a resource argument disguised as a clinical one — and correctly so, given that
acute type A dissection has a mortality measured in percent per hour.

*Access note: the abstract is substantial but describes the document's framing and the aortic team
concept. **The anaesthetic protocols themselves — the reason to read it — are not in the abstract**
and the full text was not reachable. This is the document to obtain if any on the outstanding list
is worth obtaining.*

[Read the consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.redare.2021.02.010) · PMID 35288050

## 2. Chinese consensus on cerebral protection in cardiac surgery

**Society · Published:** Cerebral Protection in Cardiac Intensive Care Group, Neural Regeneration
and Repair Committee, **Chinese Research Hospital Association**. *Zhonghua Wei Zhong Bing Ji Jiu Yi
Xue*, **February 2019** — **seven and a half years old**

Drafted by experts in **cardiac surgery, critical care medicine, extracorporeal circulation,
anaesthesiology and emergency medicine** — again, the perfusion and anaesthetic disciplines at the
table.

Stated scope:

- **Brain injury classification and risk factor recognition**
- **Treatment protocol formulation**
- **Intraoperative cerebral function monitoring**
- **Brain protection in the ICU**
- **Drug administration**

The rationale: *"Cerebral complication in the postoperative period of cardiac surgery is one of the
most important elements for the prognosis and rehabilitation."*

**Interpretation.** **The reason to report a seven-year-old document is the third bullet:
intraoperative cerebral function monitoring** — which is the only guidance this archive has found
that addresses the question today's cannula paper raises.

That paper predicts **pressure laterality of 14–23 mmHg** between hemispheres during bilateral SACP
when a high-resistance cannula is used, and the bedside signal for that is **near-infrared
spectroscopy asymmetry**. A consensus covering intraoperative cerebral monitoring is where the
threshold for acting on such a finding would live. **Whether this document contains one cannot be
determined from the abstract**, which lists topics only — the recurring limitation of guideline
abstracts noted throughout this archive.

**Its age is the honest caveat, and it is substantial.** Seven and a half years in a field where
NIRS interpretation, processed EEG and the entire debate about deep versus moderate hypothermia have
all moved. Read as evidence that the question has been formally addressed somewhere, not as current
guidance.

[Read the consensus](https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.2095-4352.2019.02.001) · PMID 30827295

## 3. Current options and recommendations for aortic arch disease — the contemporary review

**Published:** *Annals of Cardiothoracic Surgery*, **1 July 2026** — **two and a half months old**
Berger, **Czerny**, Kondov, Lescan, Kreibich, Grabenwöger

A review rather than a society document — Czerny's group in Freiburg, who wrote much of the EACTS
guidance — and the most current overview available.

- Emphasis on **anatomical classification systems, including the Ishimaru zones** and variations in
  arch morphology, *"essential for standardized reporting and procedural planning"*
- **Open arch replacement, often with hypothermic circulatory arrest and selective antegrade cerebral
  perfusion, remains the reference standard** for patients with acceptable operative risk, on
  durability and long-term outcomes
- **Branched and fenestrated TEVAR** — custom-made and off-the-shelf — expanding options for high
  surgical risk
- Conclusion: **careful patient selection on anatomy, disease acuity, comorbidity and institutional
  expertise**, requiring an **individualised, multidisciplinary approach**

**Interpretation.** Useful mainly as a current statement of where the reference standard sits, and
it sits where it has: **open replacement with circulatory arrest and selective antegrade cerebral
perfusion, for anyone who can tolerate it.**

**That is what makes today's cannula finding matter.** The reference operation for arch disease
depends on SACP; SACP through a Y-connector depends on cannula resistance; and **cannula resistance
turns out not to be predictable from the only number anyone quotes.** A review published ten weeks
ago restates the technique as standard without any suggestion that its delivery is this variable.

**The Ishimaru zones connect to a known gap.** This archive [partially covered the EACTS/STS aortic
organ guidelines on 4 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-04/), reporting the aortic
organ concept, TEM classification, Ishimaru zones, GERAADA score and BAV phenotypes from the
publisher's visible page — **with the recommendation tables still unreachable.** This review is by
overlapping authors and is a reasonable substitute for the classification content, though not for
the recommendations.

[Read the review](https://doi.org/10.21037/acs-2026-0169-aar) · PMID 42597976
