over 3–13 October returned **zero — the twelfth consecutive day.** The targeted society and acronym sweep over
1–13 October returned **264 hits and all 264 records**, the page-size discipline adopted yesterday applied

**That is a correction to yesterday's entry, which said nine of ten sources were frozen. There are eleven
sources, not ten** — `CIT`, 624 computer-science citation stubs, was missing from the baseline because it
was never queried. The count is trivial; **the omission is not, because a decomposition is only an integrity
check if it is complete.** This one now is: **source counts plus nothing else equal the whole-database total,
to the record, when measured at the same instant.** That is a sound decomposition, unlike the rolling trailing
window retracted on 11 October, and for the plain reason that sources partition the database and all eleven
were measured together. **Had it existed on 12 October, the loss of 61,746 would have been localised in one
query set instead of a day later.**

is gaining about sixty records a minute, so the question of *where* those records land is answerable by

**156 plus 13 is 169, which is the whole movement. Nothing landed after 2009, and 92% of it landed before
2000.** Meanwhile `MED` is unchanged to the record, and both fixed reference windows are unchanged to the
record — 1–30 September **148,993**, 1–7 October **20,218** — for the second consecutive day.

**So yesterday's reading was right but conflated two things.** "Both fixed windows frozen, therefore ingest
has stopped" was correct. What the whole-database total hid is that **a back-catalogue restore is running at
speed at the same time.** One number moving upward was doing two jobs: it was the 1990s coming back, and it
was the present not arriving. **That is the third time in a week that a single monitored figure turned out to
be a sum of two opposite movements**, and the lesson is becoming hard to miss: a total is not a measurement
until it is decomposed.

**The prediction is now much sharper than yesterday's.** Yesterday's was "PMC should keep rising toward about
995,000 and the frozen sources should resume together" — true so far, and weak, because almost any
restoration satisfies it. **Today's is specific: if the restore is running in date order, the 2010–2019 era
bucket should be the next to move, and 2026 should be the last.** If instead the era buckets move together, or
2026 moves before 2010–2019, the restore is not ordered by date and something else is going on. **Either way
the test is one query set tomorrow, and it distinguishes the hypothesis from its alternatives rather than
merely being consistent with it.**

**What this changes operationally: nothing yet, and that is worth saying plainly.** Retrieval is unaffected —
the Brazilian guideline below was read in full from its PubMed Central deposit today. The archive is running
from its queue and its backlog, which is what they are for.

## 1. AHA and ASA replace their 2016 stroke rehabilitation guideline — and name the thing that just failed a trial

**Society · Published:** **American Heart Association / American Stroke Association**, *Stroke*,
**27 August 2026** — **forty-seven days old**, not new, 1 citation. Typed `Practice Guideline`.
**Not open access and with no PubMed Central deposit, so what follows is the official abstract; the
recommendation set itself is behind the publisher.**

> **Aim** The "2026 Guidelines for Adult Stroke Rehabilitation and Recovery" **replaces the 2016**
> "Guidelines for Adult Stroke Rehabilitation and Recovery." This updated guideline is intended to provide a
> comprehensive, up-to-date, evidence-based set of recommendations. **The intended audience includes
> physicians, allied health professionals, and caregivers.**
>
> **Methods** A comprehensive search for literature published since the 2016 guideline … was conducted
> **between June and October 2025.**
>
> **Structure** … **Key updates include new and revised recommendations across the stroke recovery continuum,
> including the assessment and treatment of medical comorbidities, fracture risk, fall prevention,
> technology-enabled rehabilitation, caregiver support, participation, and safe return to work and driving.**
> Although this guideline reflects significant advances, **it also highlights gaps in knowledge and
> underscores the urgent need for continued research.**

**Interpretation.** **This completes the rehabilitation set, and the set is more interesting than any of its
three parts.** Within four days in late August, two independent panels issued rehabilitation guidance — this
one on 27 August, and the [Korean clinical practice guidelines on rehabilitation in critical illness](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-12/)
on 31 August, fifteen recommendations of which thirteen were conditional. **And the largest recent trial of
delivering rehabilitation at scale, reported in [yesterday's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-12/),
found nothing measurable in the survivors**: a stepped-wedge trial of integrated telehealth rehabilitation
across twenty Brazilian ICUs, adjusted quality-of-life difference **−0.045 (−0.138 to 0.045)**, its
whole-cohort benefit driven by reduced mortality rather than by recovery in the people who lived.

**The sharp point is in the AHA's own list of key updates: "technology-enabled rehabilitation."** That is the
category the Brazilian trial tested, at national scale, in the most intensively rehabilitated population
there is — and could not show working on the outcome it was powered for. **A guideline naming it as a key
update and a trial failing to find it are not necessarily in conflict**, because a recommendation can be
conditional and a trial can be testing one delivery model rather than the concept. **But it is not possible to
tell which from the abstract**, and that is the limitation here rather than a criticism of the document: the
grades are what would settle it, and the grades are behind the paywall. **The Korean guideline, which is
readable, graded thirteen of fifteen conditional. If the AHA's technology recommendations are graded the same
way, the three documents are consistent and the honest summary is that nobody yet knows whether
technology-enabled rehabilitation works.** That reading is checkable the moment the full text is reachable,
and it is logged as such rather than asserted.

**One structural detail worth noting on its own.** The intended audience includes **caregivers** — not
clinicians and allied health professionals alone. A guideline that addresses the family directly is making a
claim about where the intervention actually happens, and it sits oddly beside a trial whose remote-delivery
arm did not move survivor quality of life.

## 2. Brazil's pulmonary hypertension guideline: one strong yes built on one trial, and four ways of saying no

**Society · Published:** **Sociedade Brasileira de Pneumologia e Tisiologia (Brazilian Thoracic Society)**,
*Respiratory Research & Clinical Practice* vol. 52 issue 3, **13 August 2026** — **sixty-one days old**,
GRADE methodology, four PICO questions, 0 citations. **Open access, and read in full today.**

> Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a complex and heterogeneous clinical condition associated with high
> morbidity and mortality … **This guideline aims to define updated Brazilian recommendations for the
> treatment of PH, with an emphasis on pulmonary arterial hypertension, considering the available scientific
> evidence and the particularities of the Brazilian health system.** Four structured questions in the PICO
> format … were developed by a multidisciplinary panel of Brazilian experts. Systematic literature reviews
> were conducted for each question, based on randomized clinical trials. **Meta-analyses were conducted where
> methodologically feasible.** … **In situations when there was no consensus, votes were taken and the results
> were recorded.**

**The seven recommendations, as published:**

| Recommendation | Strength · certainty |
| --- | --- |
| For PAH, **initial combination therapy** rather than monotherapy | **Strong · high** |
| For PAH on combination therapy at intermediate risk, **replace sildenafil/tadalafil with riociguat** | Conditional · **very low** |
| For PH due to left heart disease, **do not use sildenafil** | Conditional · **very low** |
| For PH due to left heart disease, **evidence insufficient to recommend for or against riociguat** | Conditional · moderate |
| For PH due to left heart disease, **do not use bosentan** | Conditional · low |
| For PH due to left heart disease, **recommend against macitentan** | **Strong · moderate** |
| For interstitial lung disease with PH, **inhaled treprostinil** alongside management of the underlying disease | Conditional · moderate |

**Interpretation.** **Two things in this table are worth more than the table.**

**The first is what the guideline says about its own strongest recommendation.** "Initial combination therapy"
is graded strong on high-quality evidence, and the recommendation text itself carries the qualifier **"based
on the study that evaluated the combination of ambrisentan and tadalafil"** — singular. The discussion is
explicit about what happens next: **"Although the evidence for dual therapy is fundamentally based on one
study that used ambrisentan and tadalafil, the proposal of this treatment guideline expands the concept of
combined treatment to other drugs in the PDE5i and ERA classes available in the country."** **So the strongest
recommendation in the document is extended beyond the trial that supports it, by drug class, and the stated
reason is national availability.** That is a defensible piece of guideline reasoning and an unusually honest
one — most documents make the class leap silently. **It is also the clearest instance this archive has seen of
a recommendation's scope being set by what a health system stocks rather than by what was tested**, which is
the whole point of a national guideline and worth separating from the evidence grade when quoting it.

**The second is the shape of the rest.** **Four of the seven recommendations concern pulmonary hypertension
due to left heart disease — by far the commonest form — and all four are negative or null:** do not use
sildenafil, do not use bosentan, recommend against macitentan, and insufficient evidence either way for
riociguat. **For the most prevalent group of patients, this guideline's content is a list of pulmonary
vasodilators not to give.** That is a real clinical message rather than a gap, and it is the opposite of how
guidelines are usually read. Note also that the single **strong** recommendation in that group is a
**recommendation against** — on moderate certainty, which is a stronger evidential footing than three of the
four conditional statements beside it.

**For the archive's consensus-threshold catalogue, a new kind of entry: no threshold at all.** This panel
recorded that "in situations when there was no consensus, votes were taken and the results were recorded" —
**a procedure, with no agreement percentage specified anywhere.** Against the Korean 70%, the Brain Trauma
Foundation's blinded 80%, BOOTStraP's 70% at subgroup and 90% at plenary, and the STS modified Delphi above
75%, **this one declines to state a number.** The catalogue's existing rule was that thresholds are not
comparable across documents; the stronger version is that **a stated threshold is itself a choice some panels
do not make**, so its absence is not evidence of a lower bar.

**Finally, a cataloguing trap this document illustrates exactly.** It is deposited **twice**: a `MED` record
with the DOI, the full abstract and the `Practice Guideline` type, dated **13 August**; and a PubMed Central
record with the full text, no DOI, no abstract and the type `research-article`, dated **13 September** — same
journal, same volume, same issue. **The second date is the deposit, not the publication**, and a sweep keyed
on first-publication date sees the same guideline twice, a month apart. **The authoritative date is the `MED`
record's electronic publication date, 13 August**, which is what this archive logged on 11 October and what
stands today. **The rule: when a guideline appears twice with different dates, the record carrying the DOI and
the pubType is the publication; the richer full text may be the later copy.**

## 3. China's intravenous-to-oral switch guideline — stewardship written as a procedure

**Society · Published:** **National Pharmaceutical Affairs Management Medical Quality Control Center** with
the **Clinical Pharmacy Branch of the Chinese Medical Association**, *Zhonghua Yi Xue Za Zhi*,
**1 September 2026** — **forty-two days old**, 0 citations, typed `Practice Guideline`. Chinese with an
English abstract; **the recommendations themselves are in Chinese and only the abstract is quoted here.**

> **Intravenous to oral switch (IVOS) is the core strategy to optimize the route of administration and reduce
> unnecessary intravenous infusion.** Although the drug administration switching mode has been gradually
> implemented in China, **there is still a lack of unified and implementable standardized guidance in key
> links such as patient evaluation and switching opportunity.** … This guideline focuses on the core contents
> of **IVOS screening for adult inpatients, switching timing, evaluation criteria, operational procedures** …
> Finally **18 questions and 24 recommendations** were formed … aiming at providing scientific and practical
> decision-making basis for clinical rational intravenous drug use, improving medical quality, ensuring the
> safety of patients' medication, and **optimizing the allocation of medical resources.**

**Interpretation.** **This is the sixth Chinese document in the archive and the second on antimicrobial
practice, and it is written in a register the other guidelines here are not.** The content list is
**screening, timing, evaluation criteria, operational procedures** — the vocabulary of a protocol, not of a
recommendation set. Twenty-four recommendations against eighteen questions is a ratio close to one, which is
what a document built around decision points rather than around evidence questions looks like.

**The clinically substantial claim is in the diagnosis rather than the recommendations: that switching is
already happening in China and the gap is standardisation, specifically "patient evaluation and switching
opportunity."** That is a different problem from the one Western stewardship guidance usually addresses, which
is persuading clinicians to switch at all. **A guideline whose stated problem is inconsistency rather than
reluctance implies a system where the behaviour is established and the variance is the harm** — and it
explains why the document reads as a procedure.

**The honest limitation: with the recommendations in Chinese, this archive cannot say what the switching
criteria are** — which clinical stability markers, which antibiotic classes excluded, what time window. **Those
are the details that make an IVOS protocol either safe or a readmission generator**, and they are precisely
what is not readable here. It is logged with that gap stated rather than paraphrased past.
