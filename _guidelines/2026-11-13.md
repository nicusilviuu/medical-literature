## 1. Nine societies on ECPR, and an abstract that concedes the evidence is not there

**[Extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR): Consensus statement of the DGK, DGTHG, DGAI, DGPTM,
DIVI, GRC, DRG, ALKK and DGIIN](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00101-026-01770-3)** · *Die Anaesthesiologie* and
*Medizinische Klinik — Intensivmedizin und Notfallmedizin* · **both published 12 November 2026** · paywalled

**Society · Published:** nine German societies — cardiology (DGK), cardiothoracic surgery (DGTHG),
anaesthesiology (DGAI), prehospital and transport medicine (DGPTM), intensive care and emergency medicine
(DIVI, DGIIN), the German Resuscitation Council (GRC), radiology (DRG) and the ALKK.
**This updates the document published in 2018.**

> Extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) can be considered as a rescue strategy in highly
> selected patients with refractory cardiac arrest and a potentially reversible cause of cardiac arrest.
> Both ECPR itself and the subsequent treatment under extracorporeal life support (ECLS) are complex and
> resource intensive. **There is currently no robust evidence for the superiority of ECPR over conventional
> cardiopulmonary resuscitation.** The implementation and maintenance of an effective ECPR program requires
> clearly structured processes, defined communication channels and robust institutional infrastructure for
> quality assurance. … This consensus paper updates the document published in 2018 and defines a common
> framework of central structural, procedural and clinical requirements.

**Interpretation.** **A consensus statement whose own abstract says there is no robust evidence that the
intervention it governs is superior to the alternative is doing something unusual and correct.** The
document does not claim ECPR works; it claims that **if** a centre is going to do ECPR, these are the
structural, procedural and clinical requirements. **That is guidance about programme quality rather than
about patient selection, and the distinction is the whole point.** Nine societies across cardiology,
cardiothoracic surgery, anaesthesiology, intensive care, radiology and prehospital medicine is also the
right breadth for a decision that is made at an institutional level and executed by a team nobody society
owns.

**It belongs beside two documents this archive holds.** **The 2026 ILCOR consensus deposited 989 characters
on resuscitation science with no recommendation, no certainty rating and no knowledge gap — the withholding
branch of the format-decision rule.** **This one deposits 917 characters and spends them saying what the
evidence does not show.** **Same length of deposit, opposite use of it.** And the archive's own ECPR
arithmetic from an earlier brief — 29.0% survival to discharge, of whom 67.2% alive at five years, so 19.5%
unconditional — is the kind of number a programme-quality framework is built to protect.

**The method finding, and it is the cleanest instance yet.** **Both co-publications deposit abstracts of
exactly 917 characters.** Equal lengths across two editions or two journals is the red flag this archive
adopted on 22 October, and **today it fired, the hash was computed, and the hash confirmed it:
`92ab97ca10ea9f96` for both.** **Byte-identical — the second such pair after the 2026 AHA/ACC
co-publications, and the exact opposite of yesterday's SCCM/ESICM pair, which deposited 2,283 and 2,275
characters and hashed differently.** **The flag-then-hash sequence has now completed inside a single run for
the first time.**

[Read the consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00101-026-01770-3) · the co-publication is
[`10.1007/s00063-026-01519-1`](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00063-026-01519-1)

## 2. The in-development sweep, eleven days overdue — and what ESAIC's own list revealed

needs no index access and should therefore wait for a frozen-index day.** Yesterday's note said to stop
waiting for a stall. **Four of the seven societies were checked today. Three are reported here; ESC, EACTS,

joint SCCM/ELSO adult ECMO consensus and an early-versus-late tracheostomy document, **the two largest

### ESPEN — a guidelines page last updated in September 2023

**ESPEN's guidelines page shows no in-development section at all, lists its most recent publications as six
documents from 2023, and carries a page-updated date of 8 September 2023.** **That is over three years

the kind of document whose content matters — dose tables and monitoring thresholds are where errata
live — and this archive cannot read it.** **Recorded as a named gap with the route already attempted.**

## 3. An American College of Cardiology document about where guidelines stop

**[Practical Management of Multivessel Coronary Artery Disease: Evidence, Controversies, and the Boundaries
of Guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103428)** · *JACC: Advances* · **published
11 November 2026** · paywalled

**Society · Published:** a state-of-the-art review from the **American College of Cardiology Interventional
Section Leadership Council and Cardiac Surgery Leadership Council**, written from a Heart Team perspective.

> Selecting the optimal revascularization strategy requires multidisciplinary Heart Team discussion … Clinical
> guidelines provide a valuable framework for standardized, evidence-based care with reduced practice
> variability. **However, many landmark trials enrolled highly selected populations and were conducted before
> the widespread adoption of contemporary revascularization techniques, including physiologic lesion
> assessment, intracoronary imaging, and modern arterial grafting strategies.** … summarizes contemporary
> evidence, reviews areas of ongoing controversy, and provides practical guidance for managing patients with
> multivessel coronary artery disease **in clinical situations that extend beyond the scope of current
> guideline recommendations.**

**Interpretation.** **It is routed here rather than to the literature brief because its subject is what the
guidelines say and where they run out** — the 30 October rule, that a document about guidance belongs to the
watch whatever journal it appears in.

**The argument is one this archive should take seriously because it is the honest version of a common
complaint.** **The landmark revascularisation trials predate physiologic lesion assessment, intracoronary
imaging and modern arterial grafting** — so a guideline faithful to those trials is faithful to a practice
that has moved. **That is a real problem and it is also the standard excuse for ignoring guidelines**, which
is why the document's framing matters: it does not claim the guidelines are wrong, it claims there are
situations outside them, and it says what to do there.

**Two notes for a cardiothoracic reader.** **It is jointly authored by the interventional and the surgical
leadership councils**, which for a multivessel disease document is the only defensible arrangement and is
not always what happens. **And "modern arterial grafting strategies" is doing quiet work** — if the surgical
arm of the trials was single-arterial-graft surgery, then the surgical option in a 2026 Heart Team
discussion is not the one that was randomised.

[Read the review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103428)

## 4. A German update that lists what changed

**[Clinical recommendations for the inpatient management of lower respiratory tract infections in children
and adolescents with severe neurological impairment in Germany — 2026
Update](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-026-07482-5)** · *European Journal of Pediatrics* · **published
9 November 2026** · **open access** (PMC13748045) · indexed as a Practice Guideline

**Society · Published:** German expert consensus, **updating the same group's 2024 recommendations.**

> **What is New:** • Compared with the 2024 recommendations, this update provides more detailed guidance on
> secretion management and respiratory and cough support, introduces explicit discharge criteria, and expands
> decision-making for patients colonized with multidrug-resistant organisms. The antibiotic strategy has been
> further specified regarding broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy, while preventive recommendations have been
> expanded and updated.

On the substance: a stepwise risk-stratified approach, **an aminopenicillin with a beta-lactamase inhibitor
for community-acquired infection and piperacillin-tazobactam reserved for selected high-risk patients**,
with supportive care — hydration, antipyretics, oxygen, secretion management, cough support — before
antibiotics in suspected viral infection.

**Interpretation.** **The "What is New" block is the thing to praise and the thing this watch most often has
to reconstruct for itself.** Most updated guidelines announce themselves as updates and leave the reader to
diff two documents; this one names five changes in five clauses. **An update that states its own delta costs
the authors a paragraph and saves every reader the comparison.**

**Why it is in scope:** these children are admitted with respiratory insufficiency, receive respiratory and
cough support, and are a recurring intensive care population — and **the document's central move,
antimicrobial stewardship through risk stratification rather than through a fixed regimen, is the same
argument the sepsis literature has been making all week** from the opposite end of the severity scale.
**Two cautions:** it is explicitly national — "in Germany" — and resistance patterns and drug availability
do not travel; and it is **expert consensus with newly available data**, with no grading scheme named in the
abstract.

[Read the recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-026-07482-5) — open access.

## 5. Positioning on the operating table, where the responsibility is shared

**[Patient Positioning During Trauma and Orthopedic Surgeries: Risks, responsibilities and practical
recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00064-026-00962-9)** · *Operative Orthopädie und Traumatologie* ·
**published 11 November 2026** · paywalled; English abstract available

> Position-related complications range from skin and soft tissue pressure injuries and musculoskeletal damage
> to peripheral nerve injuries **up to rare but severe events such as postoperative visual loss. Prolonged
> operative time, obesity, diabetes mellitus and extreme joint positions are major risk factors. In Germany,
> patient positioning is a joint medical responsibility (surgeon, anesthetist) but primarily assigned to the
> surgeon with shared responsibility involving anesthesia and operating room personnel. A check by the
> surgeon and anesthesia at the beginning of the operation is necessary.** Standardized checklists, adequate
> padding, limitation of extreme joint positions and regular intraoperative reassessment are key preventive
> measures. **As positioning-related injuries are considered largely preventable, thorough documentation is
> of major medicolegal importance.**

**Interpretation.** **This is in scope because of the sentence about responsibility, not because of the
surgery.** Positioning injury is one of the few perioperative harms whose liability is explicitly shared
between surgeon and anaesthetist, and **the document states the German allocation precisely: a joint medical
responsibility, primarily the surgeon's, with a mandatory joint check at the start.** **An anaesthetist who
does not know their jurisdiction's allocation is exposed by a harm they are named in**, and that allocation
is national, so the German statement is a model to look up locally rather than a rule to adopt.

**The clinical content is conventional and worth having in one place:** the risk factors are time, obesity,
diabetes and extreme joint positions; the measures are checklists, padding, limits on extremes and
reassessment during the case. **Postoperative visual loss is named as the rare severe end**, which is the
complication that anaesthetists are asked about and that no single specialty owns.

**One limit, stated plainly: this is a review offering practical recommendations, not a graded guideline, and
no society is named as its promulgator.** **"Evidence-based recommendations" in its own closing sentence is
the authors' characterisation, and the abstract names no grading scheme.** **Reported for the responsibility
statement and the checklist, not as society guidance.**

## 6. A consensus threshold that is a mean score, not a proportion of panellists

**[Clinical Consensus Guidelines on Antibiotic Prescribing Practices for Infants with Gastroschisis Utilizing
Modified Delphi Methodology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpedsurg.2026.163573)** · *Journal of Pediatric
Surgery* · **published 9 November 2026** · paywalled

**Society · Published:** the **Children's Hospitals Neonatal Consortium Gastroschisis Focus Group.** Ten
recommendations drafted, scored by survey over two rounds.

> Each recommendation was sent to focus group members via REDCap survey tool and members scored responses on
> a 5-point Likert scale. … In the first survey, there were 23 respondents and **7/10 recommendations received
> a mean score >80%, meeting the criteria for consensus.** Following review of results and comments, the
> remaining statements were edited and sent out for the second round survey where all achieved consensus.

**For the Delphi threshold catalogue, this is a new kind and it is not comparable to any of the six already
held.** **Every previous threshold in this archive counts PANELLISTS: at least 70% agreeing, or 75%, or 90%
strong and 80% moderate on a nine-point scale, or the two-sided rules that also count disagreement.
This one averages SCORES — "a mean score >80%" on a five-point Likert.** **A mean can clear 80% with a
polarised panel in a way that a 75%-of-panellists rule cannot**, because two enthusiasts offset a
dissenter. **So of seven threshold schemes catalogued here, this is the only one whose criterion is
insensitive to how the panel is distributed** — the opposite property to the acute brain injury core outcome
set, whose two-sided rule was adopted precisely to fail outcomes that polarise a panel.

**On the substance.** **The clinical problem is real and well stated: gastroschisis is the commonest
congenital abdominal wall defect, antibiotics are started early and continued long, and the abstract cites
evidence that overuse delays feeding and is associated with bloodstream infection.** The recommendations
cover screening by blood culture at birth, risk stratification, spectrum, length of therapy and timing
around abdominal wall closure. **Three of ten statements failed in round one and passed after editing**,
which the paper reports openly — **and a statement that passes after being rewritten is not the statement
that failed, so the final "all achieved consensus" covers two different texts.**

**Twenty-three respondents, one clinical entity, one consortium.** **Reported as a stewardship framework
where many centres have none, which is what the authors claim for it.**

## 7. A new transfusion guideline whose whole lineage is abstract-free

**[British Society for Haematology guideline on transfusion for fetuses, neonates and older
children](https://doi.org/10.1111/tme.70117)** · *Transfusion Medicine* · **published 12 November 2026** ·
paywalled, **no abstract deposited**

**The title-stem route recovered the lineage and not one word of content.** Three editions:

| Edition | Date | Journal | DOI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original guideline | **11 November 2016** | *Br J Haematol* | `10.1111/bjh.14233` |
| **Addendum August 2020** | 18 November 2020 | *Br J Haematol* | `10.1111/bjh.17109` |
| **This edition** | **12 November 2026** | ***Transfusion Medicine*** | `10.1111/tme.70117` |

**All three deposit zero characters.** **So a British Society for Haematology guideline covering transfusion
in fetuses, neonates and children — a document that governs practice in paediatric cardiac surgery and
neonatal intensive care — has no readable abstract in this index across ten years and three editions.**

**What the route did recover, from titles and metadata alone, is worth having.** **The 2020 record's own
title carries the citation of what it amends — "Br J Haematol. 2016;175:784-828" — and declares itself an
Addendum, so the 2016 document was a full guideline of 45 pages and the 2020 intervention was partial.**
**And the new edition has moved journal, from the *British Journal of Haematology* to *Transfusion
Medicine*, and changed its title from the plural "Guidelines" to the singular "guideline".** **A reader
searching the journal where the previous two editions appeared will not find this one.**

**Interpretation.** **Reported as an identified new edition with its content unread, and that is the honest
limit of what today's routes could establish.** **This archive's rule holds: say plainly that it is
paywalled and abstract-free rather than paraphrasing a guideline from its title.** **Named as a gap, with
both routes already attempted** — the index has no abstract and the title-stem search found only siblings
that have none either.
