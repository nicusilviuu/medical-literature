## 1. The classifier's first operational run, and what 95.5% was a measurement of

**Yesterday's entry recorded that `claude/tools/classify_guidance.py` reached 95.5% agreement after two
rounds of fixes. Today it ran on fresh data for the first time and got five of sixteen records wrong.**

**That gap is not a new defect. It is the consequence of how the 95.5% was obtained.** Yesterday's four bugs
were found by inspecting all nineteen disagreements on the 15 November sweep, the fixes were written against
those nineteen cases, and the agreement was then re-measured **on that same set**. Fitting to a set and
scoring on it is an in-sample measurement, and an in-sample measurement is not an estimate of performance on
anything else. **The number was real and it was never the quantity a reader would take it for. It should
have been published as "agreement on the set the fixes were written against", and it was not.**

**This is the eighth member of the over-generalisation family in this archive and the second about a tool
rather than about the literature**, after the `core` paging misdiagnosis withdrawn as correction 13.

### The five failures, each with its cause verified rather than guessed

| Record | Verdict | Should be | Cause, confirmed by running the regexes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `10.1016/j.bja.2026.10.006` | PRIMARY | apparatus | `SELF_GUIDANCE` matched the bare word **`standard`**, from "standard preoperative fasting guidelines" |
| `10.1002/ohn.70510` | PRIMARY | about guidance | `SELF_GUIDANCE` matched **`Guidelines`** in "Impact of Non-Opioid Analgesia Guidelines" |
| `10.1186/s12885-026-16754-0` | PRIMARY | about guidance | `SELF_GUIDANCE` matched **`guideline`** in "guideline-defined indications" |
| `10.1016/s2213-2600(26)00296-1` | SECONDARY | PRIMARY | `ABOUT` matched **`insights from`**, in the document's own subtitle "insights from an international Delphi expert panel" |
| `10.1186/s13104-026-07954-9` | OTHER | PRIMARY | nothing matched: "a Delphi method consensus study" is not a `SELF_GUIDANCE` phrase |

**Three of the five are one fault.** `standard`, `Guidelines` and `guideline` are the bare-word rules, and in
all three cases the word is present *because the document discusses guidance*, not because it is guidance.
**Yesterday's entry recorded that the bare-`statement` rule had produced one false positive and called that
"not clean". It is not one false positive, it is the classifier's principal failure mode**, and the
seventh-stage design is what permits it: a title word is treated as sufficient evidence of primary guidance
with nothing able to outweigh it.

**The fourth failure is the most instructive, because the term that caused it was added deliberately.**
`insights from` entered `ABOUT` to catch "key takeaways and insights from the 2026 guideline" summary
articles. The PrECiSIOn consensus — already in this archive's threshold catalogue at 75% on a seven-point
scale with 44 panellists — describes its own provenance as "insights from an international Delphi expert
panel". **A term that keys on how a document describes where it came from cannot distinguish insights from a
guideline, which is secondary, from insights from a panel, which is primary.**

**The fifth is a plain omission:** the `SELF_GUIDANCE` list recognises "consensus statement", "consensus
document" and "clinical consensus", and does not recognise "Delphi method consensus study".

### A tool-level error in the run itself, and the one thing it establishes

**The classifier is designed to read title and pubType. Today's sweeps were collected with
`resultType=lite`, which carries no `pubTypeList`, so the first run classified sixteen records with the
pubType field empty on every one of them.** That is the 16 November rule repeating for the fourth time in
three weeks: a test cannot be run on a result type that does not carry the field being tested. It was found
while diagnosing the misclassifications, not before the run.

**The records were re-collected with `resultType=core` and the classifier re-run. Not one verdict changed.**
The reason is worth more than the re-run: **not a single one of the sixteen in-scope records carries a
guidance pubType at all** — the types present are Review, Journal Article, Letter, Preprint,
research-article and English Abstract. **Stage 3 of the classifier's decision order, the pubType stage whose
ordering was yesterday's hardest fix, was dead on this window either way.**

### What is being changed today, and what is deliberately not

**Not the regexes.** Four of the five failures have obvious local fixes, and writing them today and
re-scoring on today's sixteen records would repeat the exact error this section is about. **The four regex
fixes are deferred to the November month audit at the start of December, where a held-out set exists and the
score can be an out-of-sample one.**

**One change is made, because it is justified without reference to today's examples:** `pubType` is read
only as evidence *for* guidance and never against, so a record typed `Letter` cannot be demoted by its own
type. That is a design asymmetry, not a tuning parameter. **It is recorded as unscored.**

**Its effect on today's sixteen records, stated for the record rather than as a score:** the counts move
from 9 primary, 4 secondary, 4 other to **7 primary, 5 secondary, 4 other**, and exactly one record changed
verdict — the BJA letter, from primary to secondary. **Four of the five failures remain, by design, until
December.**

## 3. ESPEN rewrites its nutrition ethics guideline, and the method changed more than the content

**[ESPEN practical guideline on ethical aspects of medical nutrition
therapy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106622)** · *Clinical Nutrition* · **published 14 March 2026**,
pubType Practice Guideline

> "The provision of medical nutrition therapy (MNT) is a medical intervention that requires an indication, a
> therapeutic goal, and the consent of the patient... This practical, solely consensus-based guideline is
> based on and updates the 2016 ESPEN Guideline on Ethical Aspects of Artificial Nutrition and Hydration,
> which has been shortened, supplemented with flowcharts, and expanded to include 3 additional ethical
> statements in palliative care and 1 new statement concerning human rights in clinical nutrition. Two
> Delphi rounds and a consensus conference were held to reach consensus... This guideline places particular
> emphasis on end-of-life issues, palliative medicine, dementia, and specific scenarios such as nursing care
> and intensive care."

**Why it is here.** ESPEN is a tracked society and this document was never reported. **The in-development
list on the ESPEN site was recorded on 15 November as three years stale; this guideline, published in March,
is the evidence of what that staleness costs** — the society publishes without the list moving, so the list
cannot be used to anticipate anything.

**The full lineage is readable, which is unusual.** The title-stem route returns the 2016 predecessor
(`10.1016/j.clnu.2016.02.006`, 1,983 characters) alongside the 2026 edition (2,022 characters), and the 2016
abstract names *its* own parent: the 2013 **DGEM** guideline on ethical and legal aspects of artificial
nutrition. **A three-generation lineage — DGEM 2013, ESPEN 2016, ESPEN 2026 — with both ESPEN editions
carrying abstracts, so a changed-versus-prior reading is possible rather than inferred.** That is the fifth
demonstrated use of the title-stem route and the first in which both editions had content; the BSH
transfusion lineage found on 13 November had none.

**Interpretation — the method is the change.** The 2016 edition was "discussed at the ESPEN Congress in
Lisbon 2015 and accepted in an online survey among ESPEN members". The 2026 edition used **two Delphi rounds
and a consensus conference**. A membership survey and a structured Delphi are different instruments, and the
move from one to the other is a real upgrade in how the statements were agreed.

**But the 2026 abstract describes the guideline as "solely consensus-based", and that phrase deserves
weight: there is no evidence grading behind these statements, only agreement.** For an ethics guideline that
is defensible — the questions are not empirical — and it should still be read as what it is.

**The eleventh threshold scheme in this catalogue, and the first that states no threshold.** Two Delphi
rounds are named; **no agreement threshold and no panel size are given anywhere in the abstract.** By the
15 November rule the panel size is recorded with every threshold, and here there is neither to record. Of
the eleven schemes, this is the only one where the abstract does not permit the reader to know what
"consensus" meant.

**Terminology.** "Artificial nutrition and hydration" in 2016 has become "medical nutrition therapy" in
2026 — the same shift in naming that this archive has seen elsewhere, and one that matters in the
intensive-care setting where the 2016 edition's withholding and withdrawing discussion applies.

**What cannot be read from the abstract.** The 2016 edition devoted a chapter to cultural and religious
issues and covered voluntary refusal of nutrition and fluids and the force-feeding of hunger strikers.
**The 2026 abstract mentions neither. Whether they were dropped, shortened or simply not summarised cannot
be determined from the abstract, and is not inferred here.**

## 4. Nine societies on the mechanical complications of myocardial infarction

**[Ventricular free-wall rupture, ventricular pseudoaneurysm, and papillary muscle rupture complicating
acute myocardial infarction](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejcts/ezag157)** · a clinical consensus statement ·
*European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery* 68(5) · **published 11 May 2026**, co-published in the
*European Heart Journal*

**Authoring bodies: the ESC Working Group on Cardiovascular Surgery, the Association for Acute
CardioVascular Care, the European Association of Percutaneous Cardiovascular Interventions, the ESC Working
Group on Pulmonary Circulation and Right Ventricular Function, the European Association of Preventive
Cardiology, the Heart Failure Association, the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging, and the
European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery.**

> "The prevalence of mechanical complications following acute myocardial infarction has steadily declined in
> recent years owing to advances in prompt coronary revascularization, and they now occur in <1% of acute
> myocardial infarction cases. Nevertheless, significant haemodynamic impairment may already be present at
> hospital admission... Advances in transcatheter percutaneous procedures have now introduced alternative
> treatment strategies, especially for high-risk or inoperable patients. Recurrence of post-acute myocardial
> infarction mechanical complications, even shortly after the repair of the underlying lesion, has a
> critical impact on patient outcome... The role of concomitant coronary revascularization remains
> controversial, with variable effects on both early and late outcomes, and warrants further investigation.
> Temporary mechanical circulatory support has shown encouraging results, either for pre-procedural
> haemodynamic stabilization ('bridge-to-procedure') or for prophylactic, extended peri-procedural support
> to facilitate myocardial recovery ('bridge-to-recovery')... Optimal management should be guided by a
> multidisciplinary Heart Team approach (including Shock Team involvement where appropriate) with
> integration of palliative care into the decision-making process."

**Interpretation.** The document is written around a problem its own first sentence creates: **these
complications now occur in under 1% of infarctions, which means no centre accumulates experience and no
trial will be powered.** A consensus statement is the only instrument available, and the statement says so
by what it declines to settle — concomitant revascularisation is left "controversial" and "warrants further
investigation" rather than recommended either way.

**What it does commit to is a sequencing of support rather than a choice of operation.** Temporary
mechanical circulatory support is given two distinct indications with their own names, bridge-to-procedure
and bridge-to-recovery, which separates stabilising a patient before repair from protecting the myocardium
around it. **That is a more useful distinction than a device recommendation would have been**, because the
device question is where the evidence is thinnest.

**Three things in it are unusual for a surgical consensus document.** It names **recurrence after repair**
as a monitoring obligation during admission *and after discharge*, which is a follow-up recommendation in a
document about an operation. It puts the **Shock Team** alongside the Heart Team, acknowledging that the
haemodynamic problem may outrank the anatomical one. And it writes **palliative care into the
decision-making process** rather than appending it — in a condition where in-hospital mortality is described
as non-negligible even after successful repair, that is the honest placement.

**The reason it is reported today rather than in May** is section 2 above: neither of its two deposits is
typed as guidance and neither title carries a guidance word, so no index sweep this archive runs could have
surfaced it. **It joins the pre-coverage society backlog as its forty-ninth counted document, and it is the
first in that backlog identified by a journal's published list rather than a society's own page.**

## 5. A validation that separates the guideline from the era — the test the adenotonsillectomy paper did not run

**[Multimodal Approach for Early Poor and Good Neurological Outcome After Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest:
Real-World Validation of the 2025 Post-Resuscitation Care ERC-ESICM
Guidelines](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202611.1107.v1)** · **preprint, posted 11 November 2026, not
peer reviewed** · Careggi University Hospital, Florence

> "We compared the prognostic-therapeutic management and neurological outcomes of all comatose adult
> patients (aged >=18 years) admitted to the Emergency Department following OHCA between 1 June 2018 and
> 31 May 2020 with those admitted between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2025... A total of 121 and 122
> patients were included... Using the 2014-ERC/ESICM guidelines, 59/121 patients (48.7%) were correctly
> identified. In the 2024-2025 cohort, the 2025-ERC/ESICM guidelines correctly identified 106/122 patients
> (86.8%) (46/62 with poor and 60/60 with favourable outcomes)... Applying the 2025 guidelines to the
> 2018-2020 cohort increased the number of patients with correctly predicted outcomes to 93/121 (76.8%)."

**Interpretation — the third number is the whole paper.** A before-and-after comparison of two cohorts four
years apart gives 48.7% against 86.8%, a 38.1-point gap, and nothing in that comparison distinguishes the
new guideline from four years of changed practice. **The authors then applied the 2025 criteria to the old
patients, and that single analysis splits the gap: 76.8% minus 48.7% is 28.1 points attributable to the
criteria themselves on identical patients, and 86.8% minus 76.8% is 10.0 points attributable to the cohort
and the era.** Roughly three-quarters of the improvement is the guideline and a quarter is everything else.

**Two days ago this archive criticised a 133,688-patient propensity-matched study for titling itself "Impact
of Guidelines" across eight years containing a pandemic, with no way to separate the guideline from the
era.** This 243-patient preprint does the thing that study could not, with an analysis that costs nothing
once the old cohort's data exist. **Credit where the smaller study has the better design: applying the new
rule retrospectively to the old patients is the within-cohort comparison, and it is available to anyone with
two cohorts.** The arithmetic checks: 46 + 60 = 106, and 106/122, 59/121 and 93/121 all reproduce the stated
percentages.

**The asymmetry in the headline figure matters more than the figure.** 60 of 60 favourable outcomes were
correctly identified and 46 of 62 poor ones — **perfect on the good-outcome side, missing 16 of 62 on the
poor side.** A prognostication guideline is designed to be conservative in exactly that direction, so this
is the instrument behaving as intended rather than a defect, and it means the 86.8% is carried by the easier
half.

**And the abstract names the thing that makes all of this circular without naming the circularity.** Its
closing sentence is that accurate prognostication matters most "in patients with poor outcomes, who were
more frequently managed with withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment". **In a cohort where a poor-outcome
prediction leads to withdrawal, and withdrawal produces death, the poor outcome the guideline predicted is
partly produced by the prediction.** That is the self-fulfilling prophecy problem that post-arrest
prognostication research has recognised for two decades, and a validation study cannot measure accuracy on
the poor-outcome side without confronting it. **The abstract states the mechanism in its last line and does
not state the problem. Whether the full text addresses it cannot be told from the abstract, and this is a
preprint that has not been peer reviewed.**

## 6. A compliance survey that asks for the guideline to be changed rather than followed

**[Regional anaesthesia techniques for cataract surgery in India: Practice patterns and compliance with
guidelines - A survey study](https://doi.org/10.4103/ija.ija_470_26)** · *Indian Journal of Anaesthesia* ·
published 22 October 2026, **first indexed today** · 597 respondents

> "Peribulbar anaesthesia (34.2%) was the preferred choice, followed by topical anaesthesia (32.8%). Around
> 45% used injection adrenaline as an adjuvant, 20% used injection hyaluronidase >15 IU/mL, and 17% were
> unaware of the dosages administered. Pre-operative continuation of antiplatelets and anticoagulants was
> reported by 16.1% and 13.6% of respondents, respectively... pre-operative fasting was advised for sedation
> by 31%, and pre-operative allergic skin test to hyaluronidase was performed by 22.8% respondents.
> Electrocardiogram and non-invasive blood pressure monitoring were used by 26% and 44% respondents,
> respectively."

**Interpretation.** Most adherence audits end by asking for better adherence. **This one ends by asking for
the guideline to change:** the authors' own conclusion is that continuing antiplatelets, limiting
hyaluronidase and minimising adrenaline "should be incorporated in guidelines for the Indian population".
**That is the rarer and more interesting direction — a survey used as evidence that the standard does not
fit the setting, rather than that the setting does not meet the standard.**

**Two numbers are findings regardless of which direction one reads them in.** **17% of respondents did not
know the doses they were administering** — that is not a guideline-compliance figure, it is a drug-safety
figure, and it sits alongside 45% using adrenaline as an adjuvant. And **pulse oximetry is not reported at
all**, which is why the authors ask for it to be made mandatory; with ECG at 26% and non-invasive blood
pressure at 44%, monitoring is the weakest domain in the survey.

**The bound on all of it is the sampling.** An electronically distributed questionnaire with 597 responses
and no denominator has no response rate, and half the respondents are private practitioners.
**A self-selected survey measures what the people who answered do, and the direction of that bias is
unknown**; the practice pattern it reports is not a national estimate, and the paper's conclusions about
what Indian guidelines should say rest on it.

## 7. A twelve-expert Delphi with no threshold

**[Development of a comprehensive peri-anaesthetic bundle of postoperative delirium interventions for
thoracic surgery patients: a Delphi method consensus study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-026-07954-9)** ·
*BMC Research Notes* · published 13 August 2026, first indexed 11 November

> "A multidisciplinary team of experts was convened, and the Delphi method was used to develop bundle
> strategies... A panel of 12 experts was convened. After two rounds of consultation, an initial version of
> the peri-anaesthetic bundle was developed. The main content comprised four programmes: perioperative
> neurocognitive function assessment and cognitive function exercises, perioperative pain management with
> multimodal analgesia, intraoperative anaesthesia management, and post-anaesthesia recovery management.
> Conclusion... Clinical trials should be conducted to confirm its effectiveness."

**Interpretation.** **Twelve panellists is the smallest panel in this archive's threshold catalogue, and one
rater on a panel of twelve is 8.3 percentage points.** By the 15 November rule — that the panel size is
recorded with every threshold because one rater on 35 is 2.86 points — a twelve-person panel cannot support
a threshold stated to the nearest percent at all. **And no threshold is stated.** What the abstract gives is
"two rounds of consultation" and an output; what consensus meant is absent, as it is for the ESPEN guideline
above. **Two of the day's five documents are consensus products whose abstracts do not say what agreement
was required.**

**Credit for the conclusion.** The authors call the product "an initial version", say trials are needed to
confirm effectiveness, and do not claim the bundle prevents delirium. **A Delphi establishes what twelve
experts agree on and nothing about whether the bundle works**, and this abstract stays inside that limit —
unusually, for a document that could easily have been written as a recommendation.

**The content is conventional and that is the point.** Neurocognitive assessment, multimodal analgesia,
intraoperative management, recovery management: four domains that any delirium-prevention bundle in the
literature would contain. **The value of this document is not the bundle, it is that delirium bundles are
being assembled for a specific operation** — and delirium as an endpoint has now appeared in this archive
in six of the last eight days.

## 8. A verification suite written into the repository, calibrated against the corpus, and what it found

**The pre-commit suite this archive runs on every file has never existed as a file.** It was re-typed from
the notes on each run, every run for two months. **By the rule adopted on 17 November — any instrument
needed on a dated action goes in the repository, not in a scratch directory and not in a memory — it should
have been written down the day that rule was adopted.** It is now `claude/tools/verify_page.py`.

**Writing it down immediately showed that the suite in the notes was not the suite that has been running.**
Run against the published corpus, the reconstruction failed on sixty files that had all passed the real
suite on their own day. Three checks were mis-calibrated and were corrected against the corpus rather than
against memory:

| Check as recorded | What the corpus showed | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| no body line over 110 characters | the rewrapper emits lines up to 111 **visible** characters, and raw length is inflated by HTML tags and link targets | measure visible length, flag at 112 |
| no `**` nested in a blockquote **line** | a bold span legally wraps across lines of a quote | per-block check, not per-line |
| `.***` count must be zero | ten published entries contain `.***` legitimately — a bold span closing at the end of an italic paragraph | **check withdrawn**; checks 3 and 5 cover the real defect |

**A reconstruction that disagrees with two months of passing files is wrong about the files, not the other
way round.** The corpus is the specification.

### And then it found a live defect that had been visible for forty days

**`_briefs/2026-10-09.md` had an odd `**` count — 269 — which no gate had ever reported**, because the
source suite was only ever run on the day's own file and the Pages rendered-asterisk check only ever on the
day's own page. The live page was fetched and **rendered two literal asterisks**, in this sentence:

> ...and today almost none is. \*\*Stated plainly: the deposit rate for current literature has not fallen.

A bold span opened at "So in that window" was never closed, so Kramdown took the next `**` as its closing
delimiter and left the pair at the end of the sentence as text. **Fixed today by closing the first span.**

**NEW RULE: the Pages rendered-asterisk check applies to the whole archive, not only to the day's page.** A
defect that escapes the day it is written is never looked at again, and this one survived forty days and
fifty-odd subsequent runs of the suite on other files.

**One suspicion was raised by the suite and refuted by the live page, and is recorded as refuted.** The
14 November ordered-list-marker check fired on `_briefs/2026-10-14.md`, where a continuation line opens
`2000. **Every other source unchanged...` — the exact pattern that produced four rendered asterisks on
14 November. **The live 14 October page renders zero asterisks.** The check is a risk flag whose consequence
depends on context, not a proof of a defect, and the page was not touched. **Fetching the page before
editing it is the only thing that distinguished the two cases.**
