**No new guidelines from tracked societies appeared in the trailing window, and today that statement is much
stronger than usual.** Run by **index date** rather than publication date — the instrument found yesterday —
the guideline-class sweep over **8–15 October returns zero.** Not zero *published*, which a sweep cannot
establish: **zero entered the database.** The eleven-source identity still closes at **49,018,571** with
residual zero, ingest is **1 record today**, and the reference windows remain where they were rewritten to
yesterday.

## The audit: what the watch caught, what it lost, and how long it took

**The pubType sweep has been a retrospective instrument since 8 October, when this archive demonstrated that
MeSH-assigned guideline types accrue for weeks after publication.** The fix for that has been the targeted
society route plus a periodic lagged re-sweep. **What was never measurable was the watch's actual latency.
With an index-date field it is.**

**Sixteen guideline-class records entered Europe PMC in October — eight on the 1st, six on the 2nd, two on the
3rd, and none since.** That is **twelve consecutive days with no guideline-class document indexed at all**, and
it means something I should state against my own earlier framing: **the zero this watch has reported daily for
a fortnight was a true zero.** On 8 October I wrote that "a quiet result is not evidence that nothing was
published," which remains correct in general — but for this particular fortnight the quiet result was not a
window artefact. Nothing came in.

**Sixteen records are fourteen distinct documents** — serrated polyposis syndrome is deposited twice as a
co-publication with two DOIs, and the Spanish *H. pylori* consensus is deposited twice under **one** DOI, once
in Spanish and once in English. **Of the fourteen, five are in scope.**

| In-scope document | Indexed | Reported here | Lag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AHA/ACC/ACS perioperative cardiovascular management for noncardiac surgery** | 1 Oct | **30 Sep** | **−1 day** |
| ESOT guideline on chronic lung allograft dysfunction | 3 Oct | 9 Oct | 6 days |
| iCARDIO Alliance global heart failure implementation guidelines | 2 Oct | **today** | 13 days |
| VI Spanish consensus on *Helicobacter pylori* | 1 Oct | **today** | 14 days |
| SFGM-TC antibiotics after paediatric stem cell transplantation | 1 Oct | **today** | 14 days |

**The first row is the method note of 8 October being right.** This archive reported the perioperative guideline
on **[30 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) — the day before Europe PMC indexed its JACC
record** — because the targeted society route found the *Circulation* co-publication first, and then spent two entries establishing that the two 2026 records
are byte-identical and differ from the 2024 edition only by the year. **Beating the index by a day on the
largest in-scope guideline of the autumn is exactly what making targeted society searches the primary
instrument was supposed to buy.**

**And the last three rows are the blind spot, measured rather than suspected.** All three were indexed on 1 or
2 October with publication dates of 30 September 2026 and — in one case — **13 October 2027.** The sweeps at
the time were keyed on trailing *publication* date windows. **A document indexed on 1 October whose publication
date is 30 September was already behind the window by the time it existed; a document whose publication date is
a year in the future was never in any window at all.** **Thirteen to fourteen days, for three in-scope
documents, and none of them would have surfaced without the new field.**

**Nine of the fourteen are clearly out of scope** — four gastroenterological, two rehabilitation, one
haematology, one inborn metabolic, one WHO contraception guideline — and one more, a Thai practice guideline on
immunosuppressive therapy for adult kidney transplantation, sits beside this archive's transplant cluster
without falling inside anaesthesiology, critical care, cardiology or infectious disease. **Logged as adjacent
and deliberately not reported, with the reason stated rather than left as an omission.**

## A near-miss of my own, recorded because it is the third time this rule has paid

**I began this audit intending to lead the entry with a miss.** The AHA/ACC perioperative guideline appeared in
the index-date sweep, the `items`-block check came back empty, and the backlog carried it as an expected item
dated 7 November. **For a few minutes the finding was "a major in-scope guideline has been indexed and readable
for two weeks and the watch has not reported it."**

**It was reported — on [30 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) and again on
[2 October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-02/), in more depth than almost anything else in this
archive**, with an abstract-checksum comparison across three records. **The `items`-block check came back empty
because the item block carries the *Circulation* DOI and the sweep returned the *JACC* one.** Two DOIs, one
document, and a dedupe check keyed on the wrong half.

**The rule that caught it is the one written on 8 October after an identical escape: check the archive before
claiming the archive missed something.** It has now fired three times, and this is the first time it prevented
a published accusation rather than a published duplicate. **The operational refinement, which goes in the
notes: for a co-published guideline, dedupe on every DOI the document has, not the one the sweep handed you.**

## 1. Heart failure guidelines that grade their recommendations by how little money is available

**Society · Published:** **iCARDIO Alliance**, *Journal of Cardiac Failure* — **indexed 2 October 2026**,
0 citations, typed `Practice Guideline`. **An update of the Alliance's 2025 original, so a one-year cycle.**

> In these guidelines, we aim to consider **inconsistencies in healthcare access, varying infrastructure,
> resource constraints and diverse local practices as well as practical and political issues that restrict the
> global applicability of currently available guidelines.** … This document is about heart failure, including
> acute and chronic heart failure, **heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and heart failure with
> preserved ejection fraction as well as HF with improved ejection fraction and cardiomyopathies.** …
> **Most importantly these guidelines also provide recommendations on a number of issues where resource
> limitations may put constraints on the care provided to HF patients. Such "economic adjustment"
> recommendations aim to provide guidance for situations when "Resources are somewhat limited" or when
> "Resources are severely limited."**

**Interpretation.** **"Economic adjustment" recommendations are a document class this archive has not seen
before, and they are the generalisation of something it named yesterday.** The Swiss *C. difficile* and
Brazilian pulmonary hypertension documents were national adaptations — international evidence mapped onto one
country's formulary. **This goes further: rather than adapting to one country, it grades the same
recommendation across three resource tiers**, standard, "somewhat limited" and "severely limited". **A
recommendation therefore has two coordinates here — certainty of evidence and affordability of delivery — and
the second is explicit rather than implied.**

**That is a real contribution and it is also where the document is most open to question.** A resource-tiered
recommendation risks codifying a lower standard of care as acceptable, and the honest counter is that
clinicians in those settings are already making the substitution without guidance. **Which way that balance
falls is not answerable from the abstract**, because the abstract names no tier-specific recommendation, no
grading system, no recommendation count and no panel process. **What is readable is the architecture. The
content is behind the publisher.**

**And the deposit is in poor condition — three independent defects in one record, which is a first.** The
abstract is **6,869 characters because the same text is repeated three times** — the opening sentence appears
three times verbatim. It carries **36 hyphenation artefacts** from PDF line breaks ("re-source",
"in-corporation", "frac-tion", "im- proves", "guide-lines", "man-agement"), each appearing three times in the
triplication, so **twelve in the underlying text.** And the first-publication date is **13 October 2027** — a
year in the future. **Note that the day and month match the audit's upper boundary: 13 October. The most
economical explanation is a year typed wrong by one in a date that was otherwise correct**, which is a guess
and is labelled as one. **The practical consequence is not cosmetic: a document dated a year ahead is invisible
to every trailing publication-date sweep, permanently.** It is the clearest single argument for the instrument
change made today.

**One note on this entry's own `items` block, made explicit rather than left to be discovered.** It records
**2 October 2026 — the index date** — because the deposited publication date of **13 October 2027** is unusable
and no corrected date is available. **That is a departure from this archive's standing practice of recording
the actual publication date**, and it is the only honest option when the deposited date is wrong by a year.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cardfail.2026.09.001)

## 2. Spain's sixth *H. pylori* consensus sets a cure rate as the bar, not a strength of recommendation

**Society · Published:** **Spanish consensus panel**, *Gastroenterología y Hepatología* — **indexed
1 October 2026**, publication date **30 September 2026**, 0 citations, typed `Practice Guideline`. **Sixteen
experts, conference held June 2026, systematic literature search, 25 recommendations, iterative serial
anonymous voting by Delphi methodology, GRADE for certainty and strength.** The fifth consensus was **2021**.

> **As a starting point, this consensus establishes that the recommended treatments should achieve, and
> preferably exceed, a 90% cure rate when administered empirically. Accordingly, quadruple regimens are
> recommended as both first-line and rescue treatments.** As first-line treatment, a **10-day regimen with a
> proton pump inhibitor plus bismuth, tetracycline, and metronidazole** is recommended. **After failure of this
> regimen, a combination of a proton pump inhibitor, amoxicillin, levofloxacin, and bismuth** is recommended.
> This consensus also provides a detailed review of other rescue treatment alternatives.

**Interpretation.** **The methodologically interesting move is in the first sentence, and it is not a GRADE
move at all.** The panel sets a **performance floor — 90% empirical cure — and then admits only regimens that
clear it.** Strength of recommendation and certainty of evidence are about how confident the panel is; **this
is a statement about how good the treatment has to be before confidence is even relevant.** It inverts the
usual logic: instead of asking what the evidence supports, it asks what the minimum acceptable result is and
works back to the regimens capable of delivering it. **Everything downstream follows mechanically — "accordingly,
quadruple regimens," because triple therapy cannot reach 90% against current Spanish resistance patterns.**

**For this archive's catalogue of consensus thresholds, this is a different kind of threshold entirely.** The
catalogue has held agreement thresholds — Korean 70%, Brain Trauma Foundation blinded 80%, BOOTStraP 70% at
subgroup and 90% at plenary, STS above 75%, and the Brazilian panel's declared absence of one. **A 90% cure
rate is not an agreement threshold; it is an efficacy threshold, and it binds the recommendation rather than
the panel.** Worth keeping as a separate column: **thresholds about the panel, and thresholds about the
treatment.**

**Clinically this is squarely in the secondary infectious-disease scope and the direction is familiar: triple
therapy is finished in a high-resistance population, and bismuth quadruple therapy is first-line.** The
10-day PPI–bismuth–tetracycline–metronidazole regimen as first choice, with PPI–amoxicillin–levofloxacin–
bismuth as the first rescue, is an antimicrobial-stewardship document as much as a gastroenterological one —
**it is about not using regimens that fail, which is where resistance is generated.**

**Deposit note, and a new variant of a trap this archive already tracks.** This document is deposited **twice
under the same DOI** — once titled in Spanish with **no abstract at all**, once titled in English with the
full abstract. **The previously catalogued dual-deposit trap was two DOIs with two dates, one abstract-free.
This is one DOI, two language records, one abstract-free.** A sweep that happens to return the Spanish record
first sees a `Practice Guideline` with nothing in it. **The rule generalises: when a guideline record has no
abstract, look for a sibling record before concluding the deposit is empty — the sibling may share the DOI.**

[Read the consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gastrohep.2026.502847)

## 3. French recommendations on antibiotics after paediatric stem cell transplantation, built from a survey of what its own centres do

**Society · Published:** **SFGM-TC (Société Francophone de Greffe de Moelle et de Thérapie Cellulaire)**,
*Bulletin du Cancer* — **indexed 1 October 2026**, publication date **30 September 2026**, 0 citations, typed
`Practice Guideline` with an English abstract. **French in the original; only the abstract is quoted here.**

> Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is associated with **marked and prolonged neutropenia, resulting in a
> high risk of severe infections and warranting the use of broad-spectrum empirical antibiotics in patients
> with fever. The risk of infection begins before the onset of aplasia and persists beyond the neutropenic
> phase. However, the efficacy of antibiotic prophylaxis and the risk-benefit balance associated with the
> empirical use of broad-spectrum antibiotics remain insufficiently evaluated in pediatric patients.** Our
> working group **assessed current clinical practices using a questionnaire sent to SFGM-TC pediatric
> centers** and reviewed original studies and published reviews … to formulate recommendations. These
> recommendations address **the use of empirical and prophylactic antibiotic therapy according to the
> post-HSCT period and the optimization of high-dose antibacterial therapy** in pediatric patients.
> Additionally, we evaluated **the impact of these strategies on the gut microbiota and post-HSCT outcomes in
> children.**

**Interpretation.** **The development method is the notable part: a questionnaire to its own centres before the
literature review.** Most guideline panels start from the evidence and arrive at a recommendation; **this one
started by measuring what its members actually do, then read the literature against that.** It is the same
instinct as the [Chinese intravenous-to-oral switch guideline reported on
14 October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-14/), whose stated problem was
inconsistency rather than reluctance — **a panel that surveys practice first is implicitly treating variation,
not ignorance, as the thing to fix.** For a federation of transplant centres that is probably right, and it
makes the resulting document a harmonisation instrument.

**The honest statement of the evidence base is in the abstract and is unusually blunt: the efficacy of
prophylaxis and the risk–benefit of empirical broad-spectrum antibiotics "remain insufficiently evaluated in
pediatric patients."** So this is a set of recommendations issued in acknowledged near-absence of paediatric
evidence, for a population in which the intervention is universal. **That combination — unavoidable treatment,
thin evidence — is where consensus documents do their most useful and least verifiable work.**

**The microbiota element is what dates it to this decade.** Including "the impact of these strategies on the
gut microbiota and post-HSCT outcomes" as an evaluated outcome alongside infection control is a different
risk–benefit calculation from the one a 2010 version would have made, and in allogeneic transplant it is not
speculative: gut microbiota composition has become a recognised correlate of transplant outcome. **A
stewardship recommendation whose harm side includes the microbiome rather than only resistance is a change
worth noting.**

**Limitation, plainly: the recommendations are in French and only the English abstract is readable here**, so
this archive cannot say which antibiotics, which thresholds, or which post-transplant periods the
recommendations distinguish. **Those are precisely the operative details.** It is logged with the gap stated.
