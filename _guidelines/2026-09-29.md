## 1. Pulmonary antibody-mediated rejection and the GAP definition

**Society · Published:** **ISHLT**. *Journal of Heart and Lung Transplantation*, **11 June 2026** —
**three and a half months old.** Indexed as a **Practice Guideline** and a **Review**
Calabrese, **Levine**, Adam, Agbor-Enoh, Benden, Budev, Cochrane, Cozzi, Darley, Fishbein, Gelman,
Glanville and colleagues

The number that frames the whole document:

> Antibody-mediated rejection (AMR) is an increasingly recognized form of rejection and cause of graft
> failure after lung transplantation… Despite growing awareness and recent advances in our
> understanding of AMR, **outcomes remain dismal with a 2-year survival of only 20%.**

Method: a multidisciplinary workgroup **divided into 9 subgroups**, using the **modified Delphi
method**, to update the **2016 definition**.

> A **multidimensional definition** was developed to enhance precision by reporting the specific
> presenting features. This **Graft, Antibody, and Pathology (GAP) definition** is based on the
> presence of **Graft dysfunction**, the presence and characteristics of **Antibodies**, and
> **Pathological findings.**

> The workgroup emphasized that **identifying better treatments for AMR is a critical unmet need** and
> proposed that a more precise definition might allow better management by **providing a platform for
> testing and developing new therapies.**

**Interpretation.** A twenty percent two-year survival is a catastrophic number, and what the society
has produced in response is a definition. That is the right thing to have produced, and it is worth
being explicit about why.

**You cannot run a trial without a case definition.** AMR after lung transplantation has been
diagnosed inconsistently — the 2016 definition was categorical, and a condition defined differently in
different centres cannot be enrolled, stratified or compared. **The GAP framework replaces a category
with three axes: graft dysfunction, antibody characteristics, pathology.** The workgroup says plainly
that this is intended as *"a platform for testing and developing new therapies"* — **the deliverable is
infrastructure for research, not treatment**, and the document does not pretend otherwise.

**This is the same structural move this archive has now seen several times** — the EACTS/STS "aortic
organ" concept, the TEM classification, [refractory VF split into three
phenotypes](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-24/). **A field that cannot treat something well
frequently responds by redescribing it more precisely**, and that is a legitimate stage rather than an
evasion. The test is whether trials follow.

**And this document corrects a rule this archive recorded three days ago.** On 26 September I wrote
that ISHLT deposits scope-only abstracts, **"five for five"**, and prescribed budgeting a full-text
attempt from the outset. **The first half needs qualifying: this abstract carries real content** — the
survival figure, the three axes of the definition, the method. **And more importantly, the record shows
`inPMC: Y` and `hasPDF: Y`** — the full text is in PubMed Central. **It is the first ISHLT document in
this archive that is actually reachable**, and the source note has been amended rather than left to
mislead a future run.

*Clinical relevance beyond transplant medicine: AMR presents as graft dysfunction, which means these
patients present to intensive care with failing lungs and an immunological cause that requires a
specific diagnostic pathway. A definition that specifies what to measure — donor-specific antibody
characteristics, histology, graft function — is directly a statement about what the ICU should be
sending off.*

[Read the statement](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healun.2026.04.019) · PMID 42274447 · **full text in
PMC**

## 2. The first consensus on referral and selection of paediatric lung transplant candidates

**Society · Published:** **ISHLT**. *JHLT*, **8 October 2025** — **almost twelve months old.** Cited 5
times. Indexed as a **Consensus Statement**
**Hayes**, Avdimiretz, Brugha, Mullen, Paraskeva, Midyat, Solomon, **Sweet**, Melicoff-Portillo,
Danziger-Isakov and colleagues

> The timing of referral, evaluation, determination of candidacy, and listing of candidates **poses
> challenges and ethical dilemmas for pediatric care providers.**

> …to generate the **first consensus guidelines on the referral criteria and selection of pediatric
> lung transplant candidates.**

Aims: help providers **identify potential candidates**, **optimise the timing of referral**, and give
transplant centres **a framework for evaluation and selection.**

**Interpretation. "The first consensus guidelines" is the finding.** Paediatric lung transplantation
has been performed for decades, and until October 2025 there was no international consensus on **when
to refer a child or how to select one.**

**That absence matters more in paediatrics than it would in adults**, because the decision is made by
clinicians who see very few such children. A general paediatric respiratory physician managing a child
with advanced lung disease may refer once in a career — **and the evidence from elsewhere in this
archive is that low-volume, high-stakes decisions made without a framework are made inconsistently.**
The [study of regret in VV-ECMO candidacy](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-22/) showed exactly that
in adults, with inconsistency **within** single centres.

**The abstract's phrase "challenges and ethical dilemmas" is doing real work and should not be read as
padding.** Selection for paediatric transplant involves judgements about a child's future, family
capacity to manage complex post-transplant care, and the allocation of a scarce organ — **and a
consensus document that names those as ethical rather than technical problems is describing its own
limits accurately.**

*Access note: **`inPMC: N`, `hasPDF: N`** — the full text is not reachable, and the abstract states
purpose and audience without a single referral criterion. **No criterion from this document has been
read.***

## 3. Short telomere syndrome and lung transplantation

**Society · Published:** **ISHLT**. *JHLT*, **8 January 2026** — **nearly nine months old.** Cited 7
times. Indexed as a **Consensus Statement**
Courtwright, Mackintosh, Alder, Garcia, Froidure, Lowery, **Hayes**, Pali, Philippot, Borie,
**Greenland** and colleagues

> Motivated by growing evidence that the presence of **critically shortened telomeres influences
> interstitial lung disease trajectories** and is associated with **extrapulmonary conditions relevant
> to lung transplant candidacy and post-transplant complications**…

> The need for this document arises from the recognition that **short telomere syndrome is an
> underdiagnosed contributor to ILD**, and that its presence **introduces complexities that require
> dedicated, multidisciplinary attention in the transplant setting.**

**Interpretation.** The most specialised of the three and the one with the clearest perioperative
implications, which the abstract does not spell out.

**Short telomere syndrome is a multi-system disease that presents as a lung disease.** The telomere
biology that drives pulmonary fibrosis also affects the bone marrow and the liver — **so a patient
listed for lung transplantation on the basis of their ILD may also have marrow failure and hepatic
involvement.** For anyone managing that patient through transplantation, that means **cytopenias that
complicate immunosuppression and bleeding, and altered handling of drugs cleared hepatically.** **That
inference is mine, not the document's**, but it is why an ILD paper belongs in a perioperative archive.

**"Underdiagnosed" is the operative word.** If STS is under-recognised, then some proportion of
patients already being assessed for lung transplantation have it undiagnosed — and the document's
purpose is to change who gets tested. **A consensus statement whose main effect is to prompt a
diagnostic test in a population already under evaluation is a cheap intervention with a potentially
large effect on management.**

*Access note: **`inPMC: N`, `hasPDF: N`**. The abstract gives rationale and scope only — **no
recommendation, no testing threshold, no candidacy criterion has been read.***

[Read the consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healun.2025.10.028) · PMID 41504690

## 4. Haemodynamic management in congenital diaphragmatic hernia — **a preprint**

**Published:** **Children's Hospitals Neonatal Consortium CDH Focus Group**. **PREPRINT on Research
Square, 28 September 2026 — not peer reviewed.** Europe PMC ID PPR1328952
Gopal, Johng, Makkar, Nesterenko, Dariya, Noh, Ruiz, Patel, Mahmood, Keene, Grover, Gowda

Method: systematic review of literature **January 2000 to November 2023**; **GRADE** for quality of
evidence; where evidence was insufficient, **expert statements developed from a national survey of
practice**; then **three rounds of modified Delphi** among CHNC CDH Focus Group members.

> Consensus was reached on **20 statements** addressing **baseline physiological assessment,
> echocardiographic parameters, hemodynamic monitoring, physiology-based phenotyping, and
> phenotype-based vasoactive management** of infants with CDH.

**Interpretation.** Reported with its preprint status stated three times, because that status is the
main caveat and the content is genuinely interesting.

**"Physiology-based phenotyping and phenotype-based vasoactive management" is the same idea that has
run through this archive all month, arriving in neonatal intensive care.** CDH infants have distinct
haemodynamic phenotypes — predominant pulmonary hypertension with right ventricular failure, left
ventricular hypoplasia and dysfunction, systemic hypotension — and **the same vasoactive drug helps one
phenotype and harms another.** A consensus that stratifies the vasoactive choice by echocardiographic
phenotype rather than by blood pressure is doing exactly what
[today's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-29/) argues for in adults: **treating the physiology
rather than the number.**

**The method is honest about its own structure**, and the structure is unusual: GRADE where evidence
existed, and **a national practice survey where it did not.** That is a defensible way to fill gaps —
it at least documents what people actually do rather than what a panel imagines — but it means some of
the 20 statements describe prevailing practice rather than evidence, and the abstract does not say
which.

**Scope: CDH infants are neonatal intensive care and frequently ECMO patients**, which places this at
the edge of this project's primary scope rather than outside it. A [borderline CDH compliance study was
logged on 19 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-19/) for the same reason.

**It is a preprint. Nothing in it has been peer reviewed, the 20 statements are not individually
available in the abstract, and it should not be cited as guidance.** Re-check for a journal version.

[Read the preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10757450/v1)
