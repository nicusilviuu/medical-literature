nothing in the window. ESAIC, ESC, ESPEN, ESICM, SCCM, IACTS, EACTS, STS and ASA — nothing. **Eighth

## 1. Five guidelines, one appraisal framework, and a finding this archive reached six weeks early from one document

**[Perioperative management of patients taking GLP-1 receptor agonists: A comparative review of international
clinical guidelines](https://doi.org/10.4103/sja.sja_314_26)** — *Saudi Journal of Anaesthesia*,
**1 October 2026**. Open access, PMC13719104. **Published under yesterday's routing rule: a document whose
subject is what guidelines say belongs to this watch.**

**Method, and it is a real appraisal rather than a narrative comparison.** A systematic search of PubMed,
MEDLINE, Embase, society websites and grey literature for perioperative GLP-1 guidance published
**January 2023 to March 2026**; **five documents eligible**; **each independently appraised by two reviewers
using AGREE-REX**; and the primary studies underpinning each guideline reviewed against **four pre-specified
clinical questions.**

**What the five do and do not agree on:**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Converged** | **Four of five now recommend continuation with structured fasting modification, superseding the 2023 ASA cessation policy** |
| **Diverged** | **Fasting protocols "diverge substantially" — SPAQI 2025 uniquely distinguishes high-calorie from low-calorie liquids** |
| **Partly stated** | **Three of five explicitly state that absence of gastrointestinal symptoms cannot be relied upon for risk stratification** |
| **Unanimous and empty** | **All five endorse gastric ultrasound; none defines validated decision thresholds** |

**The AGREE-REX verdicts are specific and the pair of them is the useful part.** The **AAGBI/RCoA 2025**
statement had *"the broadest multidisciplinary representation and the most transparent process"*; **SPAQI
2025** had *"the most rigorous evidence base but the most complex implementation requirements."* **Those are
different virtues and the review does not pretend one document wins.**

**And the structural criticism is the sharpest sentence in the abstract:**

> **Endocrinologist and obesity medicine representation is absent from most authoring groups, and the
> recommendations reflect this.**

**Interpretation.** **This archive holds three of the five and can say what it made of them at the time.**
The [17 September entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-17/) carried the **2025 ADS/ANZCA/GESA/NACOS
recommendations** and the **Korean Society of Anesthesiologists consensus**, under the headline *"ANZCA says
continue the drug, and says plainly that symptoms cannot rule out a full stomach."* The
[20 September entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-20/) carried the **January 2025 multidisciplinary
consensus published in *Anaesthesia*** — the AAGBI's journal, so **very likely the document this review
appraises as AAGBI/RCoA 2025, though the review's label and the archive's citation are not identical and the
identity is flagged as likely rather than confirmed.** The archive also holds **SPAQI's SGLT2 consensus**, a
sibling document from the same body, not its GLP-1 guidance.

**Two of this review's five-document findings are things this archive published six weeks ago from single
sources.** *Continue the drug* was the 17 September headline; *symptoms cannot rule out a full stomach* was
the other half of it, and the review now reports that **three of five guidelines say so explicitly** —
meaning two do not. **That is the value of a cross-guideline appraisal over a daily watch: the watch can tell
you what one document says, and only the appraisal can tell you how many of the others agree.**

**The gap all five share is the one that matters clinically and the review names it twice.** **Every one of
the five endorses gastric ultrasound and none says what measurement should change the plan.** **A unanimous
recommendation to use a test with no threshold is a recommendation to look and then decide on grounds the
guideline does not supply** — and it is exactly what the [26 October brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-26/)
inferred from the other end, reading a GLP-1 safety review in which the surrogate had denominators and the
aspiration events did not. **A panel cannot specify a threshold from case reports.**

**The closing list of unaddressed risks is worth keeping as a checklist:** incomplete gastric emptying,
**hypoglycaemia in insulin-dependent patients** — a harm of fasting modification rather than of the drug, and
the one most easily lost in an aspiration-focused document — and the absent ultrasound thresholds.
*"Prospective outcome data capturing aspiration events and glycemic complications remain urgently needed."*

## 2. A global consensus on a definition, in four journals at once, with an 840-character abstract

**[Acute-on-chronic liver failure: A global multisociety consensus on
definitions](https://doi.org/10.1097/hep.0000000000001891)** — the **ACLF multisociety harmonisation
initiative**, **30 October 2026**.

**A scope correction first, because it is this archive's.** **The predecessor of this document — the Global
ACLF "Istanbul Document" — was logged in the [28 September entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-28/)
in a list of items "out of scope and passed over."** **That judgement was defensible for a regional
definitional document and is not defensible for this one.** **Acute-on-chronic liver failure is an intensive
care condition; this consensus names "critical care management" as one of the five frameworks it
establishes; and this archive's own [19 September brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-19/) carried a
study of continuous renal replacement therapy in cirrhosis and acute-on-chronic liver failure, reporting a
CLIF-C ACLF hazard ratio.** **So the archive was already carrying ACLF research while passing over ACLF
definitions. The scope rule is corrected: a definitional document for a condition managed in intensive care
is in scope, whatever specialty publishes it.** **Second correction in two days, after yesterday's routing
fix.**

**The document, as its abstract describes it:** a *"global collaborative effort to establish a unified,
evidence-based framework for the definition, diagnosis, grading, and management of ACLF"*, building on
existing regional definitions, and establishing **a common framework for patient stratification, organ
dysfunction and failure assessment, critical care management, liver transplantation, and the design of
future phase II/III trials.** **"Standardised, regulatory-aligned endpoints are expected to improve
cross-study comparability."**

**And a publication observation that is itself notable.** **The same consensus appears four times in the
index, in four journals, with four DOIs** — *Hepatology*, *Journal of Hepatology*, *Annals of Hepatology* and
*Hepatology International*. Three carry the same 840-character abstract; the *Journal of Hepatology* copy is
**abstract-free and dated 1 February 2027**, which is **the future-print-date hazard this archive logged on
26 October and confirmed journal-wide on 27 October, now appearing in a third journal family.** **A reader
who met only that copy would find no abstract and a publication date three months away.**

**Interpretation, and it is a criticism of the reporting rather than the document.** **Eight hundred and forty
characters for a global multi-society consensus on a definition is thin, and what is missing is everything
this archive's structure catalogue records.** **The abstract names no societies, gives no count of
definitional criteria or recommendations, states no grading scheme and no consensus method or threshold.**
There is no Delphi, no voting rule, no panel size. **For a document whose entire purpose is to make an
organ-failure count comparable across studies, the abstract does not let a reader see how the count was
agreed.**

**The ambition is nevertheless the right one and worth stating plainly.** ACLF has had competing regional
definitions for years — European, Asian-Pacific and North American frameworks that classify different patients
as having the same syndrome — and **the direct consequence is that trials have not been comparable.** **A
harmonised definition with regulatory-aligned endpoints is infrastructure, not guidance**, and the test of it
is whether phase II and III trials adopt it. **That is checkable in two years and the archive should check
it.**

[Read the consensus](https://doi.org/10.1097/hep.0000000000001891) · *Hepatology*

## 3. The same genre done properly: six organ systems, seven days, five levels of certainty, and a statement of what it is not for

**[Acute multi-organ dysfunction (aMOD): Brighton Collaboration case definition and guidelines for data
collection, analysis, and presentation of immunization safety
data](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2026.129264)** — *Vaccine*, **30 October 2026**. Developed by
Brighton Collaboration process and subject-matter experts under the **CEPI-funded Safety Platform for
Evaluation of vACcines (SPEAC)**; scoping review plus expert consensus; peer reviewed by a Reference Group of
vaccine-safety stakeholders and aMOD experts.

**The definition, in full, because its specificity is the point:**

> aMOD is defined as **acute dysfunction involving two or more of six organ systems: respiratory,
> cardiovascular, renal, neurologic, hepatic, and hemostatic.** A key feature is the requirement that
> **organ dysfunction occur within the same clinical episode, with onset within 7 days, allowing sequential or
> simultaneous involvement.** **Five levels of certainty are included for each of the six organs.** The
> overall case definition also comprises **five levels: Level 1 (Definite), Level 2 (Probable), Level 3
> (Possible), Level 4 (Insufficient information), Level 5 (Not a case).**

**And two boundary statements that most definitional documents leave implicit:**

> It is **not intended to guide clinical management**, but it can serve as an outcome measure of disease
> severity…
>
> Importantly, **AEFI case definitions are separate from causality assessment.**

**Interpretation, and reading this beside item 2 is the entry's point.** **Two definitional consensus
documents landed in the same two-day window, both concerned with counting organ dysfunction, and they are
opposites in how they report themselves.** **The ACLF consensus says it has produced a framework; the aMOD
definition shows you the framework** — the organ list, the time window, the certainty ladder, the
qualification that an insufficient-information case is a named category rather than an exclusion.

**That last feature deserves singling out.** **Level 4, "Insufficient information", is a reportable outcome
rather than a missing value.** **In surveillance that is the difference between a denominator you can trust
and one you cannot**, and it is the same distinction this archive has been pressing all week — the
[28 October brief's](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-28/) finding that the surrogate had denominators and
the hard outcome did not, and the [30 October brief's](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-30/) point that a
Brier score cannot be read without a base rate. **A definition that makes "we could not tell" a category is a
definition built by people who expect to be held to their numbers.**

**Two limits to state.** **This is a vaccine-safety instrument and it says so** — it was built for clinical
trials and post-licensure pharmacovigilance, and the document explicitly disclaims clinical management, so
nothing here is guidance for treating a patient with failing organs. **And "two or more of six organ systems
within seven days" is a looser construct than the sequential organ failure assessment an intensivist uses**;
the criteria *"build on established organ dysfunction frameworks while adapting them for global
application"*, which means they were deliberately made implementable where laboratory access is limited.
**That is a defensible trade and a reader should not mistake the result for a severity score.**

**Archive context: the Brighton Collaboration has not appeared in this archive before**, and **neither has a
case definition as a document class.** **That is the thing to record from today: a definition is a distinct
genre from a guideline, a consensus statement and a risk classification, and this archive's structure
catalogue should hold it as one** — because a definition is a measuring instrument, and the week this archive
has just spent on instruments is the argument for cataloguing them.

[Read the case definition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2026.129264) · *Vaccine*
