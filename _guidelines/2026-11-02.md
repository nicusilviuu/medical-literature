## A protocol, not a guideline: very old patients after unplanned ICU admission

**[Defining best practice for very old patients following unplanned intensive care admission: protocol of the
Delphi consensus study](https://doi.org/10.1111/imj.70687)** · Australia and New Zealand multidisciplinary
panel · *Internal Medicine Journal* · **published 31 October 2026, indexed 1 November** · paywalled

**This is a protocol and is reported as one.** Yesterday's month audit classified protocols as not
guideline-class and excluded one on exactly that ground. **The difference is that this archive now has a
reason to track forthcoming documents, so a protocol is reported as a calendar entry rather than as
guidance.** From the published abstract:

> Very old patients (≥80 years) represent a rapidly increasing proportion of intensive care unit (ICU)
> admissions across Australia and New Zealand. Care of this population is characterised by substantial
> prognostic uncertainty and variability in ICU triage, treatment limitation and post-ICU pathways.
> In particular, the role and implementation of time-limited ICU trials remain inconsistent. … A
> multidisciplinary, multispecialty panel of expert clinicians (intensive care, geriatrics, emergency
> medicine), doctors in training, nurses, allied health professionals, ethicists and health service leaders
> … Consensus will be defined a priori using predefined thresholds and median scores.

**And it sets up a divergence test in advance, which is why it is worth an entry now.** **ESICM already
published consensus recommendations for this exact population** — verified from the index rather than taken
from a search result:

| | **ESICM, February 2025** | **ANZ study, protocol only** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Document | `10.1007/s00134-025-07794-4`, *Intensive Care Medicine*, typed **Consensus Statement** | protocol published, consensus not yet run |
| Panel | **110 experts** — 28 steering group plus 82 additional | multidisciplinary ANZ panel, purposive and snowball sampling |
| Threshold | **≥90% strong, ≥80% moderate**, on a 9-point Likert scale | **"predefined thresholds"** — the number is not in the protocol |
| Output | **48 statements plus 2 checklists** | — |
| Time-limited ICU trials | **not named in the abstract's account of its scope** | **the central question** |

**Three things follow.**

**First, the gap the ANZ group is filling is real and specific.** ESICM's 48 statements cover fundamental
principles, goals of care, admission and treatment decisions and post-discharge management. **Time-limited
trials of intensive care — admit, treat for a defined period, then reassess against pre-agreed criteria — are
the mechanism by which prognostic uncertainty in an eighty-year-old is actually managed at the bedside, and
they are the thing the protocol says remains inconsistent.**

**Second, the protocol does not state its consensus threshold, and ESICM's did.** **A Delphi's threshold is
not a detail; it determines how many statements survive.** Publishing a protocol that defers the number to
"predefined thresholds" forgoes most of the benefit of publishing a protocol at all. **This is a
pre-registration weakness and it is worth naming in advance of the result rather than after it.**

**Third, the ESICM consensus is not in this archive.** It predates the archive, but it is the reference
document for a population two of today's items concern. **Added to the backlog as a readable document, not a
gap.**

**Interpretation.** **This is the 30 October divergence mechanism caught before it happens.** That rule says
guidelines converge on process measures and diverge on every number. **Here a regional panel is about to issue
recommendations on a population a European society has already covered, using an unstated threshold against
ESICM's stated 90% and 80%.** **The prediction worth recording: the two documents will agree on the process —
that time-limited trials, goals-of-care conversations and structured post-ICU pathways are the right
architecture — and will differ on every threshold, including the age cut-off and the trial duration.**
Checkable when the ANZ consensus publishes.

## A scope call reversed: disease-modifying therapy in ventilator-dependent spinal muscular atrophy

**[Defining the clinical benefits of disease-modifying therapy in people living with spinal muscular atrophy
who require permanent ventilation: a UK-based modified Delphi consensus
process](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2026.104278)** · UK multidisciplinary expert panel ·
*EClinicalMedicine* · **published 24 October 2026** · paywalled

**Yesterday's month audit saw this document as a title-only PMC record with no abstract and classified it out
of scope. That call is reversed today, with the abstract readable.** **The ground for reversing it is narrow
and I am stating it rather than quietly widening the scope: the document's central recommendation is about
whether ventilator dependence may be used as an access barrier to therapy** — and that is a decision made
about patients who are in, or discharged from, intensive care. **New rule: a scope call made from a title
alone is provisional, and it is re-made when the abstract becomes readable.**

From the published abstract:

> Although disease-modifying therapies (DMTs) have transformed outcomes for many, prior to this research,
> access in the UK was restricted for patients receiving PV (SMA-PV). … Excluding the Chair, 18 participants
> evaluated 22 statements across seven thematic areas … All 22 statements achieved consensus after two
> voting rounds (100% participation; agreement range 89-100%). Two statements were revised and re-voted in
> round 2: one after narrowly missing consensus (72% agreement) and the other to incorporate PAP feedback
> despite achieving 100% agreement in round 1. The panel agreed that PV alone should not preclude access to
> DMTs and that trial access with personalised treatment goals may be appropriate. … Roche Products Ltd.

**The clinical substance is one sentence and it is the right one.** **Permanent ventilation alone should not
preclude access to disease-modifying therapy.** For an intensive care or long-term ventilation service, that
removes a categorical exclusion and replaces it with trial access against personalised goals. **The panel
also widened the definition of clinical benefit to include subtle functional change and quality of life**,
which is the only defensible position in a population where a standard motor-function scale has almost no
range left to measure.

**Two appraisal findings, both of which a reader should weigh.**

**All 22 statements reached consensus, at a 75% threshold, with agreement between 89% and 100%.** **A
Delphi in which nothing fails is a Delphi that asked nothing contested.** Contrast yesterday's inguinal
lymphadenectomy consensus: **51 of 86 statements, 35 failing** — and the failures were where the useful
information was. **A 22-for-22 result is not a strength; it is a signal that the statement set was drafted
to pass.**

**And the funder is the manufacturer.** The abstract's last line is **"Roche Products Ltd."** The document's
central recommendation is that access restrictions to disease-modifying therapy should be reconsidered. **That
is not a reason to dismiss it — the clinical argument stands on its own and the restriction being challenged
was arguably indefensible — but a manufacturer-funded panel recommending wider access to the manufacturer's
therapy class is a conflict that belongs in the first line of any summary, not the last.** **The study was
also not prospectively registered**, which the authors state themselves.

**One genuinely good procedural detail.** A statement with **100% agreement in round 1 was revised and
re-voted anyway**, to incorporate feedback from the non-voting patient advisory panel. **Revising a statement
that already passed unanimously is the opposite of drafting to pass, and it is the single best thing in the
methods.**

## A better route to Chinese society documents

**[Clinical Application of Hemadsorption in Maintenance Hemodialysis Patients: An Adapted English
Republication of the Chinese HA-MHD Consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xkme.2026.101528)** · Chinese HA-MHD
Consensus Working Group · *Kidney Medicine* · **published 11 September 2026, indexed 1 November** · paywalled

**Out of scope on content and reported for its form.** The subject is maintenance haemodialysis, not intensive
care. From the abstract:

> The Chinese HA-MHD Consensus Working Group has revised and updated the 2021 Shanghai Expert Consensus on
> the Clinical Application of Haemoadsorption in Maintenance Haemodialysis Patients … Based on an extensive
> literature search, the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework, and the
> modified Delphi method, the working group formulated 27 evidence-based recommendations.

**The finding is the route.** On 6 October this archive settled a route to Chinese society documents: they are
in Europe PMC, the guideline sweep finds them, the journal title is transliterated, the language is `chi`, and
the abstracts are long. **This is a second and better route: a Chinese consensus deliberately republished in
English, in a Western journal, adapted for an international readership, with GRADE and a modified Delphi
named in the abstract.** **It is fully readable without translation and it carries its predecessor's identity
— the 2021 Shanghai consensus — so the change can be measured.**

**New standing practice: sweep for the phrase "English republication" and its variants.** A Chinese society
document that has been republished in English is both more readable and more likely to be the version a
non-Chinese reader will cite, and the transliterated-journal route will not find it.

**The near-miss worth recording.** This archive published a **consensus on polymyxin B haemoadsorption in
endotoxic septic shock** on 26 October. **Two haemadsorption consensus documents in eight days, one for septic
shock and one for maintenance dialysis** — same technology, different populations, and only the first is in
scope. **Noted so that a future sweep does not treat them as duplicates.**

## The index reading: yesterday's prediction, confirmed in one day

**Yesterday this archive withdrew its own instruction to read daily index-date buckets as a measurement of a
day's delivery, on the evidence of a per-day October scan. Today the same bucket it read yesterday has
changed, in the direction the withdrawal implied.**

| 1 November bucket | Read 1 Nov, 14:11 | Read 2 Nov, 14:23 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MEDLINE | **0** | **3,346** | **+3,346** |
| PMC | 1 | **395** | **+394** |
| Preprints | **156** | **156** | **0** |
| **Total** | **157** | **3,897** | **+3,740** |

Parts sum to 3,897 exactly.

**Read that table twice.** **The preprint component is frozen at exactly the 156 measured yesterday, while
MEDLINE went from nothing to 3,346 and PMC from one record to 395.** **So the lag differs by source:
preprints are stamped on the day, and MEDLINE and PMC are stamped into the previous day's bucket.**
Yesterday's headline — *MEDLINE delivered zero records in twenty-four hours* — was a sound statement about
MEDLINE's **total**, which was genuinely unchanged, and the brief said explicitly that the empty bucket was
not independent evidence for it. **Today that caution is vindicated: the bucket was not empty, it was early.**

**MEDLINE's total has now moved.** It went from 41,307,878 to **41,311,222, up 3,344**, and 3,346 of the
1 November bucket's records are MEDLINE — so essentially the whole of the total's growth was stamped into
yesterday's date. **Across the two days MEDLINE added 3,344 records, an average of 1,672 a day, against the
4,837 in Saturday's batch.** **Stated as a measurement and not characterised.**

**And the 31 October bucket shrank.**

| 31 October bucket | 1 Nov | 2 Nov | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MEDLINE | 4,837 | 4,837 | 0 |
| PMC | 1,527 | **1,215** | **−312** |
| Preprints | 904 | 904 | 0 |
| Bookshelf | **1** | **0** | **−1** |
| Clinical trials | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| **Total** | **7,270** | **6,957** | **−313** |

Parts sum to 6,957, and the fall is PMC −312 plus Bookshelf −1.

**So the eighth value of the week's bucket series — the +1 that this archive published yesterday, and
identified as one Bookshelf record — has been removed again.** That bucket's Bookshelf count went 0, then 1,
then 0, while Bookshelf's whole-database total went 8, 9, then **6**. **A source oscillating in single digits
across three days is the clearest possible demonstration that the series measured nothing**, and it is the
third independent reason to have closed it.

**Whole database 49,182,879 at 14:23:06, residual zero, up 3,722** — MEDLINE +3,344, preprints +455,
PMC **−74**, Bookshelf **−3**, the other seven unchanged, and the four changes sum to 3,722 exactly.
**Two sources lost records while the whole grew**, the pattern first recorded on 29 October. Today's own
bucket is **456 — preprints 455 and PMC 1, MEDLINE zero**, which on the evidence above should be read as
*not yet stamped* rather than as a delivery of zero.

**One consequence for yesterday's audit, and it is a caveat on my own figure.** Yesterday's month audit
reported **267,729** index dates for October. **Today that total reads 267,320, down 409.** The audit's
classification of documents stands — the 71 records were read, matched and classified — **but the aggregate
was a reading at an instant, not a fact about the month, and anyone reproducing it will get a different
number.** **Rule: date every aggregate in an audit, and say that it will not reproduce.** The two fixed
windows also moved: 1–30 September **178,417, down 63**; 1–7 October **27,854, down 1**.
