## 1. Eight recommendations from 9,330 abstracts, and not one of them high-certainty

**Society · Published:** **American College of Chest Physicians**, *Chest*, **2 September 2026** —
**forty-two days old**, 0 citations, typed `Practice Guideline`. CHEST standardised methodology, PICO framing,
**GRADE appraisal, modified Delphi for consensus.**

> **Results We identified 9330 abstracts, assessed 252 full texts, and included 142 studies.** The literature
> review and analysis, based on **6 PICO questions, yielded 8 recommendations. No recommendations were
> supported by high-certainty evidence specific to inpatient tobacco treatment settings.** Of the eight
> recommendations, **four were supported by moderate-certainty evidence, two by low-certainty evidence, and two
> by very low-certainty evidence, supplemented by expert consensus.**
>
> **Interpretation** Hospitalization offers a critical window to initiate tobacco treatment. This guideline
> synthesizes the existing literature to inform key components of inpatient tobacco treatment, including **an
> opt-out population-based approach that proactively provides counseling and pharmacotherapy to all patients
> who smoke unless they explicitly decline**; the initiation and selection of pharmacotherapies during
> hospitalization; counseling approaches delivered during the inpatient stay; and post-discharge counseling
> approaches following hospital discharge.

**Interpretation.** **Read the funnel and the grades together, because that is where the document's honesty
sits.** Nine thousand three hundred and thirty abstracts, 252 full texts, 142 included studies — and **zero
high-certainty recommendations.** Eighteen studies per recommendation and the certainty ceiling is moderate.
**A reader could mistake that for a weak evidence base on tobacco treatment, and it is not**: the qualifier in
the sentence is doing the work. The evidence "specific to inpatient tobacco treatment settings" is what is
missing. **Tobacco treatment itself is one of the better-evidenced interventions in medicine; what nobody has
established at high certainty is what changes when you do it to someone who is in a hospital bed.** That is a
setting gap, not a therapy gap, and the distinction matters for how a hospital should read this.

**The central recommendation is not a treatment at all — it is a default.** An **opt-out population-based
approach**, counselling and pharmacotherapy offered proactively to every patient who smokes unless they
explicitly decline. **That is a change to who has to act**, and it is the second time in six weeks this archive
has found a guideline whose most consequential recommendation is structural rather than clinical. **Opt-out
designs are powerful precisely because they do not depend on a clinician remembering**, which is also why they
are hard to study: the comparator is an organisational state, not a placebo. **A moderate-certainty grade on an
opt-out recommendation is close to the best that design can earn.**

**And there is a direct line from here to this morning's brief.** [STOP-Vanc](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-14/)
showed an accurate test changing no antibiotic behaviour at all, and its authors concluded that "additional
clinician education and antimicrobial stewardship interventions might be needed." **This guideline is what that
conclusion looks like when someone acts on it**: not more information for the clinician, but a default that
proceeds without them. **The two documents are arguing the same point from opposite ends — availability of a
good option is not uptake of it.**

**The limitation to state plainly: the recommendations themselves are behind the publisher**, so the eight
recommendations and their individual grades are not readable here. **The abstract gives the counts and the
distribution, which is more than most guideline abstracts do**, and the domains are named — initiation and
selection of pharmacotherapy, inpatient counselling, post-discharge counselling. **What it does not give is
which four got the moderate grades**, and that is the thing a hospital would want first.

[Read the guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2026.08.038)

## 2. Transplant vaccination, first update since 2019 — and live vaccines are moving

**Society · Published:** **American Society of Transplantation, Infectious Diseases Community of Practice**,
*American Journal of Transplantation*, **22 August 2026** — **fifty-three days old**, 0 citations, typed
`Practice Guideline`.

> These updated guidelines … review **safe living practices, including routine vaccinations and travel
> vaccinations, general travel advice, as well as nonpharmacologic strategies for safe living.** … Vaccination
> status should be reviewed and updated **in the pretransplant setting** and when travel is planned. **The
> optimal timing of vaccination posttransplant should be taken into account. There is accumulating data that
> live-attenuated vaccines can also be given to select posttransplant pediatric and young adult patients.**
> **Since the last update of the guidelines in 2019, several new vaccines have been recommended, such as those
> for coronavirus disease 2019 and respiratory syncytial virus.** In addition, **newer formulations of
> pneumococcal, meningococcal, and hepatitis B vaccinations are available. There are expanded age indications
> for the recombinant zoster vaccine. Close contacts of transplant patients can receive most routine live and
> inactivated vaccines.** For travel, location, duration, and activity during the trip determine the vaccine
> requirements as well as the need for malaria chemoprophylaxis. Other strategies for safe living include
> considerations for **food, water, pets, and sexual activity.**

**Interpretation.** **One sentence in this abstract is a change of principle and the rest is a change of
catalogue.** The principle is this: **"accumulating data that live-attenuated vaccines can also be given to
select posttransplant pediatric and young adult patients."** Live-attenuated vaccination after solid organ
transplant has been close to a categorical contraindication, and the word "select" is doing careful work — but
a guideline moving from *no* to *some, in defined patients* is the kind of change that alters practice, and it
is stated as a direction of travel rather than a recommendation. **This is the sentence to chase into the full
text**, because the clinical question is exactly which patients and how long after.

**The catalogue changes are substantial but unsurprising**, and they are the seven-year gap showing: COVID-19
and RSV vaccines did not exist as recommendations in 2019, and the pneumococcal, meningococcal and hepatitis B
formulations and the zoster age indications have all moved since. **A document that updates on a seven-year
cycle in a field that moves on a two-year cycle will always read partly as a catch-up**, and the useful part is
the principle, not the list.

**The quietly practical item is the one about close contacts.** "Close contacts of transplant patients can
receive most routine live and inactivated vaccines" answers a question that comes up constantly in clinic and
is usually answered from folklore. **It is also a household-level recommendation in a document aimed at
clinicians**, which puts it in the same category as the AHA stroke rehabilitation guideline's inclusion of
caregivers in its intended audience, reported [yesterday](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-13/).

**For this archive's transplant cluster, this is the fourth distinct society.** ISHLT supplied the
perioperative ECLS consensus statements and the lung-transplant frailty statement; ESOT supplied the chronic
lung allograft dysfunction guideline reported on
[9 October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-09/); AST now supplies the infection and vaccination side.
**The cluster is becoming a usable map of who writes what in transplant**, and the gap in it is still
anaesthetic management — nobody in this set writes about the intraoperative conduct of the transplant itself
except ISHLT, and only for lung.

**Limitation: no grades, no recommendation count, no methodology statement in the abstract.** This reads as a
narrative expert guideline rather than a GRADE-profiled one, and the abstract gives no way to tell how strong
any individual statement is. **That is not a criticism of the document** — much of what it covers (pets, food,
water, travel) has no trial evidence and never will — **but it does mean nothing here should be quoted with an
implied strength of recommendation.**

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajt.2026.07.033)

## 3. The Swiss C. difficile recommendations, and a flaw this archive found in its own test

**Society · Published:** **Swiss Society for Infectious Diseases**, *Swiss Medical Weekly*, **1 September
2026** — **forty-three days old**, 0 citations, typed `Practice Guideline`. A consensus statement by an
expert group appointed by the society.

> **The most recent Swiss Society for Infectious Diseases recommendations for <em>Clostridioides difficile</em>
> infection (CDI) in Switzerland were based on the 2018 American guidelines.** Since then, a large number of
> new studies have been published, and **updated international guidelines — both European and American — have
> introduced substantial innovations in CDI diagnosis and treatment.** … The objective of these national
> guidelines is to provide a **comprehensive, evidence-based and context-specific framework for the diagnosis,
> treatment, prevention and follow-up of CDI across all stages of disease.** By synthesising the latest
> clinical evidence, recent international guideline updates, and **national organisational and regulatory
> considerations**, these guidelines aim to harmonise clinical practice …

**Interpretation.** **That is the whole abstract, and it contains no clinical content whatsoever.** No
diagnostic algorithm, no testing strategy, no drug, no dose, no threshold, no recommendation count, no grading
system. **It describes the document's provenance and its intentions and stops.** The document is almost
certainly substantial — a national adaptation across diagnosis, treatment, prevention and follow-up — and
**none of it is readable from the deposit.** Stated plainly rather than paraphrased past: this archive cannot
tell you what the Swiss recommendations recommend.

**This is the second provenance-only abstract in the archive** — the first was the ESPEN practical ICU
nutrition guideline — **and it broke the test written for the first one.** That rule says: an abstract that
names no method and **no digit** is a provenance note. **This abstract contains digits — "2018" and "2026" —
and passes the check while containing nothing.** **The fix is one word: a digit that is not a year.** A
clinical abstract contains a count, a dose, a percentage or a confidence interval; a provenance note contains
dates. **The rule is now: test for a digit that is not a four-digit year, and test for `Methods` or `Results`
separately rather than as an alternative.**

**What is readable here is one genuinely interesting fact, and it is about guideline lineage rather than
C. difficile.** The previous Swiss recommendations **"were based on the 2018 American guidelines"** — stated
without embarrassment, as the normal thing for a small country to do. **So this document's purpose is to stop
being a copy**: it synthesises the newer European and American updates *plus* "national organisational and
regulatory considerations," which is the phrase that marks a national guideline as genuinely national — what
is licensed, what is reimbursed, what the laboratories can actually run. **That is a document class this
archive has now seen several times and should name: the national adaptation**, whose value is not new evidence
but the mapping of international evidence onto one country's formulary and laboratory capacity. **The Brazilian
pulmonary hypertension guideline reported yesterday is the same class**, and said so outright by extending a
recommendation to the drug classes "available in the country."

**The practical note for anyone who needs this document: it is in *Swiss Medical Weekly*, which is open
access**, so the full text should be reachable even though the deposit is empty. **That is the route to
take — the PDF, not the abstract.**

[Read the recommendations](https://doi.org/10.57187/5395)
