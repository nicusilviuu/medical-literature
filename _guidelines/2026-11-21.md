## 1. The test this archive logged twice, run and answered

**On 19 November this entry's predecessor wrote that two index readings whose query windows nearly abut
"are close to one reading", that "a stall is the most likely explanation and this run cannot demonstrate
it", and that "the test is simply a longer gap, which this run did not leave". Yesterday's brief logged the
same action again. Today the brief's readings were 132 seconds apart and said so. This watch ran the third
reading fifteen minutes later.**

| Reading | Whole database | `PMC` | 21 Nov bucket |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14:04:04 | 49,298,600 | 1,016,266 | 7,597 @ 14:05:23 |
| 14:06:16 | 49,298,598 | 1,016,264 | 7,597 @ 14:06:42 |
| **14:20:55** | **49,298,637** | **1,016,303** | **7,646 @ 14:21:28** |

**Across 132 seconds the whole database moved −2. Across the following 14 minutes 39 seconds it moved +39.
`MED`, `PPR`, `CTX` and every other source are byte-identical across all three readings, so `PMC` accounts
for the entire movement in both directions — the fourth exact single-source attribution in as many days.**

**The answer is unambiguous: the quiet readings were SHORT WINDOWS, not quiet periods.** The index is
continuously active at roughly **2.6 records per minute** on this measure, and a two-minute window cannot
resolve that. **Every within-run "nothing moved" observation taken across a window of a few minutes was
under-powered, and this archive flagged each of them as weak at the time, which was the right call.**

**And the 18 November reading is strengthened rather than undermined.** That pair was taken **thirty minutes
apart** and every one of eleven sources plus all five buckets were identical. **At today's rate thirty
minutes would predict about 78 records of movement. Zero across a window that long is a genuine stall**, and
today's measurement is what makes that claim quantitative rather than impressionistic. **Nothing from that
entry needs withdrawing; one of its claims is now better evidenced than when it was made.**

### A within-run subtraction bound, which avoids the instant-mixing problem entirely

**Yesterday's bound had to be reported as instant-mixed, because the whole-database gain came from one pass
and the bucket from another. Today both numbers come from the same two passes.**

**Between 14:06 and 14:21: the whole database grew by 39 and today's bucket grew by 49.** No source shed
records in that window — `PMC` rose and everything else held. **So at least 10 records were redated into
today's bucket inside fifteen minutes.**

**The residual slop is small and should be stated: the whole was read at 14:06:16 and 14:20:55 while the
bucket was read at 14:06:42 and 14:21:28, so the two windows are offset by about half a minute at each
end.** At 2.6 records per minute that is roughly ±2 records. **"At least 10" is therefore "at least 8 to 12",
and the useful part is that redating happens continuously at a measurable rate rather than in daily
batches.**

## 2. A French respiratory society's home ventilation guidelines

**[Evaluation of the efficacy and tolerance of home-based non-invasive ventilation: a review of the
literature and expert opinion — the 2023 guidelines of the GAVO2 group of the
SPLF](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmr.2026.10.003)** · *Revue des Maladies Respiratoires* · **published
18 November 2026**

> "Applying the Choosing Wisely® methodology, a task force mandated by the Ventilation and Oxygen-therapy
> group (GAVO2) of the French Pulmonary Society (SPLF) was commissioned to draw up a series of expert
> recommendations on practical aspects of long-term non-invasive ventilation (NIV). These guidelines are
> annually updated. Several themes were taken up, notably including NIV configuration, efficacy and
> tolerance... the experts developed a comprehensive analysis of recent insights, and went on to write out
> practical tips, which have been grouped together in a series of three articles, of which the first is
> dedicated to the monitoring of non-invasive ventilation."

**Interpretation.** The SPLF is not on this archive's tracked list, but long-term non-invasive ventilation
sits directly on the boundary of critical care, and **two things about the document's construction are more
interesting than its topic.**

**First, "these guidelines are annually updated".** Almost nothing this archive tracks is. ESAIC's index
runs from 2022 to 2026 with one or two documents a year; the ESPEN nutrition-ethics guideline reported on
18 November updated a 2016 predecessor after ten years; EACTS's own page listed nothing from 2026 as of
14 November. **An annually updated guideline is a different object from a decennial one — it trades the
authority of a long evidence review for currency, and it means the version number matters as much as the
title.** This article is the 2023 edition appearing in the index in late 2026, which is its own comment on
how well annual updating survives contact with publication.

**Second, the Choosing Wisely methodology is an unusual choice for a guideline and it shapes the output.**
Choosing Wisely is built to identify practices to stop rather than practices to start, and the abstract's
own description of the product is "practical tips" — **not graded recommendations, not statements with
strengths, and no consensus threshold or panel size given.** That is the fifth consecutive guidance document
in this archive to state none of the four.

**The bound is that this is the first of three articles and it covers monitoring.** The efficacy and
tolerance material the title promises is distributed across the series, and **this record describes the
series rather than reporting the recommendations.** 791 characters, which is a description and not a
summary. **Reported as identified with its method and cadence, not summarised.**

## 3. Two documents that deposit nothing, and what chasing them found

**Both of the other in-scope documents found this week have zero characters in the index, and the
title-stem route returned only themselves — two clean negatives, like the local-anaesthetic scoping review
on 17 November.**

### A Delphi consensus on frailty assessment, and the classifier's blind spot for the second time

**[Balancing Feasibility and Validity: A Delphi Consensus on Frailty Assessment Tools in Perioperative
Care](https://doi.org/10.1213/ane.0000000000008349)** · *Anesthesia & Analgesia* · **published 18 November
2026** · **zero characters deposited**

**Reported as identified and not summarised, because the record contains nothing to summarise and the rule
is to say an abstract is absent rather than infer a position from a title.** The title-stem search
(`frailty` + `Delphi` + `perioperative`) returns exactly one record: this one.

**Why it matters enough to report blind.** Frailty assessment is where this archive has repeatedly found the
decisive variable — the Clinical Frailty Scale appears in the cardiac surgery delirium consensus reported
yesterday as a recommended preoperative screen, the 14 November dichotomy finding showed that "CFS 5–8"
dilutes its own estimate because CFS 5 alone is null, and a frailty cohort in ventilated sepsis gave the
competing-outcome decomposition in which frailty leaves patients ventilated rather than killing them. **A
consensus specifically on which tool to use, and explicitly framed as a trade-off between feasibility and
validity, is the document that would settle which instrument those findings should be built on.** **Named
as a gap, with both routes attempted.**

**And the classifier called it OTHER — "no guidance signal" — for the second time on the same fault.** On
18 November the twelve-expert peri-anaesthetic delirium bundle, also a Delphi consensus study, was also
classified OTHER, and the cause was recorded then: **`SELF_GUIDANCE` recognises "consensus statement",
"consensus document" and "clinical consensus" and does not recognise a bare "Delphi consensus".** **Second
occurrence of a known false negative, in fresh data, three days apart.** It is on the December fix list as
item 3 and is **not fixed today**, for the standing reason that the deferred fixes are scored out-of-sample
at the audit. **What today adds is that this is the classifier's only recurring FALSE NEGATIVE, against six
recurring false positives — the two failure modes are not symmetric, and a false negative costs a document
while a false positive costs a read.**

### A NICE guidance review, and a journal series that is abstract-free by design

**[Guideline review: NICE guidance (2026) on switching from intravenous to oral antibiotics for suspected
early-onset neonatal sepsis](https://doi.org/10.1136/archdischild-2026-331149)** · *Archives of Disease in
Childhood: Education and Practice* · **published 20 November 2026** · **zero characters deposited**

**The topic is squarely relevant — intravenous-to-oral antibiotic switching in suspected sepsis is an
antimicrobial stewardship question with direct neonatal intensive care consequences — and the record
deposits nothing.**

**The title-stem route found no content for it but found a lineage, and the lineage turned out to be the
finding.** `10.1136/archdischild-2021-322822`, "Impact of the new NICE guidance 2021 on management of early
onset neonatal sepsis", also **zero characters**. Two editions five years apart, both empty, prompted a
check of the whole series:

> **`TITLE:"Guideline review" AND JOURNAL:"Archives of disease in childhood. Education and practice
> edition"` → 29 hits, 25 retrieved, and all 25 deposit ZERO characters.**

**Every retrieved article in this journal's guideline-review series is abstract-free in the index.** The
sample spans 2023 to 2026 and covers NICE meningitis, child maltreatment, paediatric tuberculosis, British
Thoracic Society asthma monitoring, NICE impetigo, RCPCH refugee health, BSACI penicillin allergy, NICE
paediatric type 2 diabetes, UK Kidney Association IgA vasculitis and the two neonatal sepsis editions.

**This generalises something this archive has only ever recorded per document.** A named gap has always been
a single paper whose abstract was missing. **This is a whole recurring series — plain-language reviews of
newly published national guidance, which is exactly the genre a guidelines watch most wants — that an
abstract-based route cannot see at all.** It is not an accident of one deposit; it is how the journal
publishes into the index.

**NEW RULE: when two documents from the same journal and series both deposit nothing, test the series before
naming either as an individual gap.** One query settled it here, and it converts twenty-nine invisible
documents from a sequence of future surprises into a known blind spot. **Named as a structural gap, with the
query recorded so it can be re-tested.**

## 4. Sweep and classifier notes

**Collected per day from the start**, applying yesterday's finding that cursorMark paging over a multi-day
window can drop a record: **sixteen collections over FIRST_IDATE 14 to 21 November, eight pubType and eight
title-word, every one matching its own hit count with shortfall zero.** PubType total 31 unique, title-word
545 unique, 550 merged.

**The pubType route's in-scope yield is 1 of 31, and the one is yesterday's lead** — the cardiac surgery
delirium consensus statement. **That is the fourth consecutive day of a very low but non-zero yield, and the
keep-the-route decision taken yesterday stands.**

**The classifier's running tally over four days of fresh data, recorded so the December audit scores
against it rather than re-discovering it:**

| Fault | Instances | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bare word in `SELF_GUIDANCE` (`standard`, `guideline(s)`, `statement`, `recommendations`) | **7** | false positive |
| bare "Delphi consensus" absent from `SELF_GUIDANCE` | **2** | **false negative** |
| `insights from` in `ABOUT` firing on a primary document's own subtitle | 1 | false negative |

**The seventh bare-word instance is today's `10.1136/archdischild-2026-331149`**, classified PRIMARY on the
word `Guideline` when the title literally begins "Guideline review:" — **a review of guidance, which is the
clearest possible SECONDARY.** The December list already carries the fix; **what is new is that "Guideline
review:" as a title prefix is a candidate for the `APPARATUS` stage rather than for `ABOUT`**, since it
behaves like "Comment on" — a series label at the start of a title announcing that the article is about
another document.
