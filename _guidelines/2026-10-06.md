**No new guidelines from the tracked French or European societies today.** The trailing sweep on
`SFAR`, `SPILF`, `SRLF`, `ESAIC`, `ESICM`, `EACTS` returned **zero** records each over 29 September to

What the week did produce is **four lung documents from two societies that have never appeared in this

## 1. The first Chinese society document in this archive — and it lands the day after the lung-biopsy trial

**Society · Published:** **Chinese Thoracic Society** (ILD Group) with the **Chinese Medical Association**
and the **Respiratory Physicians Branch of the Chinese Medical Doctor Association**, *Chinese Journal of
Tuberculosis and Respiratory Diseases*, **1 October 2026** — five days old. Chinese language with a full
English abstract, **pubType `Practice Guideline` + `Consensus Statement`**, not open access.
**Delphi method, anonymous voting, 15 recommendations each carrying an evidence level and a strength.**

> Interstitial lung disease (ILD) comprises a highly heterogeneous group of pulmonary disorders… **Lung
> biopsy is a key method for obtaining a histopathologic diagnosis, but unified standards are lacking for
> determining indications, selecting biopsy techniques, managing patients perioperatively, and integrating
> pathologic and clinical information.** … collaborating with national experts in respiratory medicine,
> radiology, pathology, **thoracic surgery**, and rheumatology… **It culminates in 15 recommendations, each
> explicitly annotated with the level of evidence and strength of recommendation.** … The main contents
> encompass: assessment of the necessity and feasibility of lung biopsy; **indication stratification
> strategies based on HRCT patterns; applicability scenarios and selection pathways for the four major lung
> biopsy techniques**; shared decision-making; **perioperative comprehensive management; management
> strategies for acute exacerbation of ILD**…

**The document names its gap in its own first paragraph: not whether to biopsy, but which technique and how
to manage the patient around it.** The four techniques it separates are **TBLB** (transbronchial forceps
biopsy), **TBLC** (transbronchial cryobiopsy), **SLB** (surgical lung biopsy) and **PTNB** (percutaneous
transthoracic needle biopsy), and the recommendations assign each a place rather than ranking them.

**Interpretation — and start with the one recommendation carrying level 1 evidence, because it is a
prohibition.** Of the fifteen recommendations, **exactly one rests on level 1 evidence, and it is
Recommendation 6**:

> **Lung biopsy is not recommended for patients with acute respiratory failure, severe pulmonary functional
> impairment, recent cardiovascular events, hemodynamic instability, significant pulmonary hypertension,
> severe coagulopathy, or end-stage systemic debilitation** (Level of evidence: 1, Recommendation: strong).

**The strongest evidence in the document supports not doing the procedure, and the patient it excludes is
the one an intensivist is looking at.** An ILD patient in acute respiratory failure is precisely the patient
in whom a tissue diagnosis feels most urgent, and this is the only statement in the consensus its authors
were willing to ground in level 1 evidence. **Worth holding onto as the single most transferable line in the
document**, whatever one thinks of the rest.

**The evidence profile, tabulated, because the shape is informative.**

| Level of evidence | Recommendations | Which parts of the pathway |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **1** (R6) | **Contraindications** |
| 2 | 7 (R4, R5, R9, R10, R11, R13, R15) | Technique selection, acute exacerbation, integrated diagnosis |
| 3 | 1 (R1) | Pre-biopsy multidisciplinary discussion |
| **4** | **6** (R2, R3, R7, R8, R12, R14) | **Indication, site selection, shared decision-making, reporting** |

**Thirteen of the fifteen are strong recommendations, and only two are conditional — but the two conditional
ones are not the weakest-evidence ones.** R5 is conditional on **level 2** evidence, while **five
recommendations stand as strong on level 4**: that a biopsy is indicated when non-invasive evaluation leaves
the diagnosis uncertain (R2), where in the lung to sample (R7), that TBLB is first-line for granulomatous
disease (R8), shared decision-making (R12), and pathology reporting standards (R14). **So the panel's
confidence tracks how indispensable a step is, not how well evidenced it is** — which is the same pattern
this archive has recorded across nine French GRADE-profile documents, and it is worth saying out loud
whenever a reader might mistake "strong" for "well evidenced."

**Two recommendations are directly anaesthetic, and one of them is unusually specific.** Recommendation 9 on
cryobiopsy:

> **TBLC is recommended as the preferred modality in expert centers**, particularly for patients who are poor
> candidates for or are averse to SLB. **The procedure should be standardized under general anesthesia with
> prophylactic balloon placement for hemorrhage control** and integrated with imaging or navigational
> guidance… (Level of evidence: 2, Recommendation: strong).

**General anaesthesia and a prophylactic occlusion balloon, written into the recommendation itself rather
than left to local practice.** The balloon is there because cryobiopsy's characteristic complication is
bleeding from a specimen larger than forceps can take, and the recommendation is effectively that **a centre
unwilling to do it under GA with bleeding control should not be doing cryobiopsy.** Recommendation 10 then
puts surgical biopsy by **VATS** second-line after a non-diagnostic cryobiopsy, with **at least two distinct
lobes** sampled and named preferred segments.

**And the recommendation that connects to today's literature brief is R11.**

> **PTNB is recommended for the diagnosis and differential diagnosis in ILD patients presenting with solitary
> nodules, masses, or focal consolidations, particularly to rule out malignancy. However, it is not
> recommended for the histopathological evaluation of diffuse fibrosing ILDs** (Level of evidence: 2,
> Recommendation: strong).

**That is the exact procedure randomised in the 2,485-patient trial reported in [today's
brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-06/)** — percutaneous needle biopsy of subpleural lung lesions,
where contrast-enhanced ultrasound guidance beat CT on yield and on complications. **The two documents
answer different halves of one question and were published two days apart by institutions in the same
country: the consensus says when a percutaneous needle biopsy is the right procedure, the trial says how to
guide the needle once you have decided.** Neither cites the other, and read together they are more useful
than either alone — the consensus restricts PTNB to focal lesions, which is precisely the population the
trial enrolled.

## 2. Two ISHLT lung-transplant practice guidelines the index can name but not describe

**Society · Published:** **ISHLT** (International Society for Heart and Lung Transplantation), *Journal of
Heart and Lung Transplantation*, three records: the **BLAD consensus document, 11 August 2026**; a
**perspective on it, 1 October 2026**; and the **frailty-assessment consensus statement, 1 October 2026.**
All three are indexed `Practice Guideline`. **None of the three has deposited an abstract.**

**Stated plainly: I cannot tell you what these documents recommend.** Europe PMC has title, authors, journal
and date for all three and **zero characters of abstract**. The DOIs resolve to Elsevier's link server,
which returns a 2.7 KB JavaScript stub; ScienceDirect returns **HTTP 403** to a request carrying a browser
user-agent. **Neither is in PubMed Central.** So this is an item recording that **three ISHLT practice
guidelines on lung transplantation now exist**, with their titles and nothing more:

- **Baseline Lung Allograft Dysfunction: Definition, Timing, and Implications for Outcomes — an ISHLT
  Consensus Document** (11 August 2026)
- **…— Perspective on the ISHLT Consensus Document** (1 October 2026), same 20-plus author list
- **Perspectives: Consensus Statement Addressing the Practical and Logistical Considerations for Performing
  Frailty Assessments in Lung Transplant Candidates** (1 October 2026)

**What can be said without the text.** **BLAD** — baseline lung allograft dysfunction — is the failure of a
transplanted lung to ever reach a normal functional baseline, as distinct from chronic rejection that
degrades a baseline once achieved. **A consensus document defining it, and defining its timing, is an
endpoint-definition exercise**, and this archive has repeatedly found that **what a field agrees to measure
determines what its trials can show.** A surrounding literature exists and is accessible — machine-learning
predictors, donor-specific antibody associations, a lower-limit-of-normal definition paper, molecular biopsy
features — **all of it published before the definition was agreed**, which is the usual and awkward ordering.

**The frailty statement is the more immediately anaesthetic of the two**, since frailty assessment in
transplant candidates is a pre-operative assessment question, and its title promises the part that is
usually missing: **"practical and logistical considerations for performing"** the assessment rather than
which instrument to use.

**One indexing trap worth recording, because I nearly walked into it.** The consensus document and the
perspective on it **share a title stem and differ only after the final em-dash** — *"an ISHLT Consensus
Document"* against *"Perspective on the ISHLT Consensus Document"* — carry the **same author list**, the
**same pubType**, and **no abstract on either.** A sweep that truncates titles for display shows them as a
duplicate deposit. **Read the whole title: on ISHLT documents the suffix after the dash is the document
class.**

[BLAD consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healun.2026.05.034) ·
[BLAD perspective](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healun.2026.05.031) ·
[Frailty assessment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healun.2026.05.035)

## 3. A French panel that ran a Delphi and published the fact that it failed

**Society · Published:** **the French heart-lung transplantation community** — 94 experts at **6 French HLTx
centres**, including pulmonologists, cardiologists, thoracic surgeons, cardiac surgeons and
**anaesthesiologists-intensivists** — *JHLT Open*, **9 April 2026**, six months old. **Open access**
(PMC13157112). Found in today's **SPLF seam audit**, the last French society on the audit list.
Chatron, Renaud-Picard, Epailly, Demant, Jougon, Boulate, Horeau-Langlard, Mercier, Fadel, Olland, Le Pavec

> Heart-lung transplantation (HLTx) is a life-saving intervention for patients with advanced cardiopulmonary
> disease. **Its use has declined significantly in recent decades, and the pulmonary indications for HLTx
> remain unclear.** … a **2-round modified Delphi survey sent to 94 experts at 6 French HLTx centers**…
> Items relevant to **9 key domains**… Both surveys had **57 items.** Consensus regarding each domain was
> evaluated by determining the **intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), with an ICC >0.8 indicating
> strong consensus.** In round 1, **40/94 experts responded.** The items were scored from 1 to 9: **11
> scored >7, 7 scored <3, and 39 had intermediate scores** and were reformulated for the second round. **The
> wide interquartile ranges indicated significant variability in responses, and for no domain was the ICC
> above 0.8.** In round 2, which was offered to all experts, **the response rate dropped to 25, precluding
> further statistical analysis.** **Key areas of disagreement included HLTx indications in severe PAH, right
> ventricular dysfunction, and congenital heart disease.** … **Rather than establishing prescriptive
> criteria, this expert review underscores the need for individualized, multidisciplinary, and
> center-specific decision-making**…

**Interpretation. This is a consensus exercise that reports, in its own abstract, that it did not reach
consensus — and it is more useful than a document that had.** Every number in it points the same way.
**No domain** out of nine cleared the pre-specified ICC threshold. Of 57 items, **39 landed in the
indeterminate middle** and only 18 (11 high, 7 low) separated — the arithmetic checks exactly: 11 + 7 + 39 =
57. And **round 2 drew 25 responses where round 1 drew 40**, which is not a statistical problem to be
adjusted for but **the panel declining to continue.**

**This archive has been tracking French panels that limit their own claims.** On [4
October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-04/) a peri-thrombectomy panel downgraded the class of
document it was writing because the evidence would not carry recommendations, and the correction appended to
that entry noted the SFMU/SFAR mild traumatic brain injury panel doing the same. **This is the end of that
spectrum: a panel that did not downgrade the document class but published the null result of the consensus
process itself.** Reporting an ICC below threshold for all nine domains, and a second-round collapse, is a
harder thing to publish than a cautious set of recommendations would have been.

**And the content of the disagreement is the finding.** The three named areas — **severe pulmonary arterial
hypertension, right ventricular dysfunction, and congenital heart disease** — are exactly the decisions where
the question "does this patient need a new heart as well as new lungs?" turns on whether a failing right
ventricle will recover after the lungs are replaced. **That is an unresolved physiological question, not a
documentation gap**, and a Delphi survey cannot resolve it. The paper says as much, pointing instead to
multicentre data collection and predictive models.

**One note for this archive's own gap list.** **Adult congenital heart disease anaesthesia** has been
carried as a named gap; **this is not that document**, but it is the first item here to record that
congenital heart disease is one of three areas where French transplant experts cannot agree on candidacy.
**The gap stays open**, now with a reason attached rather than just an absence.

[Read the Delphi survey](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhlto.2026.100561)

*Two route findings from that audit, both corrections to what was written here on 5 October. **SPLF's own
documents do not go to ACCPM** — they go to <em>Respiratory Medicine and Research</em> and
<em>Revue des Maladies Respiratoires</em>; SPLF appears in ACCPM only when it co-signs a critical-care
document. **And the same SPLF document can carry different pubTypes in its two language versions**: the 2022
lung transplantation update is `Practice Guideline` in its English deposit (<em>Resp Med Res</em>,
28 November 2022) and `Review` in its French one (<em>Rev Mal Respir</em>, 10 November 2022). **A pubType
filter would find one and miss the other**, and the two abstracts are not identical translations — the French
version carries a sentence about rising procedure numbers that the English one drops.*
