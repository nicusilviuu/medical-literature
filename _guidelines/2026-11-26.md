## 1. The document

The **Airway Division of the Societat Catalana d'Anestesiologia, Reanimació i Terapèutica del Dolor**,
updating its own previous version to take in evidence published since. `Practice Guideline` in the index,
first published 25 November 2026.

**The method is a two-stage one worth noting.** The main international guidelines on obstetric airway
management were identified, then **appraised with AGREE II and the highest-quality ones selected** — so the
starting point is other societies' guidance, filtered by instrument rather than by preference. On top of
that, **a systematic review of literature published between 2012 and 2025.** And where evidence was absent,
limited, indirect or of low quality, **recommendations were set by a Delphi consensus.**

**That is a defensible order of operations and it is also an admission:** a guideline that reaches for
Delphi wherever the evidence is thin, in a field as thin as obstetric airway management, is telling you how
much of it rests on expert opinion. The abstract does not say how many recommendations came from each route,
and that is the number this archive would want.

**What it covers**, as stated: airway assessment, preparation and management in pregnant women, including
**advance airway planning, optimisation of interdisciplinary communication, and clinical simulation as a
patient-safety strategy.**

**No thresholds, counts or algorithms appear in the abstract**, which runs to 1,332 characters, so the
architecture is reportable and the content is not. **The organisational emphasis is the notable part** —
planning, communication and simulation sit alongside the clinical recommendations rather than behind them,
the same shape this archive recorded on 30 September in the SFAR airway recommendations, where briefing and
debriefing cognitive aids and a patient-held difficult-airway certificate shipped with the clinical
algorithms.

## 2. Why the filter dropped it, and what that says about term lists

**On the tools alone, today was empty.** The pubType route returned 35 records over 17 to 26 November, the
scope filter kept 4, and the dedupe log recognised all 4 as already reported — the cardiac-surgery delirium
consensus, both ISHLT records, and yesterday's SEDAR paediatric document.

**Reading the 31 discards by eye found this guideline. Both routes of the filter had missed it, each for a
plain reason.**

**The term route: the word `airway` was not in the list.** `intubation` and `extubation` were. So were
`ventilation`, `sedation`, `analgesia` and `nerve block`. **The word that names the domain all of those
belong to was absent** — which is what a term list looks like when it has been written one incident at a
time, each entry earned by a specific record rather than by mapping the field.

**The journal route: `rev esp anestesiol reanim` was not in the guidance journal list.** The list carries
`anesthesiology` in English, which does not match `Anestesiol`.

**And here is the sharp part. Yesterday's SEDAR consensus document is in the same journal**, from the same
national society family, and it survived the same filter **only because its title happened to contain the
English word "anaesthesia"** — "best practices in paediatric anaesthesia in Spain". **Same journal, one
kept and one dropped, decided by which language a title used.** The filter was not working for that journal
yesterday either. It was lucky.

**Second find in three days from reading the discards.** On 24 November it was the ISHLT transplant-nursing
statement, journal absent from the list. On 25 November the discards were clean. Today it is a missing term
and the same missing-journal fault again. **The manual backstop has justified itself twice in three days,
and that is an argument for keeping it, not for relying on it.**

**CORRECTED 27 November.** This paragraph first read "third day in three that reading the discards has found
something" and "justified itself three times in three days" — **both wrong, and contradicted by the very
next sentence of the same paragraph, which says 25 November was clean.** Two finds in three days. The error
was arithmetic in a sentence whose own evidence was sitting beside it.

## 3. The fix, and the part of it that was measured and then withdrawn

**Added:** `airway` to the terms, and `rev esp anestesiol reanim` to the guidance journals.

**Added, measured, and removed:** `obstetric` and `obstetrics`. With them in, the 35 guidance records
yielded **6** kept instead of 4 — the SCARTD guideline **and** the Asia Oceania Federation of Obstetrics and
Gynaecology statement on **optimising caesarean delivery rates**, which is obstetric practice and
appropriateness, not anaesthesia or critical care. **A false positive.**

**And the broader terms recovered nothing that `airway` and the journal did not already recover**, because
the SCARTD title contains the word airway. **So they bought one false positive and no additional recall, and
they came back out.** Obstetric anaesthesia continues to reach this archive through `anaesthesia`,
`analgesia`, `airway` and the anaesthesiology journals, which is the right width — the ASA statements on
neuraxial analgesia in obstetrics, on remifentanil for labour analgesia and on epidural catheters for
caesarean delivery all arrived that way.

**Also added, for the same reason the journal was:** the Spanish and Catalan stems `anestesiologia`,
`anestesiologa`, `anestesia` and `reanimacion`, so that a title in either language does not depend on
carrying an English word.

**Three checks on the change, all run:**

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Recall test, both journal lists | **6 of 6** known true positives retained |
| Guidance records kept, today's 35 | 4 before, **5** after — the one addition is the SCARTD guideline |
| Brief-sweep scope, today's 625 records | **623 before, 623 after — unchanged** |

**That last row is the one that bounds the risk.** `TERMS` is shared between the guidance and brief routes,
so widening it could have flooded the brief side. On today's sweep it admitted nothing there at all.

**The `--alias` gate passed the new item**, which resolves to a single Europe PMC record, `43085577`, not in
the log.

## 4. A new classifier fault, recorded with its example

Run over today's five in-scope records, `classify_guidance.py` returned **five PRIMARY, nothing SECONDARY,
nothing OTHER** — every one through the guidance-pubType stage.

**One of those five is not primary guidance.** `10.1016/j.healun.2026.08.002` is
**"A Perspective on an ISHLT Expert Consensus Statement"** — a commentary about a guidance document, which
is this archive's definition of SECONDARY. **The classifier calls it PRIMARY because the publisher gave the
commentary a guidance pubType, and the pubType stage cannot see the difference.**

**That is a new entry for the fix list**, and it is the same record that produced yesterday's correction,
where I read the perspective as evidence of a gap rather than searching for its subject. **The record has
now caused two distinct faults: one in my reading and one in the classifier.**

**And today offers no out-of-sample test of the standing Delphi false negative**, which is the first item on
that fix list. The Delphi fault lives in the title route, and all five records reached PRIMARY through
pubType, so the title route never ran. **Worth stating, because a day on which the first stage classifies
everything correctly says nothing about the second.**

## 6. IACTS: readable, and carrying nothing

**The other standing action from 23 November.** The recorded address, `iacts.org.in`, **does not connect at
all** — curl returns HTTP 000, a failure to establish a connection rather than a 404. **`iacts.org`
redirects to `https://www.iacts.org/` and returns HTTP 200 with 222,134 bytes.**

**So the society is reachable. Its site carries no guidance index.** Scanning the page for guideline,
consensus, recommendation, position paper or statement returned **one** line, "Submission Guidelines", which
is an instruction to authors of its journal.

**That is a different state from unreachable and is recorded as such.** A society with no readable index was
previously indistinguishable from a society whose URL was wrong. For IACTS the URL was wrong **and** there
is nothing to read. **The action is closed with a negative finding rather than left open.**
