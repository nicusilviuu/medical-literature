**This is the largest structural gap this watch has found, and it is embarrassing in a specific way:
it was in the three societies the routine checks *first*.**

SFAR, SPILF and SRLF head the society list. In the whole history of this archive, **not one item has
ever been reported from any of them** — a search of every entry for `society: "SFAR"` returns nothing.
The standing explanation was a non-Anglophone blind spot: French societies publish in French and their
sites resist retrieval.

Half of that was true and the wrong half was load-bearing. Today's sweep found **three formalised
expert recommendations (Recommandations Formalisées d'Experts, RFE)** published by SFAR with six
partner societies between them, all three squarely in this project's primary scope, all three carrying
full summaries with complete GRADE breakdowns — and **none of them in Europe PMC at all.** The
mechanical reason is below. All three are reported here.

---

## 1. SFAR / CARORL / CARO / FHS / SFORL — Adult airway management in the operating room

**Published:** society date **3 September 2026**; the page went live **24 September 2026**. Type:
**Recommandations Formalisées d'Experts**, with a HAS (Haute Autorité de Santé) coordinator.
Coordinators Romain Deransy and Vincent Degos; partner societies CARORL (ENT anaesthesia), CARO
(obstetric anaesthesia), **FHS (Facteurs Humains en Santé — human factors in healthcare)** and SFORL
(French ENT society).

> **Objectif :** La Société Française d'Anesthésie et de Réanimation (SFAR), le Club
> d'Anesthésie-Réanimation en ORL (CARORL), le Collège d'Anesthésie-Réanimation en Obstétrique (CARO),
> le groupe Facteurs Humains en Santé (FHS) et la Société Française d'ORL (SFORL) se sont associés pour
> proposer un référentiel sur la prise en charge initiale des voies aériennes de l'adulte au bloc
> opératoire.
>
> **Résultats :** Le travail de synthèse des experts et l'application de la méthode GRADE ont abouti à
> **27 recommandations**. Après 2 tours de votes et plusieurs amendements, un accord fort a été obtenu
> pour 27 recommandations. Parmi ces recommandations, **1 a un niveau de preuve élevé (GRADE 1), 13 ont
> un niveau de preuve modéré à faible (GRADE 2) et 13 sont des avis d'experts.** Enfin, pour 1
> question, aucune recommandation n'a pu être formulée.

*(My translation of the results: 27 recommendations, strong agreement on all 27 — **1 at high level of
evidence, 13 moderate-to-low, 13 expert opinion**, and one question on which no recommendation could be
formulated.)*

The three fields, in the society's own table of contents:

- **CHAMP 1** — initial airway management where no difficulty is predicted
- **CHAMP 2** — initial management of the difficult airway, predicted or not
- **CHAMP 3** — **human factors and organisational dimensions**

**Interpretation.** Two things make this more than a national restatement of familiar airway doctrine.

**The first is that a third of the document is about human factors, with a human-factors society as a
formal co-author.** That is not how airway guidelines are usually built. It shows in what ships
alongside the recommendations: as well as the expected algorithms for predicted difficulty and for an
apnoeic strategy, and a cognitive aid for cricothyroidotomy, there are **cognitive aids for conducting
a briefing and a debriefing**, and a **"certificat de voies aériennes difficiles"** — a difficult
airway certificate to be given to the patient. The organisational and communication layer is treated as
part of airway management rather than as context around it.

**The second is the evidence profile, which is worth stating bluntly: 1 recommendation of 27 rests on
high-quality evidence, and 13 of 27 are expert opinion.** Airway management is among the most
consequential things anaesthetists do and among the least randomised. The panel says so in its own
grading rather than obscuring it, and it declined to answer one question altogether — [a practice this
archive has repeatedly noted as a marker of a serious
panel](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-21/).

Set against **DAS 2025** ([reported here on 21 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-21/)),
which produced 65 recommendations, this is a deliberately smaller document. The comparison of the two
national airway frameworks is worth a full read side by side; **what cannot be compared from the
summaries alone is whether they actually disagree anywhere**, and nothing here claims they do.

[Read the recommendations](https://sfar.org/prise-en-charge-des-voies-aeriennes-de-ladulte-au-bloc-operatoire/)

## 2. SFAR / SFMU / SRLF / CARO / ADARPEF / GFRUP / SFPC — Organisation of in-hospital life-threatening emergencies

**Published:** society date **3 September 2026**; the page went live **21 September 2026**. Type: RFE.
**Seven societies**, including **SRLF** — so this is the first SRLF-endorsed document in this archive as
well. Authors include Thomas Clavier, Alain Cariou, Julien Pottecher, Martin Cour, Antoine Kimmoun,
Jean-Baptiste Lascarrou and Xavier Monnet.

> **Objectif :** … harmoniser les pratiques, de structurer l'organisation de la réponse aux UVIH et de
> renforcer la coordination des équipes… ces recommandations visent à améliorer la qualité et la
> sécurité des soins, et à réduire la morbi-mortalité associée aux défaillances vitales survenant en
> établissements de santé. **Le groupe de travail s'est efforcé de produire un nombre minimal de
> recommandations afin de mettre en évidence les points forts à retenir** dans les deux champs
> prédéfinis. **Les règles de base des bonnes pratiques médicales universelles en anesthésie et
> réanimation étant considérées comme connues, elles ont été exclues de ces recommandations.**
>
> **Méthodes :** Quatre champs ont été définis : 1) Mise en place et procédure de déclenchement de la
> filière UVIH; 2) Conditions matérielles et ressources humaines; 3) Modalités de suivi et
> fonctionnement de la filière UVIH; 4) Spécificités organisationnelles… une recherche bibliographique
> extensive sur les 15 dernières années… selon les recommandations PRISMA. La qualité des données a été
> analysée selon la méthode GRADE… puis votées par tous les experts selon la méthode GRADE grid.
>
> **Résultats :** … **36 recommandations** concernant l'organisation de la prise en charge des Urgences
> Vitales Intrahospitalières. Après 2 tours de votes et plusieurs amendements, un accord fort a été
> obtenu pour 36 recommandations. Parmi ces recommandations, **4 ont un niveau de preuve faible (GRADE
> 2) et 28 sont des avis d'experts.** Enfin, **pour 4 questions, aucune recommandation n'a pu être
> formulée.**

*(My translation of the key sentence: of 36 recommendations, **4 rest on low-quality evidence and 28
are expert opinion** — and on 4 further questions no recommendation could be formulated at all.)*

**Interpretation.** **Not one recommendation in this document reaches high-quality evidence, and none
reaches GRADE 1.** That is the finding, and it is not a criticism of the panel — it is a description of
the field. How a hospital should organise its response to a deteriorating inpatient is one of the most
consequential system questions in acute care and one of the hardest to randomise, and after a
fifteen-year literature search across seven societies what emerges is **four low-certainty
recommendations and twenty-eight expert opinions.**

Two design choices deserve notice because they cut against the usual incentives. The panel
**deliberately minimised the number of recommendations** to make the important ones visible, and it
**explicitly excluded the universal basics of anaesthesia and critical care practice** on the grounds
that they are already known. Guideline documents more often expand to demonstrate thoroughness; this
one states that it contracted on purpose.

This sits directly alongside the **ERC 2025 "Systems Saving Lives"** section [reported here on 16
September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-16/) — the same problem of recognising and responding
to in-hospital deterioration, approached at national rather than European level, and reaching a
similarly thin evidence base.

**One arithmetic note.** The stated breakdown is 4 GRADE 2 plus 28 expert opinions, which is **32, not
the 36 recommendations stated in the same sentence**. The summary does not reconcile the difference and
I am not going to guess at it; the full text presumably does. The same check caught an unreconciled
total in [today's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-30/), which is why it is now run routinely.

[Read the recommendations](https://sfar.org/organisation-de-la-prise-en-charge-des-urgences-vitales-intra-hospitalieres/)

## 3. SFAR / SFCD / SFNCM / AFDN — Clinical nutrition in adult perioperative and critical care

**Published:** society date **3 September 2026**; the page went live **16 September 2026**. Type: RFE.
Coordinators Emmanuel Pardo and Émilie Occhiali, with SFNCM (francophone clinical nutrition and
metabolism), SFCD (digestive surgery) and AFDN (dietitian-nutritionists).

> **Objectif :** … proposer des recommandations destinées à améliorer la qualité et l'efficacité de
> **l'évaluation du statut nutritionnel et de la prescription du support nutritionnel** chez les
> patients adultes hospitalisés en soins critiques ou pris en charge au cours de la période
> périopératoire, et ce, à toutes les étapes du parcours de soins.
>
> **Conception :** … D'éventuels conflits d'intérêts ont été officiellement déclarés dès le début du
> processus d'élaboration des recommandations et **ce dernier a été conduit indépendamment de tout
> financement de l'industrie.**
>
> **Méthodes :** Deux champs ont été définis : 1) Périopératoire; 2) Soins critiques… une recherche
> bibliographique extensive sur les **17 dernières années**…
>
> **Résultats :** … **40 recommandations concernant 13 questions.** Après 4 tours de votes et plusieurs
> amendements, un accord fort a été obtenu pour **39** recommandations. Parmi ces recommandations,
> **11 ont un niveau de preuve élevé (GRADE 1), 9 ont un niveau de preuve faible (GRADE 2) et 16 sont
> des avis d'experts.** Pour 3 questions, aucune recommandation n'a pu être formulée (ABS). Enfin, 1
> question concerne un rappel à la réglementation.

*(My translation of the results: 40 recommendations across 13 questions, strong agreement on 39 of them
— **11 at high level of evidence**, 9 low, 16 expert opinion; 3 questions with no recommendation
possible; 1 item a reminder of regulatory requirements.)*

**Interpretation.** Read the three documents together and the most interesting thing about this one is
the contrast. **The same society, the same methodology, the same release: nutrition produced 11
high-certainty recommendations out of 40, in-hospital emergency organisation produced zero out of 36,
and airway management produced one out of 27.**

That is not a statement about which topic matters more. It is a map of where randomised evidence
exists. **Nutritional support is a drug-like intervention that can be allocated at random and measured
against hard endpoints, so it has trials. Airway rescue and hospital-wide emergency response cannot
practically be randomised, so they have expert consensus, however central they are.** Anyone reading
GRADE levels as a ranking of clinical importance has it backwards, and three documents released
together by one society make that unusually visible.

Two further features are worth recording. The panel states that **the process was conducted
independently of any industry funding** — notable in nutrition specifically, where the evidence base is
substantially industry-generated. And the composition reaches past physicians: **dietitian-nutritionists
are authors, not consultees**, and the reading group includes a patient-association representative and
the national federation of nursing assistants. This continues the multidisciplinary-convergence pattern
this archive has been tracking; **five dietitians appear among the authors of a document that
anaesthetists will be prescribing from.**

Strong agreement was reached on 39 of 40 — so **one recommendation did not achieve strong agreement
even after four voting rounds**, and the summary does not say which. That is the one to look for in the
full text.

[Read the recommendations](https://sfar.org/nutrition-clinique-de-ladulte-en-contexte-perioperatoire-et-en-soins-critiques/)

## 4. AHA / ACC and seven co-signing societies — 2026 perioperative cardiovascular management for noncardiac surgery

**Published:** *Circulation*, **28 September 2026**. DOI 10.1161/cir.0000000000001472, PMID 42804570,
indexed as a **Practice Guideline**. First listed author Thompson A, under a "Writing Committee
Members" corporate byline.

**This entry reports a problem with the record rather than a summary of new recommendations, and the
distinction matters.**

The deposited abstract reads:

> **Aim** The "2026 AHA/ACC/ACS/ASNC/HRS/SCA/SCCT/SCMR/SVM Guideline for Perioperative Cardiovascular
> Management for Noncardiac Surgery" provides recommendations to guide clinicians in the perioperative
> cardiovascular evaluation and management of adult patients undergoing noncardiac surgery.
>
> **Methods** A comprehensive literature search was conducted from **August 2022 to March 2023**…
>
> **Structure** Recommendations from the **"2014 ACC/AHA Guideline"** … have been updated with new
> evidence consolidated to guide clinicians; clinicians should be advised **this guideline supersedes
> the previously published 2014 guideline.**

**That abstract is character-for-character identical to the abstract of the 2024 edition of the same
guideline (10.1161/cir.0000000000001285, 24 September 2024), except that "2024" has been changed to
"2026."** I retrieved both records in the same run and compared them; both abstracts are 1,214
characters long. So the 2026 document, as deposited, still reports a **literature search ending March
2023** and still says it supersedes the **2014** guideline — making no reference to the 2024 edition
that exists between them.

**What I can state and what I cannot.** Verified: a document dated 28 September 2026 exists in
*Circulation* with a new DOI and PMID, titled as a 2026 guideline and indexed as a Practice Guideline;
its deposited abstract is the 2024 text with the year swapped; a JACC co-publication appears to exist
(10.1016/j.jacc.2026.06.017) but is **not indexed in Europe PMC**, whereas the 2024 edition has a JACC
record, a Guideline-at-a-Glance, and a November 2024 correction. **Not established: whether this is a
substantively revised guideline whose abstract was carried over unchanged, a re-issue, or a duplicate
deposit.** The publisher's page returns **HTTP 403** and the journal text is not open, so I could not
read the document itself.

**Interpretation.** A full revision of a major perioperative guideline two years after the last one
would be fast but not impossible, and there is a plausible innocent explanation — that the writing
committee updated the document and nobody updated the abstract. But **a clinician who reads only the
abstract is told the evidence review stopped in March 2023 and that the 2024 guideline does not exist**,
and that is a real problem in a document whose entire function is to tell people what current practice
should be.

So this is reported as **found, in scope, and unresolved.** It is squarely in this project's primary
scope — perioperative cardiovascular management is the anaesthetist's daily business — and it is the
single most consequential guideline in today's sweep. **The recommendations themselves are not
summarised here because I could not read them, and the abstract cannot be quoted as evidence of what
changed.** It goes to the top of the backlog for a full-text attempt.

[Guideline record](https://doi.org/10.1161/cir.0000000000001472)

### Why the French gap existed, and the route that closes it

This is worth stating precisely, because the wrong diagnosis was doing real damage.

**None of the three RFEs above is in Europe PMC.** Searches on the official English titles given by the
society return **nothing** for the airway and nutrition documents, and for the emergencies document only
three unrelated records; and **SFAR's own English-language journal, *Anaesthesia Critical Care & Pain
Medicine*, deposited nothing at all for August and September 2026.** French-language journals in the field deposited nothing
for the whole of September. So the Europe PMC route — which finds almost everything else this watch
reports — **cannot find SFAR recommendations at the time of release, and no amount of query refinement
will change that.**

The society website is reachable, and the earlier conclusion that it was not came from the wrong tool:
**`WebFetch` on sfar.org returns HTTP 403, while `curl` with a browser User-Agent returns HTTP 200.**
The pages carry the full French summary, the complete GRADE breakdown, the table of contents,
the participating societies, the author list and the society's own publication date — everything needed
to report a guideline properly.

Also confirmed reachable by the same route, for the next runs: **SRLF** (recommendations at
`/recommandations-referentiels-epp`), **SPILF via infectiologie.com**, and **SFMU**. The domain
`spilf.fr` does not resolve.

**The general lesson is not about France.** A negative from one retrieval route was recorded as a
negative about the world, and it then justified not looking for two months. **The Europe PMC sweep
returning zero for the French societies today was a true result and a misleading one at the same
time** — the query was sound and the window was populated, and the guidelines existed anyway.

### Also found, not reported as items

***Best Practice Guidelines for the Execution of Intraoperative Neuromonitoring in Spine Deformity
Surgery*** — *Spine Deformity*, 28 September 2026, 10.1007/s43390-026-01559-9. A modified Delphi across
**16 multidisciplinary experts including anaesthesiologists**, 80% agreement threshold, reaching
consensus on **27 items for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis and 25 for high-risk deformity**, organised
into IONM Timeline Checklists. In scope and genuinely new; held for a fuller write-up rather than
squeezed in. Note it is indexed only as `Journal Article` — **the pubType field does not tag it as a
guideline**, so a pubType-filtered sweep would miss it. It updates a 2014 predecessor with a different
purpose: the 2014 document was a checklist for **responding to** monitoring changes, this one is for
**executing** monitoring. There is also a direct link to [today's
brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-30/): motor-evoked potential monitoring **requires avoiding
halogenated anaesthetics and neuromuscular blockade**, which is one of the clinical reasons intravenous
anaesthesia is mandated — and therefore one of the places where the carbon argument and the monitoring
requirement point the same way.

**A second structural gap, found today and not yet closed: this archive contains no stroke guidelines at
all.** A *Journal of Anesthesia* review (29 September) turned out to be a narrative review rather than a
guideline — so it is not reported here — but it revealed that a **2026 AHA/ASA Guideline for the Early
Management of Patients With Acute Ischemic Stroke** exists (*Stroke*, 26 January 2026,
10.1161/str.0000000000000513, Practice Guideline), replacing the 2018 guideline and its 2019 update,
with key changes to thrombolytic choice and eligibility, thrombectomy eligibility, hyperglycaemia and
dysphagia management, and **dedicated paediatric recommendations**. It carries **two separate
corrections** from July 2026. Also uncovered: the **2025 update to the ESO guideline on blood pressure
management in acute ischaemic stroke and intracerebral haemorrhage** (*European Stroke Journal*, 1 May
2026, in PMC with a PDF), whose eight clinical questions include a **high-certainty recommendation
against intensively lowering systolic pressure below 140 mmHg in the first 24 hours after successful
thrombectomy**. That is the strongest-graded recommendation against a pressure target this watch has
seen, and it lands in the same week as [the three null pressure-targeting studies in today's
brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-30/). Both go to the backlog, and **AHA/ASA, ESO and NCS are
added to the society sweep list.**

**Borderline, logged not pursued:** *Stroke*, 28 September — **"Prehospital Stroke Treatment Trials in
Conventional EMS Settings: An Expert Consensus Statement on Operational, Ethical, and Technological
Priorities"** (indexed as a Consensus Statement). It is a consensus on **trial design methodology**, not
on patient management, so it falls outside a clinical-guideline watch; noted because prehospital consent
models and randomisation strategies bear on resuscitation research this archive does report. CIRSE
Standards of Practice for endovascular management of acute lower limb ischaemia (29 September) and an
evidence-based guideline on paediatric empyema thoracis (*Indian Pediatrics*, 28 September, 3,824-char
abstract) are also logged as borderline.

**No double-report:** the **CDH haemodynamic management consensus** surfaced again in today's sweep with
a blank journal field, which made it look like a new journal publication. It is the **same Research
Square preprint** reported [yesterday](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-29/) — same DOI, source
`PPR`. Preprints show an empty journal title in sweep output, which is a way to mistake one for a new
article; the DOI check caught it.
