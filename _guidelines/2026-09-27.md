**No new guidelines from tracked societies today, and once again the sweeps cannot say more than that.**
Europe PMC holds **6 records for 24 September, 4 for the 25th, and none for the 26th or 27th** — day
five of the ingest stall, diagnosed [yesterday](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-27/) as a fault in the
aggregator's own pipeline rather than a publishing lull. **The guideline-titled sweep for 25–27
September and the French sweep for 24–27 September both returned zero, and both zeros are
uninformative**, because a sweep of an empty window returns zero regardless of what was published.

**So the backlog again — and today it repaid the visit.** All three documents below break the pattern
this archive has recorded all month: **their abstracts contain the actual recommendations**, with
numbers.

## 1. JBDS-IP: diabetes in metabolic-bariatric surgery

**Society · Published:** **Joint British Diabetes Societies for Inpatient Care**. *Diabetic Medicine*,
**16 March 2026** — **six months old.** Indexed as a **Practice Guideline**
Stephens, **Dhatariya**, Beamish, **Pournaras**, Miras, Raafat, Noble-Bell, Cadwallader Buckland,
**Mustafa**

Unlike almost every document in this archive's glycaemia seam, the abstract states the guidance:

> **Pre-operative glycaemic optimisation, targeting HbA1c <69 mmol/mol (<8.5%) where safe to do so**,
> prevention of hypoglycaemia throughout all phases of care, and **a framework for medication
> adjustments during the liver reduction diet (LRD)**, peri-operative and post-operative phases.

> **For type 2 diabetes, oral and non-insulin therapies such as metformin, DPP4 inhibitors and GLP-1
> based therapies may be continued during LRD, while sulfonylureas, meglitinides and SGLT2 inhibitors
> should be discontinued to reduce the risk of hypoglycaemia.**

> For those with type 2 diabetes on insulin, **doses should be reduced by 35%–50% during LRD**…
> **To prevent diabetic ketoacidosis in those with type 1 diabetes, insulin must never be stopped**…

**Interpretation.** The most immediately usable document in the entire glycaemia seam, and it contains
the seam's most interesting apparent contradiction.

**Start with what is genuinely new here: numbers.** An HbA1c target, a percentage insulin reduction, a
drug-by-drug list, and an absolute rule for type 1 diabetes. **Across five documents in this seam —
SPAQI on SGLT2 inhibitors, the *Anaesthesia* consensus on GLP-1/GIP and SGLT2 drugs, the international
inpatient technology position statement, SAMBA on ambulatory surgery, SPAQI on devices — not one
abstract carried a recommendation.** This one carries several. It is worth recording that the exception
is a **British inpatient-diabetes** document rather than an anaesthesia one.

**Now the contradiction, which turns out not to be one.** This guideline says **SGLT2 inhibitors should
be discontinued.** The [SPAQI consensus and the *Anaesthesia* multidisciplinary
consensus](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-20/) are both moving *away* from blanket
discontinuation, and the [Basel cohort](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-05/) found cardiac harm rising
with each day stopped. **So why is stopping right here?**

**Because of the liver reduction diet.** Bariatric patients spend the pre-operative weeks on a very low
calorie, low carbohydrate regimen — **a deliberately ketogenic state** — and then fast for surgery on
top of it. **An SGLT2 inhibitor plus sustained carbohydrate restriction is the textbook setup for
euglycaemic ketoacidosis**, and the risk is not comparable to a single pre-operative fast. **This is
the same drug, opposite advice, for a defensible reason — and it is a sixth variety of guideline
divergence for this archive's catalogue: context-dependent divergence that reads as conflict.** A
clinician who knows only the headline "don't stop SGLT2 inhibitors" would get this wrong.

**One point of care about the wording, offered as an observation rather than a criticism.** The abstract
groups SGLT2 inhibitors with sulfonylureas and meglitinides and gives the rationale for all three as
**"to reduce the risk of hypoglycaemia."** Sulfonylureas and meglitinides do cause hypoglycaemia; SGLT2
inhibitors characteristically do not, and their perioperative hazard is euglycaemic ketoacidosis —
which the same abstract invokes for type 1 diabetes. **This is most likely a compression in the
abstract rather than an error in the guideline**, but the stated reason and the real reason appear to
differ, and a reader should know that before quoting the rationale.

**Authorship, again.** **Dhatariya is an author of this, of the *Anaesthesia* GLP-1/GIP/SGLT2 consensus
found on 20 September, and of the international inpatient CGM position statement found on the 23rd** —
three documents in this seam. **Mustafa and Pournaras also appear in the *Anaesthesia* consensus.** The
concentration this archive began documenting on 21 September now spans nine documents.

[Read the guideline](https://doi.org/10.1111/dme.70281) · PMID 41840846

## 2. Brazilian joint position statement on perioperative hyperglycaemia

**Societies · Published:** **Brazilian Diabetes Society (SBD)**, with the **Brazilian Society of
Anesthesiology (SBA)** and the **Brazilian Association for the Study of Obesity and Metabolic Syndrome
(ABESO)**. *Diabetology & Metabolic Syndrome*, **27 February 2026** — **seven months old**
Marino, Negretto, Ribeiro, Momesso, Feitosa, Toyoshima, da Silva Junior, Vencio, Lauria, de Sá,
Malerbi, Valente, Leite and colleagues

> **Hospital hyperglycemia, whether or not diabetes has been previously diagnosed, increases the risk
> of postoperative complications, particularly infections, prolonged hospital stay, and in-hospital
> mortality.**

Method: developed by the SBD's **Department of Acute Complications and Inpatient Glycemic Control**
with SBA and ABESO representatives; key clinical questions guided a **narrative review** of MEDLINE,
including RCTs, meta-analyses and high-quality observational studies.

> The expert group produced **19 recommendations and 20 key notes** addressing **screening, glycaemic
> targets, management of oral and injectable agents, insulin strategies, and prevention of
> perioperative complications.**

**Interpretation.** Reported chiefly because of who co-signed it.

**An anaesthesia society is a named co-author.** That is the third such document this archive has found
— after the [SECTCV/SEDAR aortic arch consensus](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-19/) and the
[*Anaesthesia* multidisciplinary consensus](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-20/) — and it is
exactly the category this project exists to surface. **A perioperative glycaemia document written
without anaesthetists is guidance about a period anaesthetists control; one written with them is
something else.**

**The structure is the substance available: 19 recommendations and 20 "key notes."** That split is
worth noting — a key note is typically a practical caveat that does not rise to a recommendation, and
a document with more notes than recommendations is telling you its subject resists clean rules.
**Screening in patients "whether or not diabetes has been previously diagnosed" is the most
consequential of the listed domains**, because undiagnosed hyperglycaemia is invisible unless someone
looks, and the decision to look is a pre-assessment-clinic decision.

**The method is a narrative review rather than GRADE**, which is a real limitation and stated plainly.
Compared with the SPAQI consensus's modified Delphi plus systematic review, or the ITACTAIC consensus's
60 randomised trials, this is a weaker evidentiary base — and the honest reading is that it is a
national practice statement rather than an evidence synthesis.

*Access note: the abstract gives the domains and the counts. **The 19 recommendations themselves are
not in it.***

[Read the position statement](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13098-025-02060-5) · PMID 41761348

## 3. Tracheostomy decannulation, modelled as a process

**Published:** Interprofessional Latin-American panel across **Chile, Argentina, Mexico and Ecuador**.
*Medicina Intensiva*, **2 March 2026** — **seven months old.** Indexed as a **Consensus Statement** and
a **Multicenter Study**
Basoalto, Jalil, Guzman, de la Fuente, Damiani, Ibarra-Estrada, Gorordo-Delsol, Plotnikow, Falcon,
Martin, Munoz-Gama, Sepulveda and colleagues

> Tracheostomy decannulation is a critical step in patient recovery, **yet practices remain
> heterogeneous worldwide.**

Method: **three-round Delphi using the Process-Oriented Delphi Method**, with **75 healthcare
professionals** — **physicians, nurses, speech therapists, respiratory therapists and
physiotherapists** — of whom **55 completed all rounds**. Consensus threshold **≥80% agreement**, with
predefined stopping rules on response stability. Activities were **modelled using Business Process
Model and Notation (BPMN)**.

> The final consensus model comprised **15 main activities organized into seven subprocesses. Each
> activity was assigned to specific professional roles, with conditional decision nodes** integrated
> to account for clinical variability. **No consensus was reached on precise physiological thresholds
> (e.g. cough peak flow).** Procedures such as bronchoscopy were **incorporated conditionally**.

**Interpretation.** The most methodologically interesting document in today's entry, and the sentence
that matters is the one admitting failure.

**They reached consensus on who does what and when, and could not agree on the numbers.** Fifteen
activities, seven subprocesses, explicit role assignment, conditional decision nodes — **and no
agreement on cough peak flow or any other physiological threshold.** That is an unusually clean
separation: **process consensus is achievable where physiological consensus is not**, and the panel
published both results rather than manufacturing a threshold to complete the model.

**Contrast that directly with GPR-WEAN**, the ventilator-liberation framework
[covered on 22 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-22/), which took the opposite route —
a **0–140 composite score with five strata and prescribed ventilator-free time, piloted on five
patients.** **Two consensus groups facing the same kind of problem: one produced precise numbers on
almost no data, the other declined to produce numbers at all.** The second is the more trustworthy
document, and the less immediately usable one — which is the trade-off honestly made.

**Modelling a clinical pathway in BPMN is the other notable choice.** BPMN is a software and
business-process notation, and using it forces every decision node and every role assignment to be
explicit — you cannot leave "the team decides" as a step. **For decannulation that is precisely the
problem**: the question is rarely what the criteria are so much as **who is permitted to act on them**,
across physicians, nurses, speech and language therapists, respiratory therapists and
physiotherapists. An interprofessional panel of exactly those five groups building a role-explicit
model is addressing the real ambiguity.

**It is also the fourth document this month from a non-Anglophone regional body found only by targeted
search**, after SEDAR twice and ITACTAIC — the pattern recorded on 24 September. **Latin-American
critical care societies are now on the sweep.**
