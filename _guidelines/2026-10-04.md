**No new guidelines from tracked societies today.** The guideline-titled sweep across 3–4 October returned
**seven records in total, and not one of them was in scope** — the emptiest guideline window recorded here.
So this entry is built entirely from the backlog, and it does two things: it **closes the stroke gap from
both ends**, and it reports the sharpest instance yet of something this watch has spent a week tracking.

**The ANARLF seam audit was run for the first time today** — the society was discovered yesterday as a
co-signatory on the French neurotrauma guidelines — and it found item 1 immediately.

## 1. SFAR / ANARLF / SFNV / SFNR / GFHT — anaesthetic management of thrombectomy, and a panel that renamed its own document

**Published:** *Anaesthesia Critical Care & Pain Medicine*, **1 January 2023** — **nearly four years old**,
5 citations. **Found by the ANARLF seam audit, run today for the first time.** Fifteen experts from five
societies, GRADE, **conducted independently of any industry funding** with a conflict-of-interest policy
enforced throughout.
Quintard, Degos, Mazighi, Berge, Boussemart, Chabanne, Figueiredo, Geeraerts, Launey, Meuret, Olivot,
Pottecher, Rapido, Richard et al.

> **Purpose** To provide recommendations for the anaesthetic and peri-operative management for thrombectomy
> procedure in stroke patients.
>
> **Design** A consensus committee of 15 experts issued from the **French Society of Anaesthesia and
> Intensive Care Medicine (SFAR)**, the **Association of French-language Neuro-Anaesthetists (ANARLF)**, the
> **French Neuro-Vascular Society (SFNV)**, the **French Neuro-Radiology Society (SFNR)** and the **French
> Study Group on Haemostasis and Thrombosis (GFHT)**…
>
> **Methods** Four fields were defined: **(1) Peri-procedural management, (2) Prevention and management of
> secondary brain injuries, (3) Management of antiplatelet and anticoagulant treatments, (4) Post-procedural
> management and orientation of the patient.**
>
> **Results** The panel drew up **18 recommendations** regarding anaesthetic management of mechanical
> thrombectomy procedures. **Due to a lack of data in the literature allowing to conclude with high
> certainty on relevant clinical outcomes, the experts decided to formulate these guidelines as
> "Professional Practice Recommendations" (PPR) rather than "Formalized Expert Recommendations".** After two
> rounds of rating and several amendments, **a strong agreement was reached on 100% of the recommendations.
> No recommendation could be formulated for two questions.**

**Interpretation.** **This is the first document in this archive whose panel changed the class of thing it
was writing because the evidence would not support the stronger class.**

That deserves spelling out. This watch has spent a week cataloguing how French societies report evidence
quality — the proportion of recommendations resting on expert opinion has run from 40% to 88% across eight
documents. **Every one of those published under the "Recommandations Formalisées d'Experts" label and
disclosed the thinness in a GRADE table.** Here a panel reached the same conclusion about its evidence base
and responded differently: **it declined the RFE label altogether and issued "Professional Practice
Recommendations" instead.**

**That is a more honest signal than a GRADE breakdown, because it is visible on the cover.** A reader who
sees "Formalized Expert Recommendations" and does not read the methods section learns nothing about
certainty; a reader who sees that the panel chose a weaker instrument learns the essential fact
immediately. **It is also, as far as this archive can tell, rare** — and it is the strongest version of the
point made in [Friday's entry](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-02/) that the instrument should be
named before the content.

**The four fields are well chosen for an anaesthetic audience**, and field 3 is the one that makes this
document more than a restatement of neurological guidance: **management of antiplatelet and anticoagulant
treatments**, with the French haemostasis and thrombosis study group as a co-signatory. That is the
peri-procedural question an anaesthetist actually owns, and it sits directly against [the AHA statement on
antithrombotic undertreatment reported on 2 October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-02/) — one
document about the harms of too little anticoagulation, another about managing it around a procedure where
bleeding into an infarct is the hazard.

**Strong agreement on 100% of recommendations, with two questions unanswerable.** Note that this is the
"graded counts close on their own" pattern, with the unanswered questions reported separately — consistent
with [the counting convention worked out on 1 October](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-01/).

**One retrieval note that matters, because it caught a trap.** Searching this title returns **two records**:
the guideline, and an **Editorial** with the identical title (10.1016/j.accpm.2023.101293, September 2023,
zero-length abstract) — a journal companion piece. **The pubType field is the only thing distinguishing
them**, which is the fourth time in two weeks that has been true here.

[Read the recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2022.101188)

## 2. AHA / ASA — early management of acute ischaemic stroke, and two corrections that are not the same kind of thing

**Published:** *Stroke*, **26 January 2026** — eight months old, **72 citations.** Practice Guideline. Not
in PubMed Central. **This closes the stroke gap named on 30 September**, the other half of which was [the
ESO blood pressure update reported yesterday](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-03/).
Prabhakaran, Gonzalez, Zachrison, Adeoye, Alexandrov, Ansari, Chapman, Czap, Dumitrascu, Ishida, Jadhav et al.

> **Aim** … **replaces the "2018 Guidelines for the Early Management of Patients With AIS" and the 2019
> update**… advising management **from prehospital evaluation through acute treatment and early in-hospital
> management of complications and initiation of early secondary prevention measures.** The intended audience
> includes **prehospital care professionals, physicians, allied health professionals, and hospital
> administrators.**
>
> **Methods** … literature published since the last AIS guideline in 2018 and the 2019 update … **conducted
> between September and December 2024. Additional high impact studies and articles published through March
> 2025 were added later.**
>
> **Structure** … **Key updates include the incorporation of new evidence related to thrombolytic choice and
> eligibility, determination of eligibility for endovascular thrombectomy, and management of hyperglycemia
> and dysphagia; a focused consideration of the pediatric population; and modification of the approach to
> thrombolysis contraindications.** Although this guideline reflects significant advances, **it also
> highlights gaps in knowledge.**

**Interpretation.** **Worth contrasting with the perioperative guideline this watch has been unable to
resolve for five days.** That document's abstract reports a literature search ending **March 2023** and
claims to supersede a 2014 edition while a 2024 edition exists. **This one states its search window
precisely — September to December 2024, with high-impact additions through March 2025 — and names exactly
which editions it replaces.** Same publisher, same council structure, four months apart. The difference is
not resources; it is care in the abstract.

**Three of the named updates land in this archive's scope.** *Management of hyperglycaemia* connects to the
glycaemia seam closed here on 27 September. *Eligibility for endovascular thrombectomy* is the decision item
1 exists to support anaesthetically. And **a focused consideration of the paediatric population** is new in
a stroke guideline and matches the pattern seen repeatedly in the last fortnight — paediatric sections
appearing in adult documents, and resting on the thinnest evidence in them.

**The two corrections are the part worth care, and they are not the same kind of document.**

| Record | Date | Indexed as | Abstract |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Correction to: 2026 Guideline… | **27 July 2026** | **Published Erratum** | **none** |
| **"Proposed** Corrections to the 2026 Guideline…" | **31 July 2026** | **Review** | **none** |

**The first is an official erratum from the journal. The second is titled "Proposed Corrections" and is
indexed as a Review** — which means it is almost certainly **third-party correspondence arguing that the
guideline is wrong about something**, not a journal correction at all. Those are entirely different claims
on a reader's attention: one says the published text contained an error, the other says some authors think
it did.

**Neither deposits an abstract, so what was corrected and what was disputed cannot be established from
here.** That is the same wall this archive hit with [the ERC corrigenda in
September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-12/), and the same honest conclusion follows: **no
recommendation in this guideline is known to be affected, and none is known to be unaffected.** A guideline
with 72 citations, an erratum, and a published set of proposed corrections within six months is a document
to read in its current online version rather than from any single PDF.

[Read the guideline](https://doi.org/10.1161/str.0000000000000513)

## 3. SFMU / SRLF — initial assessment of respiratory distress in the emergency department

**Published:** *Annals of Intensive Care*, **20 January 2026** — eight months old, **open access in PubMed
Central with a PDF.** Thirty experts, GRADE with GRADE Grid voting, **twenty-year literature search**,
PRISMA, industry-independent. **The newest SRLF-family guideline, and the first item off that backlog since
1 October.**
Le Borgne, Thille, Guenezan, Aissaoui, Boureau, Bally, Balen, Basset, Bilbault, Boissier, Claessens,
Decavèle et al.

> **Three key areas were defined: (1) assessment of severity in respiratory distress; (2) initial assessment
> and triage in respiratory distress; (3) diagnostic approach for respiratory distress.** …
>
> **Results** … **13 clinical questions yielding 20 recommendations. For three of these questions, however,
> no recommendation could be issued due to insufficient evidence.** … **two were supported by high-quality
> evidence and resulted in a strong recommendation (GRADE 1); six were based on moderate-quality evidence
> and led to a conditional recommendation (GRADE 2); and twelve were based on expert opinion, reflecting a
> low level of evidence.**

**Interpretation.** **Two of twenty recommendations rest on high-quality evidence; twelve are expert
opinion.** Placed in the table this watch has been building, it sits in the middle:

| Document | Recommendations | High evidence | Expert opinion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [SFAR/SFMU procedural sedation](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-02/) | 33 | 1 (3.0%) | 29 (87.9%) |
| [SRLF ICU nutrition, children](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-01/) | 29 | 1 (3.4%) | 23 (79.3%) |
| [SFAR in-hospital emergencies](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) | 36 | 0 | 28 (77.8%) |
| **SFMU/SRLF ED respiratory distress** | **20** | **2 (10.0%)** | **12 (60.0%)** |
| [SRLF ICU nutrition, adults](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-01/) | 34 | 3 (8.8%) | 19 (55.9%) |
| [SFAR airway in theatre](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) | 27 | 1 (3.7%) | 13 (48.1%) |
| [SRLF/SFC cardiogenic shock](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-01/) | 41 | 7 (17.1%) | 17 (41.5%) |
| [SFAR perioperative + ICU nutrition](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-30/) | 40 | 11 (27.5%) | 16 (40.0%) |

**Nine documents now, and the ordering has held since it was first noticed: the more time-pressured and
unselected the clinical situation, the less randomised evidence exists.** Emergency-department triage of a
breathless patient sits where you would predict — better evidenced than emergency procedural sedation,
worse than elective perioperative nutrition.

**The three areas are all assessment rather than treatment**, which is unusual and is the document's real
contribution: severity grading, triage, diagnostic approach. **A guideline about how to decide what is wrong
and how sick the patient is, rather than what to give them.** That is the decision that governs everything
downstream in an emergency department, and it is almost never the subject of a guideline.

**The arithmetic closes exactly: 2 + 6 + 12 = 20**, with the three unanswerable questions reported
separately.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aicoj.2025.100005)

## 4. SRLF / SFMU — oxygen therapy in acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure

**Published:** *Annals of Intensive Care*, **5 September 2024** — **two years old**, and **the most cited
SRLF guideline found by the seam audit (37 citations).** Open access in PMC. From a consensus conference
held December 2023.
Helms, Catoire, Abensur Vuillaume, Bannelier, Douillet, Dupuis, Federici, Jezequel, Jozwiak, Kuteifan, Labro
et al.

> …a consensus conference on oxygen therapy in ARF **(excluding acute cardiogenic pulmonary oedema and
> hypercapnic exacerbation of chronic obstructive diseases)**…
>
> **Methods** A committee **without any conflict of interest with the subject** defined 7 generic questions…
> **Fifteen experts … proposed their own answers in a public session and answered questions from the jury (a
> panel of 16 critical-care and emergency medicine physicians, nurses and physiotherapists without any CoI)
> and the public. The jury then met alone for 48 h to write its recommendations.**
>
> **Results** The jury provided **22 statements answering 11 questions**: criteria for initiating oxygen
> therapy; **targets of oxygen saturation**; role of blood gas analysis; when to insert an arterial catheter;
> **standard oxygen, high-flow nasal cannula or CPAP**; indications for non-invasive ventilation; indications
> for invasive ventilation; **awake prone position**; role of physiotherapy; criteria for ICU admission; and
> **which oxygenation device should be preferred for patients for whom a do-not-intubate decision has been
> made.**

**Interpretation.** **Question 11 is the one to notice: which oxygenation device to prefer in a patient with
a do-not-intubate order.** That is a guideline putting a ceiling-of-care decision inside a technical
recommendation set, and it is rare. This archive has been following code-status questions since the
[11 September brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-11/) — the *Annals of Surgery* code-status cohort, the
DNR-and-regional-analgesia paper — and this is the first guideline encountered that asks the question
directly rather than leaving device selection in a DNI patient to improvisation.

**The methodology is the *conférence de consensus* again**, the design this watch described on 1 October: topic
experts argue in public, a **jury that is not composed of topic experts** cross-examines, and **that jury,
not the experts, retires for 48 hours to write the recommendations.** Worth adding one detail noticed only
on this document: **the jury of 16 includes nurses and physiotherapists**, and **question 9 asks about the
role of physiotherapy.** The people who would deliver part of the intervention are on the body that writes
the recommendation about it.

**Two scope limits are doing real work and should not be skipped.** The guideline **excludes acute cardiogenic
pulmonary oedema and hypercapnic COPD exacerbation** — which between them account for a large share of the
breathless patients an emergency department actually sees. **So items 3 and 4 are complementary but not
continuous: one covers assessing any breathless patient, the other covers oxygenating a subset.**

**Note also what the abstract does not give: any GRADE breakdown.** GRADE was applied — the methods say so —
but the 22 statements are not classified by certainty, so this document cannot be placed in the table above.
**That is the same scope-only limitation as [the SRLF renal replacement therapy
consensus](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-01/), and both are open access in PMC**, so both are
readable rather than lost.

[Read the guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13613-024-01367-2)

### The ANARLF seam audit, and a refinement to this watch's own deposit rule

**ANARLF was added to the sweep list yesterday**, having appeared only as a co-signatory on the French
neurotrauma guidelines. The audit run today returns **five records all-time**, four of them Practice
Guidelines, and the term is clean:

- **Anaesthetic and peri-operative management for thrombectomy** (2023) — **item 1 above.**
- **Neurosurgical management of the acute phase of TBI** (2025) — [sent
  yesterday](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-10-03/).
- **Management of severe traumatic brain injury, first 24 hours** (*ACCPM*, 27 December 2017, Practice
  Guideline, 1,643 chars) — **nine years old, so outside the recency preference, but it is the only document
  found anywhere that addresses the acute medical management of severe TBI.** It is therefore the most direct
  candidate for [the intracranial-pressure gap named in today's
  brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-04/). **Check for a newer edition before reporting it.**
- **Targeted temperature management in the ICU: guidelines from a French expert panel** (2017), **co-published
  in *ACCPM* and *Annals of Intensive Care*** — core resuscitation territory, never covered here, and old
  enough that a successor should be looked for first.

**A refinement to the rule written on 30 September, which was too strongly stated.** That note said SFAR's
documents are not deposited in Europe PMC and that the society website is the only route. **Item 1 is an
SFAR document, and it is in Europe PMC** — because it was published as a journal article in *Anaesthesia
Critical Care & Pain Medicine*. So is the 2017 severe-TBI guideline, and so is the targeted temperature
management panel.

**The correct rule is narrower: SFAR's *Recommandations Formalisées d'Experts*, published on sfar.org, are
not deposited; SFAR documents that go out through ACCPM are.** The 30 September observation that ACCPM
deposited nothing for August–September 2026 was true of that window and wrong as a general claim.
**Mechanism, not society, again** — the third time in five days that tightening has been needed, and the
same error each time: a true observation about a sample stated as a property of a category.
