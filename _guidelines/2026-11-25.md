## 1. The one new document

**SEDAR, the Spanish society of anaesthesiology and critical care, through its paediatric section**, with
the recommendations ratified afterwards by the society's board. `Practice Guideline` in the index,
first published 24 November 2026.

**The premise stated in the document:** paediatric anaesthesia carries a higher risk of critical adverse
events, **especially under three years of age and in children with comorbidities**, and variability persists
in Spain across team training, resource availability and care pathways, which the document says produces
inequity in safety and quality. **Method: narrative review of the evidence and consensus among the expert
group**, not a GRADE exercise, and the document does not claim otherwise.

**What it actually specifies**, which is the part worth having:

**Patient selection and centre suitability criteria keyed to three variables at once — age, case complexity
and ASA status.** That is the structural recommendation: not that paediatric anaesthesia should be done
well, but that which centre may do which case follows from the three together.

**Standards for professional competence**, and **minimum requirements for staffing and material resources.**

**And separate recommendations for four settings: the operating theatre, locations remote from the operating
theatre, the post-anaesthesia recovery unit, and critical care units.** The remote-from-theatre case is the
one that usually falls through national standards, because imaging and endoscopy suites are where paediatric
anaesthesia happens furthest from help.

**No numbers are given in the abstract** — no ratios, no age thresholds, no ASA cut-offs — so what is
reportable here is the architecture of the document and not its content. **A centre-suitability rule without
the thresholds is a framework, and it is reported as one.**

**One note as printed: the abstract is bilingual by accident.** Its first sentence is in English and the
remainder in Spanish, and the Spanish begins `En Spain persiste variabilidad`, mixing the two languages
inside a phrase. Reproduced as published.

## 2. Correction: an absence claimed more broadly than the search behind it

**Yesterday's entry said, of the ESICM guideline on circulatory shock and haemodynamic monitoring, that
"the phrase circulatory shock returns nothing anywhere in this archive." That is false.** It appears in
`_briefs/2026-08-24.md`, describing a trial cohort of 1,010 adult ICU patients with circulatory shock.

**The cause is exactly the shape of yesterday's other correction.** The search I ran covered `_guidelines/`
and `claude/*.md`. **It did not cover `_briefs/`** — and the sentence I wrote claimed the whole archive.
A claim stated wider than the check that produced it.

**The conclusion survives, and was re-verified properly today** across `_guidelines/`, `_briefs/` and
`claude/*.md` together, with seven strings rather than one: `circulatory shock`, `choc circulatoire`,
`haemodynamic monitoring`, `hemodynamic monitoring`, `1971-2012`, `51(11)` and `shock and haemodynamic`.
**The ESICM guideline is not held.** The 24 August match is an unrelated research cohort. **So the backlog
entry stands and the sentence that justified it did not.**

## 3. Correction: the ISHLT statement is held, and yesterday called it a gap

**This one is worse, because it invented an action.** Yesterday's entry reported
`10.1016/j.healun.2026.08.002` as secondary — "A Perspective on an ISHLT Expert Consensus Statement" on
engagement of social support systems — and said it "names an underlying ISHLT Expert Consensus Statement
that this archive does not hold", adding the hunt for it as the first new action of the day.

**The archive holds it.** `_guidelines/2026-10-10.md` carries it as a full item, titled **"Engagement of
Social Support Systems in Patients Being Considered for and Following Advanced Heart and Lung Surgical
Therapies: An ISHLT Expert Consensus Statement"**, published 10 October 2026, with its abstract quoted on
the page and its listing-criterion implication discussed. The 9 October entry had tracked it as pending the
day before.

**Why nothing caught it.** The dedupe log was right: the perspective is a different work with a different
DOI, so it genuinely was unreported. **The error was mine and it was an omission, not a tool failure. I
searched for the commentary and never searched for its subject.** One grep of the statement's own title
would have settled it.

**The action is struck, not carried.** And the rule it earns, which also covers section 2:

**An absence claim must name the paths it searched.** If the sentence says "anywhere in this archive", the
search has to have been run over the whole archive, and the page should say which paths those were.

**When a record is secondary — a perspective, commentary, editorial or correspondence about a guidance
document — search for the underlying document by its own title before calling it a gap.** A commentary is
evidence that a document exists, not evidence that it is missing here.

**Three days running, the same failure mode.** The 24 November brief announced an identifier fix that had
not been run against the record it was built for. Yesterday's watch claimed an absence from a partial
search, and inferred a second absence without searching at all. **In each case the work was sound and the
claim was wider than the work.**

## 4. A near-miss of the same kind, caught before publication

**While checking the French societies today, "urgence vitale" returned nothing in this archive** — and the
HAS-labelled SFAR guidance on intra-hospital vital emergencies, developed with SFMU and SRLF and posted
28 September 2026, is inside the coverage window, so an absence there would have been a real miss rather
than backlog.

**It is held.** `_guidelines/2026-09-30.md` reports it with its four fields, its GRADE grid voting and its
**36 recommendations** — filed under the acronym `UVIH` and the French expansion, not under the phrase I
searched for. **The archive's own vocabulary is a search hazard**, which is the third instance today of the
same thing and the reason section 3's rule is phrased around titles rather than topics.

## 5. The rest of the sweep

**The pubType route returned 30 records** over `FIRST_IDATE` 16 to 25 November. The scope filter, with
yesterday's journal additions in place, **kept 4.** Three were already reported: the cardiac-surgery
delirium consensus of 20 November, and both ISHLT records from yesterday. **One was new**, and is section 1.

**All 26 discards were read by eye**, which is now daily practice after that habit found yesterday's ISHLT
statement. **All 26 are genuinely out of scope** — urology (nine French AFU cancer guidelines in one batch),
nephrology, dermatology, endocrinology, neurology, gastroenterology, radiology appropriateness criteria,
WHO malaria guidelines, immunisation schedules and two Chinese internal-medicine guidelines.

**One borderline record is now named rather than discarded silently for a third day.** `10.1159/000550985`,
**Paediatric Long-Term Home Respiratory Support: Recommendations of the Swiss Society of Paediatric
Pulmonology**, has appeared in the discard pile on two consecutive days. **It is excluded deliberately:
long-term home respiratory support is paediatric pulmonology, and this archive's scope is anaesthesiology,
intensive care, cardiology and cardiothoracic surgery.** Recorded so that the decision is visible and can be
reversed by someone who disagrees, instead of being re-made invisibly each morning.

**The `--alias` gate passed the new item**, which resolves to a single Europe PMC record, `43078310`, not in
the log.

## 6. Societies, and four documents checked against the archive by name

**French societies: nothing new in the trailing window.** The searches surfaced only documents already held
or already recorded — the UVIH guidance of section 4, the adult airway management update, the SFAR and
SOFMER guidance on withholding and withdrawing life-sustaining treatment, and the May 2026 cardiogenic shock
guideline led by SRLF with the French Society of Cardiology. SPILF produced nothing in any search.

**ESC, EACTS, STS and ASA: nothing dated 24 or 25 November.** Four named documents came up in the search and
all four were checked over `_guidelines/`, `_briefs/` and `claude/*.md`:

**Already held:** the EACTS, STS and AATS guidelines on temporary mechanical circulatory support; the EACTS
Ross procedure statement; the ESC 2026 guidelines on cardiac rehabilitation and on cardiovascular disease
with chronic kidney disease; and the 2025 ESC and EACTS valvular heart disease guidelines.

**Not held, and dated today rather than assumed:** the **EACTS expert consensus statement on perioperative
myocardial infarction**, `10.1093/ejcts/ezad415`, pubType `Consensus Statement`, **first published
1 February 2024.** That is two and a half years before this archive began, so **pre-coverage backlog, not a
miss** — and it is dated here precisely because yesterday's two corrections came from calling things gaps
without checking.
