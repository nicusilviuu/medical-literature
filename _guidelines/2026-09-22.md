## 1–3. ERC corrects three of its 2025 guidelines, and the corrections cannot be read

**Society · Published:** European Resuscitation Council, with ESICM on the first. *Resuscitation*,
**all three on 21 September 2026**

Three corrigenda appeared on the same day, all to sections of the **ERC Guidelines 2025**
(*Resuscitation* 215, Supplement 1, 2025):

| Corrected document | Original article no. | Corrigendum DOI | Covered here? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ERC/ESICM Post-resuscitation Care** | 110809 | 10.1016/j.resuscitation.2026.111315 | [12 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-12/) |
| **Special Circumstances in Resuscitation** | 110753 | 10.1016/j.resuscitation.2026.111314 | [14 September](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-14/) |
| **First Aid** | 110752 | 10.1016/j.resuscitation.2026.111316 | Not covered — still on the outstanding list |

The corrigenda carry the original sections' full author groups — **Nolan, Sandroni, Cariou, Cronberg,
D'Arrigo, Haywood, Hoedemaekers, Lilja, Nikolaou, Olasveengen, Robba, Skrifvars, Swindell, Soar** and
others on post-resuscitation care; **Lott, Karageorgos, Abelairas-Gomez, Alfonzo, Bierens, Cantellow,
Debaty, Einav, Fischer, González-Salvado, Greif, Metelmann, Metelmann** and others on special
circumstances; **Djärv, Rogers, Semeraro, Brädde, Cassan, Cimpoesu, van Goor, Klaassen, Laermans,
Meyran, Singletary, Mellett-Smith, Thilakasiri** and others on first aid.

**And that is the entirety of what is establishable.** All three deposit **no abstract and no text**.
The Europe PMC record for each returns `isOpenAccess: N`, `inPMC: N`, `hasPDF: N`, and a single
full-text link marked **"Subscription required"**. **What was corrected in any of the three cannot be
determined from anything reachable.**

**Interpretation.** This is reported precisely because it cannot be reported properly, and the gap
matters more than usual.

**Two of the three corrected documents are ones this archive has written up.** The
[post-resuscitation care section](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-12/) and the
[special circumstances section](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-14/) were both covered from
their abstracts, which — as recorded at the time and in the source notes — **list topics rather than
recommendations**. So the position is this: **this archive reported the existence and scope of two
guideline sections that have since been corrected, without ever having been able to read their
recommendations, and now cannot read the corrections either.** Nothing reported here is known to be
wrong. Nothing is known to be right either. **That is the honest statement, and it is recorded on
both original entries as well as here.**

**A corrigendum to a resuscitation guideline is not a trivial document.** These sections carry
numbers that get acted on in an arrest — targets, thresholds, timings, drug doses. The ERC's
corrections may be typographical, or a mislabelled figure, or a recommendation that says the opposite
of what was intended. **Three appearing on the same day suggests a batch review rather than three
independent errors**, which makes a systematic typesetting or numbering problem more likely than a
clinical reversal — but that is an inference from the pattern, not something the record supports.

**The practical consequence is a standing item, not a conclusion:** anyone relying on the printed
2025 sections should check them against the corrigenda, and this archive will revisit both entries if
the text becomes reachable. **First Aid was never covered here**, so no entry is affected, but it
remains on the outstanding list and should now be read in its corrected form.

## 4. GPR-WEAN — a consensus framework for ventilator liberation after tracheostomy

**Published:** *Australian Critical Care*, **21 September 2026** — **new this week**. An
interprofessional ICU expert panel; the authors are Colombian, and the document is not from a society
on this project's tracked list
Angarita-Camacho, Pinillos-Malagón, Rico-Barrera, Ruiz-Ávila, Hernández-Álvarez, Pérez-Cely,
Guzmán-David

The problem it addresses, quoted:

> Liberation from mechanical ventilation in **tracheostomised** ICU patients is characterised by
> **substantial interprofessional variability and an absence of standardised, multidimensional
> frameworks** to prescribe the progression, duration, and intensity of ventilator liberation.
> **Existing weaning indices are largely limited to intubated populations** and do not operationalise
> functional and physiological assessments into actionable strategies for patients with tracheostomy.

Four stages: a **review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses** to identify candidate predictors; a
**modified Delphi with an interprofessional ICU expert panel, run separately for general critical care
and neurocritical populations**; panel refinement into a framework aligned to the **International
Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health**; and a **structured pilot implementation**.

> From **77 candidate predictors**, the Delphi achieved consensus on **14 core variables for each
> population**. These were integrated into the GPR-WEAN framework, **generating a cumulative score
> (0–140) that stratifies patients into five functional impairment levels and links each level to
> explicit prescriptions for ventilator-free time.**

> **Pilot implementation involving five patients** demonstrated high feasibility and **100% protocol
> adherence**, with **potentially reduced perceived interprofessional variability**.

**Interpretation.** A real gap, a sensible method, and a validation section that must not be read as
validation.

**The gap is genuine and under-served.** Weaning indices — the rapid shallow breathing index and its
successors — were developed in intubated patients, and the tracheostomised patient is a different
problem: longer timescale, different failure modes, and decisions made by physiotherapists, nurses
and intensivists who may not agree. Building the framework on the **ICF** rather than on respiratory
parameters alone is the right instinct, and the separate Delphi for **neurocritical** patients
acknowledges that airway protection and respiratory mechanics come apart in that group.

**The number to hold onto is five.** A 0–140 composite score, fourteen variables per population, five
strata, each mapped to a prescribed dose of ventilator-free time — **piloted in five patients**. One
hundred percent protocol adherence in five patients tells you the form can be filled in. It tells you
nothing about whether the score identifies who is ready. The authors are careful — they call it
feasibility, say *"potentially reduced perceived"* variability, and ask for prospective validation —
**but a scoring instrument with an explicit prescriptive output is the kind of document that gets
adopted on plausibility long before it is tested**, and the 0–140 score will outlive the caveats.

**It belongs to a thread that has run all week.** [Today's brief](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-22/)
carried a qualitative study showing that **VV-ECMO candidacy decisions are driven by clinician regret**
because utility-based reasoning is unavailable; the [PUMA extubation
guideline](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-08-28/) reframed extubation as an elective act with a
deferral option. **Three documents in four weeks, from three fields, all responding to the same
finding — that a high-stakes ICU decision is made inconsistently by the same team — and all three
proposing structure as the remedy.** GPR-WEAN is the most explicit and the least tested of them.
Whether structure actually reduces variability, rather than relocating it into the choice of
threshold, is the question none of the three answers.
