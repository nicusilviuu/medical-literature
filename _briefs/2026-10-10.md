## 1. Four doses, and acute kidney injury falls from 52% to 28%

**Journal · Published:** *JAMA*, **1 September 2026** — five and a half weeks old, 2 citations. Multicentre
double-blind placebo-controlled randomised trial, **2 academic and 5 non-academic hospitals in the
Netherlands**, enrolment June 2023 to January 2025. **NCT05590143.**
Oosterom-Eijmael, Hulst, de Oliveira, Niesten, Wietsma, Gerritse, Scohy, Rettig, Snellen, Voogd, Godfried,
de Boer, Wink, van der Werff and colleagues

> **Importance** **Two percent to 50% of patients** undergoing elective cardiac surgery experience acute
> kidney injury (AKI) postoperatively. **Medications to prevent AKI after elective cardiac surgery have not
> been identified.**
>
> **Intervention** Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either **dapagliflozin (10 mg orally; n = 392) or
> placebo** once daily (n = 392), **beginning on the day before surgery and continuing through the second
> postoperative day (total of 4 doses).**
>
> **Results** Of **784 participants enrolled, 778 (99%) completed follow-up** (median age 68 [61–74]; **76%
> male; 97% White**; median eGFR 80 [67–89]). **Compared with placebo, dapagliflozin reduced the incidence of
> AKI (28% vs 52%; relative risk, 0.54 [95% CI, 0.45–0.65]; P < .001)** over 7-day follow-up. … **The
> incidence of atrial fibrillation was 45% (176/392) in the dapagliflozin group vs 45% (176/392) in the
> placebo group**, and the incidence of reoperation was 11% (43/392) vs 10% (39/392).

**Interpretation. A 24-percentage-point absolute reduction from four doses of a cheap oral drug is a very
large result, and the first thing to interrogate is the 52%.** The trial's own opening sentence puts the
literature range at **2% to 50%** — **so the placebo arm sits at the very top of it, slightly above it.**
That is not a flaw, but it tells you what the outcome is: **KDIGO AKI at its most inclusive, triggered by a
creatinine rise of 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours or urine output under 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12 hours.** At that
sensitivity a large fraction of events are small, transient biochemical changes, and a trial with a 52%
event rate has the statistical power to show almost anything — **which is how an NNT of about 4 appears in a
field where nothing has previously worked.**

**The mechanism cuts both ways and the abstract lets you see neither.** SGLT2 inhibitors produce an osmotic
diuresis, which bears directly on the urine-output arm of the definition; they also cause a well-described
early dip in measured renal function, **which would bias this trial against its own drug.** Without the
component breakdown — how many events were creatinine-defined and how many urine-output-defined — **it is
not possible to tell whether this is renal protection or a diuretic moving a diagnostic threshold.** That
breakdown is the single most important thing in the full paper.

**And no clinical outcome is reported in the abstract at all.** No dialysis, no mortality, no length of stay,
no renal function at discharge. **For a 24-point difference in a surrogate, the question of whether anything
followed is the whole question**, and this archive's surrogate-endpoint thread says the answer is often
nothing. **The honest summary is a large, clean effect on a sensitive biochemical definition, with the
clinical consequence unstated.**

**Two smaller observations.** **Atrial fibrillation was 176/392 in both arms — identically, to the patient**,
which is a coincidence worth noticing rather than suspecting. And **97% White, 76% male, Dutch**, with a
median eGFR of 80: **this is a trial in preserved renal function**, and the patients at highest AKI risk are
the ones with reduced baseline function.

[Read the trial](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.9268) — paywalled beyond the abstract.

## 2. All three vasopressors at once, and the tachyarrhythmia rate falls by fifty points

**Journal · Published:** *Critical Care*, **7 September 2026** — five weeks old. **Open access**
(PMC13633629). **Phase II**, explicitly **not powered for mortality.** **NCT06155812.**
Kalamar, Landoni, Gorenjak, Cestar, Fluher, Horvat, Jerenec, Kit, Marinšek, Puklavec, Svenšek, Radonić,
Šundalić, Brajković, Vujević and colleagues

> **Methods** We randomly assigned patients with septic shock receiving **≥ 0.15 µg/kg/min of norepinephrine**
> base to either intervention (**early multimodal vasopressor administration: angiotensin II, vasopressin,
> and norepinephrine**) or control (**classic stepwise** administration). **The primary outcome was change in
> plasma renin concentrations.**
>
> **Results** **79 patients** (intervention 38, control 41). Faster decline in **plasma renin** (2.61%/h vs
> 1.67%/h; p = 0.015) and **lactate** (1.53%/h vs 1.01%/h; p = 0.006) over 72 h. At 72 h, **SOFA was reduced
> by 3 points in the intervention group vs 0** (p = 0.018). **In the additional non-prespecified analyses**,
> noradrenaline-equivalent dose **decreased at a similar rate over 72 h (−5.41%/h vs −5.19%/h; p = 0.47)**,
> whereas **over the first 24 h it decreased significantly faster in the intervention group (−6.87%/h vs
> −3.03%/h; p < 0.0004).** **The incidence of tachyarrhythmias was significantly lower in the intervention
> group (15.6% vs 68.6%; p < 0.01).** ICU mortality **26.3% vs 41.5%** (RR 0.635; 0.33–1.21; p = 0.235) and
> 28-day mortality **36.8% vs 46.3%** (RR 0.795; 0.47–1.35; p = 0.494) were similar.

**Interpretation. The tachyarrhythmia difference is the largest effect in the trial and it is not the primary
outcome: 15.6% against 68.6%, a fifty-three-point gap.** Mechanistically it is exactly what the strategy
predicts — if you spare noradrenaline by adding two non-adrenergic vasopressors, you should see fewer
catecholamine-driven arrhythmias. **The control arm's 68.6% is strikingly high**, and in a 79-patient trial
that number is carrying a great deal of weight.

**And the denominators behind it do not appear to be the randomised arms.** Reconstructing: **15.6% matches
5 of 32 exactly (15.625%) and 68.6% matches 24 of 35 exactly (68.57%)** — not 38 and 41. **By contrast the
mortality percentages divide exactly into the randomised denominators**: 26.3% is 10/38, 41.5% is 17/41,
36.8% is 14/38, 46.3% is 19/41. **So roughly seven patients are missing from the arrhythmia analysis and the
abstract does not say why.** On the trial's most dramatic result, that matters, and the direction of any bias
cannot be determined from here.

**The noradrenaline-dose result is a window-selection problem the authors label honestly.** Over the full
72 hours the dose fell at the **same** rate in both arms (p = 0.47). Over the **first 24 hours** it fell much
faster with triple therapy (p < 0.0004). **Both come from "additional non-prespecified analyses" and the
abstract says so** — but the 24-hour figure is the one that will be quoted. **The defensible reading is that
early multimodal therapy front-loads the weaning of noradrenaline without changing the total**, which is a
narrower claim than vasopressor-sparing.

**The primary outcome is a rate of change in a biomarker, which is two steps from a patient.** Plasma renin
rises with angiotensin-system dysregulation in shock and falls as it resolves; a faster percentage decline
per hour is a plausible physiological marker and not an outcome. **The trial says this in its own conclusion
— "promising physiological effects… provide a rationale for a larger trial" — and it should be read exactly
that way.** The mortality numbers point the same direction (26.3% vs 41.5%) and **the interval, 0.33 to 1.21,
contains both a halving and a 20% increase**; quoting the point estimate without it would misrepresent a
79-patient phase II trial.

**One resource observation this archive should record.** The strategy requires **angiotensin II from the
start in every patient with moderate vasopressor requirement** — a drug priced far above noradrenaline and
vasopressin. **A phase II physiological signal is not a basis for that**, and the trial does not claim it is.
**What it does establish is that the comparison is worth funding**, with the tachyarrhythmia finding — denominators
permitting — as the most promising thing to power for.

[Read the trial](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-026-06323-z) — open access.

## 3. A trial stopped at 9% of its target, for money

**Journal · Published:** *Critical Care*, **3 September 2026** — five weeks old. **Open access**
(PMC13543343). The **X-COPD** trial.
Karagiannidis, Riera, Blanco-Schweizer, Strassmann, Merten, Brodie, Staudinger, Windisch, Brochard, Slutsky

> **Methods** Adults with acute hypercapnic respiratory failure due to AE-COPD requiring invasive mechanical
> ventilation **who failed or were ineligible for extubation within 24 hours** were randomized to **ECCO₂R or
> no ECCO₂R.** The primary endpoint was **a composite of death or severe disability at day 60.**
>
> **Results** **18 patients were randomized before the trial was terminated early by the sponsor for
> financial reasons (planned enrollment: 192).** The primary composite occurred in **0/8 ECCO₂R patients
> versus 3/9 evaluable** IMV patients (33%; risk difference −33%; 95% CI −65% to 6%; p = 0.21). **IMV
> duration was shorter in the ECCO₂R group (7.1 ± 2.0 vs 24.3 ± 21.4 days; median 7.0 vs 16.0; mean
> difference −17.2 days; p = 0.043)**, yielding, **in a post hoc exploratory analysis**, more **device-free
> days at day 29 (17 ± 4 vs 8 ± 7; p = 0.011).** **Ventilator-associated pneumonia occurred in 0 versus 3
> patients (37.5%)** … **Severe bleeding occurred in one ECCO₂R-treated patient (12.5%).**

**Interpretation. The headline fact is not a result, it is an ending: a device trial terminated by its
sponsor for financial reasons after 18 of 192 patients — 9% of target.** Not futility, not harm, not slow
recruitment. **So the evidence gap for extracorporeal CO₂ removal in COPD persists because the funding
stopped**, and this archive has catalogued several absences this fortnight — no outcome trial for
autoregulation-guided targeting, no analgosedation protocol study in ECMO patients across 27 years, no
successor to the 2016 severe-TBI guideline. **This one has a named cause.**

**The significant p-value should be treated with the dispersion it comes with.** IMV duration **24.3 ± 21.4
days** in the control arm — a standard deviation almost as large as the mean, in **nine patients.** One
patient ventilated for two months produces that mean and that p-value. **The medians (7.0 vs 16.0) are the
more honest comparison, and p = 0.043 on seventeen patients with that spread is fragile by any standard.**
The authors say as much: *"interpretation is limited by premature termination and the very small sample
size."*

**A denominator in the safety reporting does not match either.** **Ventilator-associated pneumonia "0 versus
3 patients (37.5%)"** — but 3 of the 9 evaluable control patients is **33.3%**, while **37.5% is 3 of 8.**
Either the VAP denominator is 8 rather than the stated 9, or the percentage was computed against the wrong
arm. **The severe-bleeding figure, 1 patient at 12.5%, is consistent with the ECCO₂R arm of 8** — so the
denominators in this abstract are of two kinds, and the VAP line is the one that does not reconcile.

**What survives is a direction and a mechanism.** Every secondary outcome points the same way, the mechanism
is not in doubt — remove CO₂ extracorporeally and a hypercapnic patient can be extubated sooner — and
**severe bleeding in 1 of 8 is the cost, which at 12.5% in a tiny sample is neither reassuring nor
alarming.** The author list includes the people who have run most of the field's trials. **The conclusion
asks for adequately powered multicentre trials, and the reason there isn't one is in the Results section.**

[Read the trial](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-026-06300-6) — open access.
