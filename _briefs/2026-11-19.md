## The scan that was overdue by six weeks

**Yesterday's entry added a rule: the Pages rendered-asterisk check applies to the whole archive, not only
to the day's page.** It was added because one brief from 9 October had been rendering two literal asterisks
for forty days. **The rule was run over every published page today, and the single case was not the
exception.**

**Twenty-one live pages carried rendered asterisks** — twelve briefs and nine guidelines entries, clustered
on 20 to 29 September and the first half of October, with counts of two, four and in one case eight.

**Seventeen of the twenty-one pass the source verification suite with no findings at all.** That is not a
failure of the suite; it is the suite's documented boundary, recorded on 6 October in the words "counting
characters in the source is not a rendering check". **What today establishes is how much that boundary was
hiding: the source suite ran on every one of those twenty-one files on the day it was written, passed
seventeen of them, and the pages were wrong anyway.**

### The cause on most of them was diagnosed six weeks ago and fixed for three days

**The 6 October entry recorded the defect exactly:** a method or footer paragraph is wrapped in single
asterisks for italics, a journal name inside it is written as `*Critical Care*`, and Kramdown reads the
nested opening asterisk as the closing delimiter of the outer italic — leaving a literal asterisk and
losing italics for the rest of the paragraph. That entry said: **"This was present on 4, 5 and 6 October and
I did not catch it on any of them... All three entries are fixed."**

**All three were fixed. Nothing else was.** The three days in view were repaired, a rule was written for
future entries, and **the fifteen earlier pages already carrying the defect were never looked at**, because
nothing ever looked at a page again after the day it was published.

**That is the generalisable failure, and it is not about asterisks.** A defect diagnosed on a given day gets
fixed on the pages that happen to be in view that day. **Unless something sweeps the back catalogue, the
diagnosis protects the future and leaves the past exactly as it was.** Every rendering rule this archive has
adopted — 6 October, 7 October, 8 October, 3 November, 14 November — has that same shape, and only this one
has now been swept.

### Two further causes, one of which the suite skips on purpose

**`_guidelines/2026-10-03.md` was not the footer defect at all.** Inside a blockquote, a bold span runs
across four wrapped lines, and the third of them begins `5. Penetrating traumatic brain injury` after the
quote marker. **Kramdown reads that as an ordered-list marker, ends the paragraph there, and leaves the bold
delimiters as text** — the identical mechanism to the defect found on 14 November, which produced the suite's
newest check.

**And the suite could not have caught it, because that check deliberately skips blockquotes.** It tests
paragraph blocks and returns early on any block whose first line begins with `>`. **The exclusion was
written to avoid false positives on quoted guideline text that legitimately contains numbered
recommendations, and it excluded the one place the defect was live.** Check extended today to look inside
blockquotes as well, and the remaining ordered-list flags across the archive were each checked against the
live page rather than edited on sight.

**`_briefs/2026-09-28.md` and `_briefs/2026-10-02.md` were a third variant:** `***Journal***` — bold-italic
— nested inside the single-asterisk footer, in one case closed with only `**`. The footer fixer skipped both
because it required the block to end in a lone asterisk and these end in `.***`.

**Which is worth recording against yesterday's own decision.** The `.***` check was withdrawn yesterday
after ten published entries were found to contain `.***` legitimately. **That withdrawal was right — the
pattern does not discriminate — and `.***` nevertheless appears in two of today's three non-footer defects.
A pattern can be useless as a test and still be present in real faults.**

### And a clean counter-case, which is why the live page stays the only ground truth

**Four pages still contain the nested-emphasis footer pattern in their source and render no asterisks at
all** — `_briefs/2026-08-29.md`, `_briefs/2026-09-30.md`, `_briefs/2026-10-01.md` and
`_guidelines/2026-09-20.md`. **They were left untouched.** The source pattern does not predict the defect in
either direction: it is absent from seventeen pages that are broken and present on four that are not.
**The rendered page is the only thing that settles it, and editing on the strength of a source pattern would
have changed four correct pages.** That is the same discipline that stopped the 14 October page being edited
yesterday.

**Twenty-one pages fixed, the suite re-run on each, and the whole-archive scan to be re-run after this
push.**

## 1. A GLP-1 safety question answered in the negative, by authors who say so four times

**[Perioperative pulmonary aspiration reports associated with GLP-1 receptor agonists: a pharmacovigilance
analysis of FAERS](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-026-06090-2)** · *Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's Archives of
Pharmacology* · **published 17 November 2026** · 41,308 perioperative reports, January 2016 to March 2024

> "Pulmonary aspiration showed non-significantly higher reporting odds among GLP-1 RA users (ROR 1.27,
> P = 0.56). In the Firth penalized adjusted analysis, GLP-1 RA exposure was associated with numerically
> higher, but statistically non-significant, adjusted reporting odds of pulmonary aspiration (aOR 2.08;
> P = 0.185). SGLT-2 inhibitor exposure showed numerically lower but statistically non-significant reporting
> odds of pulmonary aspiration than the background cohort (aOR 0.27; P = 0.059); this estimate rests on a
> single event and should not be interpreted as a protective effect. Concomitant opioid use was
> independently associated with aspiration (aOR 2.12; P < 0.001)."

**Interpretation.** This is the most-discussed perioperative safety question of the last two years, and the
answer here is that a disproportionality analysis of 41,308 reports cannot detect a signal. **The study is
worth reading precisely because it is null and refuses to dress that up.**

**Credit, stated plainly, because it is rare: the authors disarm their own most quotable number.** The
SGLT-2 estimate is aOR 0.27 at P = 0.059 — a near-significant apparent protective effect in the comparator
arm — **and the abstract itself says it "rests on a single event and should not be interpreted as a
protective effect".** An author who explains why their own borderline finding is uninterpretable has done
the reader's work for them. **This archive has spent weeks reconstructing that kind of caveat from the
arithmetic; here it is supplied.**

**The exposure is tiny and that governs everything.** 158 GLP-1 reports and 174 SGLT-2 reports out of
41,308 — **0.38% and 0.42% of the cohort.** A null result on 158 exposed reports is not evidence of no
effect; it is the absence of power, and the authors call the work "hypothesis-generating".

**One number is significant, and it is not the exposure of interest: concomitant opioid use, aOR 2.12,
P < 0.001.** That is the configuration this archive keeps finding — the adjustment variable carries the
signal while the exposure under study does not. **And it is the more actionable of the two, because opioid
co-administration in the perioperative period is a decision rather than a chronic prescription.**

**Two bounds the abstract states but does not fully draw out.**

**First, the cohort definition.** "Perioperative" was constructed by **restricting reports to those listing
a general anaesthetic or neuromuscular blocking agent as a surrogate marker.** So the denominator is
*reports that happened to name an anaesthetic drug*, not perioperative patients — and a report naming
aspiration is more likely to name the anaesthetic drugs involved than a report of an unrelated event is.
**That is a plausible route to over-counting aspiration in the surrogate cohort, and it works against the
null they found rather than for it.**

**Second, FAERS has no exposure denominator at all.** A reporting odds ratio is a ratio of reporting
patterns, not of risks, and no number in this paper is an incidence.

**What the abstract withholds: not one confidence interval.** Every estimate is a point value with a
p-value — ROR 1.27 (P = 0.56), aOR 2.08 (P = 0.185), aOR 0.27 (P = 0.059), aOR 2.12 (P < 0.001). **For
aOR 2.08 the reader cannot see the upper bound, which is the only quantity that would say whether a
clinically important effect has been excluded or merely not detected.** On a Firth penalised fit with
sparse events that interval would be wide, and by this archive's own standing exception list a Firth
penalised profile-likelihood interval is one of the six that should not be symmetry-checked — **which is a
reason to print it, not to omit it.**

**Where this sits in the archive.** Yesterday's guidelines watch recorded a *British Journal of Anaesthesia*
letter on GLP-1 agonists, delayed gastric emptying and standard preoperative fasting guidance as a named gap
with zero characters deposited. **This paper addresses the same question from the pharmacovigilance side and
returns nothing, which makes the inaccessible letter more rather than less worth recovering.**

## 2. A 3,709-patient resuscitation registry with no comparator and a conclusion that claims one

**[Five-Year Experience with Automated Head Up Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation for Out-of-Hospital Cardiac
Arrest](https://doi.org/10.1080/10903127.2026.2762131)** · *Prehospital Emergency Care* · **published
17 November 2026** · ten EMS agencies, 1.5 million people, nine US states, 2020 to 2024

> "Incidence of ROSC, HD, and HD-FN were 29.5%, 13.1%, and 10.5%, respectively. The incidence of HD-FN for
> patients presenting with a shockable rhythm meeting Utstein criteria, pulseless electrical activity, or
> asystole were 33.9%, 11.8%, and 2.0%, respectively. Among 391 neurologically favorable survivors, 91% had
> a Cerebral Performance Category score of 1... use of AHUP-CPR for the treatment of OHCA was associated
> with survival to HD of 13.1% and HD-FN of 10.5% in the overall cohort."

**Interpretation — the conclusion's verb is the problem.** "Was associated with" requires something to be
associated *with*, and **this is a single-arm registry.** There is no conventional-CPR comparator, no
matched control, no historical benchmark reported, and no adjusted contrast of any kind. A single-arm cohort
can report an incidence; it cannot report an association. **The stated basis for the intervention is animal
models, which the abstract names in its first sentence, and animal models are not a control group for a
human registry.**

**What is missing is not a subtlety — it is the one number that would let a reader judge the result.** The
conventional-CPR survival in these same ten EMS systems before implementation is not given, and no external
benchmark is quoted. **Without either, 10.5% neurologically favourable survival cannot be placed. It is not
stated here whether that is better than conventional care, because this paper does not contain the
information to say so, and no figure for conventional CPR is quoted here that was not read in this paper.**

**The intervention is defined by an interval the paper does not report.** The protocol is "immediate
conventional CPR followed by rapid transition to AHUP-CPR" — so **every patient received conventional CPR
first**, and the time to transition is the entire exposure of interest. **It appears nowhere.** The one
interval given is 9-1-1 call to professional CPR, 9.62 ± 5.78 minutes, which measures EMS response and not
the intervention. **A registry of an intervention should report when the intervention started.**

**The internal structure is sound and is where the value is.** The arithmetic reconciles: 391 favourable
survivors of 3,709 is 10.54%, matching the stated 10.5%. **And the rhythm stratification is a 17-fold
spread — 33.9% for witnessed shockable arrest, 11.8% for PEA, 2.0% for asystole.** That spread is the most
useful thing in the paper, because it means the headline 10.5% is almost entirely a statement about the
rhythm mix of these ten systems. **Change the proportion presenting in asystole and the headline moves
without anything clinical changing.**

**Two further readings.** **91% of favourable survivors had CPC 1**, so the favourable-outcome category here
is overwhelmingly normal or near-normal function rather than borderline — a genuinely reassuring internal
detail. And **the average age of survivors is 56.8 ± 16.3 years**, well below the age of the OHCA population
at large; age was among the four significant predictors, so the survivor group is selected on exactly the
covariate that predicts survival.

## 3. Thirty-seven biomarkers, twenty-nine events, and three AUROCs compared to two decimals

**[Preoperative Serum Biomarkers Improve Prediction of Poor Postoperative Outcome After Cardiopulmonary
Bypass Compared With Clinical Variables in Neonates](https://doi.org/10.1097/cce.0000000000001520)** ·
*Critical Care Explorations* · **published 17 November 2026** · 144 neonates, single centre

> "Biomarkers were available for 144 neonates... ICU-30 occurred in 29 neonates (20%). Using combined
> clinical and biomarker data, XGBoost demonstrated moderate discrimination for ICU-30 (AUROC, 0.74),
> outperformed RF (AUROC, 0.72), and LASSO (AUROC, 0.70). Biomarkers alone performed better (AUROC, 0.72)
> than clinical data alone (AUROC, 0.67) with XGBoost. The top two features in the combined XGBoost model
> were neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) and trefoil factor 3 (TFF3)."

**Interpretation.** **Thirty-seven biomarkers were measured and twenty-nine outcome events occurred.** That
is 0.78 events per candidate predictor. Conventional guidance would support roughly three candidate
predictors at this event count; the model selected its top twenty features from thirty-seven. **The paper is
not short of signal — it is short of events, and every number downstream inherits that.**

**No confidence interval is given on any AUROC, and with 29 events they would be wide.** A rough standard
error for an AUROC near 0.74 with 29 events against 115 non-events is on the order of 0.05, so a 95%
interval spans roughly 0.64 to 0.84. **Against that, the three model AUROCs being compared — 0.74, 0.72 and
0.70 — are indistinguishable, and so is the headline contrast of 0.72 against 0.67.** The word
"outperformed" is carrying an inference the sample cannot support.

**Credit where it is due: "moderate discrimination" is the authors' own phrase**, and the conclusion asks
for further investigation rather than claiming a usable tool. **The abstract's own hedging is better
calibrated than its comparative verbs.**

**The composite endpoint contains the mechanism of its own top predictor — the standing component-or-
container check.** ICU-30 is mortality within 30 days, **or ICU stay longer than 30 days**, or readmission
within 30 days. The leading feature is **NGAL, a marker of renal tubular injury.** Postoperative acute
kidney injury prolongs ICU stay. **So NGAL predicting ICU-30 is in part NGAL predicting a renal
complication that is itself one of the routes into the composite** — not circular, but not independent
either, and the abstract does not report how the composite's three components divided among the 29 events.
**Without that split, the reader cannot tell whether this is a mortality model or a length-of-stay model.**

**The feature set that emerges is coherent, which is the real contribution.** NGAL and TFF3 (renal and
gastrointestinal epithelial injury), ADAMTS13 and angiopoietin-2 (vascular injury and inflammation). **A
preoperative profile of gut and kidney injury and endothelial activation in neonates about to go on bypass
is a mechanistic finding worth following even if the discriminative claim does not survive its sample
size.** The authors say as much.

## 4. GPT-5 matches trained models on the flattering metric

**[Risk Stratification for 7-Day Mortality After ICU Admission Using General-Purpose Large Language Models
and Structured Electronic Health Records](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11428570/v1)** · **Research
Square preprint, posted 18 November 2026, not peer reviewed** · eICU-CRD, MIMIC-IV and a Korean K-MIMIC
subset

> "Among supervised models, the highest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) was
> 0.936 for XGBoost in the held-out eICU-CRD set, 0.858 for the multilayer perceptron in MIMIC-IV, and 0.957
> for XGBoost in K-MIMIC. In the corresponding 2,000-stay LLM evaluation samples, GPT-5 achieved AUROCs of
> 0.925, 0.822, and 0.926, respectively, but generally had a lower area under the precision-recall curve
> (AUPRC) and, at evaluated operating thresholds, lower specificity."

**Interpretation — the reporting asymmetry is the finding.** **AUROC is given for every model in every
cohort, to three decimal places. AUPRC is given as a direction: "generally lower".** For seven-day mortality
after ICU admission, the event is uncommon, and on a rare outcome AUROC is dominated by the easy negatives
while AUPRC is the metric that tracks whether the positive predictions are any good. **The paper reports the
flattering metric precisely and the discriminating metric qualitatively.** A reader cannot tell from this
abstract how much worse the AUPRC was, and that is the number on which "could an LLM substitute for a
trained model" turns.

**The lower specificity at evaluated thresholds points the same way** — an untrained model achieving
comparable ranking while flagging more patients is a different proposition from one that matches at
equivalent alarm rates.

**One number should not be read as generalisation.** The supervised models drop from 0.936 in the
development cohort to 0.858 in MIMIC-IV — a real external-validation penalty — **and then rise to 0.957 in
K-MIMIC, higher than development.** A validation cohort that scores above the development cohort has not
demonstrated transportability; it has demonstrated that it is an easier cohort. **K-MIMIC is 6,673 stays
from a single Korean tertiary hospital against 124,020 from 208 US hospitals, and a single-centre cohort is
systematically more homogeneous.** The honest summary of the external validation is the 0.858, not the
average of the three.

**And the LLM figures rest on 2,000 stays per cohort, not on the full cohorts** — which the abstract states
clearly, and which means the LLM arm has roughly a sixtieth of the eICU sample behind it.

**The reasoning-alignment analysis is the most interesting part and the most over-read.** Overlap with the
five most influential concepts was **precision 0.47 and recall 0.55** — about half. The abstract then
reports that mentioned concepts had greater mean patient-level importance than non-mentioned ones **in 98.1%
of patients (P < 0.001)**, which reads as strong corroboration and is a much weaker claim than it appears:
**it says the mentioned set outranks the unmentioned set on average, a bar that any model naming plausible
clinical variables clears.** The precision of 0.47 is the informative number and the 98.1% is the
comfortable one.

**The clinician rating has no agreement statistic.** 150 rationales, two raters, mean 4.03 ± 0.63 on a
five-point scale, **and in place of a kappa or an ICC the abstract offers "ratings within one point in 83%
of cases".** On a five-point scale, agreement within one point is a wide band — two raters choosing 3 and 4
are "within one point" — and with a mean of 4.03 and an SD of 0.63 most of the distribution sits inside a
single point of itself. **The 83% is close to what chance would give at that spread.**

**Credit the conclusion, which is appropriately narrow:** supervised models "generally achieved the highest
discrimination", and prospective evaluation of reasoning faithfulness is "required before clinical use".
**A preprint that could have led with "GPT-5 matches trained models" leads with the opposite.**

## 5. A bias analysis whose own falsification test failed, reported as having failed

**[Recording sampling rate as a negative-control exposure for detecting measurement-driven bias in
heart-rate-variability-outcome associations](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.15.26368154)** · **medRxiv
preprint, posted 17 November 2026, not peer reviewed** · MIMIC-IV and MIMIC-III waveform databases, PTB-XL
reference; 1,445 ICU stays with 141 in-hospital deaths

> "End-to-end reprocessing gave an RMSSD distortion coefficient of 1.1575 (95% CI 1.0835-1.2492)... Under
> the multiplicative model, an observed crude RMSSD-mortality odds ratio of 1.802 corresponded to a
> corrected interval of 1.8335-1.915 across the coefficient's 95% CI. The correction was small relative to
> confounding: the same association fell to 1.116 after age adjustment. Coarse sampling reclassified 33.70%
> of low-HRV recordings in MIMIC-IV and 20.72% in MIMIC-III... the age-adjusted HRV-mortality risk ratio did
> not increase at lower rates... all 95% CIs crossed 1. The falsification test was therefore negative in
> these cohorts, and we report it as such rather than as evidence that measurement bias is absent."

**Interpretation.** The design is clean: if coarse sampling inflates measured HRV, then databases recorded
at lower rates should show systematically different HRV-outcome associations, and that gradient becomes a
negative-control exposure. **The mechanism was confirmed — inflation was reproducible across both databases,
a median factor of 1.101 at 125 Hz and 1.270 at 250 Hz downsampled to 62.5 Hz. The gradient in the
association was not.**

**Four things make this the best-reported paper in today's five, and all four are refusals.**

**It reports its own negative falsification test as negative**, in those words, and explicitly declines to
read it as evidence that measurement bias is absent. **An under-powered negative test is uninformative in
both directions, and saying so is the correct and the unusual move.**

**It states that the bias it set out to quantify is dwarfed by the confounding it did not.** The crude odds
ratio of 1.802 corrects to a band of 1.8335 to 1.915 — a shift of roughly 0.1 — **while age adjustment alone
moves the same association from 1.802 to 1.116.** The measurement correction is about one-seventh the size
of a single confounder. **A methods paper that quantifies its own topic's importance as secondary has
subordinated its thesis to the evidence.**

**It identifies the larger bias as the one nobody models: dichotomisation.** 33.70% and 20.72% of low-HRV
recordings were reclassified above a 20 ms threshold by the sampling rate alone. **A third of a binary
exposure misclassified by a recording setting is a far bigger problem than a 16% multiplicative distortion
on a continuous scale**, and the recommendation that follows — report a sensitivity band *before*
dichotomising — is concrete and costs nothing.

**And Part I argues against the stronger design it could have claimed**, setting out why a strict
instrumental-variable analysis is inappropriate here rather than presenting the sampling rate as an
instrument.

**Two cautions.** The corrected band of 1.8335 to 1.915 lies **entirely above** the crude 1.802, which is
what a multiplicative deflation model must produce and is not a confidence interval; **the authors call it a
sensitivity band and that is the right name, but a reader skimming will take it for one.** And the outcome
cohort is 1,445 stays with 141 deaths, which is why every corrected interval crosses 1 — **the falsification
test was negative in cohorts that could not have shown much else.**
