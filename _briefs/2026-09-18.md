## A near-miss worth reporting first

Two papers surfaced in today's sweep that looked like exactly the right material — a BJA study of
**cumulative fluid balance trajectories** in circulatory failure, and a BJA **preplanned PADDI
substudy of intraoperative dexamethasone and glycaemic response in diabetes**. Both are squarely in
scope. I had read both abstracts in full and was drafting them.

**Both are old.** Checking the publication dates before writing showed the fluid balance paper was
first published **2 December 2025 — nine and a half months ago** — and the dexamethasone substudy
**16 June 2026, three months ago.** What appeared in the 16–18 September window were **different
records entirely**: a **Letter** responding to the fluid balance paper, and a **Letter** commenting
on the dexamethasone substudy. Neither letter has an abstract.

The mechanism was a bad habit on my part: searching Europe PMC by distinctive title words and
taking the **first** result, which was the original article rather than the record the sweep had
actually found. Both titles return **two to four distinct DOIs and PMIDs**. Every item below has
now been re-verified by querying **within the date window** and checking the `pubTypeList` field,
which cleanly separates `Journal Article` from `Letter`. That check is now written into the source
notes, and it removed three further candidates today — the *Resuscitation* AED-density framework
and the *Critical Care* piece on rate control in septic shock atrial fibrillation both turned out to
be letters, the latter having been on the outstanding list precisely because its title was
appealing.

**Nothing incorrect was published.** But it came close, and the correct cross-checks are cheap.

## 1 and 2. Two venovenous ECMO papers, one day apart

### Energy expenditure: the predictive equations are wrong more often than they are right

**Journal · Published:** Critical Care, **16 September 2026** — **two days old**
Wilke, Behnel, Weber-Carstens, Edel, Müller, Mahlau, Falk, Ruß, Wollersheim (Charité,
Universitätsmedizin Berlin)

The methodological problem it solves first: **conventional indirect calorimetry underestimates
energy expenditure during ECMO**, because a large share of gas exchange happens across the membrane
lung rather than the patient's own. This group used the **MEEP protocol**, which measures across
both.

Post-hoc analysis of prospectively collected data, **9 adult patients on venovenous ECMO for ARDS,
75 valid measurements.**

- **Median measured energy expenditure 1,923 kcal/day (IQR 1,594–2,222)**
- **The ECMO circuit accounted for 60.5% ± 18.2% of total gas exchange**
- **Feeding equipoise (intake 80–110% of measured EE) was achieved on only 30 of 75 days — 40%**;
  patients were **overfed on 44%** and **underfed on 16%** of days
- **Hypothetical ESPEN-guided nutrition at 25 kcal/kg/day using adjusted body weight misclassified
  feeding equipoise on 55% of days** — concordance 45%; using actual body weight, 49%
- **Respiratory quotient (0.908 ± 0.131) did not correlate with the feeding ratio (ρ = 0.024)**

**Interpretation.** **Sixty percent of gas exchange occurring across the membrane is the number
that explains everything else**, and it is why routine indirect calorimetry in these patients is not
merely imprecise but systematically wrong. A measurement that ignores the circuit is measuring a
minority of the patient's metabolism.

**The ESPEN concordance figure is the finding with a name attached.** A standard 25 kcal/kg/day
target misclassified feeding adequacy on **more than half of measurement days**, and switching
between adjusted and actual body weight barely moved it — 45% versus 49%. **Weight-based estimation
is close to a coin flip in this population**, and the choice of which weight to use, which occupies
a great deal of clinical discussion, is not the problem.

**Overfeeding was nearly three times as common as underfeeding** — 44% versus 16% of days. That
runs against the instinct that critically ill patients on extracorporeal support are being starved,
and it matters because overfeeding carries its own costs in CO₂ production and hepatic steatosis.

**The limits are severe and the authors do not hide them: nine patients.** Seventy-five measurement
days from nine people is a study of within-patient variability, not of a population. The design
supports the conclusion actually drawn — that **energy expenditure varies enough within a patient
over time that any static target will be wrong** — and does not support a specific alternative
target.

This lands on an open thread. The [ADQI XXXVI / ELSO consensus](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-11/)
on ECMO covered on 11 September called for exactly this kind of data, and the
**Chinese paediatric ECMO nutrition consensus** that appeared on 15 September has **no abstract
deposited** and remains unreadable. **Nutrition during extracorporeal support is being written
about faster than it is being measured**, and this is one of the few papers doing the measuring.

[Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-026-06334-w) · PMID 42754897

### Cytokine profiling: 48 analytes, 35 patients, and phenotypes that need validating

**Journal · Published:** Shock, **17 September 2026** — **published yesterday**
Hagiwara, Liu, DellaVolpe, Day, Michalek, **Lorusso**, **Combes**, Sousse

Prospective enrolment of **35 adults** on venovenous ECMO for refractory respiratory failure.
**48 cytokines and chemokines** by multiplex bead assay, sampled **immediately before ECMO
initiation** and longitudinally on days 1–4, 7, and weekly to decannulation. Principal component
analysis and hierarchical clustering for phenotypes.

- **Elevated pre-ECMO TNF-β and RANTES were significantly associated with reduced survival**
- **Higher IFN-α2, IL-1Ra, IL-12p40, MCP-1, IL-8, MIP-1α, IL-1 and SCF were associated with fewer
  adverse events** (P < 0.05)
- **IFN-α2, IP-10, MCP-1, MIP-1α, SCF and DPP3 correlated with prolonged ventilator dependence**
- **Cluster analysis revealed distinct immunologic phenotypes**
- The authors' claim: *"the most comprehensive cytokine and chemokine profiling in VV ECMO patients
  to date"*, with **larger multicentre studies warranted**

**Interpretation.** Take the phenotypes as a hypothesis and the individual associations with
considerable caution.

**Forty-eight analytes in 35 patients is a multiplicity problem before it is a biology problem.**
Testing 48 markers against several outcomes at P < 0.05 will generate significant associations by
chance alone; the abstract mentions no correction. **That MCP-1, MIP-1α and SCF appear in both the
"fewer adverse events" list and the "prolonged ventilator dependence" list is itself a warning** —
the same marker pointing in opposite directions across outcomes is what noise looks like.

**The pre-ECMO sampling is the genuine methodological contribution.** Measuring immediately before
cannulation separates the patient's own inflammatory state from the circuit's contribution, which is
the same confound the energy expenditure paper above had to engineer around. Two papers in two days,
both about disentangling patient from machine.

**Clustering into immunologic phenotypes is where this field keeps arriving**, and this archive has
seen it repeatedly — candidemia phenotypes, renal perfusion phenotypes, failed-weaning phenotypes.
The pattern is consistent: clustering finds groups, the groups differ in outcome, and the question
of whether they respond differently to treatment goes unanswered. **A phenotype that does not
predict treatment response is a description, not a tool.** The authors are appropriately modest.

[Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1097/shk.0000000000002943) · PMID 42752598

## 3. On-pump versus off-pump for isolated LIMA-to-LAD — 27 years, 662 patients

**Journal · Published:** Perfusion, **16 September 2026** — **two days old**
Taylor, Nagi, Walker, Rose, Bittar

Single-centre retrospective review of **all consecutive first-time isolated in-situ LIMA-to-LAD
grafts via median sternotomy, 1997 to 2024**. Propensity score matching; primary outcomes
in-hospital mortality and overall survival.

The premise is specific and worth stating: this operation **"is often performed by surgeons who do
not otherwise routinely perform off-pump surgery, despite evidence suggesting outcomes after
off-pump surgery correlate with surgeon experience."**

**662 patients, median follow-up 84 months (IQR 29–154). Off-pump in 58.2% (n = 385). 275 matched
pairs.**

- **In-hospital mortality 0.9% overall** — off-pump 0.8% vs on-pump 1.1%, **P = 0.684**
- **Overall survival similar** — log-rank **P = 0.127**; **HR for off-pump 1.309 (0.925–1.854)**
- **Postoperative stroke and blood transfusion significantly lower with off-pump**
- **Surgeon experience was not significantly associated with outcomes**

**Interpretation.** A careful null with two findings pulling against each other, and the honest
reading is in the hazard ratio.

**Less stroke and less transfusion off-pump, with no survival difference** — and a **hazard ratio of
1.309 that, while not significant, points the wrong way for off-pump** with an upper bound of 1.854.
That is the familiar shape of the OPCAB literature in miniature: better early morbidity, a
persistent unease about long-term graft patency and survival. The authors' call for **"additional
long-term studies extending beyond ten years"** is exactly right, and their median follow-up of
84 months is why.

**"Surgeon experience was not significantly associated with outcomes" is the surprising line**, and
it should be read as this cohort could not detect an association rather than as a refutation. The
premise of the paper is that occasional off-pump operators are doing this case; if that is true here,
the experience gradient may simply be too narrow to measure in 662 patients at one centre. A null
in a study not designed to find the effect is weak evidence of absence.

**What makes this operation a fair test is its simplicity.** A single graft to a single territory
removes the multivessel complexity — incomplete revascularisation, conduit choice, anastomotic
sequencing — that confounds most OPCAB comparisons. If off-pump is ever going to show a clean
advantage, it is here. **It showed less stroke and less transfusion, and nothing on survival.**

[Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/02676591261489064) · PMID 42750267

## 4. Landing zone angle and aneurysm shrinkage after frozen elephant trunk

**Journal · Published:** Interdisciplinary CardioVascular and Thoracic Surgery, **16 September
2026** — **two days old**
Kanemitsu, Maruyama, Yamasaki, Kajiyama

Total arch replacement with frozen elephant trunk using the **FROZENIX** prosthesis for **true
degenerative distal arch aneurysm**, July 2014 to June 2024. CT parameters at discharge, 6 months,
1 year, 2 years: **distal landing zone level, stent migration, landing zone angle, stent-to-aorta
diameter ratio**. Shrinkage defined as **>10% diameter decrease**.

**113 total arch replacements; 57 with FROZENIX; 9 excluded; 48 analysed.** Mean age 75.6, mean
aneurysm diameter 58.1 mm.

- **In-hospital mortality 0%**; **stroke 2 patients (4.2%)**; **spinal cord injury (paraparesis)
  2 patients (4.2%)**; **type Ib endoleak 1 patient (2.1%)**
- **Aneurysm shrinkage achieved in 31 (64.6%)**
- **Initial landing zone angle smaller in the shrinkage group: 15.1 ± 11.5° vs 26.8 ± 21.1°, a
  difference of about 12°, P = 0.046**
- **No significant difference** in angle change (P = 0.63), landing length (P = 0.95), diameter
  ratio (P = 0.91), or migration (P = 0.44)

**Interpretation.** A small, specific, plausible finding, and the authors label it correctly as an
**exploratory subgroup analysis**.

**The mechanism is intuitive: a stent graft landing in a sharply angled segment seals against a
curve**, and incomplete apposition prevents the sac from depressurising and shrinking. That four
other CT parameters showed nothing — including migration and diameter ratio, the ones most often
blamed — sharpens rather than weakens the result.

**But P = 0.046 in 48 patients across five tested parameters is exactly the situation that demands
the caveat the authors give.** Five comparisons, one marginal positive: under any correction for
multiplicity this does not survive. **The 12-degree difference is a hypothesis for a prospective
study, not a threshold for surgical planning** — and the standard deviations, 11.5° and 21.1°, are
larger than the difference between the group means, so the distributions overlap heavily.

**The perioperative numbers are the part I would carry regardless.** **0% in-hospital mortality**
with **4.2% stroke and 4.2% paraparesis** in a series averaging 75.6 years of age and 58 mm
aneurysms is a creditable result, and the spinal cord injury rate is the number that should shape the
anaesthetic conduct — spinal drainage, blood pressure targets and neuromonitoring for these cases.

It connects to a gap this archive has flagged repeatedly: the **EACTS/STS aortic organ guidelines**
remain only [partially covered](/medical-literature/guidelines/2026-09-04/), with the recommendation
tables unreachable, and the **stent graft landing zone parameters this paper measures are exactly
the kind of detail those tables would specify.**

[Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/icvts/ivag259) · PMID 42750146
