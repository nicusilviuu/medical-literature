## Correction: 20 October finished on 853 records, and yesterday's calibration was over-corrected

**Yesterday I published a new calibration of this archive's index instrument and a test that would settle it.
The test has returned and it went against the calibration.**

The claim was that a same-day index-date count is an unreliable fraction of the day's eventual total, between a
fifth and all of it, and therefore — in the published words — **"today's reading of 847 for 20 October cannot
be read as low."** The amendment added the test: **watch whether tomorrow's final count for 20 October is far
above 847; if it is, the flat end-of-run reading was sampling rather than a stall.**

**20 October finished on 853.** **The 14:05 reading of 847 was 99.3% of the day's eventual total, the flat
reading at 14:20 was a genuine stall, and 20 October really was a near-empty day** — six records arrived in the
whole of the rest of it.

**So the calibration was right about the mechanism and wrong about what follows from it.** Three days now have
very different same-day fractions:

| Day | Same-day reading | Final total | Fraction captured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sat 17 October | 3,650 at 14:08 | 3,631 | **~100%** (and now slightly lower through churn) |
| Mon 19 October | 8,717 at 14:04 | 42,358 | **20.6%** |
| Tue 20 October | 847 at 14:05 | **853** | **99.3%** |

**The correct statement is narrower than yesterday's: the same-day count is a lower bound on the day's total,
and whether it is a tight bound is not knowable at the time of reading.** **Yesterday's version converted "you
cannot tell" into "you cannot call it low", which is itself a claim, and that claim was false** — 847 was low,
and saying so would have been right. **This is the fourth revision of this instrument and the second in two
days, and the pattern in the errors is consistent: each time I have stated a stronger conclusion than the
measurement supports.**

**Two further things the instrument showed today, both of which matter more than the count.**

**First, 19 October's bucket has shrunk again, from 42,580 to 42,358 — a loss of 222 records.** The
upper-bound correction of 19 October holds and the denominators in yesterday's table have moved by half a
percentage point.

**Second, and this is new: the whole-database delta is a net figure and today it understates ingest by at least
seventeen hundred records.** The database read **49,109,455** at 14:05:53 against yesterday's 14:04 reading of
49,100,976 — **up 8,479**, with the eleven sources summing exactly to the total, residual zero
(`MED` **+8,570**, `PPR` **+1,123**, `NBK` **+6**, `PMC` **−1,220**, the rest unchanged). **But the index-date
buckets for 20 and 21 October gained 6 and 10,171 respectively — 10,177 records whose first index date is one
of those two days, against a net rise of 8,479.** **At least 1,698 records were removed while those were being
added**, and the visible removals — `PMC` down 1,220, the 19 October bucket down 222, the September reference
window down 82 — account for most of it. **The standing action needs the qualifier: the whole-database delta
measures net change, not ingest, and on a day with concurrent deletion it reads low.** Reference windows: 1–30
September **163,043** (**−82**, shrinking) and 1–7 October **43,519** (+986).

## 1. EIT-guided PEEP cuts adjudicated pulmonary complications from 72% to 49% — and this is the trial the monitoring thread was missing

**Journal · Published:** *British Journal of Anaesthesia*, **20 October 2026** — **one day old**, 0 citations.
**Single-centre, parallel-group randomised controlled trial** (ChiCTR2300076677). **117 adults** at
intermediate to high risk of postoperative pulmonary complications (**ARISCAT ≥26**) having laparoscopic
colorectal surgery, randomised to **intraoperative PEEP titration guided by electrical impedance tomography**
or **a conventional fixed PEEP of 5 cm H₂O**. **Primary outcome: PPC incidence within 30 days, adjudicated by a
multidisciplinary team using standardised criteria.**

> Pneumoperitoneum and steep Trendelenburg positioning … promote atelectasis and increase the risk of
> postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs). **Electrical impedance tomography (EIT) enables individualised
> PEEP titration by visualising regional lung collapse** … **However, evidence of its effect on clinical PPCs
> remains limited.** …
>
> Among the 117 randomised participants (mean [range] age: 62 [37-80] yr; 37% female), **EIT-guided PEEP
> (n=59) resulted in lower driving pressure (16 [15-18] cm H2O) compared with fixed PEEP (n=58; 19 [17-22] cm
> H2O; P<0.0001). The incidence of 30 day PPCs was lower among participants randomised to EIT-guided PEEP
> (29/59 [49.2%]) than those in the fixed-PEEP group (42/58 [72.4%]; hazard ratio: 0.53; 95% CI, 0.33-0.85;
> P=0.009). PPCs including atelectasis and pneumonia occurred less frequently** among participants randomised
> to EIT-guided PEEP.

**Interpretation.** **This archive has been looking for one specific trial since 18 October, and recorded the
specification exactly: a randomised comparison in which a monitored physiological target was controlled more
tightly AND a patient outcome improved, with nothing cancelling it.** **Today's trial is the first candidate,
and it is worth stating plainly against the three conditions.**

**Condition 1 — the measurement is closer to the pathology than the one it replaces.** Regional lung collapse
imaged directly, against a number (PEEP 5) that is not a measurement of anything. **Met.**
**Condition 2 — a protocol converts the number into an action.** PEEP is titrated to the image. **Met.**
**Condition 3 — the reported outcome is measured by something other than the instrument.** **Thirty-day
pulmonary complications, adjudicated by a multidisciplinary team against standardised criteria, with no EIT
involved. Met** — **and this is the condition the same technique failed in this archive five weeks ago.**

**On 19 September this brief reported [an *Anesthesiology* feasibility
study](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-19/) of EIT-guided PEEP titration in 60 postoperative ICU patients** whose primary outcome was **conversion of ventilation phenotype** — that is, the
instrument reporting on itself. **Its clinical outcome was exploratory, against a historical cohort: severe
PPCs 25% versus 41%, absolute risk difference −15.8% (−38.8 to 2.2), P = 0.17.** **Today the same intervention
is randomised, the outcome is adjudicated, and the effect is larger and conventionally significant: 49.2%
versus 72.4%, hazard ratio 0.53 (0.33–0.85).** **Same instrument, same protocol, different outcome measure —
and the thing that changed is the one the thread says matters.**

**The September paper also explains why the effect here could be this large, and the explanation is the
qualification.** That study found PEEP moving **in both directions**: raised from 5 to 9 in ventral-dominant
patients and **lowered from 7 to 3** in dorsal-dominant ones. **A fixed PEEP of 5 is therefore wrong in two
directions at once for different patients in the same operating theatre, and a trial against it measures the
cost of not individualising rather than the value of EIT specifically.** **The comparison that would isolate
the instrument is EIT against another individualisation method — driving-pressure-guided PEEP, or a
recruitment-and-decremental-titration protocol — and this trial does not make it.**

**The second qualification is the composite, and the control-arm event rate gives it away.** **A 72.4% PPC rate
is far above what most perioperative trials report**, which means the composite is dominated by its mildest
component. **The abstract confirms atelectasis is in it, alongside pneumonia.** **The absolute risk difference
of 23.3 percentage points implies a number needed to treat of about 4**, which for a 30-day clinical endpoint
would be remarkable and for a radiological one is ordinary. **The hazard ratio of 0.53 sits between the risk
ratio (0.68) and the odds ratio (0.37), as it should, so the analysis is internally consistent** — the question
is what the endpoint counts, not how it was analysed.

**Scale: 117 patients, one centre, no reported attrition — 59 plus 58 is the full randomised total, which is
unusual and worth crediting.** **The finding is that individualised PEEP by regional imaging beats a fixed PEEP
of 5 on an adjudicated composite, in one centre, at a size where a 23-point difference is detectable and a
3-point one is not.** **It is the thread's first genuine candidate and it does not close the thread**: what
cancels it is the comparator and the composite, not the measurement.

[Read the trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2026.09.016) · ChiCTR2300076677

## 2. A biomarker says who should not get hydrocortisone, on an interaction resting on 111 of 409 patients

**Journal · Published:** *Critical Care Explorations*, **21 October 2026** — **published today**, 0 citations.
**Post hoc analysis of VANISH**, the 2×2 factorial double-blind randomised trial of **early vasopressin versus
norepinephrine and hydrocortisone versus placebo** in septic shock across 18 UK ICUs
([JAMA 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.10485), **409 patients randomised**). **Proenkephalin A (PENK)
measured in 173 of them; patients dichotomised at 89 pmol/L.**

> Heterogeneity of treatment effect is thought to drive conflicting results for the benefits of corticosteroids
> in sepsis. **PENK is a biomarker of renal function.** … **PENK greater than or equal to 89 pmol/L was
> associated with 28-day mortality (odds ratio, 3.24; 95% CI, 1.62-6.74). We found no interaction between PENK
> and randomization to vasopressin or norepinephrine on outcome. However, there was a significant interaction
> between the PENK groups and hydrocortisone or placebo use in those patients who reached the maximum
> vasopressor threshold (n = 111) for kidney failure-free days** (high PENK placebo 26 [13-28], hydrocortisone
> 11 [0-26] and low PENK placebo 28 [15-28], hydrocortisone 28 [28-28]; **p = 0.01**). …
> **Although we found no significant interaction for 28-day mortality (p = 0.14)**, the patients with high PENK
> who received hydrocortisone had the highest mortality (**high PENK hydrocortisone 50%, placebo 32% and low
> PENK hydrocortisone 14%, placebo 24%**). **This exploratory analysis identified a possible interaction** …
> **These findings require validation in independent and prospective studies.**

**Interpretation.** **Read against item 1, this is the same question at a weaker level of evidence, and the
paper knows it.** A biomarker is measured, a treatment decision is attached to it, and an outcome is reported —
but the outcome is a secondary endpoint, in a subgroup of a subgroup, chosen after the fact. **PENK was
measured in 173 of VANISH's 409 patients (42%), and the interaction that generates the headline rests on the
111 who reached the maximum vasopressor threshold — 27% of the original trial.**

**The number that is not in the abstract is where the threshold of 89 pmol/L came from.** **If it was
prespecified from external data, this analysis is a test; if it was chosen in these data, it is a
description.** **That single unstated fact determines how much of the p = 0.01 survives**, and it has to come
from the methods section.

**The shape of the interaction is the kind most likely to be noise, and the authors report it honestly enough
for a reader to see that.** It is a **crossover**: hydrocortisone looks **harmful** in high-PENK patients (50%
versus 32% mortality, 18 points) and **beneficial** in low-PENK patients (14% versus 24%, 10 points the other
way). **Crossover interactions are seductive because they tell a mechanistic story and fragile because they
require both halves to be real.** **And the interaction is significant for kidney-failure-free days and not
for mortality (p = 0.14)** — the pattern this archive has recorded repeatedly, most recently in MODEST, where
one endpoint out of several crosses a threshold and the hard one does not.

**There is also a confound that is the same variable as the mechanism.** **PENK is described in the abstract's
own words as "a biomarker of renal function".** So high PENK means worse kidneys, and the finding is that
hydrocortisone is associated with fewer kidney-failure-free days in patients whose kidneys were already
failing. **That may be a drug effect; it may be that the sickest kidneys were going to fail regardless and
steroid allocation is along for the ride.** **A biomarker that measures the organ whose failure is the outcome
cannot easily separate the two**, and no post hoc adjustment fixes it.

**What the paper does well is worth saying, because it is the opposite of the usual failure.** **It reports the
non-significant mortality interaction rather than burying it, calls itself exploratory in the abstract, and
asks for prospective validation.** **The prognostic finding is also real and separable: PENK ≥89 predicts
28-day mortality with an odds ratio of 3.24 (1.62–6.74).** **But predicting who dies and predicting who
benefits are different claims, and only the first is established here.** That distinction is exactly what the
next item fails to engage with.

## 3. Pancreatic stone protein beats procalcitonin by 0.15 of an AUC, and that is the whole of what is shown

**Journal · Published:** *Intensive Care Medicine Experimental*, **20 October 2026** — **one day old**,
0 citations. **Meta-analysis of direct within-study paired AUC comparisons** (PROSPERO CRD420261340301).
**Seven studies, 803 unique participants, 21 paired comparisons**; principal conservative analysis uses **one
comparison per independent cohort and comparator**.

> **In the principal conservative analysis, PSP showed a higher prognostic AUC than CRP, with a pooled ΔAUC of
> 0.179 (95% CI 0.095-0.262; p < 0.001), although between-cohort heterogeneity was substantial (I² = 81.65%).
> Compared with PCT, the pooled ΔAUC was 0.153 (95% CI 0.114-0.193; p < 0.001), with no observed heterogeneity
> (I² = 0.00%).** The mortality-only sensitivity analysis showed a similar pooled effect … **Available
> comparative evidence suggests that PSP may have greater prognostic discrimination than CRP and PCT.** …
> **the limited number of independent cohorts and substantial heterogeneity in the CRP comparison warrant
> caution. Further prospective studies are required to confirm these findings and establish their clinical
> relevance.**

**Interpretation.** **The design choice is the good part and deserves naming: paired within-study comparisons.**
Most biomarker meta-analyses pool each marker's AUC separately across different cohorts and then subtract,
which compares populations as much as markers. **Here every comparison is head-to-head in the same patients,
so the ΔAUC is a real difference rather than an artefact of case mix.** **That is the right way to do this and
it is not how it is usually done.**

**The effect sizes are large enough to be the main reason for caution.** **A ΔAUC of 0.153 to 0.179 is not a
marginal improvement** — if procalcitonin discriminates at 0.65, that puts pancreatic stone protein near 0.80.
**Differences that size in a mature comparator are uncommon, and the abstract gives no information on who ran
the seven studies or on which assay.** **For a proprietary point-of-care marker competing against two
established ones, the question of whether the comparator was measured and thresholded as carefully as the
index test is the first one to ask, and nothing here answers it.**

**The two heterogeneity figures should be read in opposite directions from the way they look.** **I² = 81.65%
for the CRP comparison is reported and flagged by the authors, which is correct.** **But I² = 0.00% for the
procalcitonin comparison is not reassurance** — with a handful of independent cohorts, I² is estimated so
imprecisely that zero is close to uninformative, and the value is at the floor of its range. **The narrower
interval there (0.114 to 0.193, implying a standard error near 0.020, against 0.043 for CRP) follows from that
assumed homogeneity rather than from more data.**

**The decisive limitation is one the paper states and does not dwell on: "establish their clinical relevance".**
**Set against the three conditions this archive applies to a monitored variable, this document satisfies the
first and neither of the others.** **The measurement may be closer to the pathology. There is no protocol
converting it into an action, and there is no outcome measured by anything other than the marker's own
discrimination.** **An AUC is a statement about ranking patients, not about changing what happens to them** —
and the gap between the two is the entire distance between today's first item and this one. **A better
prognostic marker in sepsis is worth having; the trial that shows acting on it helps has not been done.**

[Read the meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40635-026-00990-6) · PROSPERO CRD420261340301

## 4. IPACK and tibial nerve block are called equivalent on an interval that admits a 74% difference

**Journal · Published:** *Anaesthesia*, **18 October 2026** — **three days old**, 0 citations.
**Triple-blinded randomised controlled trial, 90 patients analysed** (45 per group) having total knee
arthroplasty **under spinal anaesthesia with a femoral triangle block**, randomised to **IPACK with 19 ml
ropivacaine 0.2%** or **a selective tibial nerve block with 5 ml ropivacaine 0.75%**. **Primary outcome:
cumulative intravenous morphine on postoperative day 1.** Background analgesia: paracetamol, ibuprofen,
morphine PCA.

> **Cumulative intravenous morphine consumption on postoperative day 1 was similar** between the patients
> allocated to the IPACK and selective tibial nerve block groups (**mean (SD) 6 (7) mg vs. 5 (6) mg,
> respectively; p = 0.36**). **Four patients (9%) who received selective tibial nerve block had motor block of
> the common peroneal nerve.** No other differences were seen between groups in secondary outcomes. …
> **The IPACK and selective tibial nerve block provide similar analgesia after total knee arthroplasty under
> spinal anaesthesia with a femoral triangle block.**

**Interpretation.** **The clinically useful finding is in the safety sentence, not the analgesia sentence:
equal pain relief, and one of the two techniques caused common peroneal motor block in 4 of 45 patients
(8.9%).** **A foot that will not dorsiflex after knee replacement is a mobilisation problem, a falls risk and a
conversation with a worried patient, and it appeared in one arm only.** **If the analgesia really is equivalent,
that complication rate decides the choice** — and it is the number a reader should take away.

**But "provide similar analgesia" is more than this trial establishes, and the arithmetic shows how much more.**
**It is a superiority trial that did not find superiority, reported as equivalence.** With means of 6 and 5 mg
and standard deviations of 7 and 6 at 45 per arm, the standard error of the difference is about **1.37 mg**, so
**the 95% confidence interval around the 1 mg difference runs from roughly −1.7 to +3.7 mg.** **An upper bound
of 3.7 mg is about 74% of the tibial group's total consumption.** **A trial cannot exclude a difference of
three-quarters of the comparator's opioid use and then call the two treatments similar.** **No equivalence
margin is stated, and at this size none could have been met.**

**The floor effect is the reason, and it is a design consequence rather than a mistake.** **Both arms used
about 5 to 6 mg of morphine in 24 hours** — on top of spinal anaesthesia, a femoral triangle block,
paracetamol and ibuprofen. **A background that effective leaves almost nothing for a posterior-knee block to
reduce**, so the primary outcome was close to uninformative before randomisation began. **This is the same
structural problem as the [erector spinae meta-analysis](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-20/) reported
here yesterday, in mirror image: there, opioid
consumption moved by 19 mg and pain scores barely moved; here neither moves, because the multimodal
background has already taken the opioid requirement to the floor.**

**A note on the summary statistics: the standard deviations exceed or equal the means in both arms** (6 ± 7 and
5 ± 6). **Opioid consumption at this level is heavily right-skewed — most patients near zero, a few high — so
means and standard deviations describe it poorly**, and a median with an interquartile range, or a comparison
on the ranks, would have been the honest presentation. **It does not change the null, but it does mean the
confidence interval above is an approximation of an approximation.**

**One quiet piece of good design worth noting.** **IPACK used 19 ml of 0.2% ropivacaine — 38 mg. The tibial
block used 5 ml of 0.75% — 37.5 mg.** **The dose in milligrams is the same to within half a milligram while
volume and concentration differ roughly four-fold**, which is exactly right for a comparison of two techniques
rather than two doses. **Second day running that total knee arthroplasty has appeared in this archive**, after
yesterday's spinal-versus-general resource trial.
