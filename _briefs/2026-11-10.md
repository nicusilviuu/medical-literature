**So the freeze was a delivery pause, not a loss, and the backlog arrived with its dates intact.** **That is

## 1. OPTIPAM: a trial stopped for bleeding, reported as unable to answer its question

**[Individualized mean arterial pressure during cardiac surgery: The OPTIPAM Randomized Clinical
Trial](https://doi.org/10.1097/aln.0000000000006471)** · *Anesthesiology* · **published 6 November 2026** ·
paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Single-blind, multicentre, ten tertiary centres in France. Adults having elective cardiac surgery with
cardiopulmonary bypass, randomised to a mean arterial pressure target within 10% of their own preoperative
baseline, intraoperatively and for 24 hours afterwards, against standard management at a target of 65 mmHg or
above.** Primary outcome a composite of death, acute kidney injury or prespecified neurological complications
within seven days. From the abstract:

> A total of 172 patients were enrolled before the trial was stopped early by the data and safety monitoring
> board because of increased bleeding in the individualized group. Mean intraoperative MAP was higher with
> individualized management (80.6 vs 75.6 mm Hg; P<0.001), despite limited target adherence (33% of
> intraoperative time) and greater vasopressor use. The primary outcome occurred in 27 of 85 patients (32%) in
> the individualized group and 25 of 87 (29%) in the standard group (relative risk, 1.1; 95% CI, 0.7 to 1.8;
> P=0.76). Hemorrhagic complications occurred more frequently in the individualized group (18% vs 3%;
> P=0.003).

**Three things are happening at once and the paper keeps them apart, which is why it is the lead.**

**First, the intervention was only half delivered.** **Mean pressure differed by 5.0 mmHg, which is a real
separation, but the individualised target was met for only 33% of intraoperative time** and required more
vasopressor to get there. **A trial that achieves its target a third of the time has tested "trying to
individualise pressure", not "individualised pressure"**, and the distinction decides what a null means.

**Second, the primary outcome is null and underpowered twice over.** **27 of 85 against 25 of 87 — 31.8%
against 28.7%, a difference of 3.0 percentage points — with a relative risk of 1.1 whose interval spans
2.6-fold.** **At 172 of an unstated planned sample, stopped early, this comparison was never going to
resolve anything**, and the authors' conclusion says exactly that: *"it was not possible to determine whether
an individualized MAP strategy reduced major postoperative complications."* **That sentence is unusual and
correct. A trial stopped for harm cannot report its efficacy question as negative; it has to report it as
unanswered.**

**Third, the harm signal is the finding, and it is not small.** **Haemorrhagic complications 18% against 3%,
p = 0.003** — which on these arm sizes reconstructs to roughly **15 events against 3**. **Eighteen events
is thin, and a safety board stopping a trial on eighteen events is making a judgement under uncertainty, which
is its job.** **The mechanism is not mysterious: a higher pressure target met with more vasopressor, in
patients who have just been heparinised and bypassed, bleeds more.**

**My reading.** **The clinically actionable content is the safety signal, and it points the opposite way to
where this field has been heading.** Individualising blood pressure to a patient's own baseline is
intuitively attractive and this archive published two BOX substudies on 16 October showing that **raising mean
arterial pressure with noradrenaline moves every number a pulmonary artery catheter reports while leaving
lactate and mortality where they were.** **OPTIPAM adds the cost: in cardiac surgery, pushing pressure up
costs blood.** **What nobody now knows is whether a better-adhered individualised strategy would help, and
the honest position is the authors' — the safety signal has to be investigated before the efficacy question
can be asked again.**

## 2. ADJUST: thirty-nine deaths in each arm, and fourteen points more harm

**[Efficacy and Safety of Ulinastatin in the Treatment of Patients with Sepsis: The ADJUST
Trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2026.10.033)** · *Chest* · **published 6 November 2026** · paywalled;
abstract quoted below

**Multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled. Adult ICU patients meeting Sepsis-3 criteria
within 48 hours of admission, randomised 1:1 to intravenous ulinastatin 400,000 IU every eight hours for up
to ten days, or placebo, in addition to standard care; randomisation stratified by site and septic shock.**
347 randomised, 340 treated and analysed for safety, 335 in the intention-to-treat analysis. From the
abstract:

> At 28 days, death had occurred in 39 of 165 patients (23.6%) in the UTI group and in 39 of 170 patients
> (22.9%) in the placebo group (absolute difference, 0.7% [95% CI -8.4% to 9.7%]; P = .98; hazard ratio 1.03,
> 95% CI 0.66 to 1.61, P = .90). Secondary outcomes were similar in the two groups. Adverse events occurred in
> 56.8% (96/169) of UTI and 42.7% (73/171) of placebo patients (absolute difference 14.1%, 95% CI 3.6%-24.6%;
> P = 0.009).

**Every figure reconstructs exactly: 39 of 165 is 23.64%, 39 of 170 is 22.94%, the difference is 0.70
percentage points, and 96 of 169 against 73 of 171 is 56.80% against 42.69%, a difference of 14.11 points.**

**Thirty-nine deaths in each arm is as clean a null as this literature produces**, and the hazard ratio of
1.03 with an interval from 0.66 to 1.61 says what the trial can and cannot exclude: **a 34% reduction and a
61% increase are both still inside it.** **So "no benefit" is established and "no harm to survival" is not**,
and the authors' phrasing — *"Routine use of UTI for sepsis is not supported by these findings"* — is the
right strength.

**The adverse-event result is the part that will be skipped and should not be.** **56.8% against 42.7%, an
absolute difference of 14.1 percentage points with an interval from 3.6 to 24.6 that excludes zero.** **A drug
with no mortality benefit and significantly more adverse events is not a neutral result; it is a negative
one.** **This archive's standing practice is to put a harm signal beside the efficacy signal rather than
below it**, and here the two together make the conclusion unambiguous in a way the mortality figure alone
does not.

**Two limits.** **The trial registered in 2016 — ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02647554 — and reports in 2026**, a
decade in which the standard care it was added to changed substantially. **And 335 patients give the primary
comparison an interval 18 percentage points wide on the absolute scale**, which is why a clean null here is
a statement about a large effect being absent rather than about equivalence.

## 3. One trial is 99.3% of the weight, and a strong recommendation sits on top of it

**[Prolonged Versus Intermittent Piperacillin-Tazobactam Infusion in Critically Ill Patients: A Systematic
Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Trials](https://doi.org/10.1159/che/aceag002)** · *Chemotherapy* ·
**published 6 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Seven randomised trials, 6,080 patients.** From the abstract:

> For clinical cure, the pooled estimate favored prolonged infusion (risk ratio 1.23, 95% confidence interval
> 1.07-1.42, 6 trials, 585 participants; very low-certainty evidence). The effect on 28-day mortality was
> uncertain (risk ratio 0.47, 95% confidence interval 0.16-1.41, 2 trials, 104 participants; low-certainty
> evidence). There was no clear difference in 90-day mortality (risk ratio 0.92, 95% confidence interval
> 0.84-1.01, 2 trials, 5,532 participants; moderate-certainty evidence). BLING III contributed 99.3% of the
> weight in the analysis.

**Read the participant counts against the outcomes, because they invert the usual pattern.** **The positive
result — clinical cure, risk ratio 1.23 — comes from 6 trials and 585 participants at very low certainty.
The null result — 90-day mortality, 0.92 with an interval from 0.84 to 1.01 — comes from 2 trials and 5,532
participants at moderate certainty, 9.5 times as many patients.** **The outcome with the most evidence behind
it is the one that found nothing**, and the authors grade it accordingly rather than leading with the
favourable number.

**And one trial is almost the entire analysis. BLING III contributed 99.3% of the weight.** **A meta-analysis
in which a single trial supplies ninety-nine point three percent of the weight is that trial, reported with
a forest plot** — and the honest way to read the 90-day mortality estimate is as BLING III's result, not as a
synthesis. **The authors say this outright in their conclusion:** the findings *"are consistent with the
class-level evidence but do not independently confirm a mortality benefit specific to piperacillin-tazobactam."*

**This matters more than an ordinary null because a strong recommendation sits directly on it.** **The
document this archive routed to its guidelines watch on 5 November — the structured comparison of the 2026
Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines against the German Sepsis Society's 2025 adaptation — lists "a strong
recommendation for prolonged or continuous beta-lactam infusion" among the points on which the two guidelines
agree.** **Today's paper asks whether that class-level recommendation holds for the specific drug most often
used, and answers: the cure signal is very low certainty on 585 patients, the mortality signal is one trial,
and neither independently confirms it.** **Agreement between two guidelines is not independent evidence when
both rest on the same trial.**

**My reading.** **Nothing here argues for abandoning prolonged infusion** — it is cheap, mechanistically
sound, and the direction of every estimate is favourable. **What it argues against is the confidence with
which the recommendation is stated.** **A clinician giving continuous piperacillin-tazobactam is doing
something reasonable supported by one large trial and a handful of small ones, not something established
across a literature**, and the 2.47-day reduction in treatment duration the paper also reports comes from
**2 trials and 120 participants at very low certainty**, which is not a basis for anything.

## 4. Colchicine cuts atrial fibrillation by 30%, and one interval spans 280-fold

**[Association Between Colchicine Use and Clinical Outcomes Post Cardiac Surgery: A Meta-analysis of
Randomized Controlled Trials](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40256-026-00831-7)** · *American Journal of
Cardiovascular Drugs* · **published 7 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Nine randomised trials, 2,005 patients — 990 colchicine and 1,015 placebo, summing exactly — mean age
62.8.** PROSPERO registered. From the abstract:

> Pooled analysis demonstrated that compared with placebo, colchicine was significantly associated with a
> reduction in the risk of postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) (RR 0.70 [95% CI 0.59-0.83], p < 0.001).
> However, between the groups, there was no statistically significant difference in the risk of all-cause
> mortality (ACM) (RR 0.81 [95% CI 0.39-1.69], p = 0.57, I2 = 0%), stroke (RR 1.86 [95% CI 0.48-7.12],
> p = 0.37), and permanent pacemaker implantation (PPM) (RR 1.36 [95% CI 0.08-22.44], p = 0.83).

**The primary result is solid and is the most convincing postoperative atrial fibrillation intervention this
archive has published.** **A risk ratio of 0.70 with an interval from 0.59 to 0.83, only 1.4-fold wide,
across nine randomised trials** — against the stellate ganglion block that failed on 31 October at an odds
ratio of 0.99, and the magnesium cohort the same day that found nothing.

**The secondary intervals are where a reader should stop and look.** **The pacemaker interval runs from 0.08
to 22.44 — a 280-fold span. The stroke interval spans 14.8-fold. Mortality spans 4.3-fold.** **An interval
that wide is not weak evidence of no effect; it is the absence of evidence, reported as a null.** **Quoting
"no significant difference in pacemaker implantation" from a 280-fold interval would be a serious misreading,
and the number of events behind it must be in the low single figures.**

**One flag on the I² of 0% beside the mortality estimate.** **This archive's standing rule is that I² = 0%
across a handful of small trials is uninformative rather than reassuring** — with few events per trial there
is no heterogeneity to detect, and the statistic has no power.

**My reading.** **Colchicine for postoperative atrial fibrillation prevention is now about as well supported
as a perioperative anti-inflammatory can be on 2,005 patients, and the length-of-stay reduction of 1.16 days
is plausible as a consequence rather than an independent effect.** **The honest statement of the harms is
that they are unmeasured, not absent** — and colchicine's real-world problem, gastrointestinal intolerance,
is not in the abstract's outcome list at all.

## 5. Forty-two per cent of sepsis survivors die within two years

**[Long-Term Mortality in Sepsis Survivors: A Population-Based Study During 15
Years](https://doi.org/10.1097/ccm.0000000000007415)** · *Critical Care Medicine* · **published
5 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**A retrospective population-based cohort of every sepsis admission to an acute-care hospital in Catalonia
between 2005 and 2019, identified by diagnostic coding, with follow-up through the regional insured-persons
registry.** From the abstract:

> Among 296,554 hospital admissions for sepsis, 237,075 patients (79.9%) survived to hospital discharge. Of
> these, 42% died (99,571) within 2 years. Long-term mortality increased significantly with age, ranging from
> 5.8% in patients younger than 15 years to 62% in those older than 84 years (p < 0.0001), and with
> comorbidity burden (21%, 38%, 51%, and 67% for Charlson index 0, 1-2, 3-4, and ≥ 5, respectively;
> p < 0.0001). Neoplasia (hazard ratio [HR], 2.011), liver cirrhosis (HR, 1.562), and dementia (HR, 1.304)
> were the comorbidities most strongly associated with mortality.

**237,075 of 296,554 is 79.94% and 99,571 of 237,075 is 42.0%** — both reconstruct to the stated figures.
**Nearly a hundred thousand deaths in the two years after discharge, in one region of seven and a half
million people, over fifteen years.**

**The composition rule this archive recorded on 8 November applies directly and in the other direction.**
Sunday's entry composed two conditional fractions to show that two-thirds five-year survival after
extracorporeal resuscitation becomes 19.5% once the 29% who reach discharge is included. **Here the
conditioning is stated up front: 79.9% survive the admission, and 42% of those are dead within two years,
so unconditional two-year survival from a sepsis admission is about 46%.** **The paper does not compose them
either, and the composed figure is the one a patient is living in.**

**The gradients are the usable output.** **Two-year mortality from 5.8% under 15 to 62% over 84 — a 10.7-fold
range — and from 21% to 67% across Charlson strata.** **A single headline figure of 42% describes almost
nobody**: it is the average of a population in which a comorbidity-free survivor faces one in five and a
multiply-comorbid one faces two in three.

**Two cautions.** **This is administrative coding, not adjudicated sepsis** — fifteen years spanning both
ICD-9 and ICD-10, through a period in which sepsis coding changed substantially, and the paper's own
observation of **a progressive decline in long-term mortality during 2017–2019** could as easily be a coding
artefact as a clinical improvement. **And the design cannot separate sepsis from the frailty that led to it**;
the strongest hazard ratios — neoplasia at 2.011, cirrhosis at 1.562, dementia at 1.304 — are for conditions
that kill people who never had sepsis.

**My reading.** **The number worth carrying is not 42% but the shape behind it, and the conclusion the authors
draw from it is the right one:** structured post-sepsis care aimed at high-risk patients rather than at all
survivors. **What this archive has now published in three days is a pair of papers making the same
methodological point from opposite ends of critical care** — that survival figures conditional on surviving
an index event systematically overstate what a patient faces, and that the fractions have to be composed
before they mean anything.
