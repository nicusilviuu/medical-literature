## 1. Nociception-guided remifentanil in scoliosis surgery — a clean null, and an informative one

**Journal · Published:** Anesthesiology, **16 September 2026** — **published today**
Julien-Marsollier, Pardessus, Lagarde, Cholet, Clavaud, Church, Jurine, Maroun, Orliaguet,
Sabourdin, Constant, Guilmin-Crepon, Dahmani

Multicentre randomised controlled trial, **October 2020 to June 2023**, in children undergoing
correction of **adolescent idiopathic scoliosis**. Intraoperative remifentanil guided either by
**haemodynamic parameters** (control) or by the **Analgesia Nociception Index (ANI)**
(intervention). Primary outcome **postoperative morphine consumption at day 1**, adjusted for
non-opioid analgesic use; secondary outcome **persistent postoperative pain at 6 months**.

**247 patients included, 244 randomised — 120 ANI, 123 control.**

- **Primary outcome null: morphine at day 1, median 0.4 [0.3; 0.8] vs 0.4 [0.3; 0.5] mg/kg;
  median difference −0.005 (95% CI −0.10 to 0.09), P = 0.93**
- **Intraoperative remifentanil was significantly lower in the ANI group — mean difference
  9.5 µg/kg (4 to 15), P < 0.001**
- **No difference in any secondary outcome**, including persistent pain at 6 months

**Interpretation.** The combination of those first two results is the finding, and it is sharper
than a bare null.

**The monitor worked — the intervention it drove did not.** ANI guidance genuinely changed
intraoperative practice: **9.5 µg/kg less remifentanil**, a real and statistically robust
difference. So this is not a trial where the intervention failed to separate the arms. It is a
trial where the arms separated on the thing being titrated and **converged completely on the
thing that matters to the patient**.

That makes it a much more informative negative than most. Two readings survive. Either **ANI
measures something real that does not translate into postoperative opioid requirement** — plausible,
since intraoperative nociceptive load and postoperative pain are related but not identical — or
**the haemodynamically-guided control arm was already giving close to the right dose**, and the
ANI arm's reduction was trimming excess that was never doing harm. The absence of any difference
in persistent pain at six months argues against the fear that less intraoperative opioid causes
worse long-term outcomes, which is itself worth having.

**What it does not support is the marketing case.** Nociception monitors are sold on the promise
of reduced opioid consumption and better recovery; this trial, in a population with severe
postoperative pain and a strong rationale for the technology, found **neither**. A median
difference of −0.005 mg/kg with a confidence interval from −0.10 to 0.09 excludes any effect of
clinical size.

It belongs beside two other items from this fortnight where a monitored variable turned out not
to be a useful target: the
[Hypotension Prediction Index analysis](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-10/), where the benefit
tracked treatment intensity rather than prediction, and the
[ETCO₂ mortality cohort](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-11/), whose authors were careful to
claim a **marker for risk stratification, not a target**. **Three monitors in a fortnight, one
lesson: measuring a thing accurately is not the same as having something useful to do about it.**

Limits: a single surgical population with an unusual pain profile, and the abstract does not
report blinding of outcome assessment. Generalising to adult practice or to other operations is
not supported.

## 2. One-lung ventilation releases neutrophil extracellular vesicles — mechanism with a clinical endpoint

**Journal · Published:** American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine,
**15 September 2026** — **published yesterday**
Stephens, Heng, Cutting, Walsh, Boshier, Iki, Sugarman, Cheng, Wilson, Baldi, Shah, Patel,
**Gordon**, Hanna, **Takata**, Soni (Imperial College London)

**Serial, bilateral bronchoalveolar lavage in 42 patients undergoing oesophagectomy**, requiring
both one-lung and two-lung ventilation — so **each patient's two lungs act as their own
comparison**. Extracellular vesicle subtypes by flow cytometry, correlated with inflammatory
mediators and clinical data. Western blotting for neutrophil matrix metalloproteinases within
patient-derived neutrophil EVs, bioactivity tested in a **primary human alveolar
epithelial–macrophage coculture**, and an **in vitro alveolar stretch model** to probe biogenesis.

- **Unphysiological one-lung ventilation, but not two-lung ventilation, induced a 4.8-fold
  increase in neutrophil extracellular vesicles**
- These were **associated with alveolar inflammation and a greater incidence of postoperative
  pulmonary complications**
- The vesicles **contained abundant MMP-8 and MMP-9**, generated pro-inflammatory responses in
  vitro, **and those responses were reduced by MMP inhibition**
- In vitro, **injurious stretch of an epithelial–neutrophil coculture induced vesicle production
  through an ATP-dependent mechanism**

**Interpretation.** This is the most complete paper of the day, and the design is why.

**The bilateral BAL is the clever part.** Both lungs belong to the same patient, with the same
circulating inflammatory state, the same anaesthetic and the same surgical insult — and only one
is subjected to unphysiological ventilation. A 4.8-fold difference across that comparison is far
harder to explain away than the same finding between patients. It is, in effect, a
within-patient controlled exposure that no randomised trial could ethically construct.

**And it does not stop at association.** Most mechanistic perioperative work reports a biomarker
that correlates with an outcome and stops. This chain runs: **injurious stretch → ATP-dependent
neutrophil EV release → MMP-8/9 cargo → pro-inflammatory response in human alveolar cells →
abrogated by MMP inhibition.** Each link is tested. That is what makes it a candidate therapeutic
target rather than another correlate.

**The honest limit is the clinical end.** **42 patients**, and the abstract says the vesicles were
*"associated with… a greater incidence of PPCs"* — no effect size, no adjustment, no number of
complications. The conclusion is appropriately hedged: *"may be associated with PPCs."* **Treat
the mechanism as strong and the clinical association as preliminary.** An MMP inhibitor trial is
several steps away, and the history of anti-inflammatory interventions in ventilator-induced lung
injury is not encouraging.

For thoracic anaesthesia the practical reading is unchanged but better supported: **the injury of
one-lung ventilation is real, it is stretch-mediated, and it is happening in the ventilated lung
rather than being a systemic phenomenon.** Every argument for lower tidal volumes, lower driving
pressure and shorter one-lung time now has a mechanism attached to it.

[Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajrccm/aamag457) · PMID 42742361

## 3. Multimodal AI mortality prediction — 203,434 ICU admissions, and the external validation is the story

**Journal · Published:** Anesthesiology, **16 September 2026** — **published today**
Mamandipoor, Hsu, Krause, Schmidt, Gabriel

Multimodal deep-learning model predicting **in-hospital mortality after the first 24 hours of ICU
admission**, developed on **MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV**, externally validated in a temporally
separated MIMIC population, in **HiRID**, and in **eICU**. Inputs: **time-invariant variables,
time-variant variables, clinical notes, and chest X-ray images**.

**203,434 ICU admissions from more than 200 hospitals, 2001–2022.** Mortality **5.2% to 7.9%**
across the four datasets.

- Structured-data model: **AUROC 0.92 (0.90–0.93)**, **AUPRC 0.53 (0.49–0.57)**, **Brier 0.19
  (0.18–0.20)**
- **External validation across eight eICU institutions: AUROC 0.84 to 0.92**
- Restricted to patients with notes *and* imaging, adding those modalities improved **AUROC 0.87
  → 0.89**, **AUPRC 0.43 → 0.48**, **Brier 0.37 → 0.17**; DeLong **P = 0.0167**

**Interpretation.** Two numbers deserve more attention than the headline AUROC, and one of them
is the reason to take this seriously.

**The external validation range — 0.84 to 0.92 across eight institutions — is the honest
result.** Reporting per-institution performance rather than a pooled figure is the right thing to
do and is uncommon; the spread tells you the model degrades meaningfully at some sites. That is
the failure mode the [STS-ACSD reoperation model](/medical-literature/brief/2026-09-11/) sent on
11 September demonstrated in the other direction, where a model built at one high-volume centre
**systematically underestimated national risk** because its training population was 5.4%
urgent against 26.6% nationally. **This paper's contribution is testing for that rather than
assuming it away**, and the authors say so: *"the importance of external validation"* is half
their stated conclusion.

**AUPRC 0.53 against AUROC 0.92 is the number that keeps this honest.** With mortality at 5–8%,
AUROC flatters heavily — a model can look excellent at ranking and still be wrong most of the
time when it flags a patient. An area under the precision-recall curve of 0.53 is a far more
realistic statement of what this would feel like as an alert on a ward round.

**The multimodal gain is real but small, and one number looks odd.** Adding notes and imaging
moved AUROC from 0.87 to 0.89 — statistically significant, clinically marginal. But the **Brier
score moving from 0.37 to 0.17** in that same comparison is an enormous calibration improvement,
much larger than the discrimination change, and the abstract does not explain it. Brier 0.37 in a
population with 5–8% mortality would be very poorly calibrated indeed. **I cannot reconcile that
figure from the abstract alone and am flagging it rather than interpreting it.**

Practically: this is a well-conducted prediction study that does the thing most such studies skip.
It is not a decision-support tool — nothing here shows that acting on the prediction changes
anything, which is the same gap the nociception trial above illustrates from the other side.

[Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1097/aln.0000000000006294) · PMID 42549798
