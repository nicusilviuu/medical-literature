## The index lost a fifth of a settled day, and I cannot say from where

**26 November held 18,558 records when it was read yesterday at 14:09. Today it holds 10,277. That is a
loss of 8,281 records, 44.6% of the day, from a date already two days past.**

**The scale is the point.** The three previous days of shrinkage, recorded on 25, 26 and 27 November, ran
from **1 to 245 records.** This is **two orders of magnitude larger** and has to be treated as a different
phenomenon until shown otherwise.

**The obvious suspect is `PMC`, and the arithmetic encourages it.** The whole-database `PMC` bucket went
from 1,012,420 to **1,004,975** this morning, **a shed of 7,445** — the same order of magnitude as the
8,281 lost from 26 November, on the same morning.

**That points straight back at an association I withdrew yesterday.** On 26 November I recorded that the
shrinkage direction matched `PMC` shedding; on 27 November `PMC` gained 1,067 while three settled days still
lost 250 records between them, so I withdrew it as two days of coincidence. **Today the two move together
again, and large.**

**And I cannot settle it, because I did not keep the data.** Only each day's **total** has ever been
recorded, never its split by source. Today 26 November holds **8,265 MEDLINE records, 692 PMC records and
1,306 preprints**, summing to 10,263 of the 10,277. **For the lost 8,281 to have been PMC records, that
date's PMC component would have had to stand at roughly 8,973 yesterday and 692 today — a 92% collapse in
one source on one date.** Possible, and consistent with a bulk deposit being redated or withdrawn. **Also
unverifiable, because yesterday's split for that date does not exist.**

**The alternative is no more testable.** `MED` on 26 November is 8,265 today; if MEDLINE lost the records,
it would have had to hold some 16,500 on that date yesterday while its whole bucket **gained** 7,756
overnight, which would require a very large simultaneous gain elsewhere in MEDLINE. **Not impossible.
Not checkable.**

**So the honest statement is that the loss is real, its size is unprecedented in this archive, it coincides
with a large `PMC` shed, and attribution is not available.** The withdrawn association is **back in play and
not re-established** — a single large coincidence after a falsification is not a finding.

**The instrument is fixed rather than the conclusion asserted.** `claude/tools/index_reading.sh` now records
the **per-source split for every day it counts**, not just the total:

```
idx 2026-11-26: 10277 @ 14:07:03
  idx 2026-11-26 MED: 8265
  idx 2026-11-26 PMC: 692
  idx 2026-11-26 PPR: 1306
```

**The next loss of this kind will be attributable. This one is not, and saying so is the whole of what today
can contribute to it.** This is the second time in five days that a missing level has cost an answer: on
24 November no `PMC` delta could be computed because the note had recorded a change and not a level, and
the rule written then — record levels, not just movements — **was applied to the source buckets and not to
the per-day counts.** The same partial fix, again.

## 1. Thirty-seven studies, none of them about what to do

A scoping review of perioperative venous thromboembolism in visceral oncological surgery, by the Arksey and
O'Malley framework with PRISMA-ScR, searching PubMed from 2010 to 2025. **49 studies: 37 clinical — 20
retrospective cohorts, 12 prospective, four meta-analyses and one randomised trial — across oesophageal,
gastric, colorectal, pancreatic and hepatobiliary cancer, plus 12 experimental studies of photoacoustic
flow cytometry.**

**Reported incidence varies enormously: VTE 0.2% to 28.2%, deep vein thrombosis 4.0% to 52%, pulmonary
embolism 0.2% to 3.3%** — with the 52% coming from a single screening-based cohort of 106 patients, which
the review flags, and which is the usual lesson that a screened incidence and a diagnosed incidence are
different quantities.

**The finding worth the slot is an absence.** The review states that **none of the included clinical studies
reported therapeutic management or monitoring following confirmed VTE.** Its own introduction names that as
the hard problem: anticoagulation after a clot sits against the risk of postoperative bleeding. **So 37
clinical studies address how often it happens, how to look for it and how to prevent it, and not one
addresses what to do once it has happened.** That is a more useful result than any of the incidence ranges.

**Three limits, two of which the review declares.** Consistent with scoping methodology, **no critical
appraisal or risk-of-bias assessment was undertaken**, and **data charting was done by one reviewer** while
screening used two. And the third, which it does not flag: **the title gives photoacoustic flow cytometry
equal billing, and that half rests on 12 experimental studies with no clinical data at all.** A technology
with no patients in it is a prospect, not a co-subject.

## 2. The intervention works; no individual device does

Systematic review and meta-analysis of **randomised trials only**, adult ICU patients with central venous
catheters, four databases, January 2000 to August 2025, Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0, random-effects pooling.
**28 trials included, 19 effect sizes suitable for meta-analysis.**

**Catheter-related bloodstream infection was reduced by catheter type or connector modification, OR 0.75
(95% CI 0.59 to 0.94), and by dressing interventions, OR 0.52 (0.28 to 0.95). Antiseptic bathing showed no
significant effect, OR 0.74 (0.38 to 1.41).**

**And then the sentence that complicates all of it: in the catheter technology subgroup analysis, no
individual device technology effect was significant when analysed separately.**

**That is the interesting structure, and it is not a contradiction.** Pooling heterogeneous devices into
"catheter type" produces a significant benefit; asking which catheter dissolves it. Either the subgroups are
underpowered — which is the usual and most likely reading — or the pooled estimate is averaging
genuinely different devices into a number that describes none of them. **The abstract does not distinguish
those, and they have opposite implications for practice:** the first says buy any of them, the second says
the category is not the thing that works. **A dressing effect of 0.52 on a confidence interval reaching
0.95 is the single most actionable number here, and it is also the one with the widest interval.**

## 3. A biomarker with the wrong operating characteristics

Meta-analysis of circulating gelsolin in adult sepsis, six databases plus trial registries to 26 July 2026.
**Eight studies met inclusion.**

**In the seven reporting survival, gelsolin was lower in non-survivors, ratio of means 0.62 (95% CI 0.53 to
0.73), P < 0.00001 — with heterogeneity at I-squared 74%.**

**The diagnostic half is where it falls down.** From three studies: **pooled sensitivity 0.81 (0.71 to
0.89) and specificity 0.61 (0.49 to 0.72), with I-squared 90.6%.** The review's own words for that
heterogeneity are "considerable variability in false-positive classification."

**A specificity of 0.61 means roughly two in five patients without the condition are flagged**, which for a
sepsis rule-in test is close to useless; the sensitivity of 0.81 is not high enough to make it a rule-out
either. **Three studies, heterogeneity above 90%, and an operating point that is neither — that is a
biomarker at the stage of being interesting, not of being used.**

**Credit where it is due: the review says so about its own threshold.** One study's admission cut-off of
113.6 mg/L is reported and then explicitly called exploratory, requiring external validation and possibly
not generalisable given assay and cut-off variation across studies. **That is the correct handling of a
single-study threshold, and it is more often than not handled the other way.**

## 4. Variability associated, mean not, mechanisms speculative

Single-centre prospective observational study, **126 adults, ASA I to III, aged 18 to 65, elective
laparoscopic cholecystectomy.** Bispectral index recorded every minute, variability during maintenance
quantified as standard deviation and coefficient of variation. **Primary outcome the change in
Quality of Recovery-15 from baseline to day 1.**

**Greater variability tracked worse recovery: standard deviation rho −0.253 (95% CI −0.414 to −0.093,
p = 0.004), coefficient of variation rho −0.240 (−0.404 to −0.069, p = 0.007)**, both surviving multivariable
adjustment. **Mean bispectral index was not consistently associated across the two models.**

**Two things make this a hypothesis rather than a result, and the paper says both.** It calls the
associations weak-to-moderate — **a rho of −0.25 is about 6% of shared variance** — and states that the
underlying mechanisms remain speculative. **And the signal appears on one scale only:** no association with
the Nursing Delirium Screening Scale or with pain scores.

**The contrast with mean index is nonetheless the point worth carrying.** If depth of anaesthesia mattered
only through its average, the mean should carry the association and the variability should not. **Here it is
the other way round**, which is at least consistent with the idea that fluctuation rather than level is what
the patient experiences — on 126 patients, one centre, one operation, and one of three outcome scales.

## 5. Twenty-four cases, four dose groups, and a mediation analysis

A retrospective cohort **nested within a prospective cohort**, patients having elective laparoscopic
cholecystectomy allocated to four dexmedetomidine doses: 0, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 micrograms per kilogram. **From
an initial 361 patients, 24 cases of postoperative cognitive dysfunction were matched 1:2 with 48 controls**
on age, sex, education and operative duration. Interleukin-6, tumour necrosis factor alpha, S100-beta,
neuron-specific enolase and brain-derived neurotrophic factor were measured before surgery and at 24 and 48
hours.

**The reported findings are orderly.** At both postoperative timepoints the four inflammatory and injury
markers fell in a dose-dependent manner (p < 0.01) and the neurotrophic factor rose (p < 0.01), with the
high-dose group most changed. **High-dose dexmedetomidine was an independent protective factor against
cognitive dysfunction, odds ratio 0.82 per 0.1 micrograms per kilogram (95% CI 0.74 to 0.91, p < 0.001)**,
and mediation analysis attributed the protection to inhibition of the early inflammatory response.

**The problem is the denominator, and it is not a small one. Twenty-four cases are carrying four dose
groups, five biomarkers, three timepoints, a multivariable logistic regression and a bootstrap mediation
analysis.** That is far more model than data. **An odds ratio quoted per 0.1 micrograms per kilogram makes
the effect look finely resolved across a dose range that contained, at most, a handful of cases per group.**

**And the framing deserves noting: the authors state that their own previous trial already confirmed that
dexmedetomidine reduces this outcome**, so this is an extension of their own positive result rather than an
independent test of it. **The dose-response and the mediation path are both plausible and both deserve a
prospective study with cases in the hundreds.** Reported here because the mechanism question is a real one
and this is the current state of the answer, not because 24 cases settle it.

## The instruments

**Sweep:** four collections over `FIRST_IDATE` 27 and 28 November returning **308, 46, 145 and 17 records,
every one matching its own hit count with shortfall zero.** 516 collected, 491 unique, **490 after the scope
filter, 483 not previously reported.** Seven deduped.

**The alias gate ran with `--record` on all five shortlisted papers**, which is the practice adopted
yesterday. **None was a two-record work today**, after three of five yesterday — but the recorder still
wrote two new pairs into `claude/pmc-doi-map.txt`, which now holds 27, **because it writes a pair whenever
any returned record carries a PMC identifier, including single records that carry one.** So the map grows
faster than the two-record count implies, which is the behaviour wanted.

**One output-clarity note on that tool.** Its "records for this work" line lists record identifiers, while
the recorded pair can come from a PMC identifier held as a *field* on one of those records. **So the output
can read as though a pair were recorded for a work with only one record.** Both behaviours are correct; the
line is ambiguous, and that is recorded rather than quietly tolerated.
