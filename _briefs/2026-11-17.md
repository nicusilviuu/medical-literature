## The filter's first run on the brief, a withdrawal notice, and a bucket that moved without Bookshelf

**The scope filter rebuilt and validated in yesterday's watch had its first run on a literature sweep today,
with the major general journals added to its journal list.** Window 16 to 17 November: **45 records under the
old full-text filter, 25 under the new title-and-journal one, and the retained set contains every candidate
this brief would have wanted.** **The 44% cut is the same order as yesterday's 56% on the guidance window,
and the narrowing has now been applied to both routes.**

**One of the 25 is a record whose abstract is a withdrawal notice.** `10.2174/0115748863420846251028103616`
is titled **"WITHDRAWN: A Meta-Analysis of Efficacy and Safety of Penehyclidine for Postoperative Nausea and
Vomiting"**, and its 254-character abstract reads in full that the authors did not respond to editorial
requests and the article has been withdrawn.

**This archive has met retractions and corrections before but not a withdrawal notice occupying the
abstract field, and it is worth a standing rule.** **A sweep can return a paper that no longer exists, with
its figures replaced by an apology, and the only flag is the word WITHDRAWN at the front of the title.**
**RULE: check the title for a WITHDRAWN or RETRACTED prefix before extracting any figure from a record.**
Had the abstract been longer, or the prefix absent, a meta-analysis of an antiemetic would have looked like
an ordinary lead.

## 1. ABLATION: biatrial maze wins by ten points, with a non-inferiority margin worth examining

**[Biatrial Versus Left Atrial Maze Ablation During Cardiac Valve Surgery for Persistent Atrial
Fibrillation: The ABLATION Trial](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.083005)** · *Circulation* ·
**published 16 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Multicentre, randomised, single-blind. Patients with persistent or long-standing persistent atrial
fibrillation undergoing rheumatic mitral valve surgery, randomised 1:1 to a complete Cox-Maze IV biatrial
procedure or a left-sided lesion set. 323 patients, all analysed by intention to treat — 164 and 159, which
sum exactly. Mean age 56.5, 72.1% women, mean left atrial diameter 51.9 mm.**

> To ensure standardization and consistency of the ablation procedures, surgeons underwent pretrial training
> and certification, and all procedures were videorecorded and centrally reviewed. … At 12 months, 78.7% of
> patients (129 of 164) in the biatrial group and 68.6% (109 of 159) in the LA group were free from atrial
> tachyarrhythmias (adjusted difference, 10.7% [1-sided lower 95% CI, 3%]; P=0.01). Freedom from permanent
> pacemaker implantation was noted in 97.0% (159 of 164) of the biatrial group and 98.1% (156 of 159) of the
> LA group, **meeting the predefined noninferiority criterion with a margin of -5%** (adjusted difference,
> -1.1% [1-sided lower 95% CI, -3.9%]; Pnoninferiority=0.01).

**Every count reconstructs: 129 of 164 is 78.66%, 109 of 159 is 68.55%, and the crude difference of 10.11
points sits just under the adjusted 10.7.** The pacemaker figures likewise: 96.95% and 98.11%, crude
difference −1.16 against an adjusted −1.1.

**The methods deserve explicit credit, because surgical trials rarely do this.** **Surgeons were trained and
certified before the trial, every procedure was videorecorded, and all recordings were centrally
reviewed.** **A trial comparing two lesion sets is a trial of what the surgeon actually did, and this is the
first surgical ablation trial this archive has seen that verified it rather than assumed it.**

**Now the non-inferiority margin, which is where the reading gets harder.** **The margin is −5 percentage
points, and the pacemaker rate in the left-atrial group was 1.9%.** **A margin of five points on a 1.9%
event rate means the trial was prepared to declare biatrial ablation non-inferior even if it raised
pacemaker implantation to 6.9% — a 3.63-fold increase.** **The observed difference was −1.1 points with a
one-sided lower bound of −3.9, so the result clears the margin, and it clears it by 1.1 points on a scale
where the margin itself is 2.6 times the baseline risk.** **A reader who takes "non-inferior for pacemakers"
at face value is accepting a wide tolerance, and the trial does disclose it.**

**Both principal results are reported as one-sided intervals**, which is a legitimate design choice for a
superiority-plus-non-inferiority pair and is declared. **The consequence for the reader is that no upper
bound is available for the 10.7-point benefit** — the trial tells you the effect is at least 3 points and
not how large it might be.

**One more limit, and it is about who was studied.** **This is rheumatic mitral valve disease: 72.1% women,
mean age 56.5, mean left atrial diameter 51.9 mm.** **That is a population common in much of the world and
uncommon in the settings where most surgical ablation guidance is written**, and a left atrium of 51.9 mm is
large. **The result should transfer to degenerative mitral disease with caution.**

**My reading.** **For persistent atrial fibrillation in rheumatic valve surgery, do the full biatrial
lesion set: ten points of absolute benefit on freedom from arrhythmia is a large effect for a surgical
decision that costs operative time rather than hardware.** **The pacemaker question is reassuring in its
point estimate and only loosely bounded by its margin**, and it connects directly to the Society of Thoracic
Surgeons guideline this archive found on 14 November, where surgical ablation guidance exists and the
evidence behind the lesion set did not.

## 2. A confidence interval that excludes the null and a prediction interval that does not

**[Sugammadex versus neostigmine in adults aged 60 years and older undergoing
surgery](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2026.101996)** · *Anaesthesia Critical Care & Pain Medicine* ·
**published 16 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Eight randomised trials, 851 participants — about 106 per trial — of whom 50.2% received sugammadex.
PROSPERO registered. Primary outcome postoperative residual neuromuscular blockade.**

> Sugammadex was associated with a lower risk of PRNB (RR 0.34 [95% CI: 0.18; 0.64]; p < 0.001; I² = 54%),
> with moderate between-study heterogeneity; **however, the 95% prediction interval (0.11; 1.03) included
> the null value.** Sugammadex significantly reduced PACU length of stay (MD -8.84 [95% CI: -12.18; -5.51],
> p < 0.001, I2 = 0%), hospital length of stay (MD -1.12 [95% CI: -2.01; -0.24], p < 0.01, I2 = 16%), and
> hypoxemia (RR = 0.61 [95% CI: 0.38; 0.97]; p = 0.04, I2 = 0%). … **Although the primary analyses suggested
> reductions in hypoxemia and hospital length of stay, these associations were not statistically significant
> after the Hartung-Knapp adjustment.**

**This archive has a standing rule to read the prediction interval and not only the confidence interval, and
this is the cleanest example of it yet — because the authors applied the rule to themselves.**
**The confidence interval on residual blockade, 0.18 to 0.64, reconstructs to p = 0.0009. The prediction
interval, 0.11 to 1.03, reconstructs to p = 0.059 and crosses one. Its log width is 2.237 against the
confidence interval's 1.269 — 1.76 times wider.** **The confidence interval answers "what is the average
effect across these eight trials?" The prediction interval answers "what effect should I expect in the next
hospital?" — and the answer to the second question includes no effect at all.** With I² of 54%, that is the
honest summary.

**And the authors go further in a way that will be widely ignored.** **Two of the three secondary positives
— hypoxaemia and hospital length of stay — did not survive the Hartung-Knapp adjustment**, which widens
intervals when the number of studies is small. **So "sugammadex reduces hypoxaemia in older adults" is a
result the paper withdraws inside its own abstract.** **A reader quoting the risk ratio of 0.61 is quoting a
number its authors have already qualified.**

**What survives is narrow and probably real: less residual blockade on average, and 8.84 minutes less in
recovery with zero heterogeneity.** **Nine minutes of recovery time is a genuine operational benefit and
nobody should pretend it is a patient-centred outcome.**

**Two cautions.** **851 patients across eight trials is a small pool for the outcomes that matter**, and the
single significant hard outcome — hypoxaemia — is the one the adjustment removed. **And "adults aged 60 and
older" spans the population in which residual blockade matters most without stratifying it.**

**My reading.** **Sugammadex reliably reverses and reliably saves recovery time; the evidence that it
prevents hypoxaemia or shortens hospital stay in older adults does not survive the authors' own
sensitivity analysis.** **This is also the second sugammadex document in two days** — yesterday's watch
carried a cohort showing Spanish anaesthetists use it in 86% of patients with stage 4 or 5 kidney disease
against the product information. **Together: a drug used almost universally, including where its label says
not to, whose measurable advantages are efficiency rather than outcome.**

## 3. Frailty does not kill these patients, it leaves them ventilated

**[Baseline frailty severity and survival to ICU discharge without ongoing invasive mechanical ventilation
in patients with sepsis](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40560-026-00964-6)** · *Journal of Intensive Care* ·
**published 16 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Prospective multicentre Korean Sepsis Alliance registry, September 2019 to December 2023. 2,204 adults
with sepsis who received invasive ventilation on intensive care day 1; Clinical Frailty Scale 9 excluded.
1,458 (66.2%) had a score of 5 to 8. Mixed-effects logistic regression with a centre random intercept,
adjusted for age, sex, comorbidity index, day-1 SOFA score and infection site.**

> The primary outcome was achieved by 1,120 patients (50.8%); **crude rates were 52.9% in patients with CFS
> 1-4 and 49.7% in those with CFS 5-8 (P = 0.152). Frailty was associated with lower adjusted odds of the
> primary outcome (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 0.73; 95% CI 0.59-0.90).** Compared with CFS 1-4, the estimate
> for CFS 5 was close to the null (OR, 1.08; 95% CI 0.78-1.49), while the odds were lower for CFS 6, 7, and
> 8 … In pairwise analyses of ICU discharge status, **frailty was associated with higher odds of survival
> with ongoing invasive MV versus survival without ongoing invasive MV (adjusted OR, 1.94; 95% CI
> 1.26-2.98); the corresponding estimate for ICU death versus survival without ongoing invasive MV was 1.22
> (95% CI 0.97-1.53).**

**The decomposition in that last sentence is the paper and it is beautifully done.** **The odds of dying
rather than recovering off the ventilator are 1.22 with an interval crossing one, reconstructing to p =
0.087 — a null. The odds of surviving still ventilated rather than recovering off it are 1.94, p = 0.0025 —
decisively positive.** **So frailty does not move these patients toward death. It moves them toward
survival in a dependent state.** **That is a different clinical conversation from the one a mortality
analysis would have started**, and it is exactly the kind of competing-outcome split this archive has asked
for repeatedly when a composite endpoint lumps death and dependency together.

**Two things about the modelling that cut in opposite directions.**

**The crude comparison is null and the adjusted one is significant.** 52.9% against 49.7%, a 3.2-point
difference at P = 0.152, becomes an odds ratio of 0.73 at p = 0.0035 once age, comorbidity, severity and
infection site are in the model. **Adjustment usually shrinks an association; here it created one** — which
means frailty's effect was masked by variables correlated with it, most obviously age. **That is a
legitimate result and it is also the configuration most vulnerable to model misspecification, because the
entire finding lives in the adjustment.**

**And the chosen dichotomy includes a category with no effect.** **The exposure is "CFS 5 to 8 versus 1 to
4", yet CFS 5 by itself gives an odds ratio of 1.08 (0.78–1.49, p = 0.64).** The signal starts at CFS 6.
**So the headline 0.73 is diluted by a stratum that contributes nothing, and the real threshold is one point
higher than the exposure definition.** The spline analysis makes the gradient explicit and linear —
58.6% at CFS 1 falling to 43.3% at CFS 8, 15.3 points, with no evidence of non-linearity (p = 0.55).

**The authors' closing sentence is the right one and unusually disciplined: these findings "support
considering frailty severity when discussing recovery and care needs, but do not establish a treatment
threshold or a causal effect of frailty."** **This archive has spent the fortnight noting documents that
reach a threshold and decline to define it; here is a paper that finds a gradient and declines to turn it
into a threshold, which is the same restraint pointed the other way.**

## 4. Four blood-pressure trajectories, and a reference class holding eight events

**[Intraoperative Mean Arterial Pressure Trajectory Classes and Postoperative Acute Kidney Injury After
Sleeve Gastrectomy](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11695-026-09026-0)** · *Obesity Surgery* · **published
16 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Retrospective single-centre cohort, 1,379 adults having laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy, January 2016 to
March 2025. Mean arterial pressure from paired invasive recordings every five minutes; piecewise latent
class mixed modelling with a 30-minute knot. Kidney injury taken from database-coded KDIGO creatinine
labels.**

> Four trajectory classes were identified: moderate-decline/stable (C1, n = 492), steep-decline/low (C2, n =
> 177), gradually rising (C3, n = 293), and decline-rebound (C4, n = 417). Recorded AKI occurred in 78
> patients (5.7%), with incidences of 5.1%, 12.4%, 2.7%, and 5.5%, respectively (P < 0.001). … **compared
> with C3, C2 had higher adjusted AKI odds (OR, 3.21; 95% CI, 1.31-7.84; P = 0.011)**, whereas C1 (OR, 2.22;
> P = 0.059) and C4 (OR, 1.54; P = 0.323) were not individually significant. Apparent AUCs were 0.692,
> 0.730, and 0.743 across expanded models; **the combined extended-bootstrap-corrected AUC was 0.717.**

**The class sizes sum to 1,379 exactly and the overall rate reconstructs to 5.66%.** **The event counts
behind each class are the thing to compute, because the abstract gives only percentages: C1 about 25 events,
C2 about 22, C3 about 8, and C4 about 23.**

**So the headline odds ratio of 3.21 compares 22 events against 8 — and C3, the reference, is both the
lowest-risk class and the one with the fewest events.** **This archive's standing rule is that a reference
category is never neutral, and choosing the best-performing class as the comparator maximises every odds
ratio in the table.** **Against C3, even C1 — a stable trajectory — reaches an odds ratio of 2.22, which is
a hint that the reference is doing as much work as the exposure.**

**The clinically interesting result is one the paper does not dwell on: C3, the class whose pressure rose
gradually through the operation, had the lowest kidney injury rate at 2.7%.** **A rising intraoperative
pressure is at least as plausibly a marker of a patient who is doing well as a cause of renal protection**,
and in a retrospective cohort the two cannot be separated. **The steep-decline class at 12.4% is the finding
that matches physiological intuition, and it rests on 22 events.**

**Credit for the discrimination reporting.** **Apparent areas under the curve of 0.692 to 0.743 are reported
alongside a bootstrap-corrected 0.717, and the conclusion calls the incremental prognostic value
"modest".** **Reporting an optimism-corrected figure rather than the apparent one is what this archive wants
and rarely gets.**

**Two limits worth stating plainly.** **Kidney injury was ascertained from database-coded labels rather than
adjudicated**, so the outcome inherits the coding; and **this is one centre over nine years, during which
anaesthetic practice for bariatric surgery changed.** The authors ask for prospective external validation,
which is the correct request.

## 5. Empiric antifungal therapy: no benefit, longer stays, and no benefit even when yeast grew

**[Empiric Antifungal Therapy in Critically Ill Patients Undergoing Surgery for Severe Intra-Abdominal
Infection](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accpm.2026.101997)** · *Anaesthesia Critical Care & Pain Medicine* ·
**published 16 November 2026** · paywalled; abstract quoted below

**Retrospective single-centre data-warehouse cohort, 1,061 adults admitted to intensive care after surgery
for intra-abdominal infection, 2018 to 2024. Systemic antifungal therapy within 24 hours of surgery against
none. Propensity-score overlap weighting. Primary endpoint 30-day all-cause mortality.**

> Empiric antifungal therapy was initiated in 46.0% of patients. Peritoneal sampling was available in 957
> patients (90.2%); 21.4% had no organism identified and 25.2% had a yeast-positive sample. In adjusted
> analyses, empiric therapy was not associated with reduced 30-day mortality (OR 1.53, 95% CI 0.83-2.78),
> lower day-5 renal SOFA score, lower KDIGO stage, or fewer reinterventions. **Findings were consistent in
> patients with Peritonitis Score ≥3, with at least one identified organism, and with a yeast-positive
> sample. ICU and hospital stays were longer in treated patients after weighting.**

**Four hundred and eighty-eight patients received empiric antifungals and nothing measurable followed.** The
mortality odds ratio of 1.53 reconstructs to p = 0.168 — a null with its point estimate in the harm
direction, which this archive's standing rule reads as confounding by indication rather than as drug harm:
**the sicker patient gets the antifungal, and overlap weighting on measured covariates cannot remove
severity it did not measure.** **The longer stays in treated patients are the same signal.**

**The subgroup that matters is the yeast-positive one, and it is the most interesting sentence in the
abstract.** **A quarter of sampled patients grew yeast from the peritoneum, and empiric therapy was not
associated with better outcomes in them either.** **If empiric treatment worked anywhere, it should work
where the organism was present** — so a consistent null in that subgroup is a stronger argument than the
overall null. **It is also where the confounding is weakest, because the indication and the pathogen
coincide.**

**Two limits.** **Retrospective, single centre, and an exposure defined as treatment within 24 hours of
surgery** — which classifies by timing rather than by intent, so a patient treated at 30 hours on culture
results sits in the untreated group. **And 21.4% of sampled patients grew nothing at all**, which says
something about how often "severe intra-abdominal infection" is a surgical rather than a microbiological
diagnosis.

**My reading.** **This does not license withholding antifungals from a patient with candidaemia or septic
shock from a yeast-positive peritoneum; it argues against starting them reflexively within 24 hours of
every laparotomy for severe intra-abdominal infection.** **The null in the yeast-positive subgroup is the
result that should provoke a trial**, and the absence of any renal signal is worth noting given that the
drugs in question are nephrotoxic in some classes.
