## 1. The EEG target was pushed up, then pushed down, and delirium did not move

**Journal · Published:** *British Journal of Anaesthesia*, **22 and 23 September 2025** — **thirteen months
old**, 2 and 1 citations. **Two randomised arms of one trial, AlphaMax, ANZCTR 12617001354370**, each with
**200 patients aged 60 or over**, standardised desflurane–fentanyl anaesthesia. **Both arms reduced their
sample size for feasibility and are explicitly underpowered for delirium.** Carried on the queue since
7 October.
Gaskell, Banerji, Pullon, Termaat, García, Sleigh (part 1); Banerji, Gaskell, Voss, Pullon, Termaat, García,
Sleigh (part 2)

> **Part 1 — EEG-guided titration during maintenance.** Low alpha power has been associated with **a
> vulnerable brain phenotype at greater risk of postoperative delirium.** … The titration group **received
> more fentanyl (650 [500–850] μg vs 500 [350–600] μg, P < 0.0001) and less desflurane (3.9 [0.7] vs 4.4
> [0.8] vol%, P < 0.0001)** … **Oscillatory alpha power was greater in the titration group early after
> surgical incision (+0.8 dB, P = 0.008), but this effect was not sustained as surgery progressed.** …
> **The incidence of PACU delirium was similar between groups (37% vs 33%, P = 0.553).**
>
> **Part 2 — propofol infusion during emergence.** **Propofol infusion was associated with lower alpha power
> (−3.06 [4.1] dB vs −1.82 [3.6] dB, P = 0.026) and more burst suppression (66% vs 34%, P < 0.001) during
> emergence.** **The incidence of PACU delirium was 36.7% in the propofol group and 33.3% (P = 0.614)** in
> routine care.

**Interpretation. Read the two arms together and the result is a dissociation, not two null trials.** Part 1
**raised** alpha power, transiently and by a small amount. Part 2 **lowered** it, substantially, and doubled
burst suppression. **Delirium in the recovery unit was 37%, 33%, 36.7% and 33.3% across the four groups** —
flat, in both directions, while the monitored variable was deliberately moved each way.

**That is the cleanest separation this archive has recorded between a prognostic marker and a treatment
target.** And part 2's exploratory analysis preserves the marker: patients with disorganised thinking had
**lower preoperative Montreal Cognitive Assessment scores, lower body weight, higher surgical risk scores,
lower intraoperative alpha power, more delta inertia during emergence, and longer stays.** **So low alpha
power still identifies the vulnerable brain. Raising it does not protect that brain.** The vulnerability was
never in the oscillation.

**This is the third document in the archive's monitoring thread and it completes a set.** The
videolaryngoscopy finding was a **device that improved a measurement while degrading its diagnostic
meaning**; the remimazolam crossover trial reported on [11 October](/medical-literature/brief/2026-10-11/)
was a **drug that changed what a depth index means without changing the patient's state**; **this is a target
that can be moved without moving the outcome it predicts.** **Three distinct ways for a number to mislead:
the measurement changes, the calibration changes, or the number turns out not to be on the causal path.**

**The honest limits, which both papers put in their Results sections rather than their discussions.** **Both
were underpowered for delirium after a feasibility-driven sample-size reduction**, so a modest effect is not
excluded by either. **Part 1's intervention barely worked** — alpha power rose only early and not
sustainably, so it is as much a failure of the titration protocol as a test of the hypothesis. **And part 2
achieved its EEG effect in the wrong direction for a protective claim**: more burst suppression is itself
associated with delirium in the observational literature, and delirium still did not rise. **That null is
arguably the more interesting of the two.**

**One mechanistic detail worth keeping.** Part 1's titration **abolished the desflurane–alpha correlation**
(r = −0.020 against −0.228 in controls) — so the protocol did change the relationship between drug and EEG
even where it failed to raise the oscillation. **The titration worked on the physiology and not on the
patient**, which is the whole finding in one line.

[Part 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2025.07.045) · [Part 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2025.07.051)
— both paywalled beyond the abstract.

## 2. A meta-analysis that argues against its own significant result

**Journal · Published:** *Medical Sciences*, **7 September 2026** — five weeks old. **Open access**
(PMC13609289). **PROSPERO CRD420261334243.** Updates a previous meta-analysis with searches March 2024 to
March 2026, restricting the intervention to **hyperoncotic albumin ≥20%** and the comparator **exclusively to
crystalloids**.
Wiedermann, Turcato, Zaboli, Joannidis

> **Eight RCTs (n = 3707)** provided the data; **all administered 20% albumin.** Overall, albumin was **not
> robustly associated with reduced mortality (RR 0.94; I² = 0%), significant only under the pre-registered
> Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman correction (0.91–0.98) but null under the standard, truncated-HKSJ, and
> fixed-effects models (0.87–1.03, p = 0.17), identifying this as a low-heterogeneity artifact.** In septic
> shock (k = 6), the effect was **larger and consistent (RR 0.90, 0.82–0.99, p = 0.03)**, with **no benefit in
> sepsis without shock (RR 1.10; interaction p = 0.08). The certainty was low (GRADE).**

**Interpretation. The authors pre-registered an estimator, got a significant result from it, and then
published the conclusion that the significance is an artefact of their own method.** That is the opposite of
the usual direction of travel in a meta-analysis, and it deserves saying plainly: **reporting the same
estimate under five estimators, and leading with the four that are null rather than the one that is not, is
how this should be done.**

**The mechanism is worth understanding because it is a trap this archive flagged yesterday from the other
side.** The Hartung-Knapp correction adjusts confidence intervals for uncertainty in the
between-study variance; **when heterogeneity is near zero, that adjustment becomes unstable and can produce an
interval narrower than the standard method.** **With I² = 0% across eight trials, HKSJ is the wrong tool**,
and the authors say so. Yesterday's phenobarbital meta-analysis was the mirror image: **a precise pooled
estimate with I² = 0 that nobody questioned.** **Near-zero heterogeneity does not make a result solid; it can
make the estimator misbehave.**

**The subgroup claim is the part to resist, and the interaction p-value is why.** Septic shock **RR 0.90
(0.82–0.99)** against sepsis without shock **RR 1.10**, with **interaction p = 0.08** — which does not
support treating these as different effects. **This archive's test for a believable interaction, set out on
6 October, is replication in another cohort or specificity against modifiers that should not show the
effect.** **This interaction has neither**, and a subgroup of six trials inside an overall null, selected on
severity, is the configuration that has failed most often in critical care.

**What survives is the guideline-consistent reading the authors give:** **no statistically significant
mortality reduction, low certainty, and adequately powered trials still required** — aligned with the 2026
Surviving Sepsis Campaign's position of permitting albumin case by case without recommending it routinely.
**Which makes the next item the natural companion.**

[Read the meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3390/medsci14050550) — open access.

## 3. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign's dissent and its defence, published the same day

**Journals · Published:** *American Journal of Emergency Medicine* and *Polish Archives of Internal
Medicine*, **both 15 September 2026** — four weeks old. **Neither has been reported here**, and the
coincidence of date is the reason to take them as one item.
Spiegel, Haycock, Miller, Weingart, Farkas, Ajmo, Rola (the dissent); Oczkowski, Alhazzani, Alshamsi,
Belley-Côté, Dionne, Fox-Robichaud, Honarmand, Hylander Møller, Rochwerg, Jaeschke (the companion)

> **The dissent.** The SSC Guidelines **"once represented what was considered the pinnacle of care"** …
> **"over the past decade, successive iterations of the guidelines have drifted from what is now considered
> optimal care"** … **"the guidelines have shifted from recommendations intended to guide and support
> physicians based on the best available evidence, to mandated 'bundles' which exert substantial influence
> over what is considered 'standard of care'"** … **"intended to serve as a refutation of sorts. A rebuttal
> to the most controversial and influential recommendations"** … offering guidance on **"how clinicians might
> move beyond the protocolized, metric-based care that has become the current standard."**
>
> **The companion, by the guideline's own methodologists.** **"Some recommendations point to new directions
> … characterized by a more flexible approach to the assessment and treatment of individual patient with
> careful consideration of the benefit of critical care and post-ICU recovery interventions"** … **"a hybrid
> approach of empiric early treatment alongside a nuanced, personalized stream of care, informed by patient
> values and preferences"** … **"We emphasize key methodological points which inform potentially
> controversial recommendations."**

**Interpretation. Set the two abstracts side by side and the striking thing is how much they agree.** The
dissent's objection is that the guidelines have become **mandated bundles and metric-based care**, and asks
clinicians to move beyond protocolisation toward individual judgement. The methodologists describe the 2026
edition as **a more flexible, personalised approach informed by patient values.** **Both documents are
arguing for the same direction of travel. They disagree about whether the guidelines have already turned.**

**That reframing matters, because the usual way this argument is reported — guideline authors versus
critics — obscures the actual question**, which is empirical and narrow: **has the 2026 edition loosened the
bundle, and will the quality metrics built on earlier editions follow?** A guideline can recommend
individualisation while the regulatory and audit apparatus attached to its previous version continues to
require a bundle. **Neither abstract claims that apparatus has changed**, and the dissent's strongest point is
about the apparatus rather than the recommendations.

**A document class worth recording.** This archive has catalogued guidelines, consensus statements,
standards, practical guidelines, care pathways and algorithm companions. **A formal published dissent is new**
— not a letter, but a `Review`-typed rebuttal with seven named authors in a different journal, appearing
simultaneously with the authors' own commentary. **It is a healthier mechanism than correspondence**, and it
gives a reader something no guideline provides: the case against, written by people who treat the same
patients.

**And the self-aware part is in the dissent's own framing.** It offers **"guidance on how clinicians might
move beyond the protocolized, metric-based care"** — which is to say it offers guidance against guidance.
**This fortnight's archive has reported four documents whose stated rationale was implementation rather than
new evidence**, and a Korean rehabilitation guideline with thirteen of fifteen recommendations conditional.
**The dissent's complaint is not that guidance exists; it is that conditional recommendations harden into
mandatory metrics on the way to the bedside.** **That is the most important sentence in either paper and
neither this archive nor the dissent has evidence on how often it happens.**
